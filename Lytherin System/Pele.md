**Pele** is an ultra-hot rocky world in the [[Lytherin System]], maintained near 3000 K by manufactured stellar-energy collection and delivery infrastructure. Its global magma ocean, iron fountains and rock-vapor atmosphere are among the system's most conspicuous artificial environmental extremes. Direct starlight at its orbit cannot sustain this surface temperature by itself.

Pele remains uninhabited. It is observed from orbit and remote facilities, while human civilization occupies the moons of [[Freya]] and [[Frey]]. The planet is named for Pele, the Hawaiian goddess of volcanoes, fire and creation.

## System Context: Lytherin System

The close F-type binary has total mass 3.3 M☉, combined luminosity 11 L☉ and a relative semimajor axis of 0.1 AU. Pele is the innermost of six intact planets. Outside it lie Sunder's debris belt, [[Jaspis]], the twin gas giants [[Freya]] and [[Frey]], [[Jötunn]] and [[Zucchero]], with an outer ice belt beginning around 12 AU. Manufacture of the system architecture is established; the identity of its builders and the purpose of Pele's extreme heating are not specified.

## Orbital and Physical Characteristics

At 0.4 AU, Pele's Kepler period is approximately 50.87 days. Its mass and radius imply a dense silicate–metal interior and surface gravity slightly above 2 g. The close binary produces periodically varying irradiation and gravitational forcing. A slow, approximately synchronized spin is a possible tidal state, but an exact fixed hemisphere facing two moving stars is not assumed; the heating swarm illuminates the full planet independently of its spin.

## Manufactured Heat Budget

At the planet's orbital distance the direct stellar flux is approximately 93,600 W/m². A Bond albedo of 0.1 yields only about 2.1 × 10¹⁹ W of globally absorbed natural starlight. A blackbody at 3000 K with Pele's radius radiates approximately **4.59 × 10²¹ W**. The adopted heating design therefore delivers approximately **4.57 × 10²¹ W of additional absorbed power**.

A distributed reflector/collector swarm redirects stellar energy toward the world from multiple directions. For a nominal 80% net collection-to-absorbed-power efficiency, the effective collecting area at 0.4 AU is about **6.1 × 10¹⁶ m²**, equivalent in area to a disc about **279,000 km across**. This is roughly **1.36 millionths of the binary's total luminosity**. These numbers are engineering scale requirements, not a completed optical, orbital, materials or control design. The two extended stellar sources, collector waste heat, radiation pressure, delivery geometry and stationkeeping must be accommodated.

The reference is a 3000-K global magma world whose extreme heat rests on a quantified energy supply. Local focusing, eruptions and newly exposed melt permit hotter patches and fountains. There is no unique highest possible temperature once external engineering is allowed: still higher sustained temperatures would demand more power and increase vaporization, atmospheric loss and maintenance. The 3000-K operating reference and local 3500-K extremes are a severe but finite design choice rather than a claim to an absolute physical maximum. If the energy-delivery infrastructure fails, Pele cools; its core is not a perpetual replacement power source.

The blackbody estimate defines a conservative reference scale, not a solved rock-vapor atmosphere. Atmospheric opacity, effective emitting radius, spectral emissivity and surface–atmosphere exchange can change the delivered power needed for a specified surface temperature. Continued mass loss requires a separate evolutionary calculation before this state can be claimed to persist for a billion years.

## Surface and Atmosphere

A restless magma ocean of silica-, magnesium- and iron-bearing silicate melt contains metallic iron droplets and localized iron-rich fountains. Surface chemistry varies as material evaporates, condenses and mixes. The dynamic atmosphere contains vaporized rock and metal species including silicon-bearing vapor, sodium, iron and magnesium, with abundances controlled by temperature and melt composition. It is continuously renewed rather than a static atmosphere of unchanging composition.

Ionized iron and other metallic species rise in volcanic jets and thermal updrafts. Magnetic interactions, stellar-wind forcing and electrical discharges form luminous ferrous filaments: deep reds, molten golds and violet hues, sometimes sweeping across large sky regions. The plasma's excitation temperature is distinct from the underlying magma temperature. Their precise spectral strengths and dimensions remain fictional observational targets requiring emission modelling.

Convection and charged-vapor flows create magneto-acoustic disturbances and sculpted vapor clouds. The resulting incandescent spectacle remains the planet's celebrated “slow ballet of incandescent metal.” A global 0.5–1-T natural surface field is not required for the display and is not adopted.

## Internal Structure and Magnetism

Pele has an iron–nickel core with plausible light-element contributions from sulfur, carbon and silicon, beneath a refractory mantle containing titanium-bearing minerals. Copper and other trace metals may occur without being assigned extraordinary dynamo properties. Composition, convection, rotation, core cooling and any inner-core crystallization jointly determine whether a dynamo operates. Differential rotation and tidal forcing may contribute, but no independent unlimited reservoir is supplied by them.

| Layer | Composition and physical role | Thermal and dynamical interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Magma ocean / vapor interface | Silicate melt, iron droplets and volatile rock species; primary vapor source | Surface ~3000 K reference, local ~2800–3500 K; vigorous exchange with the atmosphere |
| Mantle | Hot silicate mantle with titanium-bearing phases and pressure-dependent melting | Conducts and convects heat; neither TiO₂ opacity nor the word “supercritical” establishes a universal insulating mechanism |
| Upper outer core | Liquid Fe–Ni with S, C and Si; electrical conductor | Buoyancy and differential flows may support dynamo action; candidate several-thousand-K temperatures require an interior model |
| Lower outer core | Denser iron alloy with possible stratification and shear | Core convection and stable layers depend on composition and heat flux; no prescribed relativistic flow |
| Central core | High-pressure Fe–Ni-rich interior, potentially including a solid inner region | Solid versus liquid phase, crystallization and an illustrative 7000–8000-K deep-core scale remain interior-model questions |

Radiogenic heating, formation heat and cooling contribute to the interior. They do not imply a surface at the core's temperature. Uranium–iron eutectics, thorium alloys or potassium compounds do not acquire extraordinary decay rates through alloying; radioisotopes are not assumed concentrated into a magical planetary reactor. TaAs-like room-temperature topological conductivity is not extrapolated to intact crystals in a molten planetary core. Bulk fluid velocities remain ordinary subsonic geophysical flows, not near-relativistic currents.

Stellar-wind induction and externally driven currents can supply local Ohmic heating and power part of the plasma display. This energy is drawn from the wind or heating infrastructure; it is not recycled without losses into a self-sustaining dynamo/thermal loop.

## Scientific Interest

Pele offers fictional research opportunities in magma oceans, chemical fractionation, vaporized rock, externally driven plasmas and extreme planetary engineering. Its manufacture makes the heat budget coherent without claiming that its detailed atmosphere or dynamo has been empirically validated. The environmental design separates external power, internal heat, mechanical work, magnetic energy and emitted radiation.

## Cultural and Nomenclature Notes

The volcanic goddess's name reflects the iron fountains and searing magma landscape. Pele is a remote spectacle within the heavily colonized Lytherin system; it has no permanent surface population.

## Physical basis

At 0.4 AU direct starlight supplies only about 2.1×10¹⁹ W after a nominal Bond albedo of 0.1, while a 1.4-R⊕ blackbody at 3000 K emits about 4.59×10²¹ W, so the additional absorbed power is 4.57×10²¹ W. An 80%-efficient collection and delivery budget needs about 6.1×10¹⁶ m² of effective collecting area at that orbital distance, equivalent to a 279,000-km disc and about 1.36×10⁻⁶ of the binary luminosity: an enormous distributed swarm, not a single rigid mirror or an ordinary internal dynamo. The stellar supply is adequate in this arithmetic sense. Collector orbits, angular-source concentration limits, optical materials, waste heat, radiation pressure, aiming, collisions, delivered spectrum and atmospheric energy exchange are unsolved engineering, and the blackbody figure is a budget scale, not an atmosphere model. Plasma excitation can have a different temperature from the molten surface. Lava-world comparisons are physical context only, not evidence for an observed manufactured planet ([Kite et al., atmosphere–interior exchange](https://doi.org/10.3847/0004-637X/828/2/80); [two-phase magma oceans](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.02864)). No claim of billion-year survival under this evaporative regime is made, and the deep-core temperatures and phases are hypotheses, not derived layers.

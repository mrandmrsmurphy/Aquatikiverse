Planetary system: [[Lytherin System]]

**Lytherin** is a binary star system in the constellation of Cygnus. It is composed of two nearly identical F-type main-sequence stars locked in a tight binary orbit separated by only 0.1 AU. The system as a whole has a combined luminosity of approximately 11 times that of the Sun (11 L☉), and hosts a diverse array of planets and debris fields in a manufactured circumbinary (P-type) configuration supported by short orbital screens.

## Stellar Evolution

Both stars in the Lytherin system are mid-mass F-type stars, with approximate masses of 1.6–1.7 times that of Earth's sun. The adopted luminosities are 6.0 and 5.0 L☉, with radii 1.70 and 1.60 R☉. Stefan–Boltzmann temperatures are approximately 6930 and 6820 K, consistent with early-F descriptions. With a combined output of ~11 L☉, the system can be treated approximately as a single source for mean flux far outside the binary, although the stellar disks remain visibly distinct at many orbital phases, due to the close (0.1 AU) separation of its components.

As main-sequence F-type stars, Lytherin A and B are currently engaged in core hydrogen fusion via the CNO cycle, more efficient at these masses than the proton-proton chain dominant in lower-mass G and K stars. Their expected main-sequence lifetimes are short compared to the Sun's, estimated at approximately 2.5–3.5 billion years. 

Post-main-sequence evolution will likely result in each star expanding into a red giant, followed by planetary nebula ejection and white dwarf formation. Because of their tight mutual orbit, interactions during later stages could potentially produce mass transfer episodes or even a common envelope phase.

## Planetary System

Lytherin is a deliberately manufactured stellar environment, arranged to support many inhabited worlds through selected orbital commensurabilities and atmospheric stewardship. Manufacture describes the system architecture; it does not establish that its separate biosphere originated artificially. The builders and construction epoch are not specified.

## Notes

Lytherin's high total luminosity creates a relatively wide habitable zone, roughly centered around 3 AU. The presence of multiple gas giants may have played a role in clearing the system of excessive debris interior to 2 AU, although the survival of the pulverized planetoid belt (Sunder) suggests either a recent collision or unusual formation history.

Despite their short main-sequence lifespans, Lytherin A and B offer a brief but stable window for the development of planetary environments. The binary has small orbital eccentricity; stellar activity, binary viewing geometry and main-sequence brightening still affect irradiation. Climate infrastructure must accommodate that evolution.

The approximate visual magnitudes use the adopted luminosities, an illustrative early-F bolometric correction of −0.05 mag, Mbol☉ = 4.74 and the 19.3-pc distance modulus; they are not independently fitted spectra. The 1-Gyr age and stellar mass/radius/luminosity tuples are an internally consistent setting reference, not a computed stellar evolution track.

## Physical basis

**Stars.** The adopted tuple is stellar A 1.70 M☉, 1.70 R☉, 6.0 L☉, about 6929 K (approximately F1–F2 V) and stellar B 1.60 M☉, 1.60 R☉, 5.0 L☉, about 6824 K (approximately F2 V), for a combined 3.30 M☉ and 11.0 L☉, in a 0.100-AU, e = 0.001, 6.358-day relative orbit. The total luminosity is allocated 6 + 5 L☉, and radii of 1.7 and 1.6 R☉ keep the Stefan–Boltzmann temperatures early-F; smaller radii with these luminosities would imply cooler F5/F6 stars. These are a consistent setting tuple, not a stellar-evolution or metallicity fit, and the spectral subclasses are approximate. The present age (~1 Gyr) is separate from the main-sequence lifetime scale (~2.5–3.5 Gyr, model-dependent), from Sunder's impact 500 Myr after ignition, from Jaspis's ~400-Myr biosphere and from the Empire's centuries-long history; the manufacture epoch is unspecified.

**Photometry and location.** Illustrative V magnitudes use M_bol,☉ = 4.74, a −0.05-mag early-F bolometric correction and negligible extinction: A has Mv ≈ 2.85 (mv ≈ 4.28) and B Mv ≈ 3.05 (mv ≈ 4.48). These are reference estimates, not separately observed photometry ([Pecaut & Mamajek](https://www.pas.rochester.edu/~mpecaut/tmp/pecaut_mamajek_2013.pdf) give the spectral calibration). The system lies 19.3 pc (about 63 light-years) away at J2000 RA 19h44m00s, Dec +36°15′00″ in Cygnus, which gives Galactic Cartesian coordinates of about (6.3, 18.1, 2.1) pc with +X toward the Galactic center.

**Limits.** Flux values use the semimajor-axis reference with central-mass Kepler periods, and the stellar tuple is not a stellar-evolution calculation.

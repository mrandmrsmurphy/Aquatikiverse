**Jaspis** is a high-mass terrestrial exoplanet orbiting the twin F-type stars of the [[Lytherin System]]. Also designated **Lytherin c**, it receives 2.75 times Earth’s stellar flux, at approximately 2.0 astronomical units (AU) from the stellar barycenter. Because of this location, its surface environment is extreme, with a dense, high-pressure carbon-rich atmosphere, a nominal mean surface temperature near 450 K and gravity of ~1.33 g, with volcanic provinces reaching 900 K and higher.

Jaspis is roughly 3 Earth masses and 1.5 Earth radii, resulting in a bulk density significantly greater than Earth’s and gravity of about 1.33 g; its toxic, hot atmosphere precludes unprotected human habitation. The planet rotates rapidly, with a 20-hour day, and orbits the Lytherin binary every 1.56 Earth years. The orbit is nearly circular (eccentricity ~0.02) and co-planar with the system's ecliptic.

## Stellar Environment
The Lytherin system consists of a close binary of two F-type main-sequence stars, each approximately 1.6–1.7 solar masses. The pair orbits one another at a separation of ~0.1 AU, and from the perspective of Jaspis, the two stars almost appear as a single intense source of light. Their combined luminosity (~11 L☉) drives powerful photochemical and thermal processes in the planet’s atmosphere.

## Atmosphere
Jaspis possesses a thick, nitrogen-dominated atmosphere with a total surface pressure of approximately 7 bar. Carbon monoxide (CO), acetylene (C₂H₂), and hydrogen cyanide (HCN) are major secondary components, contributing to both the greenhouse effect and complex surface chemistry. ## Surface and Geology
The surface of Jaspis is dominated by carbon-rich landforms, including graphite plains, crystalline diamond uplands, and titanium and silicon carbide crusts. Volcanic activity is common, with significant carbon and metal outgassing. Surface temperatures remain high across lowland regions, and the low albedo (~0.05) contributes to further thermal retention.

## Moon
**Jiwe** is the sole natural satellite of Jaspis. It is a 400 km diameter silicate moon orbiting at a distance of ~100,000 km, completing one orbit every 2.1 Earth days. In stark contrast to Jaspis’s carbon terrain, Jiwe’s basaltic and feldspathic surface reflects more light, appearing as a pale grey object in the reddish skies of its parent world. Though geologically inert and lacking an atmosphere, Jiwe slowly accumulates carbonaceous soot from Jaspis’s atmosphere, particularly on its leading hemisphere.

## Climate and Dynamics
Jaspis receives 2.75 Earth flux. A Bond albedo of 0.05 gives an elementary equilibrium comparison near 354 K; the adopted ~450-K mean is a greenhouse climate target, not a radiative-transfer result. Cooler protected basins host specified heavy-organic mixtures, while hot volcanic exposures can reach 900 K or more. Diurnal temperature variation exists due to the 20-hour day, but thermal inertia and atmospheric density moderate extremes. Strong winds and circulation patterns are expected given the planet’s rapid rotation and thermal contrasts between its graphite plains and volcanic regions.

## Biology
Main article: [[Life on Jaspis]].
Jaspis supports a biosphere of carbon-based organisms collectively termed "planimals", which exploit thermal and chemical gradients using exotic energy storage and mechanical processes. These lifeforms are structurally based on polymerized carbon-nitrogen lattices and nanotube frameworks. Detailed discussion of Jaspian biology is deferred to the separate article.

## Physical basis

A 3-M⊕ world at 1.5 R⊕ has a density of about 4.90 g/cm³, a gravity of 1.33 g and an escape speed of about 15.82 km/s; a 1.1-R⊕ radius would imply about 12.43 g/cm³, too dense for a carbon-rich bulk composition. Carbon-rich differentiated interiors are plausible model families, not a unique solution ([SiC equation-of-state and interior models](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018JE005582)). At a Bond albedo of 0.05 the simple irradiation comparison is about 354 K, so the nominal 450-K mean is a greenhouse target without a radiative-convective calculation, and volcanic provinces reach about 900 K or hotter locally. The normalized 7-bar gas table closes. Benzene's critical temperature is about 562 K, so pressure cannot produce an ordinary pure-liquid benzene sea at 900 K ([NIST phase data](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/formula?ID=C71432&Type=TC)); cool sheltered basins and subsurface pools may instead hold mixed heavy-aromatic and nitrile fluids, with anthracene and phenanthrene as candidate components, not a complete mixture phase diagram.

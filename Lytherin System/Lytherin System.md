# Lytherin System

[[Lytherin]] is a **deliberately manufactured system**, arranged to provide many habitable worlds around two closely orbiting F-type stars. Six intact planets, a disrupted dwarf-planet belt, nine inhabited terrestrial moons and an outer ice field share the architecture. Resonant/commensurate orbital design and atmosphere management are part of the setting; no claim that nine is a mathematically proven maximum is made. The builders and their construction date remain unspecified.

### Pele

![[images/Pele_Iron_Fountains.png]]

*The iron fountains of Pele.*

[[Pele]] preserves the global ~3000-K magma-ocean design through a distributed stellar-energy collection and delivery swarm. The required extra absorbed power is about 4.57 × 10²¹ W in the blackbody reference, rather than heat invented by an exotic core. A dense silicate–metal world at 2.04 g, it displays iron fountains, renewed rock vapor, red/gold/violet plasma filaments and magneto-acoustic disturbances. Local magma/fountain temperatures span roughly 2800–3500 K. It is uncolonized and observed remotely. Its natural irradiation-only equilibrium would be only ~781 K; neither its core nor an assumed tesla-scale dynamo supplies the difference.

### Sunder
![[images/Sunder_-_a_shattered_ex-planet.png]]

*Sunder, and the ring of debris it has become..*
Sunder, located between 0.8 and 1.2 astronomical units from the central binary, is the shattered remnant of a dwarf-sized, once-coalescing terrestrial precursor of roughly 0.01 M⊕. The region consists of a moderately broad debris belt, populated by several major fragments ranging from 500 to 1000 kilometers in diameter, embedded within a dense cloud of dust and smaller rubble. The belt exhibits mean orbital eccentricities of approximately 0.1 to 0.2 and moderate inclinations of 5 to 10 degrees, resulting in a dynamically "warm" structure — thicker and more turbulent than a cold planetary ring, yet maintained as a replenished debris population over long timescales, rather than as an immutable set of individual fragments. Proximity to the two F-type stars, whose combined luminosity far exceeds that of the Sun, ensures that sufficiently small dust grains (with a composition- and size-dependent blowout threshold) are rapidly expelled by radiation pressure, necessitating continual replenishment through slow collisional grinding among larger bodies. Sunder’s debris is subject to intense ultraviolet irradiation, producing a weak photoionized haze detectable in UV observations, while its abundant warm dust generates a pronounced infrared excess visible from distant stellar systems. To nearby observers, Sunder would appear as a faintly glowing belt, a scar of ancient violence visible against the brilliance of the inner system — a lasting memorial to catastrophic planetary failure.

In the early turbulence of system formation, roughly 500 million years after the binary stars ignited, Sunder was struck by a silicate-rich rogue embryo roughly 800 km across at an illustrative relative speed of 25–35 km/s. The impact was oblique and grazing, transferring enormous kinetic energy without direct annihilation. The collision catastrophically disrupted Sunder’s partially differentiated structure: metallic core fragments, silicate mantle shards, and crustal debris were flung into divergent trajectories. Within hours, the once-unified protoplanet was shattered into several major fragments and a vast cloud of dust, scattering along orbital paths between 0.8 and 1.2 astronomical units. The immediate consequence was the violent dispersal of Sunder’s mass, ending its planetary ambition in a single cataclysmic moment.



The small precursor and energetic impact replace the unsupported destruction of an unspecified Earth-mass planet by a small impactor. A rough impact energy of order 10²⁹ J is comparable to or above the gravitational binding scale of this dwarf-sized body, although coupling and impact geometry still require modelling. Collisional grinding renews the fine dust; long-term survival of the larger fragments, reaccretion and removal remain part of the managed debris history.

### Jaspis and Jiwe

![[images/Jiwe_from_Jaspis.png]]

*A view of Jiwe from the surface of Jaspis.*

[[Jaspis]] retains its dark graphite plains, metastable uplifted diamonds, SiC/TiC formations, cementite, graphene/nanotube dust and transient carbyne products. Its 3-M⊕/1.5-R⊕ reference gives ~4.90 g/cm³ and 1.33 g. The 20-hour rotation, e = 0.02, ~1.5° system-plane inclination, low Bond albedo ~0.05 and 7-bar carbon-rich air remain. Nominal mean temperature is ~450 K, with local volcanic temperatures of 900 K and higher; this is a climate target, not a solved greenhouse profile. Cool basins hold phase-compatible mixed heavy-organic fluids and tar channels, not pure acetylene lakes or benzene rivers.

[[Jaspis#Atmosphere|The normalized atmosphere]] is N₂ 80.54%, CO 15%, CO₂ 4%, acetylene 0.4%, HCN 0.05% and other trace gases 0.01%. Cyanopolyynes (including HC₃N and HC₅N), polyynes (such as C₄H₂ and C₆H₂), neon and argon share the trace budget; fullerene/soot aerosols are separate particles. N₂ is the background gas, CO is a carbon carrier, CO₂ is volcanically renewed, and acetylene/HCN feed photochemical organics. Candidate carbon-rich interiors remain a model family rather than a unique mass–radius inference.

Jiwe remains a ~400-km silicate moon, 100,000 km from Jaspis, with a ~2.1-day period. Its chemically dissimilar collision origin, basalt/feldspar highlands, olivine and pyroxene, airless inert surface, high relative albedo and accumulating soot veneer remain. The leading hemisphere can collect more carbonaceous material. Soot transport from the planet requires a lofting/escape source; it is not automatic deposition through empty space.

[[Life on Jaspis]] preserves all five broad planimal categories and seven ecological zones: autotrophs, detritivores, spring predators, external-fluid aquatic forms and armored turtles, across plains, rifts, dunes, diamond uplands, volcanic terrain, cool heavy-organic basins and subsurface tar pools. Mechanical springs store work supplied by maintained gradients, redox reactions or UV/NIR photon capture; templates carry heritable information without DNA/RNA. Thermal and vibration senses, budding, shell shedding and episodic swarms remain. The ~400-Myr solvent-free biosphere has no confirmed sapient species or civilization.

### Freya

![[images/Gas_Giant_Called_Freya.png]]

*View from the second moon of Freya.*

[[Freya]] is a rapidly rotating gas giant, ~10 hours per rotation, with four inhabited ~0.64-M⊕/0.8-R⊕ moons. Fourteen prominent atmospheric bands, rapidly changing convection, red phosphine/sulfur-associated storms, blue UV-processed hydrocarbon hazes, tan merging eddies, nested polar polygons, lightning and auroral curtains define its appearance. Thin upper haze can give clearer viewing windows, while deeper ammonium-hydrosulfide/water cloud layers depend on the temperature–pressure profile; ammonia need not condense at every altitude. Its irradiation equilibrium is ~238 K at Bond albedo 0.48, not a universal solid surface temperature.

### Frey

![[images/View_from_the_moon_of_B_or_Frey.png]]

*View of Frey dominating the sky from its second moon.*

[[Frey]] has five inhabited moons, a ~10-hour rotation and a muted blue-gray, pale-tan and white appearance. Deep water/NH₄SH/H₂S cloud chemistry, methane/ammonia and hydrocarbon opacity, ethane/acetylene hazes, soft bands, slow persistent cyclones and broad diffuse aurorae form the painterly outer giant. Its irradiation equilibrium is ~217 K at Bond albedo 0.41; individual atmospheric layers can have other temperatures. Neither giant magnetosphere guarantees safe radiation doses or stabilizes moon orbits gravitationally.

## Manufactured habitable moon systems

All nine named worlds remain: Freya 1–4 and Frey 1–5. They are terrestrial planets in composition and inhabited status, and satellites dynamically. Manufacture explains their unusually large collective masses. Each is assigned ~0.64 M⊕ and 0.8 R⊕, yielding approximately 1 g and ~6.89 g/cm³, hence iron-rich interiors. An approximately 60%-by-mass core is a possible design hypothesis, not a model result; Earth's core is about 32% of its mass rather than 55%.

The orbital layout targets adjacent **5:2 period commensurabilities**; compact unphased 3:2 starting layouts were unstable in the short screens. Nominal axes/periods are in [[Freya#Manufactured moon architecture]] and [[Frey#Manufactured moon architecture]]. A period ratio alone is not proof of resonant-angle libration. The intended engineered chains are monitored and can require slow correction; no Gyr-long passive locking or mathematical maximum number of habitable worlds has been demonstrated. Short coupled gravitational screens retain all nine with the adopted spacing.

Moons are synchronously rotating relative to their own orbits around the giant, not to the giant's 10-hour spin or permanently to the stars. All moons within a cluster receive nearly the same average direct starlight. Their climates vary through greenhouse chemistry, albedo/clouds, topography, circulation, actual eclipses, tidal heat and management. Near/far sides differ in giant visibility; both have stellar day–night cycles. Frey 5's twilight terrain and heated arcologies do not require permanent stellar darkness.

### Atmospheric stewardship

TiO₂ deposits support the reflective composites, factories, architecture and material industries of the colonies. Atmospheric engineering remains central, but widespread TiO₂ aerosol release is not assumed a chemically safe universal thermostat. Neon is an inert background gas and engineered constituent, not a strong greenhouse agent; cooling or warming is managed through energy balance, clouds, water and appropriate greenhouse inventories. The guilds monitor radiation, photochemistry and volcanic gases as well as temperature.

Representative compositions are **dry-gas fractions**. Variable water vapor changes wet-air fractions; N₂ is adjusted as the balance gas. Intermediate Freya moons have individually managed climates rather than a mandatory monotonic pressure sequence.

### Jötunn

![[images/Jötunn_and_a_few_moons.png]]

*Jötunn and a few of its moons.*

[[Jötunn]] retains its deep cyan/blue-green methane coloration, aquamarine/ivory/blue-gray pastel bands and occasional large storms. It is a Neptune-class ice giant at 7.4 AU, with 14-hour spin, 13° obliquity, ~2° system-plane inclination and ~45° magnetic tilt. Its pressure-dependent interior includes H₂/He, conducting ammonia–water-rich fluid, possible high-pressure/superionic water and a silicate/carbon core with potential diamond phases. The dynamo is proposed in a conducting ionic layer rather than a universal metallic-hydrogen shell.

The thermal reference is 150–170 K. A 50-K intrinsic-temperature component can coexist with irradiation near 164 K through fourth-power flux addition. Eighteen satellites range from captured minor bodies to differentiated moons; tilted magnetospheric structure creates variable particle irradiation, induced currents, aurorae and possible radio/IR signatures. No new moon names or colonies are invented. The Norse frost-giant association remains.

### Zucchero and Callinora

![[images/Zucchero_the_Superfluff.png]]

*Zucchero, the Superfluff.*

[[Zucchero]] retains a pale pastel-cyan optical outline, faint methane-related banding, hydrocarbon haze and visually sluggish weather. Its nominal effective temperature is around 140 K (irradiation comparison ~143 K at Bond albedo 0.4), not 30–50 K. A 6.8-R⊕ optical/haze radius is distinguished from an adopted structural 1-bar radius of ~5.8 R⊕; the apparent density is ~0.056 g/cm³ at the optical boundary. An approximately 0.8-M⊕ silicate–metal core leaves a substantial ~2.4-M⊕ envelope, not a negligible gas mass. A normalized reference deep composition is H₂ 86.5%, He 12%, CH₄ 1% and other gases 0.5%, including depth-dependent NH₃/C₂H₄/C₂H₂. Cold trapping, haze support, cooling, escape and radius evolution remain model questions; weak visible magnetism is not inferred solely from quiet cloud tops.

The complete Callinora account remains embedded in [[Zucchero#Callinora]]. The 0.5-M⊕, ~5500-km terrestrial companion has a 1.6-million-km relative axis, eccentricity ~0.16, ~35° inclination to the adopted equator and assumed synchronous spin. Including both masses gives ~121.2 days. Their barycentre lies about 216,000 km from Zucchero's centre; coupling is substantial, not negligible. Capture from the inner system or as a rogue body requires an energy-loss or multi-body mechanism, not a simple isolated flyby.

Callinora preserves dark basaltic plains, rifts, impact basins, tectonic fissures, domes, sulfur-stained ridges, reflective frost and possible warm subsurface aquifers/hydrothermal systems. Its ~0.9-atm CO₂/N₂ air, trace sulfur/methane/water chemistry and pale orange-gray hazy sky remain. The ~200-K climate is a greenhouse target requiring calculation, while localized subsurface brines need phase-compatible salts and temperatures. Distant equilibrium tides alone do not supply a large heat budget. Astrobiological potential remains marginal and unconfirmed; no new biosphere is assigned. It is less massive than the Nine Moons and cannot be called the system's largest moon by mass.

### Ice Field
Beyond the orbit of Zucchero, the system's outermost intact gas-rich planet, lies a broad and sparse belt of icy debris stretching from approximately 12 to 50 astronomical units. This outer disk, a subdued analog to the Solar System’s Kuiper Belt, contains a diffuse population of frozen bodies composed primarily of water ice, ammonia ice, and traces of carbon-rich material. The majority of objects are small and dark, with diameters below 100 kilometers, though one or two larger dwarf planets — each roughly 800 to 1500 kilometers across — wander the belt’s inner regions, cloaked in pale ices and tenuous frost atmospheres. The belt’s orbital inclinations and eccentricities remain low, reflecting a history of relative dynamical calm following planetary formation. With an estimated total mass less than 0.1 Earth masses, the outer debris disk exerts little influence on the inner planets, serving instead as a distant, frozen monument to the system’s ancient epoch of formation.

## Physical basis

**Manufactured architecture.** Lytherin is a manufactured system intended to maximize habitable worlds by exploiting resonance. All nine inhabited moons remain, with wider target commensurabilities; this is a high-multiplicity design, not a proof that nine is the largest physically possible number, and the screens do not require removing one.

**Planets.** Kepler periods are barycentric central-mass approximations; perturbations and giant–moon barycentric motion mean real conjunctions and osculating elements vary.

Freya's e = 0.004 gives apsides of 3.00294 and 3.02706 AU, and Frey's e = 0.005 gives 4.183975 and 4.226025 AU. Their mutual separation is about 5.75 mutual Hill radii in the central-mass approximation, a screening measure, not a stability theorem for the whole system. The circular circumbinary test-particle critical radius is about 0.24 AU, inside Pele's 0.4 AU, but the empirical boundary does not guarantee stability for massive interacting planets ([Holman & Wiegert](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9809315)). The formulae are P² = a³/M, S/S⊕ = L/a², ρ = 3m/(4πR³), g = Gm/R² and escape speed √(2Gm/R).

**Numerical screens.** The satellite screen covers one giant, its four or five finite-mass moons and a 3.3-M☉ stellar point source, using a fourth-order symplectic composition. The adopted layout puts the inner moons at 800,000 and 700,000 km, which reduces tidal heating and increases inner-system clearance, and those wider isolated layouts survived 1000-year trials. The full-binary screen includes both stars, six intact planets, all nine massive inhabited moons and Callinora, with a barycentric reflex adjustment for the three satellite-host systems; the debris populations and Jiwe are omitted. Planet and inhabited-moon orbits start coplanar in this screen despite the articles' small illustrative system-plane inclinations, and Callinora is inclined 35° under an aligned-equator assumption, so it is an idealized architecture screen, not an element fit to every inclination.

- Full binary, seed 0, 1000 years: survived; maximum sampled relative total-energy error about 3.5×10⁻⁹.
- Full binary, seed 1, 1000 years: survived; error about 3.0×10⁻⁹.
- Full binary, half step, 500 years: survived; error about 2.2×10⁻¹⁰.

The main timestep is 0.0001 yr (about 52.6 minutes) and the half step 0.00005 yr (about 26.3 minutes). The diagnostics sample osculating semimajor axes and eccentricities; the total energy is dominated by the binary and does not alone certify every satellite's orbital accuracy. In these runs the inner Freya moon reached e ≈ 0.033, the inner Frey moon about 0.0235, the outer inhabited moons about 0.06–0.11 and Callinora about 0.30. Surviving a trial means no tested escape or host-impact condition occurred, not that every long-term hazard was excluded. No tides, spin, oblateness, atmosphere loss, radiation forces, machinery or climate is integrated, and the simulations are roughly a million times shorter than the system's age, so long-term stability, resonance-angle libration, secular inclination robustness and tidal migration remain explicit limits. Compact unphased 3:2 starts produced strong encounters in the tested conditions, which does not prove every phase-protected 3:2 chain impossible; wider 2:1 starts also failed in the chosen trials, while 3:1 starts survived with larger eccentricities than the selected 5:2 layout.

**Resonance.** A genuine resonance needs librating critical angles, not just a ratio of periods, and these screens did not show that the 5:2 angles librate. The architecture is therefore described as engineered target commensurabilities with resonance management, not a proven passive billion-year chain; resonant chains can themselves destabilize ([Pichierri & Morbidelli](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07789)).

**Tides.** The low-eccentricity synchronous equilibrium-tide sensitivity is P_tide = (21/2)(k₂/Q) G^(3/2) M_p^(5/2) R_s⁵ e² / a^(15/2). For k₂/Q = 0.003 and e = 0.02 the inner Freya moon receives about 1.66 W/m² and the inner Frey moon about 5.30 W/m², and the heating scales as e² at higher sampled eccentricities. This is a sensitivity, not a coupled thermal–orbital solution; dissipation, eccentricity evolution and heat transport need longer modeling ([Heller & Barnes](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3549631/) discuss illumination and tidal coupling for exomoon habitability).

**Sunder and the ice field.** Sunder is a dwarf-sized precursor of order 0.01 M⊕ disrupted 500 Myr after stellar ignition by an illustrative ~800-km embryo at 25–35 km/s, an order-of-magnitude impact and binding-energy comparison. Coupling, reaccretion, the present belt mass and the collisional lifetime are not simulated.

**Limits.** The calculations are screening arithmetic and short numerical trials; they do not validate the system for 1 Gyr.

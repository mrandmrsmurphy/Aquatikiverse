**Zucchero** is a low-density, sub-Neptunian gas planet orbiting in the cold, dim outskirts of the [[Lytherin System]] at a distance of 9.8 AU. With a mass of approximately 3.2 Earth masses (M⊕) and a radius of 6.8 Earth radii (R⊕), its optical outline implies an apparent mean density of roughly 0.056 g/cm³. A high-altitude haze can inflate that outline above the deeper gas envelope enshrouding a relatively small, ∼0.8 Earth-mass silicate-metallic core. As such, Zucchero may be classified as a "puffball" world—retaining significant gaseous mass, but with an extended visible atmosphere whose physical radius depends on the pressure level used.

## Orbital and Physical Properties
Zucchero completes one orbit every 16.888 Earth years and exhibits a moderate orbital eccentricity of ~0.08. The orbital inclination is mild (~4°), without independently proving a dynamically quiet history. Its effective blackbody temperature lies around 140 K, making it the coldest planet in the system. Gravity at the optical outline is about 0.07 g. A nominal 1-bar radius of 5.8 R⊕ gives approximately 0.095 g there; the optical and pressure radii must not be conflated. The radius and haze profile remain structural design assumptions requiring an age/cooling/escape model.

## Appearance and Atmospheric Composition
Visually, Zucchero is a planet of subtle beauty. Its pale cyan coloration arises from methane's preferential absorption of red light and the scattering behavior of a persistent hydrocarbon haze. Its faint banding is generated by sluggish zonal winds and modest visible cloud-top contrasts. The atmosphere is largely composed of:

- Hydrogen: 86.5%
- Helium: 12.0%
- Methane: 1.0% in the reference deep mixture
- Other gases: 0.5% aggregate, including depth-dependent ammonia, ethylene, acetylene and minor species

These hydrocarbons undergo slow photochemical processing under ultraviolet irradiation attenuated relative to the inner system, producing the high-altitude haze that mutes the planet's features and defines its spectral signature.

## Meteorology and Magnetic Field
Zucchero's atmospheric activity is characterized by slow, stable motions. Visible upper layers are strongly stratified, and the pressure scale height is large due to low gravity, favoring stratified atmospheric layers. The observed reference appearance lacks prominent large storms; this does not establish no deep weather.

Its magnetic field is weak and likely dominated by ionospheric and atmospheric currents rather than a robust dynamo. A weak visible field is the adopted appearance-level description. Core size and cloud-top stillness alone do not establish the absence of deep convection or a self-sustaining dynamo; internal conductivity, rotation and heat flow remain unspecified.

## Callinora
**Callinora** is a geologically active, captured moon orbiting Zucchero. It is believed to have originated as either a rogue object or an inner-system terrestrial planet. With a mass of ~0.5 Earth masses and a radius of approximately 5,500 km, Callinora is a substantial terrestrial companion, less massive than each of the Nine Moons (~0.64 M⊕).

### Orbital Parameters

- Orbital radius: ~1.6 million km (~0.01 AU)
- Orbital period: ~121.2 Earth days
- Orbital eccentricity: ~0.16
- Orbital inclination: ~35° to Zucchero's equator
- Synchronous rotation (tidally locked)

At this wide separation, radiogenic and retained internal heat are more credible principal sources for subsurface activity than a large assumed tidal flux. For illustration, synchronous equilibrium tides with k₂/Q = 0.003 and e = 0.16 give only order 10⁻⁵ W/m²; this sensitivity is not a measured heat flux. Eccentricity and inclination evolve under stellar perturbations and finite-mass coupling; no perpetual eccentricity pump is assumed.

### Geological and Atmospheric Characteristics
Callinora's surface is marked by rift valleys, tectonic fissures, cryovolcanic domes, and brine pools. Its crust is composed primarily of basaltic rock, with extensive cryovolcanic sulfur deposits. Internal heating maintains possible subsurface aquifers and hydrothermal systems.

Atmospheric pressure is ∼0.9 atm, with a moderately dense atmosphere whose nominal dry composition is:

- CO₂: 66.6% (~0.5994 atm)
- N₂: 33.3% (~0.2997 atm)
- Other gases: 0.1% aggregate (~0.0009 atm), including variable H₂S, SO₂, CH₄ and very small water vapor

The nominal 190–210-K surface climate is a greenhouse design target, not a radiative-transfer result; irradiation alone gives a much colder effective temperature. Surface frost and localized volatile movement can coexist with warm geothermal fractures. Water-rich brines and aquifers are restricted to sufficiently warm subsurface sites with a specified salt/solvent composition, rather than being assumed liquid across a 200-K basalt surface.

### Appearance and Astrobiological Potential
Visually, Callinora displays jagged dark basaltic plains, sulfur-stained ridges, and scattered high-albedo frost deposits. Its sky appears pale orange-gray near the surface, becoming hazier with altitude. Though astrobiological potential is low, the presence of subsurface brine and geothermal gradients provides marginal possibility for extremophile analogues.

Callinora is about 15.6% of Zucchero's mass. The pair orbits a common barycentre about 216,000 km from Zucchero's centre, outside its optical outline; this is a strongly coupled planet–moon or binary-planet configuration. The relative-orbit period includes both masses. However, it serves as a critical object of interest for understanding capture mechanics, cryovolcanism, and potential subsurface habitability.

Capture or assembly of the pair belongs to the manufactured system’s history; a two-body flyby alone cannot capture a moon without removing orbital energy. The stated 35° inclination is to Zucchero’s adopted equator; the orbital screen takes that equator aligned with the stellar reference plane as an explicit modelling assumption. Synchronous spin is a setting assumption, not a calculated locking age. Callinora’s 0.5 M⊕ and 5500-km radius give ~0.67 g. No confirmed biosphere or colony is assigned to it.

## Physical basis

Zucchero keeps 3.2 M⊕, a 9.8-AU orbit and a roughly 0.8-M⊕ core. The 6.8-R⊕ optical radius is separate from a nominal **5.8-R⊕ 1-bar radius**, which gives about 0.095 g and about 0.0904 g/cm³ at the pressure reference. An isothermal 140-K, μ = 2.3-g/mol hydrostatic comparison gives a pressure ratio of about 4.4×10⁻⁵ between the optical and 1-bar radii, a geometrical plausibility check, not a haze or structure model; a smaller 4-R⊕ pressure radius would put the optical outline implausibly many scale heights above 1 bar in that comparison. An apparent haze-inflated radius is an established proposal for some superpuffs ([Wang & Dai](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04188)), but applying it here is an inference, and radius, envelope entropy, age, cooling, escape, haze support and any continuing manufactured maintenance still need a joint model. A 140-K effective reference (about 143 K from the irradiation comparison) does not fix the temperature of every layer. The deep H₂/He/CH₄/other composition is normalized to 86.5/12/1/0.5%, and upper abundances can change through condensation and chemistry. Quiet clouds alone do not prove a missing internal dynamo.

Callinora's finite-mass relative period is about 121.2 days and its barycentre lies about 216,000 km from Zucchero's center; the roughly 15.6% mass ratio means the coupling is substantial. At these distances the illustrative k₂/Q = 0.003 tidal heat is only of order 10⁻⁵ W/m², so radiogenic and geothermal subsurface refugia are emphasized, and the nominal 200-K CO₂/N₂ climate is a greenhouse target, not a demonstrated result.

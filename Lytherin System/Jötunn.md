**Jötunn** is a massive ice giant located in the outer regions of a circumbinary star system composed of two tightly bound F-type stars, the [[Lytherin System]]. With a mass approximately 17 times that of Earth and a radius 4.4 times larger, Jötunn is a Neptune-class ice giant, far below brown-dwarf mass. Its distinctive deep cyan coloration and formidable magnetic phenomena have inspired a name drawn from Norse mythology, evoking the ancient frost giants.

### Orbital Characteristics
Jötunn orbits at a distance of 7.4 AU from the combined barycenter of its parent stars, which are separated by just 0.1 AU and have a combined luminosity of approximately 11 times that of the Sun (L☉). The orbital eccentricity is minimal (~0.02), and the inclination of the orbit is only ~2° relative to the system's ecliptic. Jötunn completes one revolution in about 11.081 Earth years. It rotates once every 14 hours, leading to significant Coriolis effects in its atmosphere.

Its axial tilt of ~13° produces modest seasonal effects. More dramatically, the planet's magnetic axis is tilted by ~45° relative to its rotational axis, introducing complex auroral zones and dynamic interactions between the magnetosphere and surrounding moons.

### Atmosphere and Appearance
The atmospheric composition is dominated by molecular hydrogen (H₂) and helium (He), with 2–5% methane (CH₄) contributing to its coloration through red-light absorption and blue-green reflectance. The result is a hauntingly deep cyan hue that varies with lighting conditions and cloud structures.

Jötunn's cloud features are characterized by subtle pastel banding and slow-moving, large storm systems. Unlike the vivid belts of Jupiter or Saturn, these formations are more muted, displaying tones of aquamarine, ivory, and bluish-gray.

### Thermal and Internal Structure
The irradiation-equilibrium reference is about 164 K at Bond albedo 0.4. An illustrative intrinsic temperature of 50 K denotes internal heat flux, not a second measurement of total emission: T_eff⁴ = T_irr⁴ + T_int⁴ gives a total effective temperature near 164.4 K. The broader 150–170-K description represents atmospheric/model uncertainty and different sampled layers. Formation heat, secular cooling and contraction supply internal flux; the 50-K intrinsic reference is a modelling convention, not an independent claimed observation.

The internal structure is theorized to comprise:

* A thick outer atmosphere of H₂/He several thousand kilometers deep
* A middle envelope of supercritical ammonia-water ocean
* A mantle composed of exotic high-pressure ices, including superionic water and Ice VII
* A dense rocky core likely enriched in silicates and carbon phases, potentially including diamond

The convecting ammonia-water layer is hypothesized to be the principal dynamo region, generating a strong, asymmetric magnetic field. The resulting magnetosphere is chaotic and variable, with a markedly tilted geometry; Jupiter’s field also has departures from symmetry.

### Moons and Magnetospheric Environment
Jötunn is orbited by 18 known satellites, ranging from small irregular bodies to larger, differentiated moons. These moons experience variable radiation exposure due to the tilted and offset magnetic field.

Inner moons that reside within the magnetic field's reach are subject to extreme conditions, including energetic particle bombardment, induced electric currents, and auroral interactions. Some of these interactions may be detectable via radio or infrared emissions, suggesting a magnetically active system.

### Naming and Cultural Context
The planet's name, "Jötunn," references the frost giants of Norse mythology, an apt metaphor for its vast size, remote location, and frigid, stormy nature. The nomenclature reflects both the planet's physical characteristics and the cultural imagination it evokes.

## Physical basis

Jötunn keeps 17 M⊕, about 4.4 R⊕, a 7.4-AU orbit, 18 moons, a 14-hour spin, an obliquity of 13° and a magnetic tilt of 45°. Its density is about 1.10 g/cm³ and its circular orbital speed about 19.89 km/s. The irradiation comparison gives about 164 K; adding an illustrative 50-K intrinsic component by fourth powers gives a total effective temperature near 164.4 K. High-pressure water and ammonia phases and an ionic dynamo remain hypotheses; it is a Neptune-class ice giant, not a near-brown-dwarf mass.

# Lytherin Empire

The fuller imperial account governs institutions, dynastic practice and the nine moon cultures. Lytherin’s deliberately manufactured architecture provides their shared physical setting.

![[images/Insignia_of_the_Lytherin_Empire.png]]

*The royal insignia of the empire.*

The **Lytherin Empire** is the ceremonial federation uniting the inhabited moons of the [[Freya]] and [[Frey]] gas giants, orbiting the twin F-type stars of the [[Lytherin System]]. Founded under the **Compact of First Breath** in Civil Year 0 (C.Y. 0), the Empire is a constitutional monarchy balancing traditional sovereign authority with complex interplanetary institutions, noble dynasties, geoengineering guilds, religious colleges, merchant leagues, and popular assemblies.

Lytherin civilization is defined by the systemic rhythm of a 7.335-Earth-year synodic cycle, during which mass migrations, political realignments, and cultural renaissances occur across the Nine Moons.

## Government
### The Holy Couple
The Empire is ruled jointly by the **Holy Couple**, an Emperor and Empress, each representing one of the two great planetary systems: Freya and Frey. By ancient compact, one consort must be descended from the noble houses of Freya's moons (A1-A4) and one from Frey's moons (B1-B5). (A Couple is said to be *disjoint* if the husband is from Freya and the wife from Frey, and *conjoint* if the reverse.) Upon each Ascension, the houses rotate which moon supplies the consort, ensuring balanced dynastic interconnection across the Empire.

The Holy Couple embodies the duality of Lytherin life: Freya and Frey, Light and Reflection, Order and Renewal. They are the living symbols of planetary unity and cosmic stewardship.

### Succession and Marriage Rotation
Succession follows strict rotation: the heirs of the Holy Couple are betrothed in childhood to the next house in line by moon. Should unforeseen circumstances prevent a union, alternate matches are negotiated under the oversight of the High Diet. Marriage ceremonies are conducted during the period of **Ascension**, coinciding with a 7.335-Earth-year cycle of planetary conjunction, during which free immigration is granted across the moons at the Crown's expense, symbolizing unity.

### The Rotating Capital
Every Ascension, the Imperial Capital migrates between a Freya moon and a Frey moon, alternating in a permanent cycle. Imperial institutions, archives, and court are physically relocated, reinforcing political balance and ensuring that no one planetary sphere dominates the system indefinitely. The practice is enshrined in the Concords of the Great Breath.

### The High Diet
True political power resides in the **High Diet**, the grand parliament of Lytherin. It is composed of representatives from:

- **The Planetary Estates**: Local noble houses and elected officials.
- **The Atmospheric Concord**: Guilds of geoengineers, responsible for climate stewardship.
- **The Grand Synod**: Religious leaders, representing traditions that harmonize science and theology.
- **The Trade Conclave**: Merchant guilds and industrial captains.
- **The Peoples' Tribune**: Direct representatives of citizen assemblies.

Legislation requires consensus across at least three of the five branches to pass, encouraging negotiation and coalition-building. The Emperor and Empress serve as ceremonial moderators and may invoke the ancient prerogative of "Breath's Mercy" to delay legislation for one Civil Year (C.Y.), but not to veto it.

## Religion and Philosophy
Faith and science are integrated within Lytherin thought. The prevailing theology views the "Breath" — the atmospheres of the moons — as a sacred trust. Geoengineering is a religious duty as well as a scientific craft, and many of the Atmospheric Concord's engineers are trained within religious colleges affiliated with the Grand Synod.

Two dominant traditions shape religious life:

- **The Lighted Path**: Centered around the splendor of the gas giants, emphasizing stewardship, balance, and the cycles of renewal. Adherents take vows of duty, reciprocation, adoption, or creation-care.
- **The Deep Communion**: Focused on the sacred mysteries of the eclipses and shadows, promoting introspection, renewal, and resistance to corruption. Adherents take vows of devotion, meditation, purity, or cleanliness.

## Society and Culture
Lytherin society is layered and rhythmical. The nearside of moons (those facing their gas giants) are hubs of commerce, academia, and art, while the farside of moons cultivate rugged frontier cultures valuing independence and innovation.

Public life is dominated by the Cycles. Every 7.335-Earth-years, the "Conjunction" triggers massive movements of people, goods, ideas, and political realignments. Festivals of Breath mark these periods, alongside solemn rituals of Ascension and Renewal.

The moons are still referred to as Freya 1, Freya 2, Freya 3, Freya 4, Frey 1, Frey 2, Frey 3, Frey 4, and Frey 5 because nearly everyone identifies with their nation-state first, the religion second, and their planet third.

## Economy
Trade is conducted through merchant leagues and orbital relay hubs. System-wide tariffs, banking regulations, and transportation standards are renegotiated at Conjunction Summits, presided over by the High Diet.

The economy pulses in tandem with the synodic cycle, favoring speculative ventures, mass migration contracts, atmospheric resource management, and aerospace logistics.

## Military
The Empire maintains the **Sky Guard**, a spaceborne defensive force tasked with protecting trade routes, enforcing customs law, and defending against piracy or regional uprisings. Local militias supplement the Sky Guard on each moon, often rooted in noble house traditions.

## Conflicts and Crises
Despite unity, Lytherin has faced internal tensions:

- **The Shadow Revolts**: Farside colonies protesting near-moon dominance during eclipse periods.
- **The Atmospheric Schisms**: Conflicts over divergent geoengineering policies during critical environmental crises.
- **The Great Relocation Waves**: Mass migrations destabilizing fragile local economies during rapid Conjunction transitions.

## Timeline

- C.Y. 0: Compact of First Breath signed; Empire founded.
- C.Y. 87: First Atmospheric Schism; led to the establishment of the Atmospheric Concord.
- C.Y. 214: Shadow Revolts erupt on B5; suppressed by combined Sky Guard and local militias.
- C.Y. 403: Concord Reformation; expansion of the High Diet.

## Notable Figures

- **Emperor Aurelian IV** (current): Raised on A2; scholarly, reserved, a patron of environmental restoration.
- **Empress Seraphine** (current): Raised on B3; charismatic, advocate for farside colony rights.
- **High Custodian Veyra of the Breath**: Head of the Atmospheric Concord.
- **Grand Prelate Enoch of the Deep**: Leader of the Deep Communion sect.

## Major Moons of the Lytherin Empire
The Lytherin Empire encompasses nine habitable moons orbiting the gas giants Freya and Frey. Despite hundreds of years of habitation, the moons are still referred to primarily by their classical numerical designations — Freya 1 through Freya 4, Frey 1 through Frey 5 — because nearly all citizens identify with their nation-state first, their religious or philosophical order second, and their planetary allegiance third.

### Freya 1
The innermost moon of Freya, Freya 1 is the cultural and political heart of the Freya cluster. With its near-Earth gravity, abundant surface water, and mild climate, it has produced many of the Empire’s most influential Houses, religious orders, and universities. Its skyline is dominated by towering arcologies constructed of polished titanium dioxide composites, reflecting the sunlight in brilliant cascades. Freya 1 is traditionally seen as the "first among equals" among Freyan moons.

### Freya 2
A prosperous mercantile hub, Freya 2 boasts moderate temperatures and a dense network of inland seas. Known for its dynamic merchant leagues, financial exchanges, and innovation academies, it is a frequent origin of Empresses in the Imperial rotation. Its cities are renowned for their graceful canals, and its governance structure favors republican confederacies beneath the noble houses.

### Freya 3
Freya 3’s high plateaus, atmospheric circulation and managed albedo give it a cooler climate and a growing calendar distinct from those of the lowland moons. Mean stellar flux is nearly the same throughout the Freya cluster. A bastion of scientific inquiry, Freya 3 is home to the Atmospheric Concord’s oldest and most prestigious academy. Its landscape is dominated by high plateaus, vast steppe-grass regions, and sprawling high-altitude lakes. Freya 3 often favors geoengineering solutions seen as overly cautious by the warmer moons.

### Freya 4
The outermost of Freya’s major moons, Freya 4 is a frontier world marked by colder oceans, sweeping temperate forests, and a fiercely independent population. Though often regarded as provincial by inner moon citizens, Freya 4 has produced many of the most celebrated explorers, theologians, and revolutionaries of Lytherin history. Its society tends toward decentralized governance, emphasizing strong local councils and voluntary associations.

### Frey 1
Frey 1 combines a relatively mild engineered climate with its close view of the muted giant Frey. It has an extensive underground river system and is famous for its bioengineered farms, producing luxury goods for the entire system. Its ancient Houses retain a reputation for aristocratic grandeur, often seen as the most conservative bastion within the Empire.

### Frey 2
Frey 2 is the trade and industrial powerhouse of the Frey system. Temperatures are cooler than on Freya 2, and many cities are constructed in vast climate-regulated domes linked by maglev highways. It boasts a powerful voice within the Trade Conclave and has historically produced many of the Empire's Chancellors and commercial policy architects.

### Frey 3
A colder world cloaked in high-altitude photochemical hazes, Frey 3 is nonetheless rich in rare minerals and frozen hydrocarbons. It is known for its stark, austere beauty and the cultural stoicism of its people. Frey 3’s religious orders dominate the Deep Communion sect, emphasizing cycles of hardship and renewal.

### Frey 4
The fourth moon of Frey is characterized by broad glacial plains, thick coniferous analog forests, and stormy coastal regions. Colonized later than the inner moons, Frey 4 retains a frontier ethos and a complex patchwork of feudal pacts between Houses, communes, and regional clans. It frequently petitions for greater autonomy within the High Diet.

### Frey 5
The most distant and coldest of the Nine, Frey 5’s thick haze, local terrain and settlement practices give many inhabited regions a twilight appearance. Its direct stellar illumination has normal moon day–night cycles; distance from Frey does not place it in permanent stellar darkness. Its settlements cluster around geothermal hot zones and artificially warmed cities. Frey 5 is regarded as the crucible of Lytherin's most rugged pioneers, with a culture blending fierce independence, mystical traditions tied to the Shadow, and a deep reverence for endurance.

## Calendar and orbital stewardship

A Civil Year is 365.25 Earth days. The Great Cycle is the approximately 7.335-year mean conjunction interval of the Freya and Frey clusters; perturbations make actual conjunction dates differ slightly from a rigid calendar. Historical C.Y. dates remain 0, 87, 214 and 403, without conversion into conjunction counts. Ascension and capital relocation occur each Great Cycle; dynastic succession is ratified at an appropriate Ascension, not forced by a requirement that every ruler die or retire every seven years. Crown-funded migration is scheduled around these festivals using actual transfer trajectories.

The Four Freya Moons and Five Frey Moons have distinct identities. The system was manufactured to provide a high multiplicity of habitable worlds through selected orbital commensurabilities, suitable stellar flux and controlled atmospheres. Orbital monitoring and any required slow corrections accompany atmospheric stewardship; manufacture does not exempt the moons from gravity, tides or radiation hazards. The origin and motives of the original builders are not specified.

The consorts represent the two dynastic spheres simultaneously; which sphere supplies the Emperor or Empress may alternate under the house rotation. Aurelian IV’s upbringing on A2 and Seraphine’s on B3 do not by themselves establish ancestry. Nearside/farside social differences arise partly from the presence or absence of the giant in the sky; both hemispheres experience stellar daylight. Eclipse customs concern actual geometrical events, not permanent global darkness.

## Physical basis

The giant-axis reference gives a mean synodic period of 7.3354299 Earth years; actual dates vary under perturbations. The 7.335-year Great Cycle governs Conjunction and Ascension, migration festivals and alternating capital relocation, and a Civil Year is 365.25 Earth days, distinct from a Great Cycle.

This fictional setting prioritizes a continuing native biosphere and habitable geothermal belts. The atmosphere and organism designs are requirements of that setting; the calculations under Physical basis are limited physical screens, not a validated climate or posthuman model.

**Skemer** is a rogue terrestrial planet with a dense hydrogen–helium atmosphere, extensive geothermal activity, and an internally sustained biosphere despite the absence of stellar illumination. Its supplied sky position is right ascension 10h 04m and declination −24° 30′, approximately 30.7 parsecs from Earth; assuming the usual J2000 coordinate reference, this lies in Hydra rather than the supplied Antlia. Although Skemer receives essentially no sunlight, radiogenic heating in the rocky interior, secular cooling, and a smaller but continuing tidal contribution from its eccentric moon, **Maan**, together with strong atmospheric insulation maintain surface temperatures near the freezing point of water in geologically active regions.

Skemer is noted for possessing a complex, chemotrophic biosphere concentrated along tectonic boundaries. The core autotrophs are electrochemical organisms (informally called *battery trees*) that evolve and exploit galvanic gradients created by vent fluids and oxidized brines. Their metabolisms split water or peroxides, releasing molecular oxygen and forming the energetic base of the planetary ecosystem.

A population of genetically modified human descendants, the **Skemer-mense**, inhabit the planet. Originally survivors of a failed interstellar colonization mission, they underwent profound physiological adaptation to Skemer’s high gravity, dense reducing atmosphere, and total darkness. Within the setting, Skemer is an important study of long-term rogue-planet habitability.

## Discovery and Survey
### Pre-contact Detection
After the original target proved catastrophically uninhabitable, the generation ship *Verkenner* searched for refuge and detected Skemer in the far infrared. This occurred about 300 years before the present. Its cold emitting atmosphere and internal luminosity, rather than a directly visible 260-K surface, determined the distant signal. Instrument range remains unspecified. Follow-up spectra suggested a hydrogen–helium envelope with methane and ammonia; oxygen and the near-surface composition were established during reconnaissance.

### Approach and Reconnaissance
Close mapping, gravimetry and radar surveys constrained the metallic interior, fracture networks and tectonic activity. Descent probes and local samples established atmospheric composition and biological oxygen production; these were not direct near-surface samples taken remotely from orbit.

### Emergency colonization
The survivors established sealed, insulated and radiation-shielded habitats on Maan about 300 years ago. The next 150 years covered six generations of deliberate engineering, selection and testing; reproductive isolation is dated to the fourth generation. Principal descent to Skemer occurred about 150 years ago. Modern planetary society therefore has about 150 years of surface integration after the separate 150-year lunar adaptation interval. The authoritative vessel name is *Verkenner*.

## Physical Characteristics
### Mass, Radius, and Gravity
Skemer possesses a mass of approximately 1.87 M⊕ and a radius of ~1.2 R⊕, yielding a mean surface gravity of ~1.3 g. The resulting gravity is 12.75 m/s² and escape speed 13.96 km/s. Retention of the primordial gases also depends on the thermal and escape history, not size alone.

### Bulk Composition and Density
The planet’s bulk density is consistent with a rock-metal mixture slightly more metal-rich than Earth. Elevated iron and nickel fractions contribute to a large molten core capable of powering a strong magnetic dynamo. Mantle composition appears ultramafic, with high proportions of magnesium silicates typical of planets with vigorous internal heating.

## Internal structure and heat budget
![[images/Skemer_surface_pic.png]]

*Skemer’s surface under IR false color.*

### Core
Skemer’s core is estimated to occupy 55–60% of planetary radius, only modestly larger in relative size than Earth’s roughly 55%. The core is predominantly liquid iron-nickel alloy with suspended light elements (S, Si, O). Strong convection, aided by tidal forcing, sustains a powerful magnetic field.

### Mantle
The mantle consists largely of high-pressure silicate phases including bridgmanite and ferropericlase. Mantle viscosity is lower than Earth’s due to elevated temperatures, promoting rapid convection and comparatively short geological recycling times. U, Th and K are enriched in the silicate reservoirs. Their required inventory is set by the adopted radiogenic flux; the planetary age and detailed abundance model remain unspecified.

### Heat Sources

Maan remains eccentric and contributes to the heat budget. Its own approximately 0.003-W/m² tidal dissipation occurs **in Maan**, not in Skemer. The reference model follows changing spin, axis and eccentricity over 300 Myr; it does not assume an invisible perpetual eccentricity pump. Skemer's faster earlier spin supplies part of the finite mechanical reservoir.

Flux is concentrated at ridges, subduction systems and vents, sustaining much higher local geothermal output. Heat transport and hydrogen insulation must maintain connected liquid refugia through the native biosphere's several-hundred-million-year history. The assumptions and limitations are under Physical basis.

### Geodynamo
The magnetic field is maintained by core convection modified by tidal stirring. Field strength is estimated to be comparable to or slightly greater than Earth’s. The dynamo modifies charged-particle access. The massive atmosphere and sheltered habitats provide additional shielding; neither the field nor low exobase temperature alone proves gas retention or exclusion of all cosmic rays.

## Surface Geology and Geodynamics
### Plate Tectonics
Skemer hosts dozens of mobile lithospheric plates. High heat flux produces rapid seafloor-like spreading, broad ridge systems, deep transform zones, and strongly active subduction arcs. These structures form the main geobiological arteries of the planet, aligning closely with regions of high biomass. The crust is basaltic and enriched with ultramafic materials, contributing to frequent tectonic and volcanic resurfacing.

### Volcanism
Volcanic activity is dominated by effusive basaltic eruptions along ridges, punctuated by volatile-rich explosive eruptions in subduction arcs. Lava flows, geothermal lakes, and persistent fumarole fields serve as major environmental modifiers and primary energy sources for life.

### Basins, Uplands, and Plate Interiors
Plate interiors cool and thicken over time, becoming cold, hard, and geochemically exhausted. These regions are dark, dry “battery deserts” with minimal biological activity aside from spores and deep fracture microbial communities. Many interiors host fossilized remains of ancient battery forests that once thrived when the plate was positioned over active vents.

### Cryobrine and subglacial systems
In colder regions, surface ice sheets form where geothermal flux is insufficient to maintain liquid water. Beneath them, brine channels and subglacial lakes persist due to internal heat. These zones support low but resilient biological communities and act as long-range dispersal corridors for spores.

## Atmosphere and Climate
As a rogue planet, Skemer is not gravitationally bound to any star. It drifts through interstellar space along a galactic orbit, ejected from its original stellar system through gravitational interactions, during an early system instability. Its precise origin and ejection trajectory remain undetermined.

### Rotation
Skemer has a measured rotational period of approximately 40 hours. This moderate rotation rate contributes to the formation of broad atmospheric circulation patterns and organizes convection through rotational effects; buoyancy and heat loss supply the dynamo energy. The planet’s axial tilt is unknown, though negligible seasonal variation has been observed due to the lack of stellar input.

There is no solar day/night cycle. Rotation organizes circulation; tides, vents, magnetic variability and social clocks supply measurable ecological timing cues. Artificial and biological light support ordinary visible tasks.

### Greenhouse Physics
Surface warmth is produced by hydrogen collision-induced absorption, which efficiently traps thermal infrared radiation. Trace CH₄, NH₃, and H₂O enhance opacity in selected bands. Because there is no stellar input, greenhouse retention and geothermal output set the entire surface energy budget.

### Vertical Structure
A deep convective lower atmosphere lies beneath cold radiative layers. Volcanic aerosols and radiochemical products supply hazes; residual photochemical compounds are not evidence of ongoing sunlight. Water/ammonia condensates occupy pressure and temperature levels selected by condensation and transport, rather than fixed terrestrial cloud altitudes.

A 150–170-K cloud region can describe an intermediate layer, not the final emitting temperature. The present internal flux implies an effective emission temperature near **48.5 K**. Warm lower layers of roughly 230–290 K therefore require a highly insulating, spectrally structured envelope. Absorption, emission and convection redistribute heat; clouds do not manufacture additional energy. The vertical temperature profile still needs a radiative-convective solution.

### Climate Regime
The life-supporting design retains a typical global surface range of **255–265 K**, warm geothermal belts of **270–290 K**, and colder plate interiors around **230–250 K**. These are target conditions, not outputs of a solved climate model. Pressure, atmospheric inventory and interior evolution must be chosen together to sustain these conditions over the native biosphere's lifetime.

Regional differences coexist with persistent liquid vent lakes, brine networks and insulated subsurface corridors. Forest growth, reproduction, spore dispersal, dormancy and recolonization continue across the network; the world is not reduced to isolated dying pockets. Weather is driven by geothermal plumes and atmospheric circulation rather than solar days. Wind speeds and horizontal temperature contrasts remain model-dependent.

### Atmospheric Stability
Cold upper layers and high gravity favor retention, but escape, cosmic-ray chemistry, catalytic surfaces and volatile replenishment must be modeled through time. Sustained H₂/O₂ coexistence is an explicit source–sink constraint. Neither instantaneous global combustion nor permanent chemical stability is assumed from composition alone. Settlements monitor mixing, local ignition and reactive plumes. The adopted oxygen inventory is a canon requirement with an unclosed quantitative production/loss budget.

## Moon Maan
Maan is a rocky-icy satellite with a radius of ~2200 km and mass of ~0.02 M⊕. It possesses a small metallic core and a silicate-rich mantle capped by a heavily cratered crust. Maan is about 1.6 times the mass of Earth’s Moon, with derived density 2.68 g/cm³ and gravity 1.65 m/s².

### Orbital Characteristics
Maan follows a prograde orbit with **semimajor axis 300,000 km, eccentricity approximately 0.35, pericenter 195,000 km and apocenter 405,000 km**. Its period is **13.77 Earth days**, outside Skemer's approximately 73,200-km synchronous radius. Thus the moon remains conspicuously eccentric and continues to heat Skemer, rather than being moved outward into a nearly circular orbit.

The illustrative all-eccentricity tidal model gives approximately 0.012 W/m² in Skemer and 0.003 W/m² in Maan today. A 300-Myr backward screen reaches an axis near 227,000 km, e≈0.241 and a Skemer spin near 25.8 hours without close approach. This establishes a possible history under the chosen fixed response, not unique orbital history or a rheological stability proof. The model's relaxed lunar spin is pseudosynchronous; a rocky spin resonance could change the heating.

Tidal variations can modulate fractures and biological timing. Reflected planetary thermal IR is measurable, but does not create sunlight-like visible phases or shadows. Visible illumination requires actual aurorae, bioluminescence, artificial sources or incandescent lava.

### Geology
The moon’s surface features ancient volcanic plains, rift valleys, ancient impact basins, and regolith coated with atmospheric dust from Skemer. Subsurface brine pockets and sporadic cryovolcanic features suggest limited residual activity. Maan is a fully differentiated rocky body with an estimated internal structure composed of a metallic core with a working radius fraction of about 30%, surrounded by a silicate-rich mantle and crust with an icy component; this is not a 30% core mass claim. Internal heating from mild tidal flexing maintains subsurface warmth in localized regions, and evidence suggests episodic cryovolcanism or brine effusion may still occur in geologically active pockets.

### Historical significance
Maan served as the initial refuge for human survivors. Its relative stability and lower gravity allowed the colonists to establish long-term shelters while genetic adaptation programs unfolded. Today it is mostly uninhabited, used for ceremonial purposes and scientific observation.

Though not inherently habitable under Earth-like criteria, Maan was the initial refuge of the human colonists following their failed settlement attempt elsewhere in the galaxy. The moon’s relatively stable terrain, lower gravity, and engineered sealed shielding made it the most viable location for establishing a provisional habitat. Over the course of 150 years, the human population adapted themselves biologically and technologically to both lunar and planetary conditions. This transitional phase gave rise to the genetically modified descendant population known as the Skemer-mense. Once adaptation was sufficient, the population transitioned down to Skemer proper, leaving Maan mostly uninhabited except for ceremonial visits and scientific observation, as described above.

#### Cultural and scientific relevance
Maan holds significant historical and symbolic importance among the Skemer-mense, serving as both cradle and crucible of their survival. In contemporary Skemer society, the moon is often referred to as the “Womb of the Exodus,” and early habitats on Maan are preserved as archaeological and spiritual sites.

Scientifically, Maan is of interest for comparative geophysics, as its orbital effects offer a unique natural laboratory for studying tidal-thermal feedback in the absence of solar forcing. It is also used as a high-altitude platform for atmospheric and magnetospheric research on Skemer.

## Biosphere
Main article: [[Life on Skemer]].

Skemer’s biosphere is entirely chemosynthetic. Life depends on geochemical disequilibria between reduced mantle fluids and oxidized crustal brines. Productivity is concentrated along tectonic belts where steep redox gradients intersect with permeable rock.

### Oxygen Production
Oxygen originates from several sources:
* Nuclear/radiolytic processing of wet rock and water/peroxide reservoirs; sulfur-related net oxygen yield requires a reaction budget
* Oxychlorine chemistry in cold brines
* Biological electrochemical water/peroxide splitting by multicellular autotrophs

The dominant geo-electric producers use mineral-lined battery organelles charged by replenished geochemical disequilibria, radiolytic products and speculative thermogalvanic conversion across hot/cold gradients. Carbon fixation and oxygen release consume part of that work. Peroxide disproportionation also releases O₂, but peroxide formation must be paid for. They form extensive battery forests along vent belts; [[Life on Skemer]] gives the circuit and source–sink constraints.

### Battery Forests
Battery forests consist of tree-like chemotrophs supported by insulating, lignin-analog structural tissue. Their conductive inner tissues channel electrons through embedded mineral pathways, allowing them to exploit natural redox gradients. Their rapid growth reflects the instability of their habitat; tectonic shifts regularly destroy established forests and expose new growth sites.

### Secondary Ecosystems
Mixed decomposer communities break down battery-wood; nitrifiers and sulfur oxidizers exploit the resulting nitrogen/sulfur chemistry and can also fix carbon. These serve as feedstocks for higher organisms and for Skemer-mense agriculture. Many species exhibit bioluminescence for communication, dispersal, or symbiosis in the otherwise pitch-black world.

### Distribution
Biospheric density maps closely trace tectonic activity. Active belts host dense, glowing biomes, while plate interiors are largely barren except for remnant or fossilized growths.

## Human Colonization and the Skemer-mense
Main article: [[Skemer-mense]].

After abandoning their targeted exoplanet, the refugee population settled on Maan. Over several generations, controlled genetic engineering programs reshaped human physiology to endure Skemer’s gravity, pressure, atmospheric chemistry, and darkness.

### Adaptation
Skemer-mense were bred for:
* Enhanced musculoskeletal density
* Pressure-adapted gas exchange and regenerable respiratory bioscrubbers for H₂-rich air
* Dedicated thermal receptors integrated with low-light bioluminescent vision
* Chemically resistant skin
* Refined vestibular systems for tectonically unstable terrain

### Settlement Patterns
Modern settlements cluster along geothermal belts where heat, O₂, and battery forests converge. Engineered caverns channel vent chemistry into habitable spaces, supporting domestic animals and microbial food-production systems.

### Domesticates and Biotechnology
Adapted cattle, flightless-birds, canids, and specialized invertebrates are integral to food and material production. Battery-wood serves as a structural material, an electrical insulator, and a feedstock for microbial fermentation systems.

### Culture
Skemer-mense culture emphasizes stewardship of tectonic belts, continuity with ancestral exile, and symbolic reverence for Maan. The society maintains deliberate radio silence with Earth, leaving its connection to humanity open to interpretation.

## Scientific significance
Skemer provides a case study in warm rogue-planet physics, atmospheric retention under hydrogen dominance, and tidal-thermal geodynamics.

The fictional planet explores powered oxygen release without sunlight, mineral-supported bioenergetics and dark-world ecosystems; it is not empirical evidence that the complete mechanism exists.

Its adopted heat budget links continuing tectonics to biological habitats without a star; long-term thermal and atmospheric evolution remain research requirements.

Battery forests, petrified belts, and vent-centered biospheres offer a model for life on subglacial ocean worlds, early Earth analogues, and other extrastellar environments.

## Physical basis

**Bulk.** The body values follow from 1.87 M⊕ and 1.2 R⊕: a surface area of 7.345×10¹⁴ m², a volume of 1.872×10²¹ m³ and a circumference of 48,036 km. Maan (0.02 M⊕, 2,200 km, 2.68 g/cm³, 1.65 m/s²) has a working core radius fraction of 30%, which clarifies an ambiguous proportion and is not a measured mass fraction.

**Coupled tidal model.** The model uses the all-eccentricity equilibrium-tide equations of [Leconte et al. (2010)](https://arxiv.org/html/1004.0463v1) (equations 2–13 and appendix A) with zero obliquity, fixed radii, a primary moment coefficient of 0.33, primary k₂ = 0.3 and lag 100 s, and lunar k₂ = 0.03 and lag 100 s. These are illustrative material responses, not observations. Maan is on the relaxed pseudosynchronous spin branch and its small spin angular momentum is neglected; a permanently distorted rocky moon could instead occupy a spin resonance, which would change its heat and eccentricity evolution. With x = e², n = √[G(M+m)/a³], planetary spin w and I = 0.33MR², the eccentricity functions are N = (1 + 15x/2 + 45x²/8 + 5x³/16)/(1 − x)⁶, Nₐ = (1 + 31x/2 + 255x²/8 + 185x³/16 + 25x⁴/64)/(1 − x)^(15/2), Ω = (1 + 3x + 3x²/8)/(1 − x)^(9/2) and Kᵢ = (3/2) k₂ᵢ Δtᵢ G Mⱼ² Rᵢ⁵ n²/a⁶. The planetary torque is T = (2Kp/n)(N − Ωw/n) and the planetary dissipation Hp = 2Kp[Nₐ − 2Nw/n + Ω(w/n)²]; the relaxed moon has wₘ/n = N/Ω and Hm = 2Km(Nₐ − N²/Ω), with zero mean torque. Then Ėorb = −Hp − wT − Hm, L̇orb = −T, ȧ = 2a²Ėorb/(GMm), ẋ = 2(1 − x)[ȧ/(2a) − L̇orb/Lorb] and ẇ = T/I, which ties orbital work, spin work and heat together instead of fixing the spin reservoir.

At present Maan dissipates 0.00307 W/m² on the moon itself (not added to planetary heating), and the planetary total is 0.31228 W/m² (2.294×10¹⁴ W, an effective emitting temperature of 48.44 K), of which Maan's tide is about 4%. A planetary tidal term much larger than today's cannot persist at a 40-hour spin, because the spin is a finite mechanical reservoir and an isolated moon moved outward does not replenish it; the model keeps Maan's role as an eccentric heat source without assigning it perpetual free orbital energy. Eccentricity need not decay monotonically, since the faster planet transfers spin angular momentum to the orbit, and no extra perturber is invented.

The integration uses fourth-order Runge–Kutta steps of 10,000 years over ±300 Myr, a representative several-hundred-million-year native-life interval, not a measured biosphere age. Heating stays positive; the summed orbital-plus-planetary-spin angular momentum changes by about 4×10⁻¹⁵ fractionally, and halving the step changes the endpoint quantities by less than 10⁻¹⁰. These checks address numerical bookkeeping, not rheology: fixed response, neglected lunar spin momentum, unmodeled ocean tides, spin resonances and changing interior structure dominate the physical uncertainty. The orbit stays separated, but this is not an N-body stability proof or a guarantee under encounters. The semidiurnal comparison Q ≈ 1/[2|w − n|Δt] is about 130 today; it is a single-mode convention, not one constant Q for all eccentricity harmonics.

**Heat sources.** The radiogenic (0.20 W/m²) and cooling (0.10 W/m²) values are working values that need an eventual age and interior inventory; they do not follow merely from a large metallic core. Uranium, thorium and potassium enrichment belongs mainly to silicate reservoirs, and core convection, heat-producing rock, secular cooling and tidal stirring are distinct contributions. A magnetic field modifies charged-particle access but does not prove unlimited gas retention or total cosmic-ray protection.

**Atmosphere.** The 100-bar reference strengthens the insulation premise; it is not a computed minimum or sufficient pressure. Hydrogen-rich envelope studies support the mechanism, including interior-heated worlds, but no published result models this composition, flux, forest coverage and gravity, so the final pressure must follow an atmosphere and interior calculation that maintains the full biological life cycle. Because the total is 100 bar, each numerical percent in the composition table equals its partial pressure in bar; this is a coincidence, not a general conversion. Typical 260-K water vapor is pH₂O ≈ 0.0022 bar, and ice and brine activity change it. The effective emitting temperature is Teff = (F/σ)^¼ = 48.44 K; the roughly 11-μm thermal peak belongs to a 260-K material surface, while the effective outgoing thermal scale is near 60 μm and the real spectrum is non-grey. Background chemistry needs a coupled transport, deposition, reaction and escape model, and the global 0.30-bar O₂ inventory is an unsolved source–sink requirement, especially in H₂-rich air over reactive minerals. Pressure–temperature closure, net oxygen export and native and engineered productivity are the main outstanding quantitative work.

**Battery-forest energy.** Charging draws on replenished donor–acceptor disequilibria, nuclear radiolysis and a speculative thermogalvanic route with a heat sink. Carbon fixation, ATP synthesis, maintenance, electrolysis and chemical transport compete for that usable work, and radiolytic chemical energy is not counted again as independent heat. The water-oxidation half-reaction is coupled to a cathode and paid electrical work, and peroxide disproportionation requires a peroxide supply. Ideal electrolysis needs about 474 kJ/mol O₂ at standard conditions, and real overpotentials increase that; assigning all of the internal luminosity to this one task gives a ceiling of about 4.8×10⁸ mol O₂/s, an intentionally unrealistic upper bound. Peroxide made from existing O₂ and decomposed again is not net production, and nitrates, perchlorates and oxidized minerals are not inexhaustible.

**Sky position.** Taking the supplied RA 10h04m, Dec −24°30′ as J2000, the position lies in Hydra. A screening precession to the B1875 boundary epoch gives about RA 9.9703 h, Dec −23.8955°, and the [Roman boundary table distributed by Astropy](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/astropy/astropy/main/astropy/coordinates/data/constellation_data_roman87.dat) assigns the relevant RA interval 9.75–10.25 h down to −26.5° to Hydra, so the point is comfortably north of that boundary. A century-scale precession is enough to separate a multi-degree difference, not to define precision astrometry; no proper motion is supplied, and the 30.7-pc distance is unchanged.

**Limits.** No complete climate, gas-escape, redox and ecological productivity, rocky spin-resonance or posthuman physiological simulation has been performed. The design is explicit about its premises and removes the arithmetic and circuit contradictions, but it does not certify the remaining premises scientifically.

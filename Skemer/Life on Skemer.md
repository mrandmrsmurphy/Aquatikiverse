**Life on Skemer** comprises a diverse, exclusively chemosynthetic biosphere sustained by geothermal and tidal energy on the rogue planet [[Skemer]]. In the absence of stellar light, the biosphere depends on redox gradients generated by tectonic activity, radiolysis, and deep geochemical cycling. The system’s primary producers are electroautotrophic organisms capable of splitting water or peroxide using internal galvanic structures known as **battery organelles**. These autotrophs release oxygen that accumulates in the lower atmosphere, sustaining complex ecological networks and enabling habitation by the genetically modified posthumans known as the **Skemer-mense**.

Biological activity is concentrated almost entirely along Skemer’s tectonic belts—broad, geothermally active regions where heat, liquid water, and redox disequilibria coincide. In contrast, the interiors of old tectonic plates are cold, dry, and biologically sparse. Over geologic timescales, the motion of plates leaves behind extensive bands of petrified ‘‘battery forests’’ that record the shifting map of past life zones.

Since their descent to Skemer proper roughly 150 years ago, following a separate 150-year adaptation interval on Maan, the Skemer-mense have integrated into the native biosphere, engineering domesticated animals, adapting human physiology to the planet’s reducing atmosphere, and constructing geothermal cave systems aligned with natural ecosystems.

## Overview
Skemer’s biosphere is notable for operating without photosynthesis. No sunlight reaches the planet’s surface, which is shrouded beneath a deep hydrogen–helium atmosphere. Instead, life relies on geothermal heat, chemical disequilibria, and radiolytically produced oxidants.

The biosphere’s most distinctive features include:

* **Oxygenic chemosynthesis** mediated by geo-electric autotrophs

* Complex battery-like organelles enabling water or peroxide splitting

* Tree-like macrostructures (‘‘battery forests’’) that grow along vent systems

* Extensive bioluminescence for communication, dispersal, and predation

* Extreme spatial linkage between life zones and active tectonic boundaries

Across these ecosystems, Skemer supports a range of microbial, multicellular, and engineered organisms adapted to high pressure, low temperature, variable local oxidant availability and total darkness.

## Origins

### Abiotic chemical foundations
Before life emerged, Skemer’s surface and upper crust supported a rich inventory of abiotic chemistry:

* **Radiolysis of water** produced hydrogen, hydrogen peroxide, and hydroxyl radicals.

* **Radiochemical sulfur processing** in wet volcanic/mineral environments contributed to oxidized sulfur reservoirs; its net oxygen yield is not assumed from SO₂ destruction alone.

* **Cryobrine pockets** in plate interiors accumulated nitrates, perchlorates, and oxychlorine compounds.

* **Fischer–Tropsch-type reactions** at vent mineral surfaces produced organic chains and simple hydrocarbons.

These environments provided both chemical complexity and sustained redox gradients.

### Abiogenesis
Early life likely originated in deep-rock fracture systems where:

* Warm saline water circulated through iron- and sulfur-rich minerals

* Natural galvanic cells formed between oxidized and reduced surfaces

* Electron flows powered primitive metabolic cycles

Proto-organisms evolved conductive filaments similar to cable bacteria, allowing them to bridge redox gradients and harvest energy from spatially separated electron donors and acceptors.

### Pre-autotrophic evolution
Over millions of years, conductive mats diversified. Some lineages internalized mineral compartments, leading to:

* Proto-battery structures seeded with FeS or Fe–Ni crystals

* Compartmentalized electron flows

* Primitive water- and peroxide-processing reactions

These innovations set the stage for full electroautotrophy.

## Primary producers

### Geo-electric autotrophs
Geo-electric autotrophs fix CO₂ into organic matter using energy captured from spatially separated, replenished geochemical donor/acceptor systems. Their FeS/Fe–Ni-seeded **battery organelles** contain mineral electrodes, electron-transport membranes, proton-pumping chains and controlled electrolysis chambers. Conductive tissues carry current; insulating tissues keep incompatible reactants apart.

Charging oxidizes donors such as H₂, sulfide or Fe²⁺ and reduces available acceptors at a separate interface. Example balanced half-reactions are `H₂ → 2 H⁺ + 2 e⁻` and `Fe³⁺ + e⁻ → Fe²⁺`; actual favorable coupling depends on activities and local potentials. Consumed oxidants require geological/radiolytic replenishment. The stored gradient powers ATP synthesis, carbon fixation, pumping and, where enough work is available, water oxidation. Stacking cells raises voltage at a cost in current and material, not free power.

The oxygen-generating water half-reaction is:

`2 H₂O → O₂ + 4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻`

Coupled to `4 H⁺ + 4 e⁻ → 2 H₂`, its complete electrolysis balance is:

`2 H₂O + electrical work → 2 H₂ + O₂`

This consumes work; the electrons are not returned to reduced minerals as though that recharged the system. Standard-condition reversible work is about 474 kJ per mole of O₂, with real biological costs and pressure effects additional. If the released H₂ and O₂ simply recombine locally, there is no net oxygen accumulation or new primary energy.

Peroxide processing provides another route:

`2 H₂O₂ → O₂ + 2 H₂O`

That disproportionation is not equivalent to unpowered water splitting. Net oxidant production requires nuclear/radiolytic input, maintained chemical disequilibrium or another paid charging process. A speculative thermogalvanic pathway can exploit hot vent/cold surroundings, with a heat sink and finite efficiency. Radiogenic heat and radiolytic chemical production must not be counted twice.

The architecture is fictional: real cable bacteria support the conductivity analogy, not oxygen-producing trees. Product separation, net CO₂ fixation, usable current and oxygen export constrain forest productivity. Oxygen release creates enhanced local niches within a broader low-fraction but appreciable-pO₂ atmosphere.

### Tissue organization
Geo-electric autotrophs exhibit:

* Conductive interior filaments linking anode and cathode tissues

* Thick insulating outer layers composed of a **lignin-analog polymer** that prevents electrical shorting

* Root-like anchoring structures that penetrate fractures and mineral seams

Collectively, they form large-scale structures resembling forests.

## Battery forests

### Morphology
Battery forests consist of tree-like organisms standing several meters tall. They have:

* Sturdy insulating trunks

* Conductive central cores

* Branch-like electrochemical structures

* Dense bioluminescent fruiting organs

These structures bridge real chemical interfaces and support many coupled cells. Height alone does not create an exploitable voltage.

### Growth patterns
Battery trees show rapid vent blooms in warm, mineral-rich fracture systems, drawing on stored reserves and concentrated geochemical supply. Their standing biomass and growth remain limited by net power, carbon and nutrients. Their reproduction is strongly r-selected:

* Short generation cycles

* Wide dispersal of hardy spores

* Rapid colonization of new geothermal sites

Entire forests can bloom and die as vents shift or plates move.

### Ecosystem engineering
Battery forests create:

* Oxygen microclimates

* Complex microbial soils

* Organic-rich substrates for secondary consumers

* Structural habitats for bioluminescent grazers and predators

## Petrified forests

### Formation
When vent systems migrate with plate motion, battery trees lose access to redox gradients. Without power:

* Organisms starve and die

* Conductive tissues decompose

* Insulating lignin-analog polymer is infiltrated/replaced by minerals where fluid supply permits petrification

The result is a ‘‘petrified forest’’—a band of fossilized pillars tracing a former tectonic boundary.

### Geological distribution
Petrified belts:

* Occur in the interiors of old tectonic plates

* Provide markers of paleo-geothermal activity

* Preserve long-term ecological history

### Cultural and ecological roles
These regions host:

* Mixed decomposers that digest remaining organics, accompanied by nitrifying microorganisms

* Harvesting sites for Skemer-mense, who use petrified wood in tools

* Cultural memorials marking ancestral migrations of life

## Secondary producers and degraders

### Nitrifiers
Nitrifying analogues consume oxygen and process ammonia; some fix carbon and are primary producers. Polymer digestion belongs to associated decomposers or explicitly multifunctional organisms. Together these communities:

* Digest battery-wood polymer fragments through their decomposer partners

* Produce nitrate-rich soils

* Support detritivore food webs

### Sulfur oxidizers
Present in vent soils and geothermal lakes, sulfur oxidizers:

* Convert sulfides to sulfate

* Support mixed microbial mats

* Create acidic microenvironments

### Methanotrophs
Key organisms in cryobrine lakes and subglacial pockets:

* Oxidize methane using trace oxygen

* Produce low-light bioluminescence

* Stabilize chemical stratification in cold environments

### Organic recyclers
Heterotrophs break down aromatic polymers and short-chain molecules, recycling carbon into the system.

## Higher organisms

### Grazer analogues
Macro-organisms adapted to:

* Feed on nitrifying and sulfur-oxidizing mats

* Navigate by bioluminescence and electroreception

* Reduce insulating tissues for nutrient extraction

### Scavengers and predators
Predatory organisms use:

* Dedicated thermal-gradient receptors, distinct from ordinary visible-light eyes

* Vibratory detection

* Flash-pattern bioluminescence

They rely on the O₂ pockets generated by autotrophs.

### Mobility adaptations
Common traits include:

* Thick dermal insulation

* Chemical-resistant tissues

* Fully dark-adapted sensory systems

## Bioluminescence

### Evolutionary drivers
Because Skemer’s surface is naturally pitch black, bioluminescence evolved for:

* Spore dispersal

* Prey attraction

* Social signaling

### Chemical pathways
Biochemical systems use:

* Low-oxygen luciferin analogs

* Peroxide-based light emission

* Electrochemically triggered flashes

### Ecological roles
Synchronized blinking events occur during ‘‘vent blooms’’—brief population surges after eruptions.

## Spatial distribution

### Tectonic belts
Life is densest along:

* Ridges

* Subduction arcs

* Transform fault networks

These regions contain:

* High heat flow

* Liquid water

* Abundant oxidants and reductants

### Intermediate zones
Between belts and plate interiors lie:

* Thin lichen-like mats

* Small geothermal pools

* Sparse microflora

### Plate-interior deserts
Cold, dry, nutrient-poor regions support:

* Wind-scattered spores

* Subsurface microbial pockets only

### Subsurface refugia
Under ice sheets, cryobrine channels and lakes support:

* Methanotrophs

* Sulfate reducers

* Sparse grazers

## Atmospheric interactions

### Oxygen production and stability
The planetary design retains a **0.30-bar O₂ partial pressure** in a 100-bar working atmosphere, with local source and sink differences. This is not a low oxygen partial pressure relative to Earth. Warm, connected vent belts and brine corridors maintain growth, reproduction, dormancy and dispersal; regional chemical pockets supplement this continuing life cycle rather than replacing it.

A source ledger must separately count powered water oxidation, newly formed radiolytic peroxide and any externally replenished oxychlorine minerals. A sink ledger must count respiration, nitrification, sulfur oxidation, weathering, hydrogen reactions, burial, escape and deposition. SO₂/peroxide radiochemistry and perchlorate breakdown are candidate pathways with finite yields and charging costs, not unlimited oxygen sources. Peroxide manufactured from existing O₂ and then decomposed gives no net new O₂.

The present global inventory is a canon constraint, not a demonstrated steady-state flux. At 474 kJ/mol O₂, even converting the entire approximately 2.29×10¹⁴-W internal luminosity into electrolysis would give only about 4.8×10⁸ mol O₂/s; real usable work is much smaller and must also sustain the ecosystem. This deliberately generous ceiling is not a production estimate. The atmosphere's actual chemical lifetime and required biological output remain to be modeled.

### Gas cycling
Major cycles include:

* CH₄ production and oxidation

* NH₃ outgassing and biological consumption

* Sulfur cycling between H₂S, SO₂, and sulfates

### Climate feedbacks
Battery forests influence trace gases by:

* Uptake of organics

* Local modulation of CH₄ and NH₃

* Stabilization of near-surface humidity

## Evolutionary themes

### Multicellularity
Driven by:

* Need to maintain spatial electron gradients

* Advantages of vertical structures that bridge chemically separated interfaces

### Electrochemical specialization
Battery organelles represent:

* Internalized mineral electrodes

* Controlled electron pathways

* Complex compartmentalization analogous to mitochondria

### Dispersal and r-selection
Rapid tectonic turnover selects for:

* Fast reproduction

* High dispersal

* Extreme environmental tolerance

## Physical basis

The tidal, atmospheric and energy calculations are on [[Skemer]] under Physical basis, and the oxygen source and sink ledgers above remain unclosed. Cable bacteria ([Bjerg et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5984516/)) are a conductivity analogue, not oxygenic battery trees, and radiolysis ([Sauvage et al. 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-21218-z)) is a local energy source, not an unlimited oxidant flux. No complete redox, ecological-productivity or climate simulation has been run.

**Nivalis** is a cold, low-gravity terrestrial planet orbiting at 1.9 AU from the star [[Nimvion 3421]], situated just inside the system’s frost line. With a mass of 0.20 Earth masses and a radius of 0.645 Earth radii, Nivalis has a lower escape speed than Earth. Retention of its heavy atmosphere depends on the upper-atmospheric temperature, stellar high-energy flux, escape processes, and volatile replenishment. Cold trapping stores part of the volatile inventory as surface and subsurface ice.

Its surface is veiled beneath pale fog layers and colored by high-altitude nitrile hazes that give the world a muted lavender-grey sheen. Small particles carried in stable atmospheric layers create periods of clearer air below elevated cloud and haze decks. Lower gravity slows settling for otherwise similar particles.

## Physical and Orbital Parameters

Nivalis receives about 22 percent of Earth’s stellar flux. A Bond albedo of 0.35 gives a stellar equilibrium temperature near 171 K with uniform heat redistribution. The proposed surface mean of about 215 K requires greenhouse warming and remains a climate-model target.

Surface pressure averages about 0.30 atm, and rotation takes about 28 hours. Circulation depends on atmospheric heating, topography, and seasonal forcing. Stable layers and inversions support persistent local fog banks.

## Atmosphere
Nivalis’s atmosphere is thin but compositionally complex, shaped by the refrigeration effects of its orbit and a steady supply of freeze-trapped volatiles. CO₂ dominates, with nitrogen as a minor buffer gas. The quoted NH₃ and CH₄ ranges describe variable, potentially replenished abundances. Their global persistence requires a source and photochemical loss budget. A suite of reduced and nitrile-bearing species creates both the fog and the upper hazes.

Fine particles in the upper atmosphere produce the proposed **lavender and violet haze**. Organic mixtures, mineral impurities, grain size, and lighting determine the tint. Nitriles contribute to the chemistry, while their individual visible colors remain to be established. **Pearl-grey ice clouds and fog** develop where local temperatures and vapor partial pressures permit condensation.

## Surface and Geochemistry
Surface coloration comes from slow, cold chemical weathering and deposition of exotic compounds. Where fog thins, the ground shows muted but striking color:

- **Carbonate-rich basins** with pale green to blue-green mineral films whose pigments remain to be identified
- **Nickel oxalates and chloride hydrates** forming pastel green crusts
- **Organic coatings containing acrylonitrile and cyanoacetylene**, mixed with the proposed lavender and amethyst chromophores
- **Cyanide polymers** settling as fine, grey-lilac dust

Crater floors, fracture zones, and ancient channels show the most intense staining, revealing the chemistry beneath the fog.

## Climate and Fog Regime

Radiative cooling can establish local inversions in sheltered basins and during cold seasons. Condensate composition depends on temperature and partial pressure.

- CO₂ frost and suspended ice particles occur in sufficiently cold polar, nighttime, or high-altitude regions.
- Ammonia-bearing condensates can occur where vapor abundance and temperature permit.
- Trace water ice and organic particles contribute to pearl-grey cloud decks.
- Fine haze particles remain aloft through slow settling and atmospheric mixing.

The proposed 215 K surface mean allows much colder regions and layers. CO₂ condensation and redistribution must be included when modeling the 0.30 atm atmosphere. Elevated particle layers create dim landscapes with flashes of color where surface films catch low-angle light.

## Visual Appearance from Space
From orbit, Nivalis appears as a **pearl-grey sphere** with faint lavender edges at the terminator. Subtle green or amethyst patches sometimes emerge where highlands breach the fog. The upper atmosphere is thin enough that background stars remain visible through the haze, yet dense enough to obscure most surface detail.

## Astrobiological Potential
Nivalis is too cold for surface liquid water, but it is rich in molecules relevant to prebiotic chemistry. Cyanoacetylene, acrylonitrile, and HCN support the formation of nitrile polymers and vesicle-like structures. Temporary heat sources within impact melt pockets or deep brines could enable short-lived, localized chemotrophic activity.

## System Context
Nivalis orbits outside [[Stygium]], between the terrestrial planets and the [[Aurion and Argyra|co-orbital giants]]. As the primary frost-line world in the system, it hosts a unique inventory of nitriles, cyanides, and cold-trapped volatiles. Its spectral character, fog structure, and surface chemistries add a distinct outer-edge flavor to the diversity of the [[Nimvion System]].

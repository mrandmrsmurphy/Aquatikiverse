## Overview
**Stygium** is a dense superterrestrial planet in the [[Nimvion System]], orbiting [[Nimvion 3421]] at 1.206 AU. With a mass of about 1.7 M🜨 and a radius of 1.22 R🜨, it has surface gravity ≈1.14 g🜨 and a compact, metal-rich interior. The surface environment is dominated by a dense **CO₂–SO₂–H₂S atmosphere** at ≈2 atm, scant water, and widespread arsenic–sulfur minerals.

Despite its hostile chemistry by Earth standards, Stygium hosts a mature, non-water biosphere based on arsenic redox chemistry and liquid sulfur dioxide as the primary solvent. The details of this biosphere are covered in [[Life on Stygium]].

## Orbital and Physical Properties
Stygium orbits within the outer part of Nimvion 3421’s nominal habitable zone:

- Semimajor axis: 1.206 AU
- Orbital period: ≈502 Earth days
- Eccentricity: 0.03 (mild seasons)
- Stellar flux: ≈0.55 F🜨 from a 0.80 L☉ star

With Bond albedo ≈0.40, a bare-rock equilibrium temperature would be ≈210 K. The dense CO₂/SO₂/H₂S atmosphere adds ≈50 K of greenhouse warming, giving mean surface temperatures around **260 K**, with local variation between cold uplands and warmer SO₂ basins.

The planet’s 10-hour rotation produces a modest equatorial bulge and supports strong zonal winds in the middle atmosphere. The tidal quality factor Q ≈85 is a working dissipation parameter. A convecting electrically conductive core supplies the planetary dynamo. Its strength depends on core structure and thermal evolution.

## Interior Structure and Magnetosphere

Stygium’s interior is strongly differentiated and metal-rich, consistent with its high mean density and strong magnetic field. A schematic structure is:

| Zone | Composition (approximate) | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core | Fe–S–Cu with trace siderophiles (e.g., Ni, Co) | Thermal and compositional convection; main dynamo region |
| Core–mantle boundary | Fe–S alloys with localized Nd-bearing and Ta–As seams | Conductive boundary layers and thermal exchange with the core |
| Mantle | Silicates enriched in FeS, Ti, and other transition metals | Heat transport; supports volcanism and sulfur-rich outgassing |
| Crust | Veins of Au–Cu–As–S–Ni; local enrichment in Nd, Gd, Ta, W, Mo, Bi | Local conductivity contrasts and magnetic mineral anomalies define ecological field zones |
| Magnetosphere | Generated by the core dynamo, with shape set by interaction with the stellar wind | Shields atmosphere and provides gradients used by native life |

The core dynamo generates a structured planetary magnetic field. Magnetized crustal minerals and conductivity contrasts add local anomalies. Many native organisms exploit local field gradients for navigation, communication, and internal bioelectric control.

## Atmosphere

Stygium’s atmosphere is dense and sulfur-rich, with optical depth τ in the visible of order 2–3, leading to dim, amber-tinted daylight at the surface.

### Bulk Composition

A representative lowland mixture near 2 atm total pressure is given below. The fractions are rounded, and SO₂ abundance varies with condensation and temperature.

| Gas | Approximate volume fraction | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CO₂ | 50% | Major greenhouse gas and carbon source |
| SO₂ | 44% | Principal condensable and biological solvent |
| H₂S | 3% | Reducing gas and sulfur-metabolism substrate |
| N₂, Ar, and minor gases | Balance, about 3% | Buffer gases and trace constituents |
| O₂ | Trace, generally <0.01% | Rapidly consumed |
| H₂O vapor | Trace, generally <0.1% | Restricted to localized geochemical and biological exchange |
| Formaldehyde (CH₂O) | ppm–ppb | Carbon-fixation and redox intermediate |

At 2 atm total pressure, 44% SO₂ gives a partial pressure near 0.88 atm, about 0.89 bar. This is close to pure SO₂ saturation near 260 K. Lake margins occupy local conditions close to the condensation curve. Cooling, mixing, and evaporation generate fog above basins, while warmer exposed regions are drier. Dissolved salts and other solutes modify the solvent’s vapor pressure.

Sulfur and arsenic-bearing aerosols provide much of the visible opacity and UV absorption. The representative visible optical depth of 2–3 varies with aerosol loading.

The condensation relation is based on laboratory phase data. It supplies a physical starting point for lakes and fog, while the complete sulfur-rich climate still requires radiative and atmospheric-chemistry modeling. ## Surface Environment

The crust is dominated by iron–arsenic–sulfur minerals and associated conductive ores. Typical surface lithologies include:

- Arsenopyrite (FeAsS)
- Realgar (As₄S₄) and orpiment (As₂S₃)
- Scorodite (FeAsO₄·2H₂O) in localized hydrated zones
- Pyrrhotite (Fe₁₋ₓS) and troilite (FeS)
- Native gold (Au) and copper (Cu) in conductive veins

Surface landscapes feature:

- **Ferric-crust plains**: oxidized iron–arsenic surfaces with thin regolith and scattered mineral veining.
- **SO₂ lakes and basins**: low-lying depressions where pressure and temperature permit liquid SO₂, often overlain by dense sulfur fog.
- **Scorodite dunes**: pale arsenate-rich deposits in partially hydrated regions.
- **Greigite (Fe₃S₄) and magnetite reefs**: conductive mineral ridges that shape local EM topography and serve as ecological “backbones.”

Water is extremely scarce and largely bound in minerals or confined to micro-environments inside organisms or hydrothermal systems. Surface free water is effectively absent.

## Habitability Framework

By Earth standards, Stygium’s surface is highly toxic: high CO₂, pervasive SO₂ and H₂S, arsenic-saturated rocks, and scarce water. However, these same conditions support a **stable arsenic–sulfur redox biosphere**:

- Liquid SO₂ provides a polar, moderately dense solvent for biochemical reactions.
- Arsenic-rich crust supplies abundant As(III)/As(V) redox pairs.
- Sulfur gases (SO₂, H₂S) and iron-bearing minerals provide additional metabolic couples (HS⁻/S⁰, Fe(II)/Fe(III)).
- Formaldehyde and other carbon-bearing trace species support carbon fixation.

Surface temperatures and pressures allow long-lived SO₂ lakes and aerosols, especially in basins and sheltered lowlands. Volcanism and hydrothermal systems continuously refresh atmospheric gases and maintain redox disequilibria.

## Biosphere

Main article: [[Life on Stygium]]

Stygium hosts a mature, arsenic-based biosphere that has been active for roughly 1.2 billion years. Key features include:

- **Solvent:** Liquid SO₂, with water restricted to intracellular or micro-environment roles.
- **Macromolecules:** Proposed arsenosugars, arsenolipids, thioarsenates, and arsenodiester genetic polymers perform structural, metabolic, and hereditary roles. Their exact structures and stability in SO₂ remain under investigation.
- **Energy metabolism:**
  - Phototrophs (“plants”) oxidize arsenite (As(III)) to arsenate (As(V)) using green–yellow light (peak ≈540 nm), driving carbon fixation via formaldehyde intermediates.
  - Heterotrophs reduce arsenate back to arsenite while oxidizing CH₂O to CO₂, using proposed adenosine triarsenate (ATA) analogues for energy transfer.
- **Biomineralization:** Fe–As–S skeletal frameworks, gold nanowires, and greigite/magnetite chains provide structural support and high conductivity.
- **Field-guided physiology:** Many organisms use the planetary magnetic field and local EM gradients for orientation, coordination, and control of metabolically powered locomotion.

Major ecological clades include:

- **Ferriphyta** – tall, rigid phototrophs on metal-rich uplands.
- **Scorophyta** – mat-forming photoautotrophs on harsh plains.
- **Vesculophyta** – buoyant producers in SO₂ lakes and small airborne forms carried by atmospheric flows.
- **Electroskeletalia** – motile heterotrophs with field-gated biomineral skeletons, including lineages such as Monoclasts, Oscillomorphs, Tectomorphs, Aerocrysts, Syntonals, and highly plastic Polyplasts of the phylum Polyplastia.

The sapient species [[Morphfiends|Mentogymnos schematica]] (“Morphfiends”) arise within the Polyplasts and employ programmable conductive matrices rather than neurons for cognition.

## Summary
Stygium is a high-density, sulfurous super-Earth with a dense CO₂–SO₂–H₂S atmosphere, strong magnetic field, and almost no surface water. These conditions, while hostile to terrestrial life, have given rise to a stable, arsenic-based biosphere built on liquid SO₂, arsenic redox chemistry, and field-guided biomineralization, making Stygium a distinctive speculative living world in the [[Nimvion System]].

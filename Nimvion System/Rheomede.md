**Rheomede** is a warm Saturn-like gas giant located at 0.68 astronomical units from its host star [[Nimvion 3421]], a G–K borderline dwarf with a luminosity of 0.80 L☉ and a photospheric temperature of 5400 K. Rheomede is the innermost gas giant of the system, interior to the Earth-analog [[Francesca]] (0.894 AU) and the CO₂–arsenic world [[Stygium]] (1.206 AU).

With a mass of about 70.4 Earth masses and a strongly oblate shape, Rheomede is dominated by a low-density hydrogen–helium envelope, an estimated rotation period near 16 hours, and a dark, asymmetric ring complex.

## Orbital and Physical Characteristics
Rheomede completes an orbit in about 212 Earth days. Although tidal forces at 0.68 AU act on the planet, its rotation remains rapid and far from synchronous.

The incident stellar flux is about 1.7 times that received by Earth. With a Bond albedo of about 0.28, the radiative equilibrium temperature (neglecting greenhouse and internal heat) is roughly 294 K. Actual cloud temperatures depend on pressure, atmospheric circulation, and interior heat. The zero-redistribution substellar radiative limit is about 416 K. Rapid rotation and winds distribute heating across the planet.

## Oblateness and Rotation
Rheomede is visibly oblate, with its equatorial radius about 14 percent greater than its polar radius. The shape is the defining geometric constraint. A working rotation period near 16 hours is consistent with substantial rotational flattening for plausible centrally concentrated interiors. An interior model is needed to refine the period and gravity harmonics. From orbit, the bulging equator and compressed poles give the world a distinctly distorted silhouette, with cloud belts stretched around the equatorial arc.

Equatorial regions show golden and beige tones, mid-latitudes deepen to rust and umber, and the poles are mottled gray and yellow from cooler chemistry and circulation differences.

## Internal Heat and Thermal Appearance

Rheomede radiates infrared energy from its atmosphere and cooling interior. The 420 K reference describes a deeper atmospheric layer. Its intrinsic effective temperature and internal luminosity remain unspecified.

Thermal images can reveal warmer upwellings, cloud-opacity differences, and nightside circulation. Orange-red thermal renderings use false color. The planet’s visible appearance is dominated by reflected starlight.

## Ring System
Rheomede is surrounded by a dark, asymmetric ring system composed of carbonaceous particles, shattered silicates, and magnetically influenced nanoiron. The rings extend outward to **2.6 equatorial radii**, giving an outer boundary of:

**≈193,000 km from the planetary center.**

The rings possess an extremely low albedo (≤0.05), appearing charcoal-dark in reflected light, with fine particles more apparent in forward-scattering views. They cast thin, curved shadows across the planet’s daylit hemisphere. Rheomede’s gravitational quadrupole causes nodal precession and helps organize inner ring material around its equatorial plane. Inclined debris and other perturbations can produce local warps.

## Moonlet and Shedding Dynamics

Small satellites with radii up to about 100 km shepherd arcs and supply dust through impacts, collisions, and surface erosion. At Rheomede’s low bulk density, the fluid Roche limit for dense rocky material lies close to the planet. Much of the 193,000 km ring complex therefore occupies a region where debris can accumulate into moonlets.

The dark rings are an actively replenished debris complex. Collisions, small satellites, gravitational resonances, and the response of charged fine grains to the magnetosphere produce transient arcs and fragment trails. Ring mass, particle lifetimes, and moonlet orbits remain model parameters.

## Visual Appearance
From low orbit, Rheomede displays:

- Equatorial belts in beige, amber, and bronze
- Mid-latitude bands in rust-red and dark umber
- Polar regions mottled in gray and muted yellow
- A matte charcoal ring system set against a visibly oblate disk

From the orbit of [[Francesca]], Rheomede appears as a reddish crescent with a thin, dark halo. Ring shadows cut across the flattened disk at an angle, giving the planet a multi-layered and visually distinct outline.

## Role in System
Rheomede influences the inner [[Nimvion System]] through gravitational sculpting, magnetospheric activity, and the redistribution of rocky debris and transient volatile-bearing material. Though uninhabitable, it is a major dynamical agent governing resonant structures, dust transport, and interactions between minor bodies.


## Modeling notes

The equatorial and polar radii, flattening, and dark ring silhouette are the primary visual constraints. The estimated 16-hour day supersedes the original 9.2-hour value. A hydrostatic interior calculation should refine that period while retaining the specified oblate geometry. Deep cloud chemistry and intrinsic luminosity remain uncertain.

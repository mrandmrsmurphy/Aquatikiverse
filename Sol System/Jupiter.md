# Jupiter

Jupiter is the largest planet, a giant ball of hydrogen and helium with more than twice the mass of all the other planets combined. It has a strong magnetic field, a system of 95 known moons (per the NASA fact sheet) and storms that last for centuries.

## Physics and chemistry

**Bulk properties.** Jupiter has 317.8 Earth masses and a mean radius of 69,911 km (10.97 Earth radii; the equatorial radius of 71,492 km is larger because the planet spins every 9.93 hours and is flattened). Its density of 1.33 g/cm³ is a quarter of Earth's, typical of a hydrogen–helium composition with a heavier core. The gravity at the 1-bar level is about 24.8 m/s² (25.9 using the mean radius), and the escape velocity is 60.2 km/s.

**Interior.** Hydrogen turns from molecular gas to a conducting fluid at pressures of a few hundred gigapascals, and the motion of that metallic hydrogen drives the strongest planetary magnetic field in the Solar System. Jupiter radiates more heat than it absorbs from the Sun, a leftover of its formation that is slowly released as it contracts.

**Atmosphere and moons.** Bands of ammonia-ice cloud and a pattern of fast zonal jets circle the planet, and the Great Red Spot is an anticyclone larger than Earth. The four large Galilean moons are Io (the most volcanically active body known), Europa (an ice shell over a salty ocean), Ganymede (the largest moon in the Solar System, with its own magnetic field) and Callisto.

## In the Aquatikiverse

[[Ceraun]] is the vault's hot Jupiter: 150 Earth masses (about 0.47 Jupiter masses) but inflated to 1.1 Jupiter radii by heating at 0.1 AU, with a density of 0.44 g/cm³ against Jupiter's 1.33 g/cm³.

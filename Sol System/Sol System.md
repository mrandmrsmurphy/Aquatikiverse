# Sol System

The Solar System is the real-world reference system of the vault: the Sun, eight planets, five recognised dwarf planets and a large population of small bodies. Its numbers are the baseline against which the fictional systems are compared.

## Star

[[Sun]]: G2 V, 1 M☉, 1 L☉, 5,772 K, about 4.6 Gyr old. It holds 99.86% of the system's mass.

Four rocky planets ([[Mercury]], [[Venus]], [[Earth]], [[Mars]]) lie inside 1.6 AU. Beyond the asteroid belt are the gas giants [[Jupiter]] and [[Saturn]] and the ice giants [[Uranus]] and [[Neptune]], which hold most of the system's planetary mass (Jupiter alone is 317.8 M⊕) and nearly all of its angular momentum.

[[Ceres]] orbits in the asteroid belt; [[Pluto]], [[Haumea]], [[Makemake]] and [[Eris]] orbit beyond Neptune, in the Kuiper belt and scattered disc. Orbits of the dwarf planets are osculating elements from the JPL Small-Body Database; their masses and radii are less certain than the planets', especially Makemake's mass and Haumea's shape.

## Definitions

Under the IAU definition of 2006, a **planet** orbits the Sun, is massive enough to be nearly round (hydrostatic equilibrium) and has cleared its orbital neighbourhood. A **dwarf planet** orbits the Sun and is round but has not cleared its neighbourhood and is not a moon. Eris, discovered in 2005 with more mass than Pluto, prompted the definition.

## Small bodies

The **asteroid belt** between Mars and Jupiter (roughly 2.1–3.3 AU) holds rocky and metallic bodies too small to be round, with Ceres as its largest. The **Kuiper belt** beyond Neptune (roughly 30–50 AU) holds icy bodies including Pluto, Haumea and Makemake; the **scattered disc**, which includes Eris, extends much farther on eccentric orbits. The **Oort cloud**, a presumed spherical shell of icy bodies at thousands of AU, is the source of long-period comets. These descriptions are general astronomical knowledge, not drawn from the data sources below.

## Conventions

- **Semimajor axis, eccentricity, inclination:** JPL approximate planetary elements (Table 1, 1800–2050) for the planets; JPL Small-Body Database osculating elements (epoch JD 2461200.5) for Ceres, Haumea, Makemake and Eris; NASA Planetary Fact Sheet for Pluto. Inclinations are to the ecliptic.
- **Period:** Kepler's third law, P = a^1.5 years, which agrees with the published sidereal periods to within 0.2%.
- **Flux:** 1/a² in units of Earth's flux at 1 AU (about 1,361 W/m²), at the semimajor axis. The orbit-averaged flux is higher by a factor 1/√(1−e²): +0.2% for Earth, +2.2% for Mercury (a 6.7 F⊕ nominal becomes 6.8), +3% for Pluto.
- **Radius and density:** the volumetric mean radius (a sphere with the same volume), as defined by JPL; density is the bulk density from mass and mean radius. The equatorial radius is larger for fast spinners (Jupiter 71,492 km, Saturn 60,268 km) and appears in each body article.
- **Rotation:** sidereal period in days; "R" marks retrograde rotation. **Obliquity:** tilt of the spin axis to the orbit; "—" where the NASA fact sheet gives no value.
- **Moons:** known satellites per the NASA Planetary Fact Sheet for the planets and Pluto, and standard catalogue counts for Ceres (0), Haumea (2), Makemake (1) and Eris (1). Counts rise as new moons are found.
- **Mass and radius sources:** the [JPL Planetary Physical Parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/phys_par.html) table. Makemake's mass (3.1×10²¹ kg) has no printed uncertainty, and Haumea's mean radius and density are printed without uncertainties.

All computed values are produced by the Sol reference calculation.

# Mercury

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun, with a year of only 88 days. It is a dense, airless, cratered world with extreme day–night temperature swings, and it turns exactly three times for every two orbits.

## Physics and chemistry

**Orbit and spin.** Mercury has the most eccentric orbit of the planets (e = 0.206), so its distance from the Sun swings between 0.307 and 0.467 AU and the flux it receives between about 10.6 and 4.6 times Earth's. Tides from the Sun locked it into a 3:2 spin–orbit resonance: one sidereal rotation takes 58.65 days, exactly two thirds of its 87.97-day orbit, so a solar day (sunrise to sunrise) lasts about 176 days, two Mercury years. Its axis is almost perpendicular to its orbit (obliquity 0.03°), which lets the floors of deep polar craters stay permanently shadowed and cold enough to hold water ice.

**Interior and density.** Mercury's bulk density (5.43 g/cm³) is as high as Earth's even though its much lower gravity compresses it far less. That requires an unusually large iron-rich core, roughly 85% of the planet's radius, with a thin rock mantle and crust. Giant-impact stripping of an early mantle and the chemically reducing, volatile-poor conditions near the young Sun are the leading explanations.

**Atmosphere and surface.** At 3.70 m/s² gravity and 4.25 km/s escape velocity, with a dayside above 700 K, Mercury cannot hold a lasting atmosphere. It has only a thin exosphere of atoms knocked off the surface by sunlight and the solar wind, so the sunlit ground radiates away its heat each night and drops to about 100 K.

## In the Aquatikiverse

Barzél is the nearest fictional cousin: a locked, airless iron world, but far hotter and denser (7.8 g/cm³ against Mercury's 5.43 g/cm³). Mercury's polar cold traps are the model for the shadowed craters that hold tar and sulfur on Pekhám.

# Uranus

Uranus is an ice giant rolled onto its side, orbiting the Sun with its axis tilted 97.8°. It is pale blue-green from methane, very cold, and has surprisingly little internal heat.

## Physics and chemistry

**Bulk properties.** Uranus has 14.5 Earth masses and a mean radius of 25,362 km (3.98 Earth radii), a density of 1.27 g/cm³, a 1-bar gravity of about 8.9 m/s² (9.0 using the mean radius) and an escape velocity of 21.4 km/s. It orbits at 19.19 AU and receives only 0.0027 times Earth's sunlight.

**Tilt and rotation.** The planet turns retrograde every 17.2 hours, and its axis is tilted 97.8° to its orbit, so each pole points almost at the Sun for decades. The tilt may come from one or more giant impacts during formation. The 84-year orbit gives each pole about 42 years of sunlight followed by 42 years of darkness.

**Composition and heat.** A thick hydrogen–helium envelope with a few percent methane covers a deep mantle of water, ammonia and methane ices (the "ices" are hot, dense fluids at depth) and a small rocky core. Methane absorbs red light and gives the blue-green color. Unlike Neptune and the other giants, Uranus emits almost no more heat than it absorbs, which suggests that a stable compositional layering in its interior traps the heat.

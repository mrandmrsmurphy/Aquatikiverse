# Saturn

Saturn is the second-largest planet, a hydrogen–helium giant so light that it would float on water, surrounded by the Solar System's most spectacular ring system and a large family of moons.

## Physics and chemistry

**Bulk properties.** Saturn has 95.2 Earth masses and a mean radius of 58,232 km (9.14 Earth radii; the equatorial radius of 60,268 km is much larger because of fast rotation, 10.7 hours, and low density). Its bulk density of 0.687 g/cm³ is lower than water's. Gravity at the 1-bar level is about 10.4 m/s² and the escape velocity is 36.1 km/s.

**Atmosphere and interior.** The atmosphere is mostly hydrogen and helium with methane and ammonia, in bands more muted than Jupiter's. A hexagonal jet pattern surrounds the north pole. Saturn radiates more heat than it absorbs; part of the extra heat is thought to come from helium separating out and sinking through the hydrogen interior.

**Rings and moons.** The rings are made of water-ice particles from centimetres to metres across, spread across hundreds of thousands of kilometres but only tens of metres thick on average. NASA lists 274 moons. Titan has a thick nitrogen atmosphere with methane lakes and rain, and Enceladus vents water from a subsurface ocean.

No fictional world is a Saturn analog yet. It is the vault's reference for a low-density giant, for ring systems (compare the ringed worlds in other systems) and for a methane-rich moon atmosphere.

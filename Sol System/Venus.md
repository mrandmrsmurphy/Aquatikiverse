# Venus

Venus is Earth's near twin in size and mass, but it has a crushing carbon-dioxide atmosphere, a surface hot enough to melt lead, and a slow backward rotation. It shows what a runaway greenhouse does to a rocky planet.

## Physics and chemistry

**Size and orbit.** Venus has 0.815 Earth masses, 0.950 Earth radii and a density of 5.24 g/cm³, so its gravity (8.87 m/s²) is close to Earth's. It orbits at 0.723 AU, in a nearly circular orbit (e = 0.007), and receives 1.91 times Earth's sunlight at the top of its atmosphere.

**Rotation.** Venus turns once every 243 days *backward* (retrograde, obliquity 177.4°), longer than its 224.7-day year, so a sunrise-to-sunrise day lasts about 117 Earth days. The upper cloud layer circles the planet in about four days, far faster than the ground below.

**Atmosphere.** The air is about 96% carbon dioxide at roughly 92 bar at the surface, with sulfuric-acid clouds. The surface averages about 737 K (464 °C), hotter than even Mercury's sunlit ground (about 700 K) despite being twice as far from the Sun, because CO₂ and water vapor trap outgoing infrared. The clouds are so reflective that Venus absorbs less sunlight than Earth does, yet the dense atmosphere still holds the surface well above any liquid-water range. Venus probably once held more water, which was photolysed and lost to space; this is why it is the standard picture of a runaway greenhouse.

## In the Aquatikiverse

[[Aphrod]] is the Aquatikiverse's Venus analog: veiled in cloud and thick air, but with a milder 280 K surface, a 10–20 bar atmosphere and an ammonia-rich ocean beneath, so Venus is the baseline for Aphrod's greenhouse and cloud-top life.

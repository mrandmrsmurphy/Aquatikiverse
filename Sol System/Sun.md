# Sun

The Sun (Sol) is the star at the centre of the Solar System, an ordinary G2 main-sequence star that holds 99.86% of the system's mass and supplies almost all of its light and heat. It is the reference star for every stellar comparison in the vault.

## Summary

The Sun is a ball of hydrogen and helium plasma, held together by its own gravity and heated by fusion in its core. It radiates about 3.8×10²⁶ W, and the fraction that reaches Earth defines the flux unit used throughout the vault: 1 F⊕ ≈ 1,361 W/m² at 1 AU. Its light peaks in the green part of the spectrum and is a nearly blackbody curve at 5,772 K.

## Physics and chemistry

**Energy source.** In the core, at about 15.7 million K, hydrogen fuses into helium through the proton–proton chain, converting about 4 million tonnes of mass to energy each second. The energy takes thousands to hundreds of thousands of years to diffuse outward through the radiative zone, then rises through the convective outer layers to the visible surface (the photosphere), where it escapes as light.

**Structure.** From the centre outward are the core, the radiative zone, the convection zone and the photosphere, surrounded by the chromosphere and the million-degree corona, which expands as the solar wind. The photosphere's temperature range (4,400–6,600 K across its depth) is why the effective temperature is a single quoted value. Because the Sun is a fluid, it rotates differentially: the equator turns faster than the poles. Magnetic fields tangled by that rotation produce sunspots and flares in an 11-year cycle.

**Composition.** By number the photosphere is about 91% hydrogen and 9% helium; by mass roughly 74% hydrogen, 25% helium and under 2% heavier elements, a mixture inherited from the interstellar cloud that formed the system, with the helium fraction slightly raised by fusion in the core.

**Evolution.** The Sun brightens by roughly 1% every 100 million years as helium accumulates in its core. In several billion years it will leave the main sequence and expand into a red giant. This slow brightening is why Earth's carbonate–silicate cycle and the habitable-zone idea matter (see [[Earth]]).

## In the Aquatikiverse

The vault's stars are compared with the Sun by mass, luminosity and temperature. [[Thrírlitir]] has an F8 V primary hotter than the Sun (1.3 M☉, 2.505 L☉, 6,250 K), a G8 V companion slightly cooler (0.85 M☉, 0.566 L☉, 5,350 K) and an M1 V red dwarf (0.40 M☉, 0.0405 L☉, 3,800 K). The Sun's spectrum is the baseline for the photosynthesis and UV comparisons made for Aialon, Valen and Aphrod.

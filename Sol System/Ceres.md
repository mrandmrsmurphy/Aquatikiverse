# Ceres

Ceres is the largest object in the asteroid belt and the only dwarf planet in the inner Solar System. It is a rock–ice body with a brine-rich crust and bright salt deposits, visited by the Dawn spacecraft from 2015.

## Physics and chemistry

**Bulk properties.** Ceres has 9.38×10²⁰ kg (0.00016 Earth masses) and a mean radius of 469.7 km, giving a density of 2.16 g/cm³, a surface gravity of 0.28 m/s² and an escape velocity of 0.52 km/s. It orbits at 2.77 AU (e = 0.080, inclination 10.6°) with a 4.6-year period and spins every 9.07 hours with a small tilt.

**Why it is round.** Ceres is the only belt object massive enough for its own gravity to pull it into a near-spherical shape (hydrostatic equilibrium), which with its location among the asteroids makes it a dwarf planet. It is partly differentiated, with a rocky core and a weak ice-rich mantle and crust.

**Surface.** Dawn found carbonates and ammonia-bearing clays across the surface, with bright patches of sodium carbonate in Occator crater left by brines that reached the surface. These point to liquid water in Ceres's past and perhaps its present interior.

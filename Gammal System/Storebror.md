# Storebror

![[images/Storebror_render.png]]

*Artist’s impression of Storebror’s banded globe and bright rings against the crowded background.*

**Storebror**, also **Gammal c**, is the warm super-Jovian primary of [[Dold-Pärla]]. Its strong magnetic environment, rings, eleven-moon census and rapidly rotating atmosphere make it the dominant local astronomical presence in the [[Gammal System]]. It supplies a major **additional magnetic umbrella** while Dold-Pärla’s own atmosphere and field help maintain the setting’s low surface radiation.

“Saturn-like” may describe the ringed appearance; the adopted bulk mass is super-Jovian. The 1.1-R♃ radius is a volume-equivalent reference. With the fast spin, a flattening of 0.025 would be too small in a simple positive-J₂ hydrostatic estimate; 0.04 and J₂≈0.008 are an approximate consistent target rather than a solved rotating interior.

The atmosphere contains **H₂, He, NH₃, H₂O** and minor photochemical constituents. Bands and cloud structure are circulation features; low metallicity is not inferred from the corrected density. A deep conducting/metallic hydrogen region may support a dynamo. Thermal emission includes absorbed starlight and potentially internal heat; 252 K is an equilibrium reference, not a solid surface temperature or an interior retrieval.

## Magnetosphere: a protected local environment

The adopted **equatorial cloud-level field is about 3 mT (30 G)**, approximately seven times a representative Jovian equatorial surface field. This preserves a substantial difference from Earth: an inhabited moon moves within a giant planet’s much larger magnetic environment while also retaining its own shielding system.

A dipolar pressure-balance reference gives a standoff near **30 planetary radii**, about **2.36 million km**, for an illustrative external pressure near **5 nPa**. The magnetopause is not a fixed spherical wall; stellar wind, plasma loading, currents and field tilt distort it. Dold-Pärla’s orbit is at **6.1 Rp** and lies inside this reference. The simple dipolar external field there is about **13 μT**, a substantial background for its own field and magnetospheric currents.

This umbrella can redirect a substantial population of incoming charged particles. The moon’s approximately Earthlike-pressure atmosphere, intrinsic field and low energetic-particle loading in the adopted setting complete the **reported low/stable surface-dose premise**. Photon attenuation, high-energy cosmic-ray access, trapped belts and auroral deposition require separate treatment. The field does not stop all Galactic particles or X-rays, and it supplies no gravitational stability. A strong field and a low dose are distinct adopted constraints; the latter needs a transport calculation.[^mag]

The giant’s 9.7-hour nonaxisymmetric field pattern passes the synchronous moon in approximately **13.46 hours**, potentially giving an additional biological/auroral clock. It does not imply a constant magnetic storm. Frequent aurorae, induction and a magnetically conspicuous sky preserve the desired environmental difference from Earth.

## Rings

The **C, B and A rings** remain bright, sharply structured and maintained in part by small shepherds. They are now **predominantly reflective refractory debris**—including pale silicate-rich grains and fragments—with transient or locally protected ice. A recent disruption and continued impact/ejecta supply are candidate sources, not a demonstrated mass budget. Ancient exposed ice is not assumed to be automatically replenished by an unusually violent Galactic environment.

All radii are measured from the planet’s center. Dense-band working ranges are **90,000–105,000 km (C)**, **110,000–145,000 km (B)**, and **186,500–187,500 km (A)**. Gaps and a much fainter, nonuniform debris extension can reach **250,000 km / 3.18 Rp**. The outer extension is not a uniform dense disk through the shepherds’ orbits.

The fluid Roche scale depends on particle density: approximately **244,000 km for 1-g/cm³ material**, or **170,000 km for 3-g/cm³ material** with the adopted globe. Porous debris and material strength change those comparisons; a single 190,000-km boundary does not apply to all grains and moons. Dense refractory bands, aggregation, narrow gaps, drag and replenishment still need a dynamical/thermal model. Micrometeoroids can remove and darken material as well as supply dust.

**Minut and Zotra** are neighboring shepherds, with nearly equal periods but no assumed Janus-like horseshoe exchange. The revised small eccentricities avoid obvious crossing of the narrow A-band reference; masses, torques and long-term confinement remain unmodeled. Ajaga’s volcanism is retained as a setting feature requiring an ongoing heat/eccentricity source.

**Saxa** uses an illustrative inclination near **150°** relative to the planetary orbital plane; **Trasp** uses approximately **155°**. Trasp is a distant irregular on a compact, lower-eccentricity retrograde candidate orbit; a nearly polar orbit at 5.2 million km with e = 0.41 would not be long-lived. Saxa’s apocenter is about 2.415 million km; Trasp’s pericenter is about 2.944 million km, avoiding an obvious radial crossing. These are candidate geometries, not a complete stability demonstration.

The Archive reports captures or orbital adjustments of some minor moons near the human-arrival epoch. This is a mystery clue, not proof of a particular mechanism or an excuse to skip energy/angular-momentum accounting. Trasp’s tumbling, captured character and distant role remain; automatic enhanced capture rates from Galactic radius are not claimed.

## Long-term tidal context

Dold-Pärla’s native history must cover about **four billion years**, not the entire 9.5-Gyr stellar age. A simple fixed-spin, fixed-radius primary-tide branch can move it from approximately **413,000 to 480,000 km** over four billion years **only with a weak illustrative response, Qp/k₂p≈3×10⁷**. This is substantially weaker dissipation than familiar Jovian estimates at some satellite frequencies. It is a **design requirement to test**, not a measured or physically validated interior response.

The moon's eccentricity is **0.003**. Its associated heat, damping and possible recent excitation are discussed on [[Dold-Pärla]] and under Physical basis below. Primary and lunar dissipation are different quantities. Present magnetic strength does not establish a small tidal response.

## Physical basis

**Oblateness.** For Storebror's fast spin, q = Ω²R³/(GM) ≈ 0.0565 using its mean-radius reference. The first-order hydrostatic relation f ≈ q/2 + 3J₂/2 would need a negative J₂ for f = 0.025, so f ≈ 0.04 and J₂ ≈ 0.008 are approximate positive-J₂ targets. This does not solve a self-consistent rotating figure, and using the equatorial radius shifts it slightly.

**Sky from the moon.** A 5° giant obliquity, a nearly equatorial satellite and a small forced Laplace-plane adjustment preserve an equatorial orbit together with a several-degree mutual tilt to the star's plane. Storebror's angular diameter, 2 asin(R_planet/a_moon), is about 18.86°, in a nearly fixed direction for a synchronous moon. The prograde stellar cycle, 1/(1/P_moon − 1/P_planet), is about 1.4598 days against an orbital period of 1.4477 days. A central point-source eclipse, 2 asin(R_planet/a_moon)/(n_moon − n_planet), lasts about 1.835 hours, roughly 5.24% of the stellar cycle; off-center crossings, the finite stellar disk and moon, and observer latitude change the partial and total contact durations, so the roughly 1.6-hour substantial eclipse is not identical at every site. The rings are nearly edge-on from the moon's near-equatorial vantage; surface parallax, warp and small orbital inclination let a narrow presentation vary, but a wide open ring ellipse is not a permanent naked-eye view. Reflected planetshine changes with phase even though the planet's position is fixed.

**Tidal branch.** The requirement is about 4 Gyr of native biological persistence, not 9.5 Gyr at one exact axis. For a primary spinning faster than the satellite, a simple constant-Q approximation is da/a = 3(k₂p/Qp)(M_m/M_p)(R_p/a)⁵ n dt; with fixed primary spin and radius n ∝ a^(−3/2), so da/dt ∝ a^(−11/2) and a(t_lookback) = a_now [1 − (13/2) t_lookback/τ_a,now]^(2/13). Adopting Qp/k₂p = 3×10⁷, a_now = 480,000 km and the adopted masses and radius gives a local timescale of about 41.5 Gyr, and a four-Gyr lookback gives approximately 412,600 km. This response is unusually weak compared with the giant-planet values inferred at some observed satellite frequencies, so it is a requirement to justify a frequency-dependent interior model, not a value inferred from the star, the strong field or the wish for four-Gyr survival. Fixed spin and radius, omitted stellar torques and other moons, and a simplified Q prescription all limit the branch, which does not solve initial capture or formation, pre-biological history, giant evolution or a changing climate; frequency-dependent tides can differ strongly, but that does not validate the selected weak response.

The moon's synchronous eccentricity-tide reference is P_tidal = (21/2)(k₂m/Qm) G Mp² Rm⁵ n e² / a⁶. For e = 0.003 and k₂m/Qm = 3×10⁻⁴ the surface-mean heat is 2.39 W/m², and varying the lunar response from 10⁻⁴ to 10⁻³ gives about 0.8–8 W/m², a response sensitivity, not a measured Q. The corresponding eccentricity-damping scale, (2/21)(Qm/k₂m)(Mm/Mp)(a/Rm)⁵/n, is about 0.81 Myr, so the present excitation need not have persisted unchanged through four Gyr; recent forcing could coexist with the Archive anomalies, but no causal event is assigned. The giant and the moon have different response parameters: a large magnetic field does not establish weak tidal loss, and lunar heat can be significant while the giant's response is weak, but maintaining that heat for long intervals needs an eccentricity and energy source. Neither a recent massive orbital relocation nor its unaccounted energy deposition is assumed. Context: [Barnes & O’Brien (2002)](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0205035), [Fuller et al. (2016)](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/458/4/3867/2613868) and [Lainey et al. (2009)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08108) motivate caution, and none validates Storebror's adopted response.

**Magnetic umbrella.** The giant's equatorial field of 3 mT gives a pure-dipole field of about 13.2 μT at 480,000 km, and a reference magnetopause at 30 R_p corresponds to an external magnetic-equivalent pressure B_s²/(30⁶·2μ₀) ≈ 4.9 nPa. These are pressure and dipole scales, not a magnetodisk, current or transport model. The moon's own 30-μT surface reference and one-bar atmosphere add shielding, its low surface dose is an adopted observational premise to be reproduced, and trapped belts and high-energy photons and particles need separate modeling ([Heller & Zuluaga](https://arxiv.org/abs/1309.0811)). The relative prograde magnetic-pattern period, 1/(1/9.7 h − 1/34.744 h) ≈ 13.46 h, differs from the stellar cycle, and field geometry, current coupling and particle access determine whether it produces a strong biological signal.

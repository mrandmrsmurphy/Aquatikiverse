# Gycklare

![[images/Gycklare_render.png]]

*Artist’s impression of Gycklare. The simulated appearance and spectral interpretation are part of the fictional future setting.*

**Gycklare**, also **Gammal b**, is the inner mini-Neptune of the [[Gammal System]]. It preserves the setting’s **churn and visual interest**: a deep hydrogen–helium atmosphere over a heavy-element interior, patchy high-altitude haze, changing bands and vortices, and variable upper-atmosphere escape. It is interior to the terrestrial habitable zone rather than classified as a habitable-zone world.

The bulk quantities are derived from the mass and radius. A heavy-element-dominated interior with a smaller H/He mass fraction is the starting model; a core of ≤40% with a ≥60% envelope is not supported. An optically thick envelope does not imply that gas contains most of the planet’s mass. Radius, composition, irradiation and age need a joint interior/evolution calculation.[^interior]

There is no accessible solid surface at the visible atmospheric boundary. Deeper rock, volatile fluids, supercritical layers or high-pressure phases may occur; their exact distribution and pressures are unspecified. The reference radius is not a demonstrated 1-bar level.

## Atmosphere, weather and appearance

Hydrogen and helium dominate the atmospheric gas, with **water vapor, methane and possible hydrogen cyanide** among minor constituents. Rayleigh scattering, molecular absorption and aerosols shape a blue-white to pale blue-grey appearance. Patchy haze can hide or reveal deeper bands; overturning and zonal flow create vortices, waves and changing cloud textures. Those visible contrasts are the design goal, not a claim that every cloud species or wind speed has been retrieved.

Heat redistribution and the day–night contrast need a **pressure-dependent circulation model, still to be determined**; efficient redistribution and a fixed >900-K/<300-K contrast are not assumed. Hotter deep layers may reach 670 K or exceed 900 K under suitable opacity/greenhouse conditions, while high-altitude or night regions can be much colder. These numbers are possible layer temperatures, not simultaneously established measurements or an equilibrium calculation. Synchronous rotation alone does not establish extreme contrast.

Vigorous equatorial jets and episodic upwelling remain. Supersonic flow is a hypothesis requiring a pressure/temperature-dependent dynamical model, not an assumed terminator condition. Thermal emission falls chiefly in infrared bands and must be predicted using the atmospheric spectrum rather than a single-temperature blackbody.

## Escape and upper atmosphere

Gycklare retains **variable escape and a possible comet-like UV halo/tail**. A loss of **7×10¹³ kg/year** is an **uncertain high-state estimate**, not an established continuous loss rate across 9.5 Gyr, and a 10¹²-kg/year value likewise needs an XUV model. Stellar flares, heating efficiency, absorption radius, ionization and magnetic interaction determine whether either figure is appropriate.

For scale, the higher rate sustained for 9.5 Gyr would remove about **0.11 M⊕**, potentially important to a thin envelope despite being a small fraction of total planetary mass. A simple energy-limited estimate with efficiency 0.1 and XUV radius equal to the bulk radius requires roughly **5 W/m² XUV flux** for that rate; an old quiet K dwarf does not automatically supply it. Tail extent of tens of radii remains an observational/modeling target, not a fixed proven geometry. Neutral H, ionized material and UV absorption should be distinguished.

Qualitative comparisons include [HD 209458 b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD_209458_b) and [GJ 436 b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gliese_436_b), without treating their temperatures or mass-loss mechanisms as measurements of Gycklare.

## Observation and naming

The common observer inclination **89.8°** allows transits under the adopted stellar/planet radii. The circa-2439-CE photometric discovery and RV follow-up remain fictional future events. At roughly 3000 pc, observations are difficult; the spectral detail is an in-setting interpretation. No secondary eclipse is confirmed in the supplied record, limiting thermal constraints.

**Gycklare** means “jester” or “trickster” in Swedish, reflecting its atmospheric behavior and changing appearance. The formal naming followed recognition of [[Dold-Pärla]]’s inhabited nature; this need not mean that local ancient knowledge and Earth-based discovery share a calendar or observing site.

## Physical basis

The 414-K full-redistribution equilibrium uses a Bond albedo of 0.3 and is a reference, not a surface temperature: the weather and spectrum are a pressure-dependent problem, and the high escape rate needs an XUV history and cannot be derived from optical brightness alone. External interior models are illustrative rather than evidence for fictional detections.

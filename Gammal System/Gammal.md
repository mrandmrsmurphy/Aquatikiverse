# Gammal

![[images/Gammal_and_system.png]]

*Artist’s impression of Gammal and its planetary system.*

**Gammal** is the quiet K-type main-sequence host of the [[Gammal System]], approximately **3,000 pc** from Earth toward Sagittarius. Its old, slowly rotating primary provides the stable local illumination around which a native biosphere developed on [[Dold-Pärla]], despite the wider inner-disk environment’s episodic hazards. The star’s age is not the age of the inhabited moon’s current orbital configuration or human population.

## Photometry and Galactic environment

Mv=6.21 and d=3000 pc give mV≈18.60 before extinction. Adding Av=1.5–3.0 gives **20.10–21.60**, consistent with the supplied range; it does not imply the star itself varies by 1.5 magnitudes. The extinction and bolometric correction are inherited estimates, not newly retrieved observational measurements.

A 3-kpc Earth distance nearly toward the Galactic Center implies a Galactocentric radius around **5.3 kpc**, taking the Sun’s distance as about 8.3 kpc. A crowded Sagittarius sightline and an inner-disk setting remain, but foreground extinction, local stellar density, asteroid flux and local radiation are distinct quantities. No fixed 64-km/s Galactic circular speed is adopted without a Galactic orbit model.

The main index's Earth-frame tuple (3000,260,0) is a map annotation with undefined axes and units, not a measured position. A 2° inclination is an approximate **Galactic orbit-plane** setting parameter, separate from near-edge-on planet orbits as seen from Earth. The Galactic orbit and its environmental history remain unmodeled.

## Planetary system

**[[Gycklare]]**, a 6.3-M⊕, 2.4-R⊕ mini-Neptune at **0.22 AU**, completes its orbit in about **41.6 days**. It lies interior to the habitable region. Its atmospheric circulation, patchy haze and variable escape give it the visual and meteorological character behind its “jester” name.

**[[Storebror]]**, a 2.2-M♃, 1.1-R♃ warm gas giant at **0.572 AU**, completes its orbit in about **174.3 days**. It has eleven known satellites in the setting’s provisional census, a bright predominantly refractory ring system, and a strong magnetosphere. Neither rapid spin nor a magnetic field guarantees moon-system stability.

The major moon **[[Dold-Pärla]]** has about **four billion years of native biological history**. Its present 480,000-km orbit gives a 1.45-day spin/orbit and a 1.46-day stellar cycle. Its present tidal history, unexplained human arrival roughly 100,000 years before the Archive reference, and later evacuation belong to distinct clocks.

## Habitability

At Storebror’s orbit, normal incident stellar flux is about **1414 W/m² / 1.04 Earth flux**. A representative conservative terrestrial HZ calculation is roughly **0.58–1.05 AU**, depending on adopted temperature, mass and climate assumptions. A moon’s eclipses, albedo, planetshine, internal tides and atmosphere require a separate climate model.[^hz]

Dold-Pärla is designed as a temperate ocean world near the warm edge. Its low reported surface radiation depends on **nested planetary/lunar magnetic environments and a substantial atmosphere**, not on excluding all Galactic radiation. The strong local protection and stable star contrast with a less predictable wider region. The evacuation’s trigger and timing are not inferred merely from the star’s age or proximity to the Galactic Center.

## Physical basis

**Constants.** The calculations use G = 6.67430×10⁻¹¹ SI, M☉ = 1.98847×10³⁰ kg, M♃ = 1.89813×10²⁷ kg, M⊕ = 5.9722×10²⁴ kg, R☉ = 695,700 km, R♃ = 71,492 km, R⊕ = 6,371 km, AU = 149,597,870.7 km, L☉ = 3.828×10²⁶ W, σ = 5.670374419×10⁻⁸ SI and a 365.25-day year; rounded narrative values are not precise measurements. Bulk quantities follow ρ = 3M/(4πR³), g = GM/R², v_esc = √(2GM/R), a spherical area 4πR² and a volume 4πR³/3.

**Star.** The Stefan–Boltzmann relation T = 5772 K × (0.34/0.79²)^¼ gives about 4,959 K. Photometry m = M + 5 log₁₀(d/10 pc) + Av gives 20.10–21.60 for Mv = 6.21, d = 3,000 pc and Av = 1.5–3.0. For a sightline near the Galactic Center, an Earth distance of about 3 kpc and a solar Galactocentric radius of about 8.3 kpc imply roughly 5.3 kpc from the center.

**Orbits and transits.** For near-circular motion P = 2π√[a³/G(M+m)] and v = √[G(M+m)/a], and the stellar radial-velocity semi-amplitude is √(G/a) m sin i / √(M+m), with a negligible eccentricity correction at e = 0.005. A transit requires |a cos i| ≲ R★ + R_body in the circular reference: Storebror needs approximately i > 89.58°, so 88° would not work, and the adopted 89.8° also permits Gycklare's transit. The stellar inclination, giant spin obliquity, moon-equatorial inclination and Galactic orbit inclination are distinct.

**Habitable-zone context.** A roughly 0.58–1.05 AU terrestrial habitable-zone polynomial at this stellar temperature is context only, not a climate result.

# Dold-Pärla

![[images/Dold-Pärla_at_eclipse.png]]

*Artist’s rendering of Dold-Pärla orbiting Storebror. The giant is nearly fixed in the moon’s sky, while stellar illumination and planet phase vary.*

Image description: Terrestrial moon with a gas giant visible in the sky.

**Dold-Pärla**, also **Storebror II**, is the ocean-rich inhabited moon of [[Storebror]] in the [[Gammal System]]. Its distinguishing history is **about four billion years of native life, the unexplained appearance of modern humans about 100,000 years before the Archive reference, and a subsequent evacuation**. The evacuation’s trigger, dates and completion status are not yet specified. Native life predates humans; human origin is not assigned an explanation.

This fuller article takes priority over the earlier overview. Dold-Pärla has a nearly equatorial orbit, a substantial nitrogen–oxygen atmosphere, active tectonics and a diverse carbon-based biosphere. A powerful planetary magnetic environment and its own atmospheric/magnetic shielding make it a **protected local habitat in a less predictable inner-disk region**. Its giant-filled sky, long eclipses, tides and magnetic rhythms distinguish it strongly from Earth.

The mass and radius imply the gravity and escape speed. The reference temperature is a setting climate target, not the moon’s radiative equilibrium temperature. Widespread clouds can contribute to the albedo; open ocean is not assumed highly reflective at every illumination angle.

The landscape includes several large forested continents, extensive archipelagos, broad shallow seas, deep ocean trenches and reef-like ecosystems. Robust plate tectonics and volcanism recycle nutrients and reshape land. “Slightly more active than Earth” is a qualitative setting property supported in part by a tidal-heat premise, not a measured plate-speed census.

The moon's orbit is nearly equatorial for Storebror while having a small inclination to the **star’s** plane: those are different reference planes. A slight Laplace-plane departure from the equator and nodal precession can occur. A 1.28-day period would not match the 480,000-km axis and the primary's mass.

**Both hemispheres experience stellar day and night.** The subplanetary hemisphere sees Storebror nearly fixed; the far hemisphere does not. The planet’s reflected phase changes through the lunar orbit. Planetshine is bright at favorable phases but is not constant full-brightness daylight. Storebror’s thermal emission provides a smaller, more persistent near-side contribution.

At this distance, a five-degree mutual tilt does not automatically eliminate eclipses for part of the year: the planet’s angular radius is large. Eclipse duration and track vary with the node, stellar position, finite stellar disk, observer location and precession. The quoted 1.6 hours is a representative substantial occultation, not an identical contact interval everywhere. Weather, ocean response and eclipse timing can produce recurring ecological rhythms; no unsupported multi-week orbital clock is assigned.

Because the moon is almost in the ring plane, Storebror’s rings usually appear as **thin bright extensions or a narrow band**, rather than a permanently wide Saturn-like ellipse. Small opening angles, surface parallax and ring warps alter their appearance. Some other moons can be visible as moving points; naked-eye visibility of every small shepherd is not assumed.

## Atmosphere and climate

The explicit **illustrative dry-air reference** is **N₂ 78.08%, O₂ 20.95%, Ar 0.93%, CO₂ 0.04%**, at about 1 bar. Trace constituents displace a corresponding small part of the N₂ balance. Water vapor is variable and excluded from that dry normalization. This implements near-Earth pressure/composition; it is not claimed to be uniquely retrieved from the supplied article or constant across deep time.

The canonically breathable air and regional mean temperatures support human habitation. Oceans dominate heat storage and redistribute energy through currents and atmospheric circulation. Equatorial jets, eclipse-related cooling, cloud changes, tides and geothermal activity create distinct local rhythms. Axial seasons are modest under the low-obliquity reference; regional variability remains.

A transparent energy illustration uses direct stellar input, central-eclipse duty about 5.2%, lunar Bond albedo 0.32, and modest planetary heat. It gives about **228 W/m² absorbed direct starlight**, plus a few W/m² from planetary light/heat and a chosen tidal response. One reference total is about **232–233 W/m²**, with an effective emission temperature around **253 K**. An atmospheric greenhouse of roughly **35 K** would connect that to the 288-K surface target. This is an accounting illustration, not a validated circulation/cloud/greenhouse solution. The actual tilted eclipse geometry and ring shadows alter the budget.

### Tides and four-billion-year persistence

The chosen **e=0.003** retains the desired dynamical character without reducing it to the earlier very-low-e proposal. In a simple synchronous tide model, **k₂m/Qm=3×10⁻⁴** yields about **2.4 W/m²** internally. A response range 10⁻⁴–10⁻³ gives about **0.8–8 W/m²** at the same eccentricity. These illustrative rheological responses are requirements to test, not measured material properties. They can make tectonics vigorous and affect climate materially.

The reference eccentricity damping time is about **0.8 Myr**. Present excitation therefore need not have persisted for all four billion years. A recent perturbation or forcing is compatible with the Archive’s reported minor-satellite anomalies, but a causal link has not been established. An ordinary dissipative event must not be invoked to shift a massive ocean world dramatically while ignoring its heating.

For the primary tides, a simplified weak-response branch with **Qp/k₂p≈3×10⁷** allows a reference axis to evolve from about **413,000 km four billion years ago to 480,000 km now**. This protects the desired four-billion-year native interval in the working scenario, but **does not demonstrate that Storebror has the required response**. It is much weaker than familiar giant-planet dissipation at some observed frequencies. Earlier history, giant contraction/spin, interactions, moon rheology and changing stellar luminosity remain to be modeled. Native history is retained as canon; the orbital/thermal realization is explicitly under investigation.[^tides]

## Magnetosphere and radiation environment

Storebror’s adopted **3-mT equatorial field** and pressure-variable magnetosphere provide the large external magnetic environment. The reference magnetopause is about **30 Rp**, while Dold-Pärla orbits at **6.1 Rp**. A dipolar external field at the moon is around **13 μT**. The moon also has an illustrative intrinsic dipole scale around **30 μT**, with topology, induction and current coupling still unresolved.

The resulting nested environment is the intended major difference from Earth: stellar-wind particles encounter a giant’s magnetosphere before reaching the moon, while its own magnetic system and dense atmosphere provide additional filtering. The setting reports **low, comparatively consistent surface radiation**, frequent aurorae and conspicuous magnetic variations. Trapped energetic belts must be sufficiently weak or access-limited to satisfy that premise; a large field alone does not prove it.

Photons, cosmic rays and charged particles require energy-specific dose modeling. Neither field blocks X-rays or every Galactic cosmic ray; atmospheric columns perform much of the surface shielding. The five-kpc-scale inner-disk position is not immediate proximity to Sagittarius A*. Regional hazards and the stable local niche are preserved without exaggerating routine central irradiation. The evacuation is not automatically explained by a calculated failure of this shielding; its actual trigger remains unspecified.[^mag]

Magnetic anomalies near tectonic boundaries can include ordinary remanent minerals and induction effects. The separate large-scale crustal pattern discussed below is the Archive’s unexplained anomaly, not a claim that any aligned magnetic mineral is unnatural. Storebror’s approximately **13.46-hour** relative field-pattern cycle and the 1.46-day solar cycle are distinct possible cues.

## Native biosphere

Native life is carbon-based and uses DNA/RNA-like molecular structures compatible with terrestrial biochemical materials despite its **independent origin** in the setting. Chemical compatibility does not establish shared ancestry. Native ecological diversity spans marine, land and aerial communities: forests, trench ecosystems, reef analogs and flying arthropod-like organisms.

Distinctive retained adaptations include:

- **Bioluminescent fungal analogs** whose visible blooms track Storebror eclipses.
- **Eusocial herbivores** with primitive symbolic communication, without assigning them the human civilization’s origin or an undocumented technological status.
- **Marine organisms exploiting tidally organized thermal/redox microenvironments**. Useful work requires chemical disequilibrium or a temperature gradient and sink; warm tidal water alone is not an inexhaustible metabolism.

Dold-Pärla’s native biosphere precedes the human arrival by almost four billion years. No precursor hominid sequence or native phylogenetic branch leading to humans is part of the record. Further taxonomy, organismal physiology and food-web productivity are not supplied; no additional species pages or invented civilization are needed to fill those gaps.

## Human inhabitants

Modern humans appear abruptly in the Archive reconstruction approximately **100,000 years before its reference epoch**. The source attributes the population to **three male and nine female founders**, with no genetic relationship to the native lineages. These are retained as in-world historical/genomic conclusions; an exact founder headcount is not treated as something that can be uniquely inferred from three surviving Y lines and nine mitochondrial lines alone.

The small founding cohort creates a severe demographic/genetic problem to be investigated: initial relatedness, genetic load, growth, surviving lineages and documentary evidence matter. Present viability and interfertility remain canonical observations, rather than being declared impossible or explained by an invented intervention. The mystery includes why a viable population arose under those conditions.

The Archive reports associated variants and observed traits including **enhanced night vision and magnetoreception**, **denser cortical synaptic branching**, and **modestly elevated radiation/hypoxia tolerance**. These traits do not replace environmental shielding or prove extraordinary intelligence from one anatomical feature. Humans remain fully interfertile and established in local ecosystems during their inhabited era.

Local food digestion and breathing the air are observed compatibility premises. The existence of shared molecular building blocks can make digestion possible without making native organisms genealogically human relatives. A complete nutritional, molecular-chirality and metabolic explanation remains a biological research task.

Cultural records associate rapid technical development with conspicuous orbital mechanics, predictive eclipse astronomy and magnetic observation. Those opportunities can encourage measurement and models, but do not make technological progress automatic or explain the original human arrival.

## Astronomy and observation

Local inhabitants directly observed their own world and its primary from antiquity; “indigenous observation” refers to **local human astronomy**, not an indigenous hominid ancestry. Circa-2439-CE transit/RV detection is a separate Earth/instrumental chronology. An absolute conversion between that calendar, the Archive’s cycles and its 100,000-year lookback is not supplied.

Storebror dominates near-side skies at almost 19° across. Bands rotate in 9.7 hours; the narrow ring presentation, visible brighter companions, phases, eclipses and aurorae give strong observational cues. The starfield is crowded along the adopted inner-disk location, but its exact local brightness and extinction require a model. Alignments occur regularly; reported near-miss objects are a monitored small-body population rather than proof that disastrous encounters are continuously common.

## Orbital context and the mystery

The moon is geometrically well inside Storebror’s roughly 8.1-million-km Hill sphere and the reference magnetopause. **Ajaga**, **Saxa** and **Trasp** retain their volcanic-inner, retrograde-irregular and distant-captured roles. The full eleven-moon inventory remains provisional; the short numerical screen on [[Gammal System]] under Physical basis does not include every moon.

The Archive reports that some minor-satellite captures or orbital adjustments date near human appearance. It also reports a coherent high-density magnetic-mineral structure not reproduced by its current tectonic/thermal models. Its **“Gammal–Storebror flux-axis”** description is treated as a phase-referenced field/alignment relation: the star–planet line is not a single permanent direction on the synchronously rotating moon.

These are associated observations in the setting, not proof of a creator, transport mechanism or causal sequence. Ordinary magnetic alignment is not itself impossible; the anomaly concerns the specific geometry, chronology and mineral history. The absence of precursor humans and the sudden founder cohort remain unresolved.

The record is attributed to the **Unified Cycle Archive, Rev. 9.4410**. Unsupported origin hypotheses remain outside the primary account unless supported by an in-world observational record, as in the supplied article.

## Evacuation and historical sequence

The sequence is **native biosphere → sudden human arrival → human development → evacuation**. The article describes the inhabited era; the evacuation is a later event. Its **cause, date, destination, population fraction, completion status and ecological aftermath are not yet set**. No stellar catastrophe, radiation failure, human extinction or sterile abandoned world is invented to supply that missing chapter.

The 288-K climate, founders, adaptations and native ecology therefore describe a physical/historical reference, not a claim that every human settlement remains occupied at an unspecified post-evacuation present. Later information can locate the article’s present and update those tenses without erasing the earlier inhabited-world record.

## Physical basis

At 0.572 AU the stellar flux is F = L/(4πa²) ≈ 1414.5 W/m². For Dold-Pärla's Bond albedo of 0.32 and the central eclipse reference, the global-mean illustration is:

| Contribution | Global-mean illustration |
| --- | --- |
| Absorbed direct star | about 227.9 W/m² |
| Storebror thermal emission | about 1.54 W/m² |
| Absorbed reflected planetshine | about 0.61 W/m² |
| Lunar eccentricity tides | about 2.39 W/m² (response specified on [[Storebror]]) |
| Other interior heat | 0.08 W/m², an illustrative background, not a solved interior flux |
| Total | about 232.5 W/m² |

The planet's thermal input uses a uniform 252-K equilibrium globe and disk dilution, divided by four for the lunar area. Reflection uses an illustrative Lambertian globe with a geometric albedo of 0.35/1.5 ≈ 0.233, a full-phase scale A_g F (R_p/a)², a mean phase factor of 4/π², a lunar area factor of 1/4 and a lunar absorption of 0.68. Rings, thermal bandpass absorption, real cloud anisotropy and partial shadowing are omitted. For the Lambertian reference the phase integral is 1.5, so a geometric albedo of 0.233 is paired with a Bond albedo of 0.35 to conserve reflected and absorbed power; the real cloud phase function can differ.

The effective emission temperature is about 253 K, and an approximately 35-K greenhouse would connect it to the 288-K surface reference. This is a target budget, not a radiative-convective or circulation solution: the tilted geometry changes the eclipse duty, and ocean and cloud feedbacks could alter the result substantially ([Heller & Barnes 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.5323)).

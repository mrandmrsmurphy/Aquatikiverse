**Euryphessa** is the outermost planet in the [[Chiaroscuro System]], a massive, highly reflective gas giant residing at a distance of approximately 26 AU from the central binary. With a radius of 1.2 Jupiter radii and a mass near 1.9 Jupiter masses, it is a deep hydrogen–helium world whose core likely originated in the ice-giant regime before runaway gas accretion. It is a bright body in the outer system, outshining even nearer giants at favorable geometries.

At 26 AU from the ~6 L☉ [[Chiaroscuro]] binary, Euryphessa receives about 0.89% of the stellar flux that Earth receives from the Sun. A working Bond albedo near 0.66 gives a stellar equilibrium temperature near 65 K. A working total effective temperature near 100 K includes interior heat, allowing a dynamically active deeper atmosphere.

## Composition and Internal Structure
Euryphessa’s atmosphere is composed primarily of molecular hydrogen (H₂) and helium (He), with dominant cloud decks formed from water ice, ammonia ice, and ammonium hydrosulfide, overlain by photochemically produced hazes. High-albedo condensates and fine ice particles in the upper troposphere contribute to its extraordinarily high geometric albedo of ~0.9 — the brightest of any major body in the system. Beneath the bright upper clouds, deeper convective zones host thicker, more absorbing cloud layers and likely detached stratified haze decks.

The planet’s mean density near 1.36 g/cm³ is compatible with a hydrogen–helium giant containing heavy elements. Their abundance requires an interior model. Interior models suggest a compact, enriched core containing silicates, metals, and high-pressure ices, overlain by a massive, convective hydrogen–helium mantle. The transition between core and envelope is probably chemically graded rather than sharply layered, with heavy elements partially mixed into the envelope by long-term convection.

A key feature of Euryphessa's evolution is a cataclysmic accretion event occurring roughly one million years ago. During this episode, the planet is believed to have consumed a Super-Europa–class moon or planetesimal rich in silicates, water ice, and radioactive isotopes (notably uranium, thorium, potassium, and rubidium). This ingestion event injected substantial heavy-element mass into the deep interior, prolonged vigorous convection, and altered the atmospheric abundances of trace species. Residual energy deposited during accretion and subsequent mixing can contribute to the present interior heat budget. The radionuclides are compositional tracers with a potentially smaller heating role. The event’s mass and retained energy remain to be quantified.

## Appearance, Rings, and Moons
Visually, Euryphessa is striking: a brilliant white disk ringed with stark, low-albedo bands. The contrast between the bright atmosphere and its deep-black ring system is dramatic. The rings themselves—composed of dark carbonaceous coatings and dust over mixed icy and rocky particles—extend from roughly 2.2 to 3.1 planetary radii and reflect little light, with a mean albedo near 0.02. The result is a planet–ring system with several magnitudes of surface-brightness contrast per unit area: a luminous globe suspended within a dark, thin, almost ink-like hoop.

Porous ice-rich particles can place much of the main ring near their fluid Roche boundary. The disruption limit depends on bulk density and strength. Material outside it can form transient clumps and moonlets. Small embedded moonlets and edge shepherds maintain sharp ring boundaries and narrow gaps. Occasional impacts by outer-system debris briefly brighten localized ring sectors, creating transient “sparks” on the otherwise black ring plane.

Euryphessa also hosts a modest family of regular satellites outside the main ring system. These moons are mid-sized icy bodies with mixed water-ice and rock compositions, some showing patchy, moderately reflective surfaces. Against the planet’s dazzling disk they appear subdued, but when seen in scattered light near ring-plane crossings, they cut crisp, moving silhouettes across the bright planetary disk or against the projected ring structure in resolved images.

## Energy Budget and Atmospheric Activity
Euryphessa’s energy budget is powered by both absorbed starlight and internal heat. The low incident flux at 26 AU would by itself yield a cold, relatively quiescent atmosphere. However, primordial cooling, contraction, and residual accretion heat maintain an internal luminosity. Its history depends on the event’s energy deposition and the planet’s thermal transport.

This thermal input drives persistent upward convection, maintaining high-altitude ice and haze layers that would otherwise settle gravitationally. The contrast between bright upper clouds and darker deeper decks produces subtle banding and limb-brightening effects when viewed at high resolution. Episodic convective outbreaks can punch through the stable layers, forming bright, short-lived storm cells that momentarily increase the hemisphere-averaged reflectivity.

In the infrared, Euryphessa glows faintly but measurably, with emission features shaped by water vapor, ammonia, and collision-induced hydrogen absorption. These diagnostics reveal a temperature profile that is somewhat warmer at depth than would be expected for a purely radiatively cooled, passively heated giant.

## Orbital Properties and Observability

Euryphessa’s reference semimajor axis is 26.0 AU with eccentricity near 0.03. Its reference distance spans about 25.2–26.8 AU. The nominal Keplerian period is approximately 89.4 years and the circular reference speed about 8.7 km/s. The surrounding companions perturb its elements over time.

The cloud deck’s geometric albedo near 0.9 makes it a conspicuous pale target in resolved optical images. Its dark rings contribute little to total reflected flux. From Eirda, the planet appears as a remote point whose cloud and ring details require telescopic resolution.

A rough solar-spectrum, full-phase estimate at the nominal opposition distance gives apparent magnitude of order +1 to +2 from Eirda. This is an illustrative estimate pending the actual stellar spectrum, phase function, and Eirda’s atmospheric transmission. It supersedes the earlier −4 magnitude claim.

The visibly white disk is reflected starlight. Interior heat supports atmospheric circulation and contributes infrared radiation. Optical and thermal images therefore reveal different aspects of the planet.

A geometric albedo near 0.9 and Bond albedo near 0.66 require a scattering phase integral near 0.73 if treated with compatible spectral weighting. This working non-Lambertian model must be checked with actual condensate optical properties.

## Formation History
Euryphessa is believed to have formed as a native gas giant beyond the frost line, accreting icy and rocky planetesimals in the early protoplanetary disk before runaway gas accretion built its massive envelope. The later ingestion of a differentiated, Super-Europa–type body supplied gravitational accretion energy and heavy elements, perturbing its thermal and compositional evolution.

The event provides a proposed history for enhanced mixing and lingering heat. Reflective high clouds remain the defining visible feature, supported by the deeper circulation. In comparative planetology across the [[Chiaroscuro System]], Euryphessa stands out as the luminous counterpoint to dark giants like [[Kharon]] and brooding worlds like [[Ereba]] and [[Skotos]].


## Modeling notes

The white cloud deck, black rings, and proposed ingestion event are preserved. Mass, radius-derived quantities, geometric and Bond albedos, and total effective temperature now use distinct definitions.

The approximately one-million-year accretion history needs an ingested mass, deposition depth, retained gravitational energy, and cooling calculation. For a reference effective temperature near 100 K with stellar equilibrium near 65 K, the corresponding intrinsic effective temperature is about 95 K. The reference temperature is a target for the combined cooling and accretion-history model.

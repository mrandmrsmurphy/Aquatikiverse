**Phothinos** is a highly reflective, strongly irradiated terrestrial planet in close orbit around the binary star [[Chiaroscuro]]. Classified as an ultra-bright cloud-covered terrestrial world, it is the innermost and optically brightest planet in the [[Chiaroscuro System]].

## Stellar System
Main article: [[Chiaroscuro]]
Phothinos orbits the Chiaroscuro binary, a compact stellar pair composed of an F1V primary (~1.4 M☉, ~5.5 L☉) and a K1V secondary (~0.8 M☉, ~0.4 L☉), separated by only 0.07 AU. The combined luminosity of the pair is approximately 6 L☉, shifting the classical Earth-equivalent insolation distance outward to around 2.4 AU and placing the habitable zone at several astronomical units from the barycenter. The apparent separation of the two stars varies with viewpoint and orbital phase. They are widely separated in Phothinos’s sky and can also be resolved farther out at favorable geometry.

## Orbital and Physical Characteristics

Phothinos occupies a near-circular circumbinary reference orbit at 0.20 AU, close to the approximate inner stability boundary. Its period is about 22 days, while the binary completes an orbit in about 4.6 days. Binary forcing produces departures from a simple Keplerian ellipse. Its spin is not yet specified.

At the nominal distance, Phothinos receives about 150 Earth flux. A working Bond albedo of 0.80 gives a uniform-redistribution stellar equilibrium temperature near 650 K. A corresponding zero-redistribution substellar estimate is about 920 K, with additional variation from the moving stars.

The 1.3 R⊕, 2.5 M⊕ body has a rocky interior and a highly reflective atmosphere. Extensive exposed lava seas are no longer part of the reference description. Local volcanic hotspots and transient melt remain possible where interior heat reaches the surface.

## Atmosphere and Reflectivity

Phothinos’s defining feature is an optically thick white cloud and aerosol deck, with target visible geometric albedo near 0.85 and Bond albedo near 0.80. These are working scattering parameters. A non-Lambertian phase function relates them.

Reflective refractory particles are candidate contributors. Alumina, titanium dioxide, spinel, MgO, calcium aluminates such as CaAl₁₂O₁₉, and CaTiO₃ are proposed condensate or dust species, subject to pressure–temperature and optical checks. NaCl and KCl may occur in suitable layers. Calcite-bearing deposits require a compatible local temperature and CO₂ pressure. These solid formulas do not specify the dominant atmospheric gases.

Cloud circulation and lofted volcanic dust may replenish the bright particles. The bulk gas composition, pressure profile, cloud microphysics, and particle lifetimes remain to be modeled. Reflectivity is the primary constraint on those choices.

From Eirda, Phothinos is a very bright point close to the suns. Its elongation is only a few degrees, limiting useful views to favorable horizon geometry or observations that suppress stellar glare. It can be prominent in twilight and potentially detectable in daylight under favorable conditions. It remains many orders of magnitude fainter than the stars.

## Geological and Atmospheric Dynamics

Strong irradiation and a reflective upper atmosphere establish a pronounced vertical energy budget. Winds redistribute heat, with convective plumes and cloud-opacity changes producing variable brightness. Supersonic flow is a model possibility requiring a pressure and circulation calculation.

The surface may contain volcanic fields and local melt associated with interior heat or tidal dissipation. Mantle composition includes magnesium and aluminum silicates. The extent of melting, volcanic power, and mineral aerosol supply remain linked research questions. High visible albedo takes precedence over the original global lava-world interpretation.

## Significance
Phothinos is an extreme case of a highly irradiated terrestrial planet with a reflective aerosol-rich atmosphere. It serves as a natural laboratory for the study of refractory aerosol chemistry, radiative transfer in high-temperature regimes, and interactions between volcanism, atmospheric circulation, and bright cloud decks. Its brightness, short orbital period, and periodic appearance in the skies of other planets lend it cultural and navigational importance across the Chiaroscuro system.


## Modeling notes

The albedo values are targets for a physically consistent cloud model. The bright appearance is retained while global lava seas, a guaranteed synchronous spin, and brightness comparable to the binary are retired. Refined spectral reflectance, condensate chemistry, and stability close to the binary remain scientific work.

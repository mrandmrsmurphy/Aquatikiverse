**Chiaroscuro** is a nearby binary star system in the constellation [Puppis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppis), approximately 100 parsecs (326 light-years) from Earth. The system is named for the dramatic interplay of light and darkness across its planetary bodies, resulting from the tight orbit of its contrasting stellar pair: a white-hued F1V star and a more subdued orange K1V companion. Their close proximity — a mere 0.07 astronomical units (AU) — makes the system an exemplary case of a "tight main-sequence binary", wherein the dynamic interactions between the two stars produce both orbital resonance and radiative extremes.

The Chiaroscuro system has garnered interest for hosting a distinctive planetary architecture, including several planets believed to experience stark hemispheric light gradients, complex magnetic environments, and extreme tidal locking behaviors.

## Stellar components


The primary star, **Chiaroscuro A**, is an F1V-type main-sequence star with an estimated mass of 1.4 solar masses (M☉), a radius of about 1.5 solar radii (R☉), and an effective temperature of approximately 7,200 K. These parameters show a luminosity of roughly 5.5 times that of the Sun (L☉), in line with a mid-F dwarf. It contributes the majority of the system’s total light output and exhibits a high surface brightness consistent with other early– to mid-F main-sequence stars.

Its counterpart, **Chiaroscuro B**, is a cooler K1V-type main-sequence star with an estimated mass of 0.8 M☉ and radius of about 0.8 R☉, with an effective temperature near 5,150 K. This yields a luminosity of about 0.4 L☉, making it significantly dimmer than Chiaroscuro A, but still a substantial contributor to the system’s combined irradiance at large distances. Visually, it would appear as a warm orange star contrasting with the bluish-white primary.

Together, the binary pair radiates a combined luminosity of approximately 6 L☉. Their close orbit — less than one-fifth the distance between Mercury and the Sun — corresponds to an orbital separation of 0.07 AU and an orbital period of about 4.6 days around their common barycenter. This configuration produces significant mutual tidal effects and gravitational harmonics that propagate throughout the surrounding planetary disk.

Both stars exhibit solar-like metallicities, with [Fe/H] ≈ +0.2, suggesting a slightly enriched environment consistent with second- or third-generation stellar formation. The system is estimated to be around 3 billion years old, an epoch compatible with both stars remaining on the main sequence (though the more massive F-type primary is somewhat closer to the end of its hydrogen-burning lifetime than the K dwarf).

## Planetary system

Main article: [[Chiaroscuro System]]

Seven major circumbinary companions are described in this system, comprising six planets and the brown dwarf Umbryl:

- **[[Phothinos]]** – a bright Lava World
- **[[Kharon]]** – dark Jupiter
- **[[Eirda]]** – an inhabited large Earth
- **[[Umbryl]]** – a brown dwarf
- **[[Skotos]]** – a dark Mega Terrestrial World
- **[[Ereba]]** – a dark Methane Giant
- **[[Euryphessa]]** – a bright Gas Giant

The outermost described planet orbits at approximately 26 AU. Dynamical estimates, given the binary’s total mass of about 2.2 M☉, place the approximate coplanar circumbinary stability boundary near 0.175 AU for the working binary eccentricity of 0.02. Phothinos at 0.20 AU lies close to this boundary. Stellar motion modulates illumination on all the planets. The inner planet’s spin state requires a tidal model.

Several of these worlds are theorized to have substantial atmospheres capable of redistributing heat, while others may host extreme weather systems along their terminator zones. Umbryl is a brown dwarf and Ereba a methane-rich ice giant. Their thermal emission and reflected-light properties create contrasting observing targets.

## Galactic location and motion


Chiaroscuro lies in the galactic thin disk at galactic coordinates (l ≈236.6°, b ≈−6.5°). It is believed to be part of the Local Arm (Orion Spur) and follows a standard Population I orbit around the Milky Way, with no peculiar velocities of note. Radial velocity surveys indicate that the system's barycenter is stable to within ±1 m/s over decade-long baselines after modeling out the close 4.6-day binary signal and known planetary contributions, enhancing its utility as a dynamical benchmark.

## Physical basis

The stellar components are F1V and K1V, totaling 2.2 M☉ and approximately 5.9 L☉, rounded to 6 L☉ in illumination estimates, at an adopted distance of 100 pc. The binary eccentricity of 0.02 is a working assumption for stability and illumination calculations. In a central mutual eclipse the K star can obscure about 28% of the F star's projected area, roughly a 27% reduction in total bolometric light with the quoted luminosities; the actual wavelength-dependent eclipse depth depends on stellar surface brightness and limb darkening.

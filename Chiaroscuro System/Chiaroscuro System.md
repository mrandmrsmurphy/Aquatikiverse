The **Chiaroscuro System** is a highly dynamic, high-contrast planetary system dominated by a tight binary of an F1V and a K1V star. Combining intense stellar dynamics with a collection of uniquely terrifying and awe-inspiring planets, Chiaroscuro represents one of the most visually and emotionally extreme known planetary architectures. The stars orbit each other at a separation of about 0.07 AU with a period of roughly 4.6 days, and combine to a luminosity of approximately 6 L☉.

### System Features and Environmental Experience

- The binary [[Chiaroscuro]] moves through a 4.6-day cycle of shifting shadows and recurring mutual eclipses.
- [[Phothinos]] is a dazzlingly reflective white planet close to the suns in Eirda’s sky.
- [[Kharon]] is an exceptionally dark giant whose silhouettes cross small parts of stellar disks during transit seasons.
- [[Umbryl]] produces rare impact-powered infrared bursts, occasionally accompanied by brief visible sparks.
- [[Skotos]] contrasts a dark planetary surface with bright rings and moons in resolved images.
- [[Ereba]] carries a swarm of reflective icy satellites, described telescopically as “white bees.”
- [[Euryphessa]] supplies a white-cloud, black-ring contrast in the distant outer system.
- [[Eirda#Nyx|Nyx]] is the immediate source of overwhelming lunar brightness, extreme tides, and deep sudden eclipses.

## Cultural and Psychological Impact on Eirda

Eirdan civilization develops under astronomical anxiety shaped by real events and their interpretation.

- The contrasting stars supply recurring myths of conflict and betrayal.
- A recent massive moon drives exceptional tides and total stellar eclipses.
- Transits and individual background-star occultations become omens of concealment.
- Rare impact sparks mark the otherwise inconspicuous position of Umbryl, whose strongest events are infrared.
- Astronomy, ritual, and long records help communities anticipate seasonal, tidal, and eclipse hazards.
- Art repeatedly uses light and darkness, order and chaos, loyalty and rebellion.

Skotos’s halo, Ereba’s “white bees,” and Euryphessa’s brilliant clouds and dark rings become particularly important after telescopic observation. The constancy of physical orbital laws coexists with changing seasons, tidal evolution, cloudy weather, and rare impacts.

## Planetary System

The system contains six planets and a brown dwarf on circumbinary orbits, plus a dynamically excited comet population. The main orbital references are deliberately more widely separated than in the original overview. Kharon retains its 1.91 AU orbit, while Eirda and the outer companions move outward.

The binary’s working eccentricity is 0.02. A low-eccentricity coplanar estimate places the inner circumbinary stability boundary near 0.175 AU. Phothinos at 0.20 AU is close to that boundary, requiring a separate binary-resolved stability and tidal check.

Eirda’s reference orbit is 2.90 AU at eccentricity 0.03, receiving about 0.71 Earth flux. Its 38° obliquity supports strong seasons. Greenhouse warming and heat transport are needed for the proposed habitable climate, with exact atmospheric fractions remaining open.

### Comet Belt

The revised comet population occupies a diffuse region near 8–10 AU, between Umbryl and Skotos. It includes resonant pockets and bodies on crossing trajectories. Its size distribution spans sub-kilometer fragments through large, hundreds-of-kilometers parent bodies. Collisions and earlier gravitational perturbations supply a heterogeneous, partially processed population.

Umbryl’s nominal exterior 3:2 and 5:3 resonances lie near 7.73 and 8.29 AU. Their actual stability in the full system, the surviving parent-body mass, and the rate of transport into Umbryl remain to be modeled. The population is treated as dynamically excited and evolving, with a finite supply of impactors.

Water ice, silicate-rich material, ammonia hydrates, and complex organic coatings are plausible surface or near-surface constituents. Methane, CO, CO₂, and other volatiles may survive in protected interiors or trapping phases. Exposed volatile coatings change as bodies approach the warmer inner system.

### Impact Phenomena

Umbryl focuses and accelerates incoming debris. A sufficiently weak comet can break into a chain before impact, producing a sequence of flashes and thermal disturbances. Near its atmosphere, an unbound impactor approaches an entry speed comparable to the roughly 260 km/s local escape speed before braking.

Shock heating vaporizes and dissociates material. Gravitational and kinetic energy power the event. Recombination releases part of the energy previously absorbed in dissociation, while any additional chemistry depends on the available reactants.

Cooling plumes radiate strongly in the infrared and can persist as arcs or disturbed layers. Rare energetic impacts can also produce a brief visible spark. Brightness and duration depend on impactor mass, radiative efficiency, viewing angle, and atmospheric absorption. Eirda’s observers do not ordinarily receive a new visible light source strong enough to cast distinct shadows. Infrared instruments reveal a much more dramatic transient.

Dust plumes, transient arcs, and post-impact turbulence remain potential observing signatures. Their extent and lifetime are modeling questions. In Eirdan tradition, rare points appearing in a familiar dark part of the sky can retain strong cultural significance without lighting the landscape.

## Orbital screening

The expanded layout preserves Kharon at 1.91 AU. Eirda’s reduced reference eccentricity and strong tilt preserve unusual seasons while making space for its giant neighbors. More compact trial layouts developed close encounters in numerical screening.

A planar Newtonian model included Kharon, Eirda, Umbryl, Skotos, Ereba, and Euryphessa around a 2.2 M☉ central point mass. Reference eccentricities were 0.005, 0.03, 0.02, 0.03, 0.04, and 0.03, respectively. Three different sets of initial orbital phases were each integrated for one million years with a 0.005-year velocity-Verlet step.

All three runs retained the companions without a close encounter inside one initial mutual Hill radius. Minimum pair separations were approximately 3.93–4.19 initial mutual Hill radii. Maximum relative total-energy error was about 2.2×10⁻⁶. Eirda’s eccentricity remained below approximately 0.075 in these runs. Outer-body osculating eccentricities and semimajor axes varied appreciably, so the tabulated elements identify a reference epoch.

A further 100,000-year run at half the timestep for one phase sample also retained all companions, with maximum relative energy error about 4.3×10⁻⁷. This checks numerical sensitivity over that interval.

The point-mass approximation omits the resolved stellar binary, Phothinos, Nyx, inclinations, tides, and stellar evolution. One million years is a finite screening interval within the three-billion-year system age. These results support a candidate layout and leave full three-dimensional, binary-resolved longevity as further work.

For reproducibility, the six outer companions use the listed order, masses, and axes. Initial mean anomalies follow `[(j + 1) × 2.3999632297 + s × 0.734 × (j + 1)] mod 2π`, for `j = 0…5` and phase samples `s = 0, 1, 2`. Initial periapses are aligned. Velocities are initialized astrocentrically, then the model is shifted to its center-of-mass frame. Units are AU, years, and solar masses with `G = 4π²`.

## Remaining model work

- Resolve the full binary and planetary architecture over longer intervals and include the small mutual inclinations that set eclipse seasons.
- Model Nyx’s capture, dissipation, orbital precession, and eventual fate with Eirda’s spin and interior structure.
- Match Eirda’s pressure, greenhouse gases, clouds, surface water, and 38° obliquity to its revised irradiation.
- Find a cloud and circulation model that achieves Phothinos’s reflectivity without requiring a global lava surface.
- Calculate Umbryl’s cooling spectrum and impact light curves, including the rare visible component.
- Model the comet population, satellite systems, and ring longevity.
- Match the dark surfaces and hazes of Kharon, Skotos, and Ereba, and the bright clouds of Euryphessa, to wavelength-dependent optical properties.

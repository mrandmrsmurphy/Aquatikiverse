**Kharon** is a super-jovian planet in a circumbinary orbit around the twin stars of [[Chiaroscuro]], at a distance of 1.91 AU from the system barycenter. It is distinguished by its exceptionally low reflectivity and imposing mass, making it the darkest non–brown-dwarf body in the [[Chiaroscuro System]]. Its very low proposed Bond albedo gives strong absorption of incident light. It is faint in reflected light and most readily studied through thermal emission and silhouettes during stellar transits.

## Physical Characteristics
Kharon possesses a mass of approximately 3.8 Jupiter masses and a radius of 1.1 R_J. Its mean density of roughly 3.6 g/cm³ is consistent with strong self-compression. Core mass and heavy-element enrichment require an interior model. The planet’s bulk structure is dominated by deep layers of molecular hydrogen and helium overlying a region of increasingly degenerate, metallic hydrogen at depth.

The upper atmosphere is capped by an extremely dark haze or dust layer. This coating, potentially containing carbonaceous aerosols and strongly absorbing condensates, makes Kharon difficult to detect in reflected optical light. In reflected light, it is darker than charcoal or fresh asphalt, with only a faint reflected signal in sufficiently deep exposures. Its visible spectrum remains to be determined.

Kharon's optical properties resemble those of the known exoplanet [TrES-2b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TrES-2b), one of the darkest exoplanets observed, though Kharon is larger, more massive, cooler, and resides at a much greater distance from its stars.

## Orbital Configuration

Kharon retains its 1.91 AU near-circular circumbinary orbit. The orbital period is about 650 days and the reference orbital speed about 32 km/s. The planetary layout is expanded around this orbit to provide room for Eirda and Umbryl.

When Eirda, Kharon, and a stellar disk align, Kharon produces a partial eclipse or a complete occultation of the smaller star if their angular sizes permit. The depth and timing depend on Eirda’s orbital distance, mutual inclination, and the moving binary. Eclipse seasons can appear irregular without a detailed ephemeris.

Outside stellar transits, Kharon can occult individual background stars in its small angular patch of sky. The ominous “moving void” is a cultural description of its low reflectivity and small-area occultations.

## Optical and Thermal Properties

At 1.91 AU from approximately 6 L☉, Kharon receives about 1.64 Earth flux, or roughly 2,240 W/m². A Bond albedo near 0.001 gives a uniform-redistribution stellar equilibrium temperature near 315 K. Interior cooling adds to the thermal luminosity. Greenhouse effects determine the temperature profile and observing depth, while the effective temperature follows the total outgoing energy flux.

Its extreme optical darkness is a setting constraint. A compatible cloud, haze, and absorption model remains to be established. The comparison with TrES-2b concerns low reflectivity, while Kharon’s much cooler chemistry must be modeled separately.

Thermal emission is expected primarily in the mid-infrared. A 315 K blackbody peaks near 9.2 μm, with real observing windows determined by atmospheric absorption. Reflected-light detectability depends on geometric albedo, phase, and separation from the stars.

## Atmospheric Composition
Spectroscopic modeling suggests an atmosphere dominated by molecular hydrogen (H₂) and helium (He). Photochemical hydrocarbons and absorbing aerosol particles are candidate sources of optical opacity. Sodium, potassium, titanium, and vanadium species may occur at hotter depths, but their abundance above cold traps requires a pressure–temperature and transport model. The upper haze or soot layer likely forms from photochemical reactions driven by ultraviolet radiation from the F-type primary, polymerizing carbon-bearing gases into complex, strongly absorbing aerosols.

At higher pressures and temperatures, deeper layers are expected to transition into regimes with significant metallic hydrogen, strong pressure gradients, and embedded cloud decks of silicates and refractory condensates. These deeper clouds, however, are largely invisible at optical wavelengths due to the overlying soot and haze.

## Significance
Kharon presents a case study in atmospheric opacity, heat retention, and photometric invisibility in giant exoplanets. Its combination of large mass, moderate irradiation, and extreme darkness makes it an important analogue to exceptionally low-albedo exoplanets like TrES-2b, but in a cooler, circumbinary context.

Dynamically, its orbit and mutual alignments with [[Eirda]] give it a unique role in the lived experience of the system’s inhabitants. Its transits remove small portions of stellar light, while background-star occultations briefly extinguish individual points. These events can be interpreted as deliberate concealment or predation by observers without predictive astronomy. This visual effect has made Kharon a central figure in myths of judgment, concealment, and cosmic predation throughout Eirdan cultures.


## Modeling notes

Low albedo and the 1.91 AU orbit are retained. Eclipse depth and frequency depend on the revised Eirda orbit and the three-dimensional geometry. The upper-atmospheric absorber and intrinsic luminosity remain model parameters. The dark appearance constrains its wavelength-dependent reflectance. At favorable phase it may still be detectable as a point from Eirda despite its nearly black resolved disk.

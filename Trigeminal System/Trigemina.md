**Trigemina** is a hierarchical sextuple star system about 200 parsecs from Earth. It has three gravitationally bound binaries, each pairing an F-type star with a K-type companion in a tight orbit of a few days. The binaries A and C orbit each other, and binary B orbits the A–C pair far out. The system is dynamically stable on long timescales. It holds no known planets, but each binary has a computed circumbinary habitable zone. It is a rare sextuple system that could host three independent habitable planets, each associated with its own F+K stellar pair, and its moderate eccentricities and inclinations are consistent with formation by core fragmentation.

## System architecture

### Close binaries

| Binary | Stars | Total mass (M☉) | Period | Separation | Luminosity (L☉) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | A1 + A2 | 1.80 | 4.0 d | 0.060 AU | 2.59 |
| B | B1 + B2 | 1.96 | 5.0 d | 0.072 AU | 3.74 |
| C | C1 + C2 | 1.77 | 4.5 d | 0.065 AU | 2.38 |

All three have near-circular orbits (e ≈ 0.01) and are tidally circularized, as is typical of F+K binaries with periods under about 8 days. The periods are adopted (the original gave only "under 10 days"); circumbinary planets must orbit outside P-type stability limits of about 0.15, 0.18 and 0.16 AU respectively.

### A–C orbit

Binaries A and C orbit each other at a semimajor axis of 10.2 AU with eccentricity 0.20 (8.2–12.2 AU) and a period of 17.2 years, almost coplanar (about 3°). A planet around binary A can be stable only out to about 2.14 AU, and around C to about 2.11 AU (Holman–Wiegert limits), so the habitable-zone centres at 1.61 and 1.54 AU sit at 75% and 73% of the limit, and the outer parts of the habitable zones lie beyond it (see the N-body screen below). Seen from a planet at A's habitable-zone centre, C is a second sun that delivers 0.016–0.036 of Earth's sunlight (about 2,000–4,600 lux, as bright as an overcast day, so the planet's nights are never dark).

Small bodies on circumquadruple orbits around the A–C pair can be stable only in a narrow band from about 30 AU (the P-type limit of the A–C binary) to about 46 AU (the limit set by binary B); such orbits receive too little stellar flux for Earth-like conditions.

### Orbit of binary B

Binary B orbits the A–C barycentre at 220 AU (154–286 AU) with eccentricity 0.30, inclined about 10° to the A–C plane, with a period of about 1,388 years. The triple is stable: the outer-to-inner semimajor-axis ratio is 21.6 against about 3.9 required by the Mardling–Aarseth criterion. Planets around binary B are stable out to about 32 AU, well beyond its habitable zone, and the A–C pair adds about 4.6×10⁻⁵ to 1.6×10⁻⁴ of Earth's sunlight at a planet around A.

## Habitable zones

Each binary's habitable zone is circumbinary. Conservative zones scale as √(L/1.1) to √(L/0.53) AU:

| Binary | Combined L (L☉) | Centre (AU) | Conservative zone (AU) | Stability limit (AU) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | 2.59 | 1.61 | 1.53–2.21 | 0.15 to 2.14 |
| B | 3.74 | 1.93 | 1.84–2.66 | 0.18 to 31.9 |
| C | 2.38 | 1.54 | 1.47–2.12 | 0.16 to 2.11 |

The system could potentially host three independent habitable planets. Around B the whole zone is stable, and B's isolation, high luminosity and distance from A and C make it the most favourable location. Around A and C only the inner part of the zone (to about 2 AU) is stable. No planets are adopted yet.

## N-body screen

A fourth-order symplectic integration of all six stars plus a 1 M⊕ planet on an initially circular, coplanar orbit around each binary (step 0.05 d, 10⁵ years, energy error below 10⁻⁷) keeps every planet stable at the adopted architecture (A–C 10.2 AU, B 220 AU, binary periods 4.0, 5.0 and 4.5 d):

| Planet orbit | Result over 10⁵ yr (osculating e about its binary) |
| --- | --- |
| A at 1.61 AU | stable; e up to 0.06–0.09 (mean 0.03–0.05) |
| C at 1.54 AU | stable; e up to 0.07–0.08 (mean 0.03–0.04) |
| B at 1.93 AU | stable; e below 0.001 |
| A at 1.90 AU, C at 2.0 AU, B at 4 AU | stable; e up to 0.13 (A, C), 0.002 (B) |
| A at 2.1 AU, C at 2.2 AU, B at 20 AU | stable; e up to 0.16 (A), 0.21 (C), 0.06 (B) |
| inner binaries inclined 20° to the A–C plane | A and C at 1.61 and 1.54 AU stable; e up to 0.09 |
| binary periods 1.7, 1.9 and 1.6 d | stable; e up to 0.09 |

The Holman–Wiegert limits (2.1 AU for A and C) are a conservative bound: orbits just beyond them survive 10⁵ years but with large eccentricities, so the outer parts of the A and C habitable zones are marginal and could be unstable over gigayear timescales (not tested). The eccentricity forced by the other binary, about 0.03–0.05 on average and 0.06–0.09 at most for the habitable-zone centres, makes a planet's stellar flux swing by roughly ±5–10% each 17-year cycle. The screen is not a proof of gigayear stability.

## Physical basis

**Stars.** The listed luminosities agree with L = R²(T/5,772 K)⁴ within the stated errors: the formula gives 2.67, 0.078, 3.59, 0.133, 2.63 and 0.065 L☉ for A1, A2, B1, B2, C1 and C2, against the listed 2.52, 0.07, 3.62, 0.12, 2.31 and 0.07. The binary luminosities are 2.59, 3.74 and 2.38 L☉ (8.71 L☉ in total), and the binary masses are 1.80, 1.96 and 1.77 M☉ (3.57 M☉ for A+C and 5.53 M☉ for the whole system).

**Photometry.** A bolometric magnitude of 2.39 for 8.71 L☉ puts the system at V ≈ 8.9 at 200 pc; a visual magnitude of 10.5 would need a distance of about 419 pc.

**Lifetimes.** The main-sequence lifetime scales as about 10 Gyr × M/L:

| Star | Lifetime (Gyr) |
| --- | --- |
| A1 (F5 V) | 4.8 |
| B1 (F3 V) | 3.6 |
| C1 (F4 V) | 5.2 |
| A2, B2, C2 (K dwarfs) | 54–84 |

At the adopted age of about 2–3 Gyr the three F stars are therefore in the middle or later part of their lives, with B1 the shortest-lived, while the K companions are barely begun.

**Orbits and stability.** The Kepler periods are 17.24 yr for A–C (10.2 AU, 3.57 M☉) and 1,388 yr for B (220 AU, 5.53 M☉). The Holman–Wiegert limits use e = 0.2 and a mass ratio μ = 0.496 for A–C and e = 0.3 for B: S-type limits of 2.14 AU about A, 2.11 AU about C and 31.9 AU about B; a P-type limit of 30.0 AU about the A–C pair; and 46.1 AU for orbits about A+C limited by B. The Mardling–Aarseth criterion requires an outer-to-inner ratio of 3.9 (mass ratio 0.549, e = 0.3, i = 10°), against an actual 21.6. The close binaries (periods 4.0, 5.0 and 4.5 d) have separations of 0.060, 0.072 and 0.065 AU and P-type limits of 0.146, 0.175 and 0.157 AU.

**Light from the other binaries.** For a planet in A's habitable zone, binary C delivers 0.036 F⊕ at periastron (8.2 AU) and 0.016 F⊕ at apastron (12.2 AU): about 4,600 lux at periastron (129,000 lux per F⊕), an apparent magnitude of about −23. Binary B delivers 1.6×10⁻⁴ to 4.6×10⁻⁵ F⊕.

**N-body screen.** The screen is a fourth-order Yoshida integration (step 0.05 d, 10⁵ years, seeds 1–3, energy errors 10⁻⁸–10⁻⁶), and the planet at A stays within a semimajor axis of 1.585–1.647 AU for a start at 1.61 AU.

**Adopted values.** The orbital separations (10.2 and 220 AU), the stellar properties and the 640 ly distance are the adopted constraints. The close-binary periods, the system age (~2–3 Gyr), the 8.9 apparent magnitude and the circular, coplanar planet starts in the screen are adopted or assumed values, and the coordinates are placeholders. The 10⁵ years of the screen is a tiny fraction (0.005%) of the system's age, so it is not a proof of gigayear stability, and no planets are adopted.

# Trigeminal System

The Trigeminal System is the stellar system of [[Trigemina]]: six stars in three close F+K binaries (A, B and C) arranged in two nested orbits. It has no known planets; its three computed circumbinary habitable zones are candidate sites for future worlds.

## Structure

Main article: [[Trigemina]]

The two innermost binaries, A (1.21 + 0.59 M☉) and C (1.19 + 0.58 M☉), orbit each other every 17.2 years. Binary B (1.31 + 0.65 M☉), the brightest, orbits the pair at 220 AU every ~1,388 years.

## Stability

Planets about a binary must lie outside its P-type limit (0.15–0.18 AU) and inside the S-type limit set by the other binaries: about 2.1 AU for A and C, and about 32 AU for B. The habitable zones of A and C therefore extend past their stable region; their centres (1.61 and 1.54 AU) do not. Circumquadruple bodies around the A–C pair could be stable only between about 30 and 46 AU. The triple is stable by the Mardling–Aarseth criterion (21.6 against 3.9 required). In a six-star N-body screen, test planets at the habitable-zone centres were stable for 10⁵ years (eccentricity up to about 0.09), as were orbits out to 2.1–2.2 AU for A and C, but with eccentricities up to 0.2. See [[Trigemina#N-body screen]].

## Sky

From a planet near A's habitable-zone centre, binary C is a bright second sun (about 2,000–4,600 lux, 1.6–3.6% of full sunlight) that swings between 8.2 and 12.2 AU over 17.2 years, binary B is a brilliant point source (~5×10⁻⁵ to 2×10⁻⁴ of Earth's sunlight), and the planet's own pair is a close F+K double about 2° across (the stars 0.06 AU apart).

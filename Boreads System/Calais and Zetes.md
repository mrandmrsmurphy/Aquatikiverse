# Calais and Zetes

![[images/Boreads.png]]

**Calais and Zetes** are the hydrogen-atmosphere white dwarfs of the [[Boreads System]], with masses of approximately 0.65 and 0.55 M☉. Their present nearly circular relative orbit is about 0.006 AU, with a Kepler period near 3.72 hours. [[Chronmismer]] is a second-generation circumbinary planet at the revised reference semimajor axis of **0.030 AU**, whose approximately **3.5-billion-year biosphere** is now in its **diaspora and sanctuary epoch**.

## System Properties

The present is 3.5 Gyr after biological seeding, not 3.5 Gyr after both stars formed. Component cooling clocks are separate. The reference below uses the Montréal thick-hydrogen, carbon/oxygen-core cooling sequences for the two adopted masses, linearly interpolated in age from selected published grid points. These are isolated-star cooling illustrations, not a binary mass-transfer/reheating fit. [Model grid and documentation](https://www.astro.umontreal.ca/~bergeron/CoolingModels/), [Bédard et al. (2020)](https://www.astro.umontreal.ca/~bergeron/CoolingModels/Bedard2020.pdf). The reference files are [0.65 M☉ thick-H](https://www.astro.umontreal.ca/~bergeron/CoolingModels/CoolingModels/seq_065_thick.txt) and [0.55 M☉ thick-H](https://www.astro.umontreal.ca/~bergeron/CoolingModels/CoolingModels/seq_055_thick.txt); ages are in years, radii in cm, and luminosities in erg/s.

## Stellar Evolution

The source’s approximately 2.0 and 1.3 M☉ progenitors and roughly 1 AU initial separation remain a qualitative starting hypothesis. The initially more massive star evolved first, removing or perturbing close planets and exchanging mass with its companion. Accretion can change both mass and internal mixing; an automatic extension of the secondary’s lifetime is not assumed.

Subsequent mass transfer and one or more common-envelope episodes produced the double-white-dwarf remnant. Envelope ejection supplied debris and removed or destabilized the earlier habitable planetary architecture. The adopted component cooling-age offset is one billion years; it is a target for a binary-evolution calculation, not a result derived solely from the nominal progenitor masses. Reheating, residual burning, envelope thickness, and different remnant formation times can affect interpretation.

The last major common-envelope event precedes biological seeding by approximately 0.8 Gyr in the reference chronology. The pair was then wider than today: under an idealized detached circular gravitational-wave evolution, its separation was approximately 0.00917 AU at that event, about 0.00879 AU at seeding, and 0.006 AU now. The earlier shrinkage from roughly 1 AU belongs to stellar-envelope evolution; the later detached inspiral is a separate process.

The current circular quadrupole coalescence reference is approximately 0.97 Gyr. Mass transfer/contact occurs before literal point-mass merger, and eccentricity or other interactions alter that prediction. This is a future hazard to sanctuary infrastructure, not the primary reason for the already-occurring cooling-driven diaspora. No inevitable supernova is asserted. [Peters (1964)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.136.B1224).

## Second-Generation Planet Formation

Common-envelope ejecta, disrupted planetary material, comets, and rocky debris supplied a proposed second-generation reservoir. The source’s several-hundred-thousand-year fallback-disk phase is retained as an early material-processing stage. Cooler outer regions can assemble solids while the young remnants are too luminous for stable exposed water at the final inner orbit.

Chronmismer’s assembly, delivery or migration to approximately 0.030 AU, volatile replenishment, and tidal settling must occur before biological zero. The model does not assume that a wet Earthlike planet condensed immediately at that radius during the hot-remnant phase. The transport and mass/energy budgets remain a formation hypothesis.

The final orbit is screened against the wider historical binary as well as the present one. Its 41.58-hour Kepler period is a reference; the time-dependent binary potential perturbs actual motion and mean rotation. Gravitational-wave inspiral reduces the circumbinary cavity over time, rather than leaving its ancient stability boundary equal to today’s.

Biological zero is late panspermia into established wet habitats, approximately 0.8 Gyr after the last common envelope in the reference. External microbe-bearing cometary or planetary fragments are the retained source hypothesis. Delivery, shielding, water survival, and the absence of indigenous abiogenesis are setting assumptions needing a viable physical history; no survival of exposed surface microbes through the common-envelope catastrophe is required.

## Nomenclature

The system is named after the **Boreads**, Calais and Zetes, figures in Greek mythology associated with speed and twinship, reflecting the rapid orbit and binary nature of the white dwarf pair. The circumbinary planet, [[Chronmismer]], derives its name from the Greek phrase *ὁ χρόνος εἶναι μισή μέρα* ("time is half a day"), an inherited poetic reference to short cycles rather than an exact statement of the revised 41.6-hour orbit. The quoted Greek attribution is preserved as the author’s naming tradition, not independently validated philology.

## Model status and remaining work

The cooling illustration is supported by published single-remnant grids, but its initial age offset, mass-transfer history, planet delivery, and water replenishment are not a demonstrated binary formation channel. Fit the progenitor masses, mass loss, remnant masses, envelope histories, and independent cooling ages jointly before assigning a precise total system age.

The reference grid assumes thick hydrogen layers and equimassic carbon/oxygen cores; composition, crystallization and binary interactions can change the schedule. Validate detached inspiral, eccentricity, full stellar/planetary tides, planetary mean rotation, and the climate response. The wider planet and present diaspora replace the inconsistent fixed 0.015 AU, shared 6,000 K, and common 2.5–3 Gyr clock. The revised [[Boreads System]] page gives calculations and screening limits.

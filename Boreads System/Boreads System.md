# Boreads System

![[images/Boreads.png]]

*Calais–Zetes and Chronmismer: conceptual illustration, not an orbital-scale diagram or a fixed-epoch spectrum.*

The **Boreads System** is a fictional second-generation planetary system around the compact white dwarfs **[[Calais and Zetes]]**. Their surviving planet **[[Chronmismer]]** preserves approximately **3.5 billion years of biological history**. The present is the **Cenlotl diaspora and sanctuary era**: extensive natural ecosystems have contracted, spacefaring populations live elsewhere, and microbial refugia, biovaults, and limited custodians remain on the homeworld.

The later planet and biosphere pages govern this reconciliation. The long history takes priority over the original short planetary year. Chronmismer’s working orbit is now **0.030 AU**, with an approximately **41.58-hour Kepler period**, rather than 0.015 AU and 14.7 hours. Independent stellar clocks, a cooling reference, and binary inspiral replace the inconsistent single-temperature, single-age interpretation.

## Stellar evolution and second-generation origin

*Main article: [[Calais and Zetes]]*

The source’s approximate A-type 2.0 M☉ and F-type 1.3 M☉ progenitors, initially separated by roughly 1 AU, remain a qualitative hypothesis. Mass transfer and common-envelope evolution destroyed or destabilized the earlier habitable architecture. Possible early S-type and P-type companions were never assigned validated orbital axes.

The two remnants formed on different schedules. Their adopted one-billion-year cooling-age offset is a target for binary modeling, not a consequence already established from the nominal progenitor masses. Accretion, internal mixing, envelope removal, and remnant reheating affect the schedule; the secondary’s life is not automatically extended by gaining mass.

The last major envelope ejection supplied metal-rich fallback and disrupted planetary/cometary material. A several-hundred-thousand-year debris-disk stage could process solids in cooler regions. Chronmismer’s eventual assembly or delivery to the inner orbit, circularization, and late volatile replenishment remain a physical formation hypothesis. Immediate condensation of a wet planet at the final orbit around very hot new remnants is not assumed.

Panspermia occurs later, into existing estuaries and wet sediments, approximately 0.8 Gyr after the last common-envelope reference event. Protected external cometary or planetary carriers are the retained source hypothesis. The prior system’s destruction does not require exposed microbes to survive that catastrophe, nor does it establish a viable interstellar transit without further work.

## Cooling reference and chronology

The authorial history is indexed to **biological zero**, the arrival and establishment of life. The current time is **t ≈3.5 Gyr**. A representative cooling schedule uses Calais at 1.8 Gyr and Zetes at 0.8 Gyr on their individual tracks at biological zero; both advance by 3.5 Gyr to the present.

The reference uses selected rows of the Montréal **thick-hydrogen, carbon/oxygen-core cooling sequences**, linearly interpolated in age at 0.65 and 0.55 M☉. Evolving radii and photon luminosities are used rather than holding both stars at 6,000 K. This is an isolated-remnant cooling illustration; a common-envelope/binary model has not been fitted to it. [Montréal model documentation](https://www.astro.umontreal.ca/~bergeron/CoolingModels/), [Bédard et al. (2020)](https://www.astro.umontreal.ca/~bergeron/CoolingModels/Bedard2020.pdf).

The exact component properties and the planet’s reference flux/temperature table are on the detailed pages. The illustration starts near **1.93 kW/m²** at the planet, declines to about **0.58 kW/m²** by t=1.2 Gyr, and reaches **0.31 kW/m²** now. The previous 6,000-to-4,500 K / 2,048-to-620 W/m² pair is superseded as a chronology; a shared stellar temperature was never a necessary feature of the story.

## Binary inspiral and historical orbital space

At 0.006 AU, the detached circular quadrupole coalescence reference for 0.65+0.55 M☉ is approximately **0.97 Gyr**. Mass transfer/contact begins before point masses literally merge. This future evolution is distinct from the present cooling-driven diaspora and does not automatically imply a supernova. [Peters (1964)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.136.B1224).

Backward integration of `a⁴` under that idealized gravitational-wave law gives approximately **0.00879 AU** at seeding and **0.00917 AU** at the last common-envelope reference event. An empirical circular circumbinary test-particle fit places the critical axis near 2.42 binary separations: about **0.0222 AU** in that earlier envelope-era snapshot and **0.0145 AU** now. Thus the new 0.030 AU orbit has screening margin at both epochs, unlike the old fixed 0.015 AU layout. The fit is a guide, not a proof for a massive, tidally evolving, irradiated planet. [Holman and Wiegert (1999)](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9809315).

Low eccentricity, tides, relativity, planetary spin, and the whole slowly changing architecture still need modeling. Binary-driven oscillations are not equivalent to a single Keplerian eccentricity or to two suns fixed in the sky.

No trial crossed the screening thresholds of planetary radius below 1.5 binary separations or above twice the nominal planet axis. These thresholds are diagnostics, not a measured boundary. The initial Kepler circles include free and forced responses to the binary; the quoted osculating eccentricities do not directly define dissipative tidal forcing.

Reproducibility: AU–Julian-year–M☉ units, G=4π², 1 M⊕=3.0034896×10⁻⁶ M☉. The stars initially lie on the x-axis about their barycenter, with circular relative velocity. Planet phase is k×2.3999632297 radians and its speed initially sqrt(G×1.20/0.030). Direct pairwise accelerations are integrated by the fourth-order symmetric composition of velocity-Verlet with coefficients w₁=1/(2−2^(1/3)), w₀=−2^(1/3)/(2−2^(1/3)), w₁. Steps are 2×10⁻⁶ year for the wider binary snapshots and 10⁻⁶ year for the present one and the envelope-era half-step check. Radius and orbital-element diagnostics are sampled every 25 steps and energy every 2,500 steps.

This is a short screening result, not a 3.5 Gyr certification. It omits evolving binary separation, relativistic precession, tides, oblateness, inclination, climate, mass loss, and formation/capture transport. Long integrations initialized on low-free-eccentricity circumbinary trajectories and coupled to the inspiral and spin history remain necessary.

## Tidal heating is a separate constraint

A planet around a moving binary experiences a varying tidal tensor even when its mean orbit is nearly circular. A habitability assessment must include the individual moving stars and the planet’s frequency-dependent solid and ocean response. The old assertion that low eccentricity alone ensures negligible tidal heating is not used.

As a diagnostic, for prescribed circular stellar and planetary trajectories and synchronous mean rotation, the degree-two tidal tensor is `Eᵢⱼ = Σ Gm [3rᵢrⱼ/r⁵ − δᵢⱼ/r³]` in the body frame. Fourier amplitudes through harmonic 16 were sampled at 2,048 binary-relative phases. An illustrative constant imaginary Love response **|Im k₂|=10⁻⁶** at those forcing frequencies gives a direct binary-tide flux of approximately **1–3 W/m²** across the present-to-wider reference snapshots at 0.030 AU. This is a chosen low-loss test response, not a measured or validated rheology. Increasing it by a factor of 100 increases the diagnostic power by the same factor.

The degree-two tensor normalization reduces to the standard 21/2 eccentricity-heating coefficient for a synchronous eccentric two-body reference, providing an algebraic consistency check, not empirical validation.

The diagnostic uses `F_tide = R³/(48πG) Σ ω |Im k₂(ω)| Σᵢⱼ |Eᵢⱼ,ω|²` for real sinusoidal amplitudes, and R=0.91 R⊕. It omits the planet’s actual forced orbital and libration response, free eccentricity, ocean resonances, feedback of heating on viscosity, and spin evolution. It therefore cannot certify the planet’s temperature. A viable model must keep total dissipative heating compatible with the retained wet habitats; if realistic rheology fails that requirement, the orbit and cooling reference need another revision.

White-dwarf irradiation and tides can remove water before a radiative habitable zone arrives. Late water supply, protected reservoirs, and an explicit physical loss history remain required. [White-dwarf habitability study](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.6467). Long-duration cool-white-dwarf irradiation windows are possible models, not a guarantee for this binary. [Agol (2011)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.2791).

## Chronmismer’s climate and atmosphere

*Main article: [[Chronmismer]]*

Chronmismer retains its rocky mass/radius, approximately 0.93 g gravity, 10.29 km/s escape speed, high-albedo substellar scar, east-side estuaries, historical jungles and shallow seas, cold nightside provinces, and geothermal/subglacial refugia. The mean near-synchronous geometry provides day and night regions, but the binary suns visibly move about the average stellar direction and modulate illumination. At the present reference, the binary/planet relative cycle is about 4.09 hours; their exact motions require the full orbit.

A 1.4-atm dry managed-atmosphere reference retains O₂ at 15%, Ar at 18%, Kr at 7%, Xe at 4%, CO₂ at 0.2%, CH₄ at 0.05%, N₂O at 0.03%, and historically managed SF₆ at 0.01%; nitrogen supplies the 55.71% balance before separately specified PFC traces. Water is variable and reported separately. This is not the same atmosphere at every epoch or site. The detailed page gives partial pressures and basis conventions.

The unusual noble-gas inventory needs an enrichment and retention history. Ar/Kr/Xe affect density and collision physics, but strong intrinsic molecular infrared bands are not assigned to them. Molecular gases, water feedback, clouds, pressure effects, and engineering must provide the actual greenhouse. Human suitability is not inferred from the oxygen partial pressure alone.

Early silicate weathering can cool a hot world by drawing down CO₂. Later reduced weathering permits CO₂ accumulation where geological supply continues. Clouds can both reflect shortwave light and retain longwave radiation. Significant SF₆ and PFC enhancement is assigned primarily to **Cenlotl industry**, with species, sources, lifetimes, concentrations, and ecological costs needing a model. SF₆ gas is not casually treated as a warm water-cloud substitute.

The source’s numerical day/night surface temperatures have been replaced by clearly labeled radiative limits and modeling tasks. Cooling can shift suitable habitats toward the substellar region; it does not force them to remain centered on the terminator. Engineered refuges can persist even when extensive natural surface ecosystems no longer do.

## Life, languages, and civilization

*Main article: [[Life on Chronmismer]]; embedded Cenlotl account: [[Chronmismer#Cenlotl]]*

Microbial panspermia, early mats and stromatolites, photosynthesis and chemosynthesis, six-limbed amphibiform prey, ambush predators with bioelectric senses, giant fungal forests, arboreal hexapods, burrowing omnivores, and reef/filter-feeder communities remain. Photosynthetic lineages can prefer 480–500 nm without being restricted to one narrow band; action spectra adapt to the broad, evolving stellar spectra and water/air filtering. Visible blue-green coloration does not by itself demonstrate blue-green absorption.

Night habitats require advected organic carbon, chemosynthetic power, or locally adequate scattered light; heat transport supplies warmth rather than food or photons. Net oxygen buildup depends on carbon burial and geochemical sinks as well as photosynthesis. CH₄ and N₂O are contextual possible biosignatures; industrial fluorinated gases have a different interpretation.

The **Cenlotl lineage** retains moisture-retaining skin, gill slits and lung sacs, three limb pairs with anterior manipulation, prey-to-generalist/hunter transitions, social signaling, and ecological engineering. Aquatic **Deepcall** uses low-frequency sound, dermal colors, and pressure/body pulses; terrestrial **Breathsong** supports richer spoken, gestural, and chromatophore expression. Both media support code-switching and different pragmatic functions. No unsupplied species anatomy or separate page is invented.

Cities retain sculpted ceramic composite, basalt, fungal polymers, reed and stone precursors, geothermal power, thermal-exchange towers, and astronomical modeling. Industrial climate control prolonged selected habitats; it is not a demonstrated guarantee of planet-wide stabilization.

## Infrastructure and sanctuary

The source’s conventional synchronous elevators and parked substellar polar stations are retired. In the new reference, Chronmismer’s elementary Hill radius is about 38,700 km while its synchronous two-body radius is about 55,800 km, both center-measured. A standard geostationary cable does not fit the ordinary planetary sphere. Polar platforms move across the sky and surface rather than staying above one longitude.

Mass drivers, launch vehicles, moving low-orbit installations where stable, circumbinary light collectors, free-flying habitats, archives, living collections, and arkships preserve the intended functions. Carbon-ceramic construction remains, with actual trajectories, propulsion, structures, and stability left for engineering design.

The diaspora preserves broad cultural and ecological continuity as cooling and maintenance demands increase. Chronmismer is a sanctuary rather than an absolute prohibition on permanent human-scale engineering. Limited Cenlotl custodians, pilgrimage settlements, biovaults, and natural microbial life can remain. A future binary mass-transfer/merger episode is an additional risk to local infrastructure, not evidence that cooling has already extinguished all life.

## Model status and remaining work

The four supplied pages are reconciled to the author’s priorities, with the present diaspora and long biosphere history fixed as canon. The model is **conditional**, with these explicit research tasks:

- Fit the initial stellar binary and mass-transfer/common-envelope sequence to the two remnant masses and adopted independent cooling ages.
- Demonstrate second-generation solids, final orbital delivery/assembly, late water supply and panspermia survival; account for the hot young remnants.
- Integrate long-term three-dimensional circumbinary, relativistic, inspiral, spin and tidal evolution; derive a plausible frequency-dependent solid/ocean response and habitat-compatible heat.
- Calculate spectrum-dependent climate, circulation, clouds, ice feedbacks, atmospheric escape, the noble-gas inventory, greenhouse chemistry, and industrial gas effects.
- Quantify ecological productivity, oxygen budgets, dark-habitat food supply, managed sanctuary energy, and station/collector/arkship engineering.

The stars’ precise total age, absolute Aquatikiverse calendar date, and fitted binary history are not invented here. The 3.5 Gyr is elapsed biosphere history, not a guaranteed universal naturally temperate lifetime. Source labels and useful lore remain; superseded values are documented rather than left as competing current facts.

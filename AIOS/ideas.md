## From 2026 science news (added 2026-11-15)

- [ ] **2026 ED138 page in `Sol System/`** — Rubin Observatory's first dwarf-planet candidate, announced 2026-10-27 at the DPS meeting. ~46 AU mean distance, diameter only an albedo-dependent estimate (600–1,500 km), traced across 89 years of archival plates. Fits the existing sourced-body-article style (Pluto, Eris, Haumea, Makemake). Emphasize candidate (not confirmed) status. Source: [Rubin Observatory press release](https://rubinobservatory.org/news/rubin-first-dwarf-planet-candidate).
- [ ] **Encyclopedia article: colors of white dwarfs** — Hollands et al. (Warwick), *RAS Techniques and Instruments*, Oct 2026. Colors reconstructed from spectra through human color-vision models (never photographed): carbon-rich → pink/purple; sodium-polluted (accreted rocky debris) → green (WD J1330+6435, ~326 ly, ~4,000 °C); cool mixed H/He → cyan. ~1 in 30 nearby white dwarfs is non-white. Could be a section in `Stars.md` or standalone. Source: [Warwick press release](https://warwick.ac.uk/news/pressreleases/astronomers-reveal-true-colours-of-white-dwarf-stars/).
- [ ] **Encyclopedia article or section: black hole stars / quasi-stars** — MoM-BH\*-1 (Nature, 2026-08-12; JWST "Mirage or Miracle" survey): a ~10⁵–10⁷ M☉ black hole (model-dependent) inside a solar-system-sized pristine gas envelope, shining far beyond any fusion-powered star; candidate explanation for Little Red Dots at cosmic dawn. Real ones are short-lived early-universe phenomena. Source: [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2026/astronomers-discover-brand-new-type-astrophysical-object-black-hole-star-0812).
- [ ] **Fictional system seed: quasi-star system** — a mature, stable black-hole-star analog would be chosen speculation (real ones live briefly at cosmic dawn). Distinct from `Tom (black hole).md`: the envelope itself is the "star," with the BH inside. Open questions to explore: envelope dynamics, what a planet embedded in or orbiting such an object experiences.
- [ ] **Fictional seed: green or pink white dwarf companion** — add a colored white dwarf to an existing or new system; the sodium-pollution mechanism ties the star's color to the fate of its former planets (nice storytelling hook: the star is green because it ate its rocky worlds).
- [ ] **Fictional seed: "sunscreen" lava world** — TOI-561 b analog ([JWST phase curves, Oct 2026](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261026225603.htm)): 10.5-hour orbit, ~75% albedo from dayside silicate clouds (SiO₂, Mg-silicates), atmosphere sustained by magma-ocean outgassing. Closest existing analog is Gigafusc (~700 K) but nothing in the vault has the reflective silicate-sky mechanism.
- [ ] **Fictional seed: nearby quiet-M-dwarf HZ super-Earth** — GJ 887 d analog ([RedDots/HARPS, A&A, Mar 2026](https://www.astronomy.com/science/astronomers-confirm-potentially-habitable-exoplanet-in-the-solar-neighborhood/)): ~6.5 M⊕ minimum, 50.7-day orbit, HZ spans 43–122 days, exceptionally quiet star. The vault already has M-dwarf worlds; the hook here would be a *quiet* old M dwarf versus the flare-premise worlds.

## Biosphere and environment seeds (added 2026-11-15)

Redox-cycle-centered world concepts from 2025–2026 science. Verification status noted per item.

- [ ] **Life under a green sun (polluted white dwarf)** — VERIFIED components: WD J1330+6435 (green, sodium/Ca/Li-polluted, ~4,000 °C) + Shields et al. 2025 (UCI, *ApJ*): synchronously rotating WD HZ planets run ~25 K warmer than Sun-like-star analogs because fast rotation smears heat dayside→nightside ([UCI News](https://news.uci.edu/2025/02/13/uc-irvine-astronomers-gauge-livability-of-exoplanets-orbiting-white-dwarf-stars/)). Setup: HZ at 0.005–0.02 AU, 10–30 h "year," tidally locked. Hooks: pigments tuned to absorb green (black/purple/crimson flora); steady meteoritic fallout (Fe, Na, P) feeding surface iron/sodium-coupled redox instead of vent reliance. Caveats to reconcile: WD cools so the HZ migrates inward over Gyr; planet must survive the red-giant phase; early WD phase is UV-harsh. Also note: Earth's plants reflecting green is NOT simply "because the Sun peaks in green" — build the pigment argument on the WD's actual filtered spectrum.
- [ ] **Black-hole-star aeroplankton biosphere** — SPECULATIVE extension of verified object (MoM-BH\*-1). No rocky surface possible (cosmic dawn, ~Myr lifetimes); instead a floating ecosystem in the 300–500 K outer shells of the gas envelope, with metals mixed in from early supernovae. Redox engine: accretion X-rays reprocessed to UV crack H₂O/CH₄/NH₃ in upper decks into radicals + peroxide; convection lifts reduced H₂ from below; life harvests the gradient where they meet. Overlaps conceptually with Lunara's radiolysis — differentiate if both get written.
- [ ] **Mantle-controlled redox worlds (IW buffer)** — PARTIALLY VERIFIED: mantle fO₂ controlling outgassed atmosphere composition is established (Ortenzi et al. 2020; Sossi et al. 2020, ΔIW scale) and a July 2026 LIFE-mission paper models reading oxidation state from spectra ([arXiv 2607.28121](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28121)); the specific "core:mantle ratio → IW−6…+6 map" claim is untraced. Hook: atmospheric disequilibrium without photosynthesis — reducing mantle (IW−4) outgasses NH₃/CH₄, oxidizing mantle (IW+4) pumps CO₂/SO₂; pair a reducing crust with photochemically oxidized rain for ammonia-nitrate or methane-sulfate ecosystems, no free O₂. Connects directly to `Encyclopedia/Redox Chemistry.md`.
- [ ] **Stagnant-lid carbon worlds (STEHM)** — VERIFIED: Hill et al. 2026 (Stanford, *Planet. Sci. J.*), stagnant-lid planets down to ~0.7–0.8 R⊕ hold multi-Gyr atmospheres given high mantle carbon and radiogenic heat ([Universe Today](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/new-model-finds-the-lower-size-limit-for-habitable-exoplanets)). Hook: no subduction-driven nutrient return, so biology evolves aggressive bio-weathering — acid-secreting lithotroph networks mining phosphorus and metals from stagnant basalt.
- [ ] **Silicate-sunscreen terminator world** — VERIFIED seed (TOI-561 b, Oct 2026), extension is speculation: move the setup outward so the dayside stays magma while the nightside is temperate; rock vapor (silicates, Na, Fe) condenses as mineral snow over the terminator and dissolves into dark oceans — continuous dissolved-metal supply for life.
- [ ] **Sub-Neptune suspended cloud biosphere ("euhabitability")** — UNVERIFIED LABEL: no Aug-2026 "euhabitability framework" found; the concept itself is real and older (Seager et al. 2021 aerial biospheres in temperate sub-Neptunes; ice-sandwich oceans on sub-Neptunes). Hook: H₂-filled buoyancy bladders; organisms use atmospheric H₂ directly as electron donor, reducing CO₂ or N₂ from the air. Rename the seed when written.

## Habitat and system seeds (added 2026-11-20)

- [ ] **Life on a planet around a rogue brown dwarf: late-entry habitable window** — SPECULATIVE design built on verified objects; the cooling law and every derived number below are from memory (Burrows & Liebert-style scaling, good to about a factor of 2) and must be checked against Baraffe/Saumon–Marley evolution tables (run as a dated script in `AIOS/scripts/`) before any page states them. Hook: a civilization that evolves on a dimming clock and has to leave, the same dimming-clock premise as `Boreads System/Chronmismer.md` and `Life on Chronmismer.md` (white dwarf binary cooling track, panspermia seeding after the hottest remnant epoch, Cenlotl civilization leaving about 2.5 Gyr after seeding), with a brown dwarf in place of the white dwarfs; compare the two cooling clocks before writing, and differentiate so the pair do not duplicate each other.
  - **Verified anchor:** PSO J318.5−22, a free-floating member of the β Pictoris moving group: ~8.3 M♃, T_eff ~1,100–1,200 K, ~23 Myr, rotation period 8.6 h ([Gemini](https://www.gemini.edu/node/12481), [Liu et al. 2013](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2013ApJ...777L..20L/abstract), [Biller et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.03746)). It is bright only because it is young; at its own age it gives a habitable zone of ~0.004–0.005 AU (~8–10 R♃, ~1–1.5 day orbits) that closes within tens of Myr, so it is a poor host itself.
  - **Design knobs:** radius is not free (degeneracy holds every brown dwarf near 0.08–0.1 R☉ across a 10× mass range). Mass sets orbit size, not duration. Duration is set by the age at which the planet enters the zone: on L ∝ t^-1.3 a 1.1→0.35 S⊕ window lasts ~1.4× the entry age (1 Gyr needs entry at ~0.7 Gyr; entry at 2 Gyr gives ~2.8 Gyr; entry at 5 Gyr gives ~7 Gyr). A thick CO₂/H₂ greenhouse stretching the outer edge to 0.2 S⊕ gives 1 Gyr from ~0.4 Gyr entry. Old hosts are better clocks (L falls 68% over a Gyr starting at 0.7 Gyr, 21% starting at 5 Gyr).
  - **Candidate designs** (planet enters at 1.1 S⊕): 52 M♃, entry 0.7 Gyr, T_eff ~1,700 K, 18 host radii, 26 h period, habitable to ~1.7 Gyr; 63 M♃, entry 2 Gyr, ~1,400 K, 12 host radii, 12 h, habitable to ~4.8 Gyr (preferred); 52 M♃, entry 5 Gyr, ~900 K, 5 host radii, 3.8 h, habitable to ~12 Gyr (faint, tide-heavy).
  - **Why late entry suits seeded life:** before entry the planet is a steam world (about 14× Earth's flux at 0.1 Gyr in the 1-Gyr design); it only becomes clement as the host cools, so life can arrive late. Seeding routes: (1) relay panspermia between sibling planets as the zone sweeps inward (compact-system ejecta exchange; Lingam & Loeb 2017 on TRAPPIST-1, recalled and not checked); (2) birth-cluster lithopanspermia in the first ~10–100 Myr (Adams & Spergel 2005, recalled and not checked), needing dormant survival until entry. A rogue host has almost no later encounters, so seeding must be early. Suggested combination: cluster or sibling seeding, then relay to whichever planet is in the zone.
  - **Ingesting inner planets does not extend the window:** infall releases GM/R ≈ 1.4×10¹⁰ J/kg, so one Earth mass gives ~8.5×10³⁴ J, only ~0.4 Myr of PSO J318.5−22's current output (already ~50 Earth masses' worth radiated over 23 Myr). Only a Jupiter-mass-scale ingestion would re-brighten a host appreciably, which is more than a brown dwarf disk holds. Better use of the idea: metal-polluted host spectrum, the same hook as the green white dwarf seed.
  - **Open problems:** (a) early water loss in the steam phase; cool brown dwarfs have little XUV so retention may be possible (unverified), and a water-rich planet formed beyond the ice line is the safe choice; (b) tides: orbits at 5–25 host radii sit outside corotation for a typical 2–10 h spinner (PSO J318.5−22 corotation ≈ 4 R♃), so tides push the planet outward, away from the shrinking zone; a slow-spinning host would pull it inward in step, rate unknown; (c) spectrum peaks at ~1.7–2 µm with no UV, which fits a far-red biosphere like Stellula's but makes oxygenic photosynthesis hard, so a chemosynthetic or radiolytic base is safer; (d) magnetic and particle environment at 5–25 host radii not examined; (e) deuterium burning above ~13 M♃ shifts early luminosity and was ignored.

**Pekhám** is the carbon-rich dwarf planet circling the M-type star [[Thrírlitir#Thrírlitir C|Thrírlitir C]] at 0.05 AU. It is one of the darkest and hottest small bodies in the [[Thrírlitir System]]: a black, charcoal-like world that absorbs nearly all visible light and glows in the thermal infrared. It lives inside the narrow band where an S-type orbit around one star of a close binary is stable. Its companion world is [[Barzél]], circling Thrírlitir B.

## Orbit and stability

The B–C binary has a separation of 0.3 AU (period 53.7 d, e = 0.01). The Holman–Wiegert critical radius for a planet orbiting C is 0.061 AU, so Pekhám at 0.05 AU sits at ~82% of the limit, the tighter of the two dwarf planets. Fourth-order symplectic N-body screens of A, B, C, Barzél and Pekhám (10⁵ years, several random phase sets) keep Pekhám stable at 0.04, 0.05 and 0.06 AU and lose it at 0.07 AU. The orbit must stay within roughly 30° of the B–C orbital plane; at 50° mutual inclination it is destabilised within ~5×10⁴ years (Kozai-like). Its 6.46-day period is 1/8.3 of the binary period, not at a low-order resonance.

Thrírlitir B pulls on Pekhám every 53.7 days, so the osculating eccentricity swings between about 0.05 and 0.12, with the pericentre direction precessing quickly. This is a real, periodic distortion of the orbit, not a steady eccentricity. The star C subtends about 4.9° from Pekhám's sky (about 9 times the Sun's disc from Earth), B about 1.6° (1.3–1.9° as the binary turns), and A a 0.1° point. The Roche limit of Pekhám for C is 0.007 AU, far inside the orbit.

## Light and heat

Pekhám is tidally locked to C within ~10⁴ years, so the "560 K" mean temperature is a reference for a uniformly redistributed, zero-albedo body, not the actual surface. An airless locked world has a hot face and a changing second half.

- **Hot face.** The meridian facing C is lit all the time: ~790 K at the substellar point for C alone, ~860 K once B's light is added (A = 0). Carbon and silicon-carbide crust here is bare, dry and thermally cracked.
- **B-lit "night" hemisphere.** B holds a fixed direction in space while Pekhám turns once per 6.46-day orbit, so every point away from C's meridian is lit by B for roughly half of each orbit: B delivers 4.6–9.1 F⊕ at peak and drives the surface to ~620 K, then the ground radiates it back (thermal skin depth ~0.1 m), falling toward ~100 K in the dark half. The time-averaged temperature of the antipodal equatorial ground is ~470 K, and at ~1 m depth the ground stays near that mean.
- **Poles.** C and B lie in the orbital plane, so the poles receive only grazing light. Permanently shadowed craters poleward of ~80° stay near 60–120 K, the cold traps of the planet (as on Mercury).
- **Internal heat.** Radiogenic heat is 0.005–0.012 W/m² for 4–10 pW/kg. Tidal heating from B's forcing (effective e ≈ 0.03) is ≲0.04 W/m². Together they are ~10⁻⁵ of the 7,000 W/m² a 600 K surface radiates, so internal heat does not power eruptions.

## Surface and composition

Pekhám is made of refractory carbon: graphite, amorphous carbon, silicon carbide, and a heavy hydrocarbon fraction. At 2.1 g/cm³ it is lighter than silicate rock and denser than ice. Soot-black graphite and amorphous carbon give an albedo below 0.01 (adopted), darker than any common natural material.

- **Dayside crust.** Above ~600 K heavy hydrocarbons and tars break down (pyrolysis) to coke, graphite and light gases, so the C-lit hemisphere is bare carbon and carbide, not tar.
- **Tar and sulfur belts.** Tars and molten sulfur (melting 388 K, boiling 718 K) survive where temperatures stay below ~600 K: a metre or more below the surface of the B-lit hemisphere (~470 K), in vent plumbing, and in the shadowed polar craters, where vapor re-freezes as tar and sulfur frost. Pools of sulfur and tar are therefore buried or polar, not on the hot face.
- **Atmosphere.** Pekhám is airless apart from a thin vapor exosphere of sulfur, carbon and hydrocarbon fragments in equilibrium with its surface. C's X-ray and ultraviolet output (assumed Lx/Lbol ~10⁻⁴, 2.2 W/m² at Pekhám) would strip a thick atmosphere of this mass on timescales much shorter than a billion years, so any outgassed atmosphere is temporary.

## Activity

Heat from within is too weak for volcanism, so the activity is driven by starlight. Each 6.46-day orbit swings the ground between ~100 K and ~620 K across the B-lit hemisphere, and B's 53.7-day forcing adds a second cycle. The thermal stress cracks the carbon crust into polygonal networks. The cracks glow in the infrared when they open onto hot sub-surface layers. Buried tar and sulfur vaporise where heat reaches them, venting vapor plumes of sulfurous gas and carbon-rich aerosols through the cracks, and the vapor re-freezes at the polar traps and on the cooler ground. In thermal infrared Pekhám appears as a shimmering lattice of hot cracks and intermittently radiant vents.

Outbursts are sized by the available buried vapor, not by internal heat, and are sporadic. Plausible extension, not directly attested.

## Sky

From the hot face C hangs fixed in the sky as a ~5° dull red-orange disc, never rising or setting. B crosses the sky every 6.46 days as a ~1.6° pale yellow-orange disc, and A is a 0.1° point. Away from the C meridian the day–night cycle is B's 6.46-day rise and set. [[Barzél]] circles B at 0.05 AU and is a dark ~11″ speck lost in B's glare.

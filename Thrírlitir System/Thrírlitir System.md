## Stars

Main article: [[Thrírlitir]]

The system has three gravitationally bound stars of distinct spectral types: an F8 V primary (**Thrírlitir A**), a G8 V secondary (**Thrírlitir B**) and an M1 V tertiary (**Thrírlitir C**). The mix produces a wide range of planetary environments, from intensely irradiated inner orbits to dim, cold outer regions.

**Thrírlitir A** is the most massive and luminous star: 1.3 M☉, 1.35 R☉, 6250 K, 2.505 L☉. Its conservative habitable zone runs from about 1.54 to 2.76 AU, and the terraformed world [[Aialon]] orbits at its inner edge.

**Thrírlitir B** (0.85 M☉, 0.88 R☉, 5350 K, 0.566 L☉) and **Thrírlitir C** (0.40 M☉, 0.464 R☉, 3800 K, 0.0405 L☉) orbit their common barycentre at 0.3 AU (e = 0.01) with a period of 53.7 days. Together they give 0.6065 L☉, comparable to a late K star, but the spectrum is a composite of G-type visible light and M-dwarf near-infrared. B supplies most of the photons in both the red (600–700 nm) and near-infrared (800–1,100 nm) bands at the circumbinary planets; C adds a small near-infrared shoulder.

The B+C barycentre orbits A at 7 AU (e = 0.02, inclination ~2°) with a period of 11.60 years. The triple is stable by the Mardling–Aarseth criterion (outer-to-inner orbit ratio 23 against ~3.8 required). The hierarchy supports planets bound to A, circumbinary planets around B+C, close-in dwarf planets around B and C, and the distant circumsystem giant Mot.

## Planets

The system has five planets: two around A ([[Ceraun]] and [[Aialon]]), two around the B+C binary ([[Valen]] and [[Aphrod]]) and one distant giant ([[Mot]]) around all three stars.

### Ceraun

Main article: [[Ceraun]]

Ceraun is a tidally locked hot Jupiter (150 M⊕, 1.1 R_J) orbiting A at 0.1 AU in 10.13 days, receiving about 250 times Earth's flux. It has a dark, hot, absorptive dayside (Bond albedo ~0.1) with bright silicate and iron clouds on the cooler limb and nightside. Moderate atmospheric escape (~9×10⁶ kg/s) feeds a telescopic glow tail of ultraviolet, hydrogen-alpha and sodium emission bent back ~15° from the anti-stellar direction. It has no moons, and from Valen it is lost in A's glare except when it transits.

### Aialon

Main article: [[Aialon]]

Aialon orbits A at 1.58 AU. Its companion pair B+C raises and lowers the flux between about 0.87 and 1.25 times Earth's every 53.7 days over the planet's 1.742-year orbit, so orbit-driven seasons dominate, with the 15° tilt secondary. Originally hosting only sparse native microbes, Aialon was terraformed by industrial means over millennia and now has 20% oxygen and a thriving biosphere. A small moon (~1.0×10²² kg at 250,000 km) stabilises the tilt. Continuous-thrust ships link Aialon with Valen.

### Valen

Main article: [[Valen]]

Valen is an Earth-like circumbinary world at 0.80 AU with a 0.640-year year. The B+C pair delivers 0.82–1.18 times Earth's flux (recurring every 69.7 days as the binary phase beats against the orbit), plus ~0.05 from A. The sky has three suns: B (0.59°), C (0.31°) and distant A (0.10°). Valen was sterile before human arrival and is about 70% terraformed (~300 years to go); the air is thin in oxygen and unsafe without precautions, and UV is the main hazard. A mutually bound moon pair (Valen I and II, 12,000 km apart) raises tides of ~1.7 m.

### Aphrod

Main article: [[Aphrod]]; biosphere: [[Life on Aphrod]]

Aphrod orbits the B+C pair at 1.0 AU with a period 1.398 times Valen's (within 0.15% of 7:5, no locked resonance), receiving about 0.66 Earth flux from all three stars. A 10–20 bar atmosphere holds the surface near 280 K. Beneath veiled, Venus-like clouds lie an ammonia-rich ocean (15–25 wt% NH₃) and a mildly warm, humid climate with native microbial and aerial life that runs iron–methane and nitrogen redox cycles with photoferrotrophic recharge.

### Mot

Main article: [[Mot]]

Mot is a ~30 M⊕ frozen ice giant at 117 AU (34,000 km radius, T_eff 35 K), orbiting all three stars every 792.5 years. A stably layered interior keeps it cold, and slow weather, rare methane megastorms, stratified haze towers and polar hexagons mark a world almost forsaken by sunlight. Mot I (~1,200 km radius, retrograde at 580,000 km) carries over 99.5% of the satellite mass, with at least eleven irregular minor moons.

## Dwarf planets

The two dwarf planets are close-in, tidally locked, airless worlds on S-type orbits at 0.05 AU inside the B–C binary. They are rare examples of close-in, stable dwarf planets around individual stars of a tight binary, and are valuable for the study of refractory planetary chemistry, tidal heating and exotic mineralogy.

### Pekhám

Main article: [[Pekhám]]

A carbon-rich, soot-black world (0.008 M⊕, 2.1 g/cm³, albedo <0.01) circling C every 6.46 days at 16 times Earth's flux from C plus 4.6–9.1 from B. Its hot face reaches ~860 K, tars and sulfur survive buried and in polar shadow, and thermally driven vents and glowing cracks make it shimmer in the infrared.

### Barzél

Main article: [[Barzél]]

An iron–nickel world (0.010 M⊕, 7.8 g/cm³) circling B every 4.43 days at 226 times Earth's flux, with a dayside at ~1,280 K mean and ~1,530 K at the substellar point. Lead lakes and sulfide melts cover the hot face, zinc survives as night frost, and fissures glow like a forge.

## Stability

Planetary orbits were screened with a fourth-order symplectic N-body integration of the three stars and the planets (10⁶ years for the adopted configuration), using eccentricities of 0.01 (B–C) and 0.02 (A–(B+C)) and low mutual inclinations. The result is a screen, not a proof of gigayear stability.

| Body | Position | Margin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Aialon (1.58 AU) | A-side critical radius 1.896 AU | 83% of the limit |
| Valen (0.80 AU) | B+C circumbinary critical radius 0.730 AU | 1.10 times the limit; 4 of 4 stable for 10⁶ yr with Aphrod |
| Aphrod (1.0 AU) | same | 1.37 times the limit; near the 6:1 binary resonance at 0.99 AU |
| B+C subsystem | critical radius against A 1.846 AU | Aphrod inside it |
| Barzél (0.05 AU around B) | Holman–Wiegert limit 0.101 AU | 50% of the limit; stable 10⁶ yr |
| Pekhám (0.05 AU around C) | limit 0.061 AU | 82% of the limit; stable 10⁶ yr |

Valen at 0.80 AU keeps clear of the binary's 4:1 resonance (0.757 AU) and of Aphrod (minimum separation 0.045 AU in the screen). The dwarf planets must stay within about 30° of the B–C plane. Mot and Ceraun are outside the integrations (Mot at 117 AU is 16 times farther out than the inner stellar orbit).

## Cycles and illumination

Named cycles: Valen's year (0.640 years), Aialon's year (1.742 years), the 53.7-day binary cycle and the 11.60-year stellar cycle. At the B+C barycentre A delivers ~0.051 Earth flux (2.505/7²) and B+C deliver ~0.0124 at A. Valen's 69.7-day binary beat and the Aialon–Valen conjunction every 1.012 years are used for record-keeping.

## Physical basis

**Valen's orbit.** Valen orbits at 0.80 AU because a 4:1 mean-motion resonance with the B–C binary lies at 0.757 AU: at 0.779 AU (4.19 times the binary's period) it is unstable, and the binary-forced osculating eccentricities of 0.06–0.16 would also make Valen and Aphrod cross orbits when only 0.22 AU apart. At 0.80 AU Valen receives 0.948 Earth flux from B+C (circular-orbit mean 0.965, ranging 0.82–1.18 over the 53.7-day binary phase) plus about 0.051 from A, a mean of about 1.0 Earth flux (1.016 with the finite binary); its period is 4.35 times the binary's and its period ratio with Aphrod is 1.398, within 0.15% of 7:5. Aphrod at 1.0 AU receives 0.614 (0.535–0.721) plus 0.051 Earth flux, about 0.66 in total, and sits near the 6:1 resonance at 0.99 AU.

**Stability screen.** A fourth-order symplectic N-body integration (energy drift about 10⁻⁷) of the three stars, Aialon, Valen and Aphrod, with random phases, gave:

| Configuration | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Valen 0.779 AU, Aphrod 1.0 AU, 10⁵ yr | 4 of 6 runs lose Valen (between 131 and 22,973 yr) |
| Valen alone, 3×10⁴ yr, at 0.74 / 0.76 / 0.779 / 0.80 / 0.82 / 0.85 / 0.90 AU | 0 / 2 / 3 / 0 / 0 / 0 / 1 unstable of 6 |
| Valen 0.80 AU with Aphrod 1.0 or 1.1 AU, 10⁵ yr | 6 of 6 stable each |
| Valen 0.82 AU with Aphrod 1.0 or 1.1 AU, 10⁵ yr | 6 of 6 stable each |
| Valen 0.85 AU with Aphrod 1.0 AU / 1.1 AU, 10⁵ yr | 5 of 6 / 6 of 6 stable |
| **Adopted: Valen 0.80 AU, Aphrod 1.0 AU, 10⁶ yr** | **4 of 4 stable; minimum Valen–Aphrod separation 0.045 AU** |
| Valen 0.82 AU, Aphrod 1.0 AU, 10⁶ yr | 3 of 4 stable; one run loses Aphrod and Valen at 170,000 yr |

Aialon is stable at 83% of the A-side critical radius, but the companion pair raises its osculating eccentricity as far as 0.06–0.16.

**Other numbers.** A's conservative habitable zone is about 1.54–2.76 AU, so Aialon (1.0 Earth flux) sits at its inner edge. Valen's Hill radius is 1.22 million km, and its moon pair, a bound binary orbiting at about 250,000 km, must be closer than about 17,600 km (half the pair's 35,100-km mutual Hill radius) to be stable.

**Limits.** The screen is a 10⁶-year test (0.05% of the system's age) with circular coplanar starts at the stated inclinations, not a proof of gigayear stability. Ceraun and Mot are not in the integration, and the dwarf planets have their own screens. Detailed planet and biosphere pages govern all planetary figures, and each has its own Physical basis section.

# Aialon

![[images/Aialon_at_dusk.png]]

*Aialon at dusk: a representative illustration.*

**Aialon** is a habitable, fully terraformed terrestrial planet orbiting the F-type primary star [[Thrírlitir#Thrírlitir A|Thrírlitir A]] in the [[Thrírlitir System]]. Slightly smaller and less massive than Earth, it receives about the same mean stellar flux from a hotter, more UV-bright star, and the nearby companion pair B+C bends its orbit enough to give it strong, orbit-driven seasons. It is notable for a stable nitrogen–oxygen atmosphere, a dense ozone layer, and a photosynthetic ecology of lavender and blue-white vegetation tuned to the F-type spectrum. Once cold and without a complex surface biosphere, it was reshaped into a livable world by an automated, millennia-long terraforming campaign and is now the origin world and main population center of Valenic civilization. Numbers are mostly scaling arithmetic; see the physical basis at the end.

## Orbital and rotational characteristics

Aialon orbits Thrírlitir A at 1.58 AU. Given the star's luminosity of 2.505 L☉, the flux from A alone is about 1.0 times Earth's at that distance. The orbital period is about 1.74 years. The planet sits at 83% of the A-side critical radius of 1.896 AU, close to the dynamical edge set by the companions; in a fourth-order N-body screen with the other planets it stays stable (see the stage page).

**The companion pair shapes the orbit.** The B+C pair, 7 AU from A, tugs on Aialon so that its instantaneous eccentricity swings up to about 0.06–0.12 (rarely 0.18) whatever its nominal value, an effect that scales as (a/R)³ with the separation. The orbit's long axis precesses with a period of about 210 years. Total stellar flux therefore typically ranges between 0.87 and 1.25 times Earth's over each year, with extremes of 0.77–1.42 over millennia, and the year's seasons are driven mainly by orbital distance, with the 15° tilt secondary. Perihelion summers, cooler distant winters and century-scale climate cycles from the precessing orbit are part of the planet's character.

The planet rotates once every 28 Earth hours, a day slightly longer than Earth's, which lunar tides can account for after about 2 Gyr. Its axial tilt of 15° adds a mild tilt-driven seasonal signal on top of the orbital one. The orbital plane is inclined about 2° to the B+C plane.

## Sky and light

Thrírlitir A is bluer than the Sun (Wien peak 464 nm against 502 nm) and emits, per unit of total light, 1.38 times the Sun's UV-B, 1.57 times its UV-C, 1.24 times its UV-A and 1.12 times its blue light. When the B+C pair is above the horizon it is a bright pair of second suns: it adds 0.008–0.021 Earth flux (about 800–2,100 lux, magnitude about −22, some ten thousand times a full Moon), so nights on Aialon are as bright as a heavy overcast day for much of the year. The pair's positions move slowly against the sky over the 11.60-year stellar cycle and appear to circle each other every 53.7 days.

## Physical properties

With a mass of 0.87 M⊕ and a mean radius of 0.96 R⊕, Aialon's surface gravity is about 9.2 m/s² (0.94 g) and the escape velocity about 10.6 km/s. Its mean density of about 5.4 g/cm³ suggests an Earth-like composition: a metallic core surrounded by a silicate mantle and crust. The calculated surface area (about 470 million km²) and volume (about 9.6×10¹¹ km³) place it slightly below Earth. The Hill radius of 2.07 million km is a scale, not a guarantee of satellite stability in the triple system.

## Atmosphere and climate

Aialon's atmosphere is the product of deliberate engineering and subsequent biological feedbacks. The dry reference mixture is 77% nitrogen, 20% oxygen, 2.96% argon and 0.04% carbon dioxide at a total pressure of 1.1 bar (partial pressures of about 0.85 bar N₂, 0.22 bar O₂, 0.033 bar Ar and 0.00044 bar CO₂); water vapor varies regionally. The mass of the atmosphere is 5.6×10¹⁸ kg (1.09 times Earth's).

The F-type host star emits more ultraviolet radiation than the Sun, and the terraformers exploited this by seeding an ozone-producing biosphere. The resulting stratospheric ozone layer is thicker and more sharply stratified than Earth's, with a well-defined tropopause; it has to be thicker to hold surface UV-B near Earth's level against the star's 1.38 times higher UV-B fraction.

Globally averaged surface temperatures are near 288 K, maintained by a mean stellar flux of about 1 F⊕, a moderate greenhouse effect from water vapor and trace CO₂, and a relatively high albedo (about 0.32) from cloud cover and pale vegetation. Aialon sits at the inner edge of A's habitable zone (about 1.54–2.76 AU), and its peak flux (0.87–1.25 F⊕ typically, above the roughly 1.05 F⊕ moist-greenhouse threshold for an F8 star) is carried by the oceans' thermal inertia rather than by a small orbital swing. Mid-latitude climates resemble cool temperate zones and the equatorial belt is warm and humid. Persistent subtropical high-pressure cells and broad storm tracks organize the weather into recognizable but slightly slower patterns than Earth's because of the longer day.

## Surface and hydrosphere

Aialon's land–sea distribution is broadly Earth-like, with significant ocean basins, mid-latitude continents and archipelagos. The surface ocean fraction is slightly lower than Earth's, giving more extensive continental interiors but a predominantly maritime climate.

Tectonic activity continues: mid-ocean ridges, subduction zones and hotspot chains create a geologically diverse surface with active volcanism in some regions. Many large basins and coastal plains were modified or flooded during terraforming to control albedo and hydrological cycling.

Hydrospheric engineering increased the volume of surface water and adjusted salinity toward Earth-like values, though some enclosed seas retain higher salinities or unusual chemistries inherited from pre-terraforming brines.

## Biosphere

Before intervention, Aialon supported only sparse native microbial life (it lacked a complex surface biosphere but was not sterile), primarily iron-reducing, ammonia-tolerant extremophiles in cold brine pockets and simple atmospheric methanotrophs in the upper troposphere. These lineages were preserved where possible and incorporated into the new biosphere as specialist guilds.

Terraforming introduced a curated mix of Earth-derived and engineered organisms adapted to the F-type spectrum:

- **Primary producers** are dominated by plants and algae with pigments tuned to strong blue-violet light and screened against the star's excess ultraviolet. Many macroscopic species have lavender, blue-white or silvery foliage rather than green.
- **Soil and aquatic microbes** often show UV-induced fluorescence, the side effect of protective or signaling compounds. Under twilight or artificial UV illumination, wetlands and shallow tidal pools exhibit faint, multicolored glows.
- **Fauna** are broadly Earth-compatible but genetically adjusted for slightly lower gravity, higher UV exposure and local pathogens. Several lineages have evolved reflective or iridescent integuments that screen UV and reflect excess visible light.

Native pre-terraforming microbes persist in specialized niches such as deep crustal aquifers, high-salinity basins and iron-rich soils, where they continue to cycle nitrogen, iron and trace gases.

## Terraforming and human colonization

Terraforming of Aialon was initiated by automated, continuous-thrust "torpedoes" launched from earlier industrial civilizations closer to the galactic core, and was then carried on by industrial machines over millennia. These unmanned vehicles:

- Delivered volatiles and organics to the surface and atmosphere.
- Seeded engineered microbial consortia designed to fix nitrogen, produce oxygen and stabilize soils.
- Deployed orbital mirrors and aerosols to modulate albedo and trigger controlled warming.
- Occasionally impacted deliberately to release energy, melt ice and drive large-scale geochemical change.

Phase-one torpedoes largely did not decelerate, acting as high-velocity impactors; later waves inserted into orbit or soft-landed infrastructure packages and factories.

**Oxygen.** Building the atmosphere's oxygen (4.3×10¹⁹ mol, 1.4×10¹⁸ kg) and burying the matching reduced carbon (5.1×10¹⁷ kg, a layer about 0.9 m thick over the whole planet) was an industrial task. Biological burial alone at Earth's productivity would take 0.4–4 million years, so automated splitters and orbital collectors did it faster: about 2.0×10²⁵ J, or 6.4×10¹⁴ W over 1,000 years (0.4% of the starlight Aialon intercepts, from about 2.3×10¹² m² of 20%-efficient collectors, a 1,500-km square); a 100-year campaign would need 4% of the intercepted light and a 4,800-km square.

Once the atmosphere reached near-breathable conditions and liquid water became widespread, human and post-human settlers arrived. Early colonists lived in UV-shielded domes and subsurface habitats while the ozone layer and biosphere matured. Over centuries, the environment was tuned to near-Earth standards, balancing oxygen production, carbon sequestration and climate control. Aialon later became the staging ground for the terraforming and colonization of [[Valen]], exporting technology and biological packages.

## Moon and tides

Aialon has a single small natural satellite on a prograde orbit at 250,000 km, about 12% of the planet's Hill radius and well outside its Roche limit (about 18,200 km for a rocky body). It is a small rocky body of about 1.0×10²² kg (0.19% of Aialon's mass, 0.14 lunar masses) with a radius of about 930 km (rocky density 3.0 g/cm³) and a surface gravity of about 0.78 m/s². It orbits in 15.4 days and appears 0.43° across (about 80% of the Moon's apparent size from Earth). It provides:

- Regular, moderate **ocean tides**: about 0.17 m of equilibrium lunar tide (roughly half Earth's lunar tide), with the star adding about 0.05 m, which aids coastal nutrient mixing.
- Weak but non-negligible **tidal heating** in shallow seas and estuaries.
- Long-term **stabilization of axial tilt**. Without a moon, Aialon's spin precession constant would be about 4.6 arcsec per year (a 285,000-year period), within reach of the planets' secular frequencies of about 1 arcsec per year, with chaotic tilt a risk. The moon adds about 15 arcsec per year, bringing the total to about 19, well clear of those frequencies, so the 15° tilt stays steady over millions of years. A smaller moon would not stabilize the tilt.

## Transportation and infrastructure

Aialon is the central transport hub of the Thrírlitir system. Key features include:

- High-capacity **orbital elevators and tether systems** in equatorial regions.
- Synchronous-orbit logistics stations that coordinate traffic to [[Valen]], [[Ceraun]] and outer system destinations.
- Frequent continuous-thrust transfer routes between Aialon and Valen, enabled by Aialon's relatively low escape velocity and mature industrial base. The two worlds are 4.0–8.5 AU apart, with a conjunction every 1.012 years (the Valen–Aialon synodic period); a trip takes 59–83 days at 0.01 g of continuous thrust and 19–26 days at 0.1 g.

These links make Aialon the economic and cultural anchor of the Valenic sphere.

## Cultural significance

The name "Aialon" is a truncation of "Gaialon", meaning "second Gaia", coined during the early planning stages of the terraforming project. Over time the shorter form became standard.

In Valenic culture, Aialon is regarded as both homeworld and proof-of-concept: a demonstration that entire planets can be reshaped. "Origin world" refers to the Valenic civilization, not necessarily the origin of humanity or of its precursor industrial cultures. Its pale blue-white sky, violet-tinted dawns and lavender forests are common motifs in art, religion and literature. The bright second suns of the B+C pair, the 1.742-year year, the 53.7-day binary cycle and the 11.60-year stellar cycle are the named cycles of its calendar. Despite later expansion to Valen and beyond, Aialon remains the political and symbolic heart of the civilization.

## Physical basis

**Orbit and seasons.** The B+C pair 7 AU away drives Aialon's instantaneous eccentricity to about 0.06–0.12 (rarely 0.18) even from a circular start, an effect that scales as (a/R)³ (8 times smaller with the pair at 14 AU, 64 times smaller at 28 AU). Total stellar flux over 10-day samples typically spans 0.87–1.25 Earth flux (extremes 0.77–1.42) with a mean of 0.97–1.04, and the orbit's axis precesses with a period of about 210 years. Seasons are therefore orbit-driven (perihelion summers, century-scale cycles), with the 15° tilt secondary. A's moist-greenhouse threshold for an F8 star is about 1.05 Earth flux and runaway about 1.17, so the peak flux is carried by ocean thermal inertia.

**Moon.** A small moon of about 1.0×10²² kg (0.19% of Aialon's mass, radius about 930 km at a rocky density of 3.0 g/cm³, surface gravity 0.78 m/s²) orbits at 250,000 km (12% of the Hill radius; Roche limit about 18,200 km) with a period of 15.4 days and an apparent diameter of 0.43°. Its equilibrium tide is about 0.17 m (Earth's lunar tide is about 0.36 m) and the star adds about 0.05 m. The spin-precession constant is 4.6 arcsec/yr without a moon (a period of 285,000 yr, near the planets' secular frequencies of about 1 arcsec/yr) and about 19 with this moon (14.8 from the moon, 4.6 from the star).

**Sky, UV and atmosphere.** The B+C pair adds 0.008–0.021 Earth flux (800–2,100 lux, magnitude about −22), so nights are as bright as a heavy overcast day. A emits, per unit bolometric flux, 1.38 times the Sun's UV-B, 1.57 times its UV-C, 1.24 times its UV-A and 1.12 times its blue (Wien peak 464 nm). The dry atmosphere is 77% N₂, 20% O₂, 2.96% Ar and 0.04% CO₂ at 1.1 bar (O₂ partial pressure 0.22 bar, Earth-normal), with a mass of 5.6×10¹⁸ kg.

**Terraforming.** The oxygen inventory is 4.3×10¹⁹ mol (1.36×10¹⁸ kg) and the matching reduced carbon is 5.1×10¹⁷ kg (a layer about 0.9 m thick over the planet). Biological burial alone at Earth's productivity (0.13–0.5% of 8×10¹⁵ mol/yr) would take 0.4–4 Myr, so the campaign was industrial: 2.0×10²⁵ J, which is 6.4×10¹⁴ W over 1,000 years (0.4% of intercepted starlight, 2.3×10¹² m² of 20%-efficient collectors) or 6.4×10¹⁵ W over 100 years (4%, a 4,800-km square). The delivery of volatiles by automated torpedoes from earlier civilizations nearer the galactic core is setting canon.

**Travel and cycles.** Aialon and Valen are 4.0–8.5 AU apart with a synodic period of 1.012 years; continuous-thrust trips take 59–83 days at 0.01 g and 19–26 days at 0.1 g. Aialon's named cycles are its year (1.742 years), the 53.7-day binary cycle and the 11.60-year stellar cycle.

**Limits.** The flux and eccentricity numbers come from the N-body screen of the stars and inner planets (10-day samples, random phases); precession constants are scaled from Earth's with a dynamical ellipticity scaled as ω²R³/M; the planets' secular frequencies are rough estimates; the UV and ozone consequences are ratios, not photochemistry, and the terraforming figures use assumed efficiencies, not a climate model. The torpedo-terraforming premise and the earlier industrial civilizations are setting canon, to be harmonized with the wider Aquatikiverse history.

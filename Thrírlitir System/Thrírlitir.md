# Thrírlitir

**Thrírlitir** is a hierarchical triple star system composed of an F-type primary (Thrírlitir A) and a close G+M binary pair (Thrírlitir B and Thrírlitir C). Together, the three stars host a rich planetary system that includes the terraformed world [[Aialon]] around A, the circumbinary planets [[Valen]] and [[Aphrod]] around B+C, the distant frozen ice giant [[Mot]] orbiting the system barycenter, and the close-in dwarf planets [[Pekhám]] (around C) and [[Barzél]] (around B).

The system’s total age is estimated at around 2 billion years, with all three stars on the main sequence. Thrírlitir is metal-rich enough to support efficient planet formation, consistent with the observed diversity of terrestrial and giant planets.

## System Architecture
Main article: [[Thrírlitir System]]

Thrírlitir A is the most massive and luminous component. The G-type star Thrírlitir B and the M-type star Thrírlitir C form a tight binary subsystem. The B+C pair orbits a common barycenter with a separation of ~0.3 AU and a period of 53.7 days, while the barycenter of this pair orbits A at ~7 AU on a long-period (~11.60 year) orbit.

This hierarchy produces three distinct dynamical regimes:

- **Inner S-type zone around A**, hosting the hot Jupiter [[Ceraun]] and the terraformed terrestrial world [[Aialon]]. Close-in S-type zones around B and C hold the dwarf planets [[Barzél]] and [[Pekhám]].
- **Circumbinary zone around B+C**, hosting [[Valen]] and [[Aphrod]].
- **Outer circumsystem zone**, where [[Mot]] orbits all three stars at ~117 AU.

The adopted configuration has passed N-body screening (10⁶ years, see [[Thrírlitir System#Stability]]), and yields significantly different illumination and seasonal patterns for planets around A versus planets around B+C.

Thrírlitir A is an F-type main-sequence star with a mass of about 1.3 M☉ and a luminosity of roughly 2.5 times that of the Sun. Its effective temperature is around 6250 K, placing it in the F8 V class: hotter, brighter, and whiter than the Sun, with enhanced output in the blue and ultraviolet.

With a radius near 1.35 R☉, Thrírlitir A has:

- A **conservative habitable zone** extending approximately from 1.5 to 2.7 AU.
- A **stellar flux** at 1.58 AU of about 1.0 F🜨 from A alone, where [[Aialon]] orbits; B+C make the flux swing between about 0.87 and 1.25 F🜨 over each 53.7-day binary period.
- Stronger **UV emission** than the Sun at comparable flux, which has shaped the design of Aialon’s terraformed ozone layer and biosphere.

The planetary architecture around A is:

- [[Ceraun]], a tidally locked hot Jupiter at 0.1 AU, receiving ~250 times Earth’s insolation.
- [[Aialon]], a slightly smaller-than-Earth terrestrial planet in the habitable zone, now fully terraformed.

Thrírlitir A’s main-sequence lifetime is expected to be somewhat shorter than the Sun’s, but still on the order of several billion years. At the system’s present age (~2 Gyr), A is a stable main-sequence star with modest activity and a stellar wind strong enough to shape exospheres and ion tails (notably Ceraun’s).

Thrírlitir B is a G8 V main-sequence star with a mass of about 0.85 M☉ and a luminosity of 0.566 L☉. Its effective temperature is around 5350 K, making it slightly cooler and redder than the Sun. The stellar radius is ~0.88 R☉.

If isolated, B’s habitable zone would lie inward of 1 AU, but in the actual system its role is as one component of the B+C binary. Together with C, it contributes most of the visible light in the circumbinary region where [[Valen]] and [[Aphrod]] reside.

Key properties:

- Dominant contributor to the **visible-band flux** at the orbits of Valen and Aphrod (central wavelengths ~600–700 nm).
- Provides a relatively **quiet solar-like spectrum**, favorable for conventional photochemistry and human habitability.
- Combined with C, yields a composite stellar spectrum which Aphrod’s native biosphere and Valen’s introduced biosphere exploit via dual-band photosynthetic pigments.

Thrírlitir C is an early M-type main-sequence star (M1 V) with a mass of about 0.40 M☉, a luminosity of 0.0405 L☉, an effective temperature of ~3800 K, and a radius of ~0.464 R☉. It is much dimmer than B in visible light but contributes significantly in the red and near-infrared.

C orbits B at a separation of ~0.3 AU; both stars circle their common barycenter in 53.7 days. From the perspective of a circumbinary planet:

- C appears as a **smaller, orange-red sun**, often offset in the sky from the brighter G-type primary.
- Its output is critical in the **near-IR range** (~800–1100 nm), which drives many of the phototrophic systems on [[Aphrod]] and [[Valen]].
- The presence of C adds a **secondary spectral peak** that life can tune to, resulting in dual-band pigment systems and complex daylight cycles.

Close to C lies the carbon-rich dwarf planet [[Pekhám]], which orbits at 0.05 AU (6.46 days) and is subject to intense heating, making it one of the darkest and hottest small bodies in the Thrírlitir system. [[Barzél]] orbits B at the same distance (4.43 days).

## Binary Pair B+C

The B+C subsystem behaves as a single effective source of 0.6065 L☉ for circumbinary orbits, with a period of 53.7 days. For [[Valen]] (0.80 AU) the B+C flux ranges from 0.82 to 1.18 F🜨 and recurs every 69.7 days (the beat between the binary phase and Valen's orbit); with A's ~0.05 and the orbit's own breathing the total spans about 0.82–1.48 F🜨 (mean about 1.0). For [[Aphrod]] (1.0 AU) the B+C flux ranges 0.535–0.721 F🜨, plus 0.051 from A (mean about 0.66). These are top-of-atmosphere fluxes perpendicular to the incoming light, for circular coplanar orbits and point-source stars.

The B+C circumbinary critical radius (Holman–Wiegert, e = 0.01) is 0.730 AU, so Valen at 0.80 AU lies at 1.10 times it and Aphrod at 1.37 times it. Valen's semimajor axis keeps clear of the binary's 4:1 resonance at 0.757 AU. See [[Thrírlitir System#Stability]].

## A–(B+C) Triple Configuration

The barycenter of the B+C pair orbits Thrírlitir A at ~7 AU with a period of ~11.60 years. The triple system is hierarchically stable: the inner binary (B+C) is much tighter than the outer orbit around A, providing a hierarchical architecture whose planetary stability is screened, but not proven, in [[Thrírlitir System#Adopted stellar orbit and stability screening|the system review]].

From the viewpoint of planets:

- Worlds around A (e.g. [[Aialon]]) see B+C as a bright double star, slowly moving against the background over an 11.60-year cycle.
- Worlds around B+C (e.g. [[Valen]] and [[Aphrod]]) see A as a distant, bright F-type sun contributing a small but non-negligible fraction of their total flux:
  - At Valen, A adds ~0.05 F🜨 (about 5% of the B+C flux) to the total insolation.
  - At Aphrod, A contributes on the order of ~0.05 F🜨, enough to slightly warm its climate and influence its upper-atmosphere photochemistry.

This architecture allows the Thrírlitir system to host multiple habitable or potentially habitable planets in distinct orbital regimes.

## Evolution and Future

All three stars are currently on the main sequence:

- Thrírlitir A, at 1.3 M☉, will exhaust core hydrogen on a timescale somewhat shorter than the Sun’s, eventually evolving into a subgiant and red giant, which will disrupt the inner planetary system.
- Thrírlitir B, at 0.85 M☉, will remain on the main sequence for much longer than A, outliving it by many billions of years.
- Thrírlitir C, as a 0.4 M☉ M dwarf, will be a stable, slowly evolving star for hundreds of billions of years.

The long main-sequence lifetimes of B and especially C mean that circumbinary worlds like Valen and Aphrod occupy a habitable zone that will persist long after A has left the main sequence, although the detailed dynamics of the system during and after A’s red-giant phase remain an active subject of modeling.

## Cultural Significance

For the inhabitants of [[Aialon]] and [[Valen]], Thrírlitir’s three suns are central to cosmology, navigation, and timekeeping:

- The triple system defines natural **cycles** (daily, the 53.7-day binary cycle, Valen's 0.640-year year, Aialon's 1.742-year year, and the ~11.60-year stellar cycle) used in calendars and agriculture.
- The distinct visual colors of A, B, and C give rise to rich **symbolic associations** and artistic motifs.
- The stability and multiplicity of habitable zones within the same stellar system are often cited in philosophical arguments about contingency and design in the broader Aquatikiverse.

## Physical basis

**Luminosities.** L = R² (T / 5,772 K)⁴ gives 2.505 L☉ for A and 0.572 L☉ for B, against the adopted 2.505 and 0.566. C's radius of 0.464 R☉ makes its radius, temperature (3,800 K) and adopted luminosity (0.0405 L☉) agree.

**System extent.** The outer boundary of about 1.5 light-years is the effective extent of the system's comet cloud and debris, not a tidal Hill radius (the rough Galactic tidal radius for 2.55 M☉ is about 6 light-years).

**Limits.** Flux values assume circular, coplanar orbits and point-source stars.

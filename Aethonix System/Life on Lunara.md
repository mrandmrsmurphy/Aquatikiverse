**Life on Lunara** is an exotic, low-light biosphere that thrives not on sunlight, but on chemical gradients and ionizing radiation. Hosted by the tidally heated moon [[Lunara (moon)]] of the sub-Neptune [[Noctilux]], it is built on water–ammonia brines, carbon-based macromolecules, and a dense nitrogen–hydrogen–carbon dioxide atmosphere, with methane and ammonia traces; usable oxidants are localized or biologically stored. Panspermia-delivered extremophiles seeded the world roughly a billion years ago, and a later “Radiogenic Cambrian Explosion” transformed its once-microbial chemistry into a three-dimensional web of chemosynthetic and radiotrophic macro-life. Dominant guilds include towering vent colonies, drifting predator zeppeliforms known as Scorchers, and hexapodal generalists called Janusthropes, whose sensory world is defined by radiation gradients, magnetic fields, and vibration alongside specialized optical/bioluminescent tracking rather than dependence on ordinary daylight.

## Origins
Life on Lunara began approximately one billion years ago, seeded not by native abiogenesis but by panspermia. A rain of comets, rich in carbonaceous material and extremophilic lifeforms from a distant system, delivered the first organisms: anaerobic, metabolically versatile microbes adapted to cold, brine-rich environments. These colonists established themselves slowly in geothermal fissures, ammonia-rich melt zones, and deep briny pockets, where chemosynthetic metabolisms allowed them to endure.

For the first 250 million years, the biosphere remained microbially simple. Metabolic activity centered on hydrogen, sulfur, and methane redox cycles. Anaerobic decomposers and methanotrophs stabilized Lunara's atmosphere, maintaining trace methane, ammonia vapor, and a developing dense atmosphere whose later hydrogen-bearing state requires continued geochemical supply and loss balancing.

## The Radiogenic Cambrian
Around 750 million years ago, a tectonic and geochemical upheaval dramatically expanded the biosphere. Tidal fracturing, brine infiltration, and lithospheric overturn exposed uranium-, thorium-, and potassium-rich zones in Lunara's subsurface. Radiolysis of water and crustal reactions liberated hydrogen, radicals, and heat—transforming marginal microbial niches into dynamic radiogenic oases.

This triggered a "Radiogenic Cambrian Explosion": the sudden appearance of multicellular, structurally complex life that could exploit high-intensity but transient resource blooms. Radiolysis-fed chemistry, local resource exposure and possibly supplemental direct radiotrophy, in tandem with pre-existing geochemical metabolism, enabled the rapid diversification of macroorganisms.

## Energy Regimes
Lunaran biology is built around two primary energy strategies:
- **Chemosynthesis:** Based on redox reactions involving H₂, H₂S, Fe(II)/Fe(III), CH₄, and NH₃. Stable but spatially and temporally constrained.
- **Radiotrophy:** In radiogenic bloom zones, organisms chiefly exploit radiolytically produced hydrogen and separated oxidants through symbioses and chemical metabolisms. Melanin-like direct harvesting remains a possible supplemental adaptation, not a proven self-sufficient source for large animals. Fluid access, freshly exposed radionuclides and nutrient transport make usable bloom resources intermittent; radioactive decay itself is not seasonally switched on and off.

The two regimes overlap in complex ways. Some organisms specialize; others adaptively shift between them.

## Major Guilds
### Basal Chemotrophs ("Planters")
Sessile or mat-forming organisms that occupy stable chemosynthetic zones:
- Sulfur and methane oxidizers
- Brine mat colonies and vent-stalk clusters
- Tube-dwelling symbiotic worms

### Radiotrophic Opportunists ("Bloomers")
Lifeforms adapted to radiogenic surges, blooming explosively and dying quickly:
- Gas-vesicle swarmers
- Melanin-skinned floaters
- Rapidly-growing fungi-like colonizers
- Raptorial feeders tracking radiation gradients

### Apex Predators
**Scorchers (Predator Zeppeliforms)**: Massive, gas-buoyant predators that drift between bloom zones, sensing radiation with dorsal chromatophores. Feed on bloomers and smaller floaters. They maintain separate hydrogen-fuel and concentrated-oxidant reservoirs and mix them in protected reaction chambers for brief hot-gas pulses. Peroxide-rich stores or other chemically maintained oxidants are candidate biological mechanisms. Recharging, ignition/control, tissue protection and waste heat must be paid for; the surrounding reducing air is not assumed to sustain an open flame by itself.

### Mesofauna ("Pedestrians")
Organisms active in mid-energy zones:
- Slugs and brinewalkers
- Leech-like scavengers
- Detritovorous troughworms
- Arthropoidal tunnelers with magnetic and vibrational senses

### Dual-Substrate Omnivores ("Janusthropes")
The most adaptive and ecologically dominant creatures, capable of surviving in both energy regimes:
- Tend chemosynthetic "gardens"
- Harvest bloomers during surges
- Use six multijointed limbs for crawling, climbing, swimming
- Possess six eyes: two front (high resolution), two dorsal (radiation), two ventral (ground tracking)
- Display early signs of sociality, spatial memory, and tool use

These generalists are considered analogous to early mammals: slow-breeding, mobile, and behaviorally plastic.

## Sensory Evolution
Due to Lunara's three-dimensional habitats—dense air, briny lakes, vertical geothermal gradients—organisms evolve spherical perception rather than flat-plane awareness:
- Coarse radiation-gradient/dose detection with shielding and repair; not automatically optical-resolution image formation
- Magnetic-field orientation/mapping, distinct from electroreception
- Pressure-sensing organs for brine-borne vibrations
- Advanced chemotaxis for detecting blooms, corpses, or geysers
- Bioluminescence for signaling and local illumination, with separate photoreceptors receiving the light

The dominant body plan among mobile generalists is hexapodal, dome-bodied, with manipulators, float bladders, and independently orienting eye-stalks. Their attention and body orientation are often **gradient-facing**: they orient to resource and hazard gradients while still learning geographic routes and using directional sensory organs.

## Evolutionary Trajectory
The current biosphere is stable but dynamic, shaped by episodic bloom events and long interludes of low activity. Janusthropes and other dual-adapted forms represent a major evolutionary success. They remain in an analog stage to Earth’s early mammals—social, mobile, intelligent—but not yet civilization-forming.

Further evolution of symbolic thought, abstract memory, or permanent tool infrastructure remains a question for future epochs.

The warm278 K mean remains a climate target of the 20-bar reference, with local cold brines and pressurized hot vents. A planetwide 0.05 atm of free O₂ is not assumed; hydrogen/oxygen production, rapid recombination, mineral sinks and biological transport must support separate local oxidant inventories. OH•is an extremely reactive intermediate, not a long-distance stored food source. Sulfate, iron and carbon pairs need complementary reactants and a balanced fixation/waste budget. 

Towers/Planters and vent mats anchor dependable food webs. Bloomers and their predators exploit ephemeral access to stored geochemical/radiolytic resources. High biomass is concentrated in vents, caverns and bloom fields; extensive drifting regions between them are sparse and low-activity. Radionuclide amounts, absorbed dose, conversion efficiency, damage repair and mass transport must sustain the full trophic budget. Radiation shielding is an adaptation with material/energy costs, not a guarantee of arbitrarily high safe energy uptake.

Dense air and low gravity are favorable to flotation, but lifting gas, tissue/envelope mass, leaks, gas exchange, turbulence and thermal changes still constrain Scorcher/zeppeliform sizes. Their hydrogen-fuel organ is distinct from any lifting bladder. Surface-fluid and aerial buoyancy are different mechanisms.

Janusthropes retain six limbs, dome bodies, manipulators, float bladders, independently orienting eye stalks and six specialized eyes: two forward high-resolution trackers, two dorsal radiation-associated detectors and two ventral substrate trackers. They tend chemical gardens, harvest bloomers, navigate/remember space and use simple tools. “No intelligent species” is replaced by **no demonstrated sapient civilization**; their animal cognition and early tool use are retained. The early-mammal analogy describes behavior rather than a necessary ladder toward humanlike civilization. Future symbolism, abstract cognition and permanent tool infrastructure remain open.

## Physical basis

The energy accounting, atmosphere and warmth requirement for the biosphere are on [[Lunara]] under Physical basis. Radiolysis and rock reactions create separated reactants, with useful oxidants held in fluids, mineral interfaces or biological stores; direct melanin-like radiotrophy is supplementary and speculative. Total radionuclide inventories, usable yields, dose damage, repair and transport must support the ecological productivity. The Janusthropes are intelligent animals without a demonstrated sapient civilization.

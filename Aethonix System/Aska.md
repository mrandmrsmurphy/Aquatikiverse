**Aska** is a superheated terrestrial exoplanet orbiting a tight G0V+K4V binary star system at a distance of 0.6 astronomical units (AU). The system’s nominal habitable zone is estimated to span roughly 1.24–2.18 AU, placing Aska well interior to the inner edge. Its extreme atmosphere and geological activity have transformed it into a "Super-Venus" — an uninhabitable world dominated by supercritical fluids, acid storms, and hyperactive volcanism.

## Overview
Aska resides in a P-type orbit around a close binary pair, composed of a 1.1 solar-mass G0V primary and a 0.7 solar-mass K4V secondary. The binary separation is only 0.15 AU, and the stars orbit each other every 15.8 days. The combined stellar output is approximately 1.7 L☉, resulting in a solar flux at Aska's orbit of ~4.7 S⊕.

Despite orbiting a system with a broad habitable zone, Aska itself is rendered completely uninhabitable by runaway greenhouse conditions, dense supercritical fluids, and a surface temperature exceeding 1200 °C.

## Atmosphere
Aska’s atmosphere is among the most extreme of any terrestrial planet discovered. It is vertically stratified into three distinct layers:

### Lower Atmosphere
- Dominated by dense supercritical fluids: CO₂ and H₂O  
- Traces of organofluorines and high-pressure reaction products  
- Temperatures exceed 1000 °C  
- Nearly opaque to infrared radiation, effectively trapping heat  

### Middle Atmosphere
- Rich in SO₂ aerosols and sulfur clouds  
- Also contains residual halocarbons, COS, and CF₄  
- Some scattering, limited optical reflectivity  

### Upper Atmosphere
- Thin H₂O vapor, CF₄, COS, CH₃Cl, trace halocarbons  
- Cooler (~300–500 °C), low albedo  
- Allows significant solar radiation to penetrate

## Geology and Internal Structure
Aska is characterized by extreme internal activity and a thin, unstable crust prone to frequent overturn.

### Interior Structure
| Layer | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Core | Small Fe-S rich core (20–25% of mass); weak magnetosphere |
| Mantle | Massive silicate mantle (~70–75%); enriched in radioactive elements; drives extreme volcanism |
| Crust | Thin and tectonically unstable; frequent resurfacing |


Surface conditions include:
- Semi-molten basalt plains  
- Lava lakes and rift zones  
- Global lightning megastorms  
- Halogen- and sulfur-bearing cloud precipitation aloft, with hot lower-layer evaporation and chemical processing  

## Evolutionary History
Aska is believed to have undergone catastrophic thermal evolution driven in part by biological activity during its early epochs:

### Early Stage
- Atmosphere rich in volcanic and biological gases: CO₂, CH₄, SO₂, H₂O  
- Cool upper atmosphere; sulfuric and water vapor clouds  
- High albedo, partial temperature moderation  

### Middle Stage
- CH₄ photodissociation produced organic hazes (brief Titan-like phase)  
- Oceans began to evaporate  
- CO and halocarbons accumulated  
- Cloud layer thinned, albedo dropped  

### Late Stage: Runaway Greenhouse
- Oceans fully vaporized  
- Supercritical water formed a dense lower atmosphere  
- Surface exceeded 1000 °C  
- Outgoing thermal radiation limited by the opaque hot CO₂/H₂O mixture; this is not a superfluid  
- Atmospheric structure stratified and self-reinforcing  

## Absence of Current Life
No present biosphere is part of the adopted setting. An ancient microbial biosphere remains possible rather than established. Past microbial activity likely contributed to greenhouse gas accumulation (e.g., CH₄, halocarbons), ultimately accelerating the transition to current extreme conditions.

## Extreme greenhouse reference

The natural Super-Venus state retains 1200–1300 °C (~1473–1573 K) at about 800 atm. A volatile-inventory reference is approximately 0.05% of planetary mass in atmosphere-accessible volatiles: P×4πR²/g is roughly 7.44×10²¹ kg at the stated pressure, radius and gravity. It need not consist entirely of water; CO₂ and steam share the inventory. Direct-irradiation equilibrium is only about 388 K at Bond albedo 0.2, so the surface temperature is a deep-atmosphere greenhouse target, not the effective emitting temperature. A multi-component radiative/convective/chemical profile must reproduce that target; opacity does not create additional energy.

Dense lower CO₂/H₂O, sulfur-rich intermediate chemistry and a more transmissive upper layer remain the vertical design. The greenhouse-table 700–1200-°C interval describes cooler sampled layers or stages rather than redefining the 1200–1300-°C surface reference. CF₄, COS, CH₃Cl and other organics require appropriate formation/removal pathways; old biology is a possible ancestral source of chemical reservoirs, not proof that individual molecules survived unchanged. Core20–25%/mantle70–75% are correlated composition ranges with crust/remainder, not independently summable maxima. A small Fe–S core alone does not prove a weak dynamo.

The rotation convention is 3:2 in angular frequency, equivalently a rotation period two-thirds of the orbital year. It is an adopted tidal-state reference; circumbinary capture and long-term maintenance have not been integrated.

## Physical basis

Aska's 800-atm, 1200–1300 °C target is not cooled merely because the irradiation equilibrium is about 388 K. A nominal pressure-supported volatile inventory is of order 7.44×10²¹ kg (about 0.050% of the planet's mass), with composition and radiation chemistry still to be solved. Volatile-rich runaway-greenhouse calculations reach surface temperatures above 1500 K, a precedent for the target that does not validate Aska's exact state ([Kasting 1988, runaway and moist greenhouse atmospheres](https://www.pku-atmos-acm.org/static/pdfs/Runaway%20and%20moist%20greenhouse%20atmospheres%20and%20the%20evolution%20of%20Earth%20and%20Venus.pdf)). Acid rain falls in compatible cooler layers and evaporates or reacts before the hottest surface. The 84.35-day rotation is 3:2 in spin frequency, not in rotation period, and long-term capture is unmodeled. The orbital screens are on [[Aethonix System]] under Physical basis.

**Tsurai** is a massive cold sub-Neptune or failed gas giant located in the [[Aethonix System]], orbiting a close binary composed of a G0V primary and a K4V secondary star. With 25 Earth masses and a radius near 4.85 Earth radii, Tsurai is a cold sub-Neptune with a substantial heavy-element interior. Failure to undergo runaway gas accretion is an interpretation of its formation, not uniquely inferred from its present mass/radius. It is surrounded by a dense and complex ring system and hosts more than a hundred catalogued satellites when inner moonlets and outer irregulars are included, including several major ones with exotic atmospheres, active interiors, and seasonal photochemistry.

## Physical Characteristics
Tsurai’s heavy-element interior mass is assigned a reference of 19.5 M⊕, including silicate rock and pressure-dependent water/ammonia/carbon-bearing phases rather than a universally frozen small core. A modest hydrogen–helium envelope of ~5.5 M⊕ overlays this dense core, resulting in a bulk density of 1.21 g/cm³—consistent with a dense sub-Neptune interior family rather than a unique comparison with every gas giant. The planet's modest surface gravity (1.06 g⊕) and equilibrium temperature (~86 K) permit a persistent stratified atmosphere with seasonal hazes and cloud decks.

Tsurai is visually striking. Its atmosphere is composed of vertically distinct zones: high-altitude orange haze layers composed of photochemically derived tholins and polymeric nitriles; mid-altitude layers where methane absorption produces a deep cyan coloration; and lower cloud decks with hydrogen sulfide and other sulfur-bearing aerosols producing a murky greenish layer. This produces a tiered banding structure—red above, blue in the middle, and green near the cloud base—easily distinguishable in visible wavelengths.

The planet’s rotation is rapid, driving atmospheric banding and storm systems, with equatorial jets producing thin streaks of convective turbulence. Aurorae occasionally illuminate the polar latitudes in faint red and violet hues, excited by interactions with the magnetic field and ring-derived plasma. At the poles, seasonal cold-trapping of nitriles produces episodic red polar caps, not of ice but of concentrated paracyanogen smog.

Tsurai's ring system lies close to the planet, extending from roughly 1.5 to 7.5 planetary radii (~46,350–231,750 km) from the centre, distributed near the equatorial plane, and is composed of fine-grained carbonaceous and silicate dust. Low reflectivity and high density give the rings a sharply etched appearance. Several narrow arcs and gaps are sculpted by embedded shepherd moons, while vertical warping in outer regions may indicate past collisions or inclined perturbers.

## Atmosphere
The planet's atmosphere is chemically rich and photochemically active:
- **Methane (CH₄, 2–3%)**: Deep absorber responsible for the blue midbands.
- **Ammonia (NH₃, ~0.1%)**: Pale cloud decks near the tropopause.
- **Hydrogen Sulfide (H₂S, ~0.05%)**: Forms greenish clouds in the deeper troposphere.
- **Phosphine (PH₃, trace)**: Reddish-brown mid-cloud colorant, deep atmospheric tracer.
- **Cyanogen (C₂N₂, ~700 ppm)**: Crucial to polymer haze formation; reacts to UV and cosmic ray inputs.
- **Acetylene and Diacetylene (C₂H₂, C₄H₂, trace)**: Contribute to yellow-orange limb coloration.
- **Hydrogen Cyanide (HCN, trace)**: Toxic but photochemically significant byproduct.
- **Paracyanogen ((C₂N₂)ₙ, episodic)**: Causes reddish haze and polar seasonal deposition.

Tholin aerosols and high-altitude haze layers scatter red–orange light and reduce visibility at limb angles. The chemistry and photolysis rates are dominated by UV from the binary suns, with irradiation changing according to actual binary distances, geometry and eclipses; projected conjunction does not create additional luminosity or make their spectra begin overlapping only then.

## Moon System
Tsurai hosts an extensive and hierarchically structured system of moons, consistent with both in-situ formation and captured populations.

### Inner Moonlets
Within the main ring region orbit dozens of shepherd and debris moons, typically under 100 km in diameter. These moons confine the dusty rings and produce wave structures, arcs, and asymmetric clumps. Repeated microcollisions generate regolith clouds that replenish ring material.

### Major Moons
Tsurai’s six named major moons use explicit planet-centred axes in the adopted table below. Their listed kilometre sizes are radii, and literal 0.1–0.5 AU-equivalent axes would partly exceed the ~0.289 AU Hill scale:
- **Aurenne** (~2600 km): A dense, Titan-like moon with a CH₄/N₂ atmosphere, orange haze, and hydrocarbon lakes and rivers. Surface temperatures hover near methane’s triple point, supporting an active methane cycle.
- **Halego** (~1800 km): Reddish-brown desert world with extensive tholin fields, dark polar deposits, and dry methane-rich basins. May contain evaporite salt crusts from dried hydrocarbon seas.
- **Mizari** (~1600 km): Tectonically active with cryovolcanic rifts, ammonia plumes, and venting of nitrile-rich gases such as C₂N₂ and HCN. Ice fractures suggest ongoing tidal heating.
- **Keltra** (~1000 km): Geologically quiescent; covered in ancient highlands with frost-coated regolith and a high albedo.
- **Iphrix** (~1200 km): Uses a ~30°inclined reference orbit, screened under stated assumptions; features green-tinged ice veils, high-latitude caps, and a seasonally shifting terminator line.
- **Droven** (~700 km): Retrograde and highly eccentric. Surface mix of ice and iron-rich rock suggests origin beyond Tsurai’s primordial disk.

### Inclined and Eccentric Moons
Moons in intermediate orbits include both perturbed native bodies and early capture candidates. Some exhibit chaotic tumbling, others exhibit orbital precession and seasonal illumination patterns. Surface spectra are varied, with signatures of hydrated silicates, complex organics, and nitrile ices.

### Outer Irregular Moons
Farther out orbit over 80 small irregular moons, many in retrograde orbits. These moons are likely remnants of an early outer planetesimal population, either captured during disk dispersal or during inward migration of Tsurai itself. Their surfaces are dark and ancient, showing few signs of resurfacing.

## Visual Appearance
From the surface of Halego or Keltra, Tsurai appears immense and richly banded. Its upper atmosphere glows in rusty red tones, beneath which a band of vibrant blue wraps around its equator, undercut by a sickly green limb where sulfurous clouds dominate. The rings can appear as sharp dark bands in appropriate geometry. Shadows on a moon occur during particular alignments; parallel stripes are not imposed permanently on every surface.

Twin suns cast dual shadows—hard-edged and golden from the G0 primary, soft and amber from the K4 companion. During conjunctions, the planetary limb glows with refracted orange light due to acetylene haze layers, and polar regions shimmer faintly with magnetic aurorae.

High-speed jet streams carve narrow bands into the planet’s mid-latitudes, while trailing convective clouds spiral around transient storm systems. The overall appearance is one of tiered complexity—layer upon layer of chemical, visual, and thermal contrast.

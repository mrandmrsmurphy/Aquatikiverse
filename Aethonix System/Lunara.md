**Lunara** is a large, geologically active moon orbiting the sub-Neptune [[Noctilux]] at a distance of approximately 400,000 kilometers. It is the primary site of complex biology in the outer [[Aethonix System]], supporting an exotic biosphere supported chiefly by geochemical and radiolysis-fed chemosynthesis, with supplemental speculative direct radiotrophy.

## Overview
Lunara is roughly half the size of Earth in radius and about 8–9% its mass, making it dense for a moon but well within the expected range for a silicate–metallic object with substantial internal heating. Its surface exhibits evidence of cryovolcanism, geothermal basins, and ammonia-rich brine pools, with active resurfacing over long timescales.

The moon uses a dense atmosphere (~20 bar) composed mainly of nitrogen, hydrogen and carbon dioxide, with minor methane and trace ammonia/other gases, providing the adopted greenhouse/redox setting for its biology, with actual chemical and radiative viability still to be modelled. The adopted warm mean near 5 °C is a greenhouse design target for the revised dense atmosphere. It has not been demonstrated by a radiative-transfer/escape model; internal heat chiefly sustains local geological and ecological gradients.

## Orbital Characteristics
Lunara's orbit around Noctilux is mildly eccentric (e ≈ 0.1), giving rise to significant tidal stress and associated geothermal activity. It orbits at a semimajor axis of ~400,000 km, completing one circuit in roughly 9.2 Earth days. The orbital plane is inclined by about 1.5° relative to Noctilux’s equatorial plane.

Strong tidal coupling has led to synchronous rotation: Lunara keeps one hemisphere permanently facing Noctilux. Tidal heating, coupled with radiogenic decay of U/Th/K isotopes within its mantle, supports extensive subsurface hydrothermal systems and long-lived internal activity.

## Internal Structure
Lunara possesses a differentiated structure:
- A differentiated metallic core beneath a radionuclide-bearing silicate mantle; U/Th/K are not assumed preferentially concentrated into an unlimited core reactor
- A silicate–oxide mantle with olivine, perovskite, and metasomatic vein networks
- A crust marked by alternating layers of basaltic flows, ammonia-rich lenses, and sedimentary tholin deposits

Subcrustal zones contain zeolites, uraninite, and amorphous silica, producing localized pressurized hydrothermal fluid temperatures in the range of 120–300 °C. These serve as ecological hotbeds and major sources of chemical energy for native life.

## Climate and Surface
Despite receiving only ~0.11 Earth insolation at Noctilux’s orbital distance, Lunara maintains a mean surface temperature around 5 °C, as a target of the hydrogen-bearing greenhouse, with internal heat supporting local vents and regional refugia. Its surface is heterogeneous:
- Dark, tholin-stained plains and dune-like deposits
- Cryobrine lakes and ammonia slush basins in low-lying basins
- Hematite-rich crusts from oxidized geyser and fumarole eruptions
- High-albedo reflective patches near polar fissures and cryovolcanic domes

Day–night contrasts are muted by atmospheric mixing and geothermal flux, with regional contrasts driven by terrain, circulation and vent distribution. The near-side brine/haze provinces and far-side tholin-dominated terrain remain geographic patterns, not a guaranteed consequence of weak parent irradiation.

## Biosphere
Main article: [[Life on Lunara]].

Lunara hosts a unique biosphere based on chemosynthesis and radiotrophy. The initial colonization was likely seeded by comet-borne extremophiles ultimately from a distant system; early system bodies may have been an intermediate carrier, with extremophiles adapting to cold, brine-rich environments. Roughly 750 million years ago, improved access to radiogenic crustal zones triggered a "Radiogenic Cambrian Explosion," giving rise to:
- Sessile chemosynthesizers anchored around vent fields and fracture zones
- Radiotrophic bloomers with pigment/mineral systems that manage radiation exposure, with radiolysis-fed symbionts and a possible supplemental direct energy-harvesting role
- Massive predator-floaters (e.g., “Scorchers”) that cruise brine layers and sheltered stratified dense-air pools
- Adaptive, omnivorous generalists (e.g., “Janusthropes”) capable of exploiting both surface and subsurface niches

Organisms are adapted to Lunara's three-dimensional habitats and sense through magnetoreception, vibrational mapping, and chemical plume detection alongside specialized optical/bioluminescent tracking, rather than relying only on conventional daylight vision. Many exhibit layered shielding tissues or mineralized skins to manage radiation and cold.

For a detailed treatment of the biosphere, see [[Life on Lunara]].

Total pressure: 20 bar. Fractions are a normalized reference dry mixture; variable water vapor and local/vertical chemistry require wet-gas renormalization. These are adopted climate inputs, not the output of a radiative-transfer calculation or a human exposure certification.

The reference is20 bar total: about 13 bar N₂,6 bar H₂ and 1 bar CO₂, with trace adjustment for methane/ammonia. Free O₂ is not retained at a global0.05 atm; oxidants are rapidly consumed in the reducing environment and concentrated in local fluids, reaction compartments or biological stores. This air is not human-breathable, and no human surface colony is assigned. Dense air helps a volumetric buoyant ecology while imposing actual gas-envelope, pressure and transport constraints.

At Bond albedo0.28, the globally absorbed stellar flux is about 26 W/m². An illustrative synchronous-tide model withk₂/Q=0.003 ande=0.1 supplies~0.38 W/m², useful for geology but not proof of a planetwide warm climate. A278-Ksurface radiates~339 W/m² as a blackbody; atmospheric greenhouse effects must reduce top-of-atmosphere loss to the absorbed-plus-internal budget. The difference is not automatically an internal-power requirement.

The molecular-hydrogen collision-induced greenhouse is a physically motivated mechanism, but20 bar and the mixture are adopted modelling inputs, not solved conditions. H₂ escape/replenishment, photochemistry, cloud opacity, ammonia stability and radiative/convective structure remain explicit open scientific limits. The radioisotope inventory, tidal dissipation and eccentricity maintenance are likewise not presumed permanent or limitless.

The near 278 K mean does not imply uniform temperature: cold elevated/reflective regions and phase-compatible brines host ice lenses/slush, while pressurized vents reach120–300 °C. Pure ammonia lakes or slush at the global mean are replaced by locally specified mixed water–ammonia brines and cold regional deposits. Coexisting phases must satisfy their own pressure/composition conditions. The 1-Gyr biosphere and 750-Myr transition remain; warmth and complex life are preserved as setting goals rather than replaced by a sterile frozen moon.

## Physical basis

At the source radius of 3200 km, mass 0.086 M⊕, axis 400,000 km and e = 0.1, Lunara's two-body period is 9.175 days. Global absorbed starlight is about 26 W/m² at a Bond albedo of 0.28, and the standard synchronous-tide sensitivity for k₂/Q = 0.003 gives about 0.38 W/m². Surface blackbody emission at 278 K is about 339 W/m², but the roughly 313 W/m² difference is not automatically an internal heat requirement: atmospheric insulation must bring actual top-of-atmosphere loss into balance. A grey Eddington optical-depth diagnostic gives the opacity scale required, not a computed molecular absorption, and it is not a solved climate. Hydrogen collision-induced absorption is a physical greenhouse mechanism outside a classical CO₂ habitable zone ([Pierrehumbert & Gaidos](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.0021); [Ramirez & Kaltenegger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.08618)), but neither those models nor this arithmetic establish that this low-gravity moon and mixture give 278 K, and radiative transfer, cloud chemistry and H₂ escape and replenishment remain unverified; no artificial heating system is assumed. Laboratory radiation-associated fungal growth ([Dadachova et al. 2007](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0000457)) does not demonstrate a self-sufficient megafaunal radiation ecosystem, and radiolytic hydrogen has an independent microbial-energy basis ([measured radiolytic H₂ yield](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016703704006271)).

**Rimeas** is the fifth planet from the binary stars of the [[Aethonix System]], located 1.728 AU from the system barycenter. It is a cold super-Earth with an estimated mass of 2.6 M⊕ and a radius of 1.65 R⊕. Although it lies technically within the system’s extended habitable zone, its harsh climate, marginal warmth, and industrial dominance place it on the fringe of long-term settlement. Rimeas is best known as the only known natural source of the rare isotopic mineral [[Glaciumite]], which drives its economic and strategic relevance despite its otherwise inhospitable conditions.

## Orbit and Rotation
Rimeas orbits the [[Aethonix]] binary in a low-inclination circumbinary path with a modest eccentricity (e ≈ 0.02), giving rise to weak seasonal cycles buffered by its dense atmosphere. Its orbital period is approximately 1.693 Earth years. The planet has a slow rotation period of ~50 hours and an axial tilt of 5–7°, resulting in modest but consistent diurnal and seasonal light variation. Polar climatic zones extend from the poles to roughly 30° latitude, giving the planet broad belts of glacial and periglacial terrain.

## Physical Characteristics
Rimeas has a surface gravity of 9.38 m/s² and a calculated density of ~3.19 g/cm³, suggesting a rocky composition with volatile-rich layers, including hydrated silicates and deep ice-bearing strata. The surface area of the planet is roughly 1.39 billion square kilometers, dominated by mountainous highlands, tectonic rift basins, and briny ice-covered lakes. Named geological features include the Threnic Ridge (a massive subpolar foldbelt) and the Kaldran Rift Zone, where most of the planet’s Glaciumite mining is concentrated.

## Atmosphere and Climate

Rimeas has a four-atmosphere nitrogen–argon-dominated envelope, with oxygen adjusted to a near-Earth partial-pressure reference rather than an Earthlike percentage at four times Earth's pressure. It remains an industrial pressure-and-breathing environment, not ordinary outdoor air made safe by a filtration mask.

## Hydrology
Approximately 40–50% of the surface is covered by liquid or frozen water, often trapped in subglacial lakes, briny fissures, or permafrost. Open water bodies exist primarily near equatorial rift basins during seasonal thaws. Precipitation takes the form of freezing rain, brine fog, or low-density snow. Terraforming initiatives have seeded several valley lakes with microbial biomass to test biosphere retention under native conditions.

## Moons
Rimeas has two natural satellites: **Esau**, a large moon roughly 1 lunar mass, and **Jacob**, a smaller companion at ~0.2 lunar mass. Both moons are inert and uninhabited. Their torques and tides contribute to the spin–orbit environment, without proving long-term obliquity stabilization solely from their masses. Esau is tidally locked and visible from the surface as a pale, cratered disk.

## Glaciumite
Main article: [[Glaciumite]].

The economic and strategic relevance of Rimeas stems from its rich subsurface deposits of Glaciumite, a violet-blue crystalline mineral composed of rare-earth silicates and fluorine compounds. Forming only in high-pressure, sulfur-rich brine aquifers under specific geothermal gradients, Glaciumite contains unusual Nd-isotope ratios and metastable charge/defect-ordered crystal matrices valuable for high-density data storage, waveform encryption, and compact shielding applications. Its yttrium is chiefly stable 89Y; useful Nd-isotope distributions and rare-earth site ordering are part of its optomagnetic role, not a set of magically stabilized Y radioisotopes.

Glaciumite emits low-frequency electromagnetic pulses in electric fields and is notoriously difficult to synthesize artificially. Bulk unshielded stockpiles can induce noise in magnetometers within a 3–5 meter radius, complicating navigation and survey work. Mining occurs in pressurized tunnels beneath ice domes using temperature-controlled rigs. Samples freshly exposed to air may emit faint blue corona discharges, a phenomenon nicknamed “the aurora bite.” Possession and export are tightly regulated under interstellar compacts, and smuggling carries Class-5 felony status under most treaty systems.

## Human Settlement
Rimeas was colonized in the mid-24th century by industrial expeditions under the Aethonix Colonial Development Accord. Despite the existence of three more comfortable Earth-like worlds in the system, Rimeas supports a population of roughly 250,000—almost entirely driven by its role as a refining and export hub for Glaciumite. Because the useful crystal ordering relaxes after extraction, it must be purified and stabilized on-planet before intersystem transport.

Most of the planet's surface is owned and operated by a single megacorporate entity, which administers the territory, infrastructure, and transit rights under a semi-extraterritorial charter. Settlements range from hardened mining camps to partially terraformed cities, often constructed in alpine basins or near subglacial thermal outlets. Civic life is heavily regulated, with employment, housing, and food linked to corporate tenure or contract.

## Cultural Notes
Locals refer to Rimeas as "The Throat" due to its air density and choking cold. Architectural style favors steel-framed composite domes, geothermal cores, and vaults recessed into glacial cliffs. The standard visual palette is stark: white snows, navy skies, and fluorescent blue ore seams. Sunset is considered a peak aesthetic moment and features prominently in settler folklore and corporate marketing. Despite its industrial reputation, Rimeas maintains a growing subculture of artists, archivists, and long-term technicians seeking isolation and silence.

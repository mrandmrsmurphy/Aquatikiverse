**Aethonix** is a tightly bound binary star system located approximately 42 light-years from Earth in the constellation of **Vela** (near Carina). It consists of a G0V-type primary and a K4V-type secondary, orbiting each other every 15.8 days at a separation of 0.15 AU. The system is notable for hosting an extensive planetary system, including the volatile world [[Aska]], and several terrestrial and gas giant analogues.

## Overview

Aethonix is classified as a main-sequence binary, composed of two hydrogen-burning stars in a nearly circular mutual orbit. The system's combined luminosity (~1.7 L☉) and metallicity ([Fe/H] = +0.15) make it a fertile environment for planet formation, consistent with the observed population of eight diverse planets.

## Stellar Components

### Aethonix A (Primary)

- Spectral Type: G0V  
- Mass: ~1.1 M☉  
- Radius: ~1.161 R☉  
- Effective Temperature: ~5930 K  
- Rotation Period: ~26 days  

Aethonix A is a slightly hotter and more massive analog of the Sun. Its stable rotation rate and lack of extreme variability suggest a relatively quiet magnetic cycle.

### Aethonix B (Secondary)

- Spectral Type: K4V  
- Mass: ~0.7 M☉  
- Radius: ~0.7 R☉  
- Effective Temperature: ~4600 K  
- Rotation Period: ~41 days  

A cooler and redder companion, Aethonix B contributes modestly to the system's total luminosity but contributes gravitational forcing to the circumbinary disk; stability depends on the full architecture.

## Orbital Configuration

- Mutual Orbital Period: 15.8 Earth days  
- Orbital Separation: 0.15 AU  
- Eccentricity: ~0.01 (nearly circular orbit)  
- Inclination: Coplanar with planetary orbits  

Far outside the 0.15-AU relative binary orbit, a central-mass approximation is useful for mean Kepler periods and fluxes. It does not remove periodic binary forcing or prove multi-planet stability. Direct short screens include both stars; their limits are documented in the adopted calculation note.

## Planetary System

Main article: [[Aethonix System]]

The Aethonix system hosts eight planets:

- **[[Aska]]** (0.6 AU): A super-Venus with extreme greenhouse conditions  
- **[[Velora]]** (1.2 AU): A warm terrestrial planet near the inner habitable zone  
- **[[Goku (exoplanet)|Goku]]** (1.345 AU): A true Earth twin with near-ideal surface conditions  
- **[[Thalor]]** (1.53 AU): A slightly cooler terrestrial planet with extended temperate regions  
- **[[Rimeas]]** (1.728 AU): A cold super-Earth, marginally habitable with greenhouse warming and a dense atmosphere  
- **[[Noctilux]]** (4.0 AU): Sub-Neptune with thick H/He atmosphere and moons  
- **[[Cryodon]]** (6.0 AU): Uranus analog with methane weather and moons  
- **[[Tsurai]]** (12.0 AU): A failed super-Neptune with exotic chemistry  

All major planets follow P-type circumbinary orbits. The retained architecture survives the short adopted screens, while its exact formation and migration history are not inferred uniquely from present low eccentricities.

## Evolution and Stability

With an estimated age of about 4 billion years, Aethonix is roughly midway through its main-sequence lifespan. No exceptional flare or mass-loss state is part of the present reference. Stellar brightening, tides and secular planet interactions still require evolution modelling; a short orbital screen cannot establish future billion-year stability.

## Cultural and Etymological Notes

The name **Aethonix** is derived from the ancient term *Aethon* (meaning “blazing” or “burning”) and the suffix *-ix* to denote duality. It reflects the binary nature and luminous intensity of the system.

## Physical basis

**Stars.** The primary's radius of about 1.161 R☉ preserves its 5930 K temperature and the 1.70 L☉ total luminosity; a 1.05 R☉ radius at that temperature would give only about 1.426 L☉. The tuple satisfies Stefan–Boltzmann arithmetic (about 1.502 and 0.198 L☉ for the two stars) but is not a fitted stellar-evolution or metallicity track. The 26- and 41-day stellar spins are reference values, not proof of weak tidal or magnetic evolution, and the 7.5–8 Gyr lifetime scale is distinct from the present 4-Gyr age. The binary's relative orbit is 0.150 AU with e = 0.01 and a period of 15.816 days.

**Location.** The shared J2000 position (RA 09h20m, Dec −50°) and 12.9-pc distance are used in the documented Galactic frame.

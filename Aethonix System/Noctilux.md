**Noctilux** is a cold sub-Neptune class exoplanet orbiting at 4.0 AU from a G0V–K4V tight binary system in the [[Aethonix System]]. With an estimated mass of ~10 Earth masses and radius of ~3.0 Earth radii, it is characterized by a puffy hydrogen–helium envelope overlying a high-pressure ice mantle and a silicate-metal core. Noctilux receives only ~0.106 Earth flux, but emits a substantial amount of intrinsic heat due to ongoing internal contraction and radiogenic decay.

## Physical Characteristics
### Internal Structure
Noctilux is composed of:
- **Core**: Dense silicate/metal mix, enriched in uranium, thorium, and potassium-40.
- **Mantle**: Thick supercritical layer of water, methane, ammonia, and hydrogen compounds.
- **Envelope**: A deep hydrogen–helium layer making up roughly 5–10% of total planetary mass.
- **Heat Output**: Intrinsic heat output likely rivals or exceeds absorbed stellar energy; strong thermal emissions are observed in the infrared.

With a radius of ~3 R⊕ and a mass of ~10 M⊕, Noctilux has a mean density of ~2.04 g/cm³ and a surface gravity of roughly 1.1 g at the 1-bar level.

### Atmosphere
- **Upper Haze**: Methane photochemistry produces a reddish, tholin-rich haze in the upper atmosphere.
- **Blue Layer**: Strong Rayleigh scattering from hydrogen-rich gases imparts an electric blue hue to the deeper atmosphere.
- **Cloud Belts**:
  - NH₃ → white or cream bands
  - NH₄SH, H₂S → tan to gold belts
  - PH₃ → dark streaks, spots, or vortices

Wind speeds are highest in the equatorial jets, with alternating east–west banding and occasional long-lived polar vortices.

### Visual Appearance
Noctilux appears deep blue with ochre bands, reddish high-latitude haze, and sharp, turbulent boundaries. Rapid rotation has deformed it into an oblate spheroid with a noticeably flattened polar profile. From the surface of its outer moons, it dominates the sky as a constantly shifting, phase-changing disk or crescent, according to the moon–star geometry, with auroral arcs occasionally visible along its limb.

## Moons and Ring System
Noctilux has an elaborate ring–moon system:
- **Inner Rings**: Icy particles, 25,000–32,000 km from the planet’s center; tholin-tinted, giving a faint rust-colored sheen.
- **Outer Rings**: Dusty, ammonia-rich rings, 32,000–40,000 km; more diffuse and clumpy.
- **Ring Moons A/B**: Shepherd moons (~0.01 R⊕) constraining ring boundaries and maintaining sharp edges through resonant interactions.
- **Moonlets**: Numerous co-orbitals and arc-like small moons from ~60,000 out to ~150,000 km.

### Major Moons
Main article: [[Lunara (moon)]].
- **Narela** (180,000 km): Ice-covered ocean moon (~0.03 M⊕); with an ice-covered internal ocean and possible quiescent surface intervals; shell thickness and present activity depend on tidal/thermal response.
- **Lunara** (400,000 km): Habitable, volcanically active, and magnetized; hosts radiotrophic and cryophilic life, with a globally substantial hydrogen-bearing atmosphere, patchy haze and surface/subsurface brines (see separate article).
- **Thyxis** (1,200,000 km): Displays oblique tectonics and heavily fractured terrain; likely undergoing slow tidal flexing and crustal recycling.
- **Boril** (3,000,000 km): Cold, dark, with a brittle, impact-scarred crust and minimal current activity; may be a captured outer-system body.

### Outer Satellites
- **Minor Resonants** (1–2.5 million km): Small bodies in orbital resonances with the major moons; mildly inclined orbits (~15°) and often grouped in families.
- **Irregulars A–E**: Retrograde, highly inclined (90–160°) objects, possibly captured planetesimals or fragments from ancient collisions in the Aethonix outer system.

## Habitability and Use
Noctilux is uninhabitable directly, but serves as:
- A heat source and tidal driver for its moons, especially Lunara and Narela.
- A potential site for floating atmospheric habitats in narrow pressure–temperature bands where temperature and pressure are useful to protected engineering, with sealed life support rather than unmodified breathing of H₂/He.
- A gravity well for slingshot maneuvers used by interplanetary traffic in the outer Aethonix system.
- An outer-system resource node for hydrogen, helium, trace volatiles, and magnetic energy harvesting.

While human activity is limited to orbital platforms and occasional atmospheric skimmer missions, the Noctilux system as a whole is considered a critical logistical hub for operations beyond the snow line of the [[Aethonix System]].

## Physical basis

Noctilux's four moons (Narela 180,000 km, Lunara 400,000 km, Thyxis 1,200,000 km, Boril 3,000,000 km) were tested together, and tighter Thyxis and Boril layouts produced a failure or near-escape; the adopted spacing is a working reference, not a stability proof. The irradiation-only equilibrium comparison is about 143 K at a Bond albedo of 0.35. The screens and rejected layouts are described on [[Aethonix System]] under Physical basis.

# Ghulheim and Ferruva

![[images/Ghulheim_seen_from_Ferruva.png]]

*False-color render of Ghulheim seen from Ferruva, a fictional brown dwarf–moon pair near the Galactic Center.*

The **Ghulheim–Ferruva system**, formally **GXN-1**, is an approximately seven-billion-year-old brown dwarf–moon system now orbiting near [[Sagittarius A*]] in the Milky Way’s Nuclear Star Cluster. **[[Ghulheim]]** is a faint infrared primary; **[[Ferruva]]** retains an approximately **3.5-billion-year-old biosphere**, including an earlier K-star-illuminated ocean epoch. Its present inhabitants live beneath an airless, scarred exterior in a regional network of insulating **Lid**, briny **Zone**, and hydrothermal **Ground**.

The later, fuller planet and biosphere accounts govern the details. The model rests on a cool brown dwarf, a geothermal foundation for the liquid habitat, geological hemispheric contrast, PFC-bearing carrier fluids with unresolved biosynthesis, separate historical clocks and consistent metabolic accounting. It is an adopted speculative working model with explicit open modeling tasks, not a demonstrated habitable nuclear-cluster system.

## Origin and Galactic context

Ghulheim and Ferruva formed roughly seven billion years ago in a metal-rich inner-disk environment, with **about 3 kpc** retained as the provisional Galactic birthplace. Ghulheim is now modeled below the hydrogen-burning threshold, rather than as a 0.075-M☉ object that barely failed to ignite. Its bulk metallicity is primarily inherited; swallowing minor bodies may affect trace enrichment and spin without explaining the entire metal inventory.

Ferruva became a bound companion **before the dated biosphere**. Early capture remains a possible origin, but its energy-loss mechanism is unresolved. The ancestral pair belonged to a wider K-star system, allowing the moon’s oceans to receive stellar illumination. A later encounter detached an **already bound** dwarf–moon pair. This avoids requiring the same event to both capture a formerly independent planet and preserve its oceanic ecology, but the actual pre-encounter architecture still needs modeling.

The subsequent path toward the nucleus remains under investigation. **0.5 kpc** is retained as a possible later waypoint, replacing the contradictory second birthplace. Encounters and cluster dynamics must change the pair’s orbital energy and angular momentum; an object does not gently settle near Sgr A* merely by drifting inward. The present 0.05-pc orbit and its mild eccentricity are an adopted endpoint, not a computed capture result.

### Orbital geometry and stability limits

For the chosen primary mass, a circular 0.05-pc reference gives a Hill radius of about **17.0 AU**. Using the illustrative outer pericenter gives about **16.2 AU**. Ferruva at 0.05 AU lies at roughly **0.0031** of that pericenter Hill scale, so immediate disruption by the central point-mass tide is not the obvious geometric problem. This screening is not a stellar-encounter, inclination/secular, relativistic, satellite-decay or gigayear survival calculation.

A density of **10⁵ stars/pc³** is an **unverified order-of-magnitude environmental parameter**, not a measured density assigned to this precise orbit. Cluster density, stellar mass spectrum, high relative speeds, remnants and encounters must be modeled together. A recent arrival into the current nuclear orbit is allowed; the biosphere need not have spent its full history there.

## Radiation field and the Galactic Center sky

A crowded sky with bright individual stars, uneven dust and an infrared-rich environment remains the visual goal. Earth’s heavily extincted line of sight is not the same as local extinction between Ferruva and nearby cluster stars. The original assertion that almost all local light must be red/IR is therefore not adopted without a local radiation-transfer model. A familiar Earthlike Milky Way band need not dominate this central vantage.

Ghulheim’s present normal incident flux at Ferruva is about **3.92 W/m²**. A **10²⁹-W** isotropic quiescent Sgr A* luminosity reference would give about **0.0033 W/m²** at 0.05 pc, while a **10²⁶-W X-ray** reference gives about **3.3 μW/m²**. These are order-of-magnitude inverse-square examples, not a complete spectral or radiation-dose calculation. Quiescent and flare powers differ by band.[^flares]

An **ambient cluster IR flux of 0.17 W/m²** is an unverified illustrative background requiring a stellar/dust model and an angular/band definition. It is not enough by itself to support an exposed warm sea. Nearby luminous stars and historical activity can change the actual radiation environment.

IR/radio brightness is not visual brightness. Micro-/milli-lunar comparisons and visible shadows from ordinary Sgr A* flares need a calibrated visible spectrum. Photon irradiation, charged-particle transport and cosmic-ray dose are distinct. No particular particle hazard is assumed to be inferable from an X-ray flare light curve alone.

## Ghulheim

Main article: [[Ghulheim]].

Ghulheim’s adopted **0.058-M☉**, seven-Gyr, [M/H]≈+0.5 reference comes from an illustrative Sonora evolution track, giving it an approximately **1050-K** infrared identity. Its five-hour rotation, kilogauss field, condensate-rich appearance and trace enrichment remain setting parameters requiring a spectral/magnetic model. The precise spectral subtype is unspecified, and δ is not a high-gravity suffix.

A magnetopause at **tens of dwarf radii** remains a variable pressure-balance hypothesis. Ferruva is about **133 dwarf radii** away and is not assumed continuously inside it. Episodic magnetotail interactions may influence chemistry. The field cannot block X-rays or permanently shield one hemisphere. Ghulheim itself can host atmospheric aurorae.

## Ferruva’s five strata

Main article: [[Ferruva]].

- **Lid:** A tens-to-hundreds-of-meters biological–mineral cap over a thicker insulating ice/mineral substrate. The illustrative total roof is order **10–15 km** in inhabited regions. Fossil mats, metal-bearing pigments, Pb minerals, sulfides, Al₂O₃, SiO₂ and localized vitrified residues form the cap; living repair communities occupy warm basal pores and fractures. Pillars and compartment walls limit unsupported spans.
- **Zone:** Regional interconnected brine basins and fractures inherited from the former ocean. Candidate inhabited fluids are **275–300 K**, with hotter vent niches. PFC phases, colloids, biofoams and slow macrofauna occur where ongoing chemical supply permits; a global unsupported warm cavern is not required.
- **Ground:** Fractured fossil seafloor, reactive minerals, hydrothermal vents and wet actinide microdomains. Chemosynthesis and radiolytic products sustain much of the primary-production premise.
- **Mantle:** A silicate interior with depth-dependent high-pressure phases, convection and chemical recycling. Appropriate rocky reservoirs contain much of the U/Th/K inventory; exact heat evolution is unmodeled.
- **Core:** A Fe–Ni interior contributing stored heat and perhaps dynamo action under suitable conditions. An intrinsic field and externally induced response are separate; neither is established by assigning radiogenic elements to the metal.

The more intact sub-Ghulheim habitat and scarred far hemisphere remain. Their contrast follows geology, fluid pathways, insulation and biological repair, rather than a permanent magnetic umbrella or routine radiation-driven silicate melting. Localized glassy terrain records earlier impact/thermal events whose detailed history remains unspecified.

## Heat and ecology

Main article: [[Life on Ferruva]].

A **0.08-W/m²** surface-equivalent geothermal flux is adopted as an illustrative design input to test, not a solved seven-Gyr interior heat budget. A simple conductive ice column from a 260-K ice interface to a 70-K exterior needs about **10.7 km** at that flux. More realistic convection, brine advection, fractures, mineral composition and regional heat concentration can change it. Insulation retains heat; it does not create it.

The ecosystem is **materially enclosed at the surface and energetically open**, with ongoing mantle/crust chemical exchange. Its main metabolic foundation is reduced vent fluids interacting with oxidants/minerals and local water-radiolysis products. Thermal IR can warm the cap; it does not automatically drive useful net synthesis. Flare-responsive chemistry and particle access are supplementary and unquantified.

The six detailed ecological subdivisions remain: **Armored Re-Emitters**, **Biofoam Relays**, **PFC-Breathing Protectors**, **Lid-Side Scavengers**, **Open-Sea Macrofauna**, and **Ground-Side Synths**. There are no sapients or civilization and no apex-predator guild. Cooperative vertical networks dominate the setting, while local competition and independent microbial refugia remain possible.

PFC-bearing circulation retains **C₈F₁₈ and C₁₀F₂₂** as carrier/storage constituents. Gas carriage is physical dissolution in separate fluorous phases. Their natural synthesis is explicitly **under investigation** and requires an energy-dependent, speculative Ferruvan pathway. The balanced chemical examples, isotope-chain correction and qualified uranyl sensitizer proposal are on [[Life on Ferruva]]. Balancing does not establish a biochemical mechanism or enough ecological power.

## Evolutionary timeline

The epoch clock below starts at **abiogenesis**, about **3.5 Gyr after body formation / 3.5 Gyr before the present**. It replaces the mixed clocks in the source overview. Boundaries are approximate ecological phases, not accurately dated observations.

### I. The Near-Starless Cradle (biological 0–0.5 Gyr)

Ferruva is already a satellite of Ghulheim within the wider K-star system. The K-star, rather than Ghulheim alone, illuminates the oceans and supports photosynthetic life. “Near-starless” is the inherited epoch name; the habitat is not actually without starlight. Ghulheim is younger, warmer and brighter than now, but remains predominantly an infrared object in the chosen cooling reference.

### II. The Blooming Seas (0.5–1.5 Gyr)

Reef-building microbes proliferate. Domes, mineralized mats and pigments develop metal binding, redox control and damage mitigation. These traits later contribute to a repairable Lid; their origin need not require catastrophic Galactic Center radiation from the outset.

### III. The Sundering (1.5–1.6 Gyr)

An encounter disrupts the ancestral host-star architecture and detaches the pre-bound Ghulheim–Ferruva pair. Some minor bodies are scattered or ingested; trace composition and rotation may change. Loss of persistent K-star illumination triggers decline of the exposed photosynthetic ocean. The exact encounter and retention of a low-eccentricity satellite orbit remain research tasks.

### IV. The Great Retreat (1.6–3.0 Gyr)

Exposed water freezes, some sublimates or escapes, and some survives in buried reservoirs. Life contracts into vent-fed fractures and insulated pores. Mineral–biological sealing and thicker ice/mineral insulation develop together. Loss of the former host star is the primary illumination change; background starlight is not asserted to fade monotonically during inward travel.

Tidal synchronization can occur separately from eccentricity damping. The present low-eccentricity state is adopted without claiming it necessarily emerged through rapid circularization at the present orbit. Radiation exposure depends on the route and local sources; a continuously increasing nuclear dose throughout a simple inward drift is not assumed.

### V. The Emergence of the Zone (about 3.0–3.4 Gyr)

By around biological age 3.0 Gyr, regional Lid/Zone/Ground networks have matured. Over the following interval brine basins, biofoams, chemically specialized mats and PFC-carrying macrofauna reorganize into the current energy-limited ecology. Hydrothermal and radiolytic chemistry anchors the food webs. The interval is adjusted from the source’s 3.0–3.5 boundary to leave a present sealed-world epoch while preserving the 3.5-Gyr total biological age.

### VI. The Sealed World (about 3.4–3.5 Gyr; present)

The present regional network persists beneath its cold cap. The pair occupies its adopted nuclear orbit by now; the entry date, possible 0.5-kpc waypoint and detailed capture route remain undetermined. This epoch does not assert a hundred-million-year residence in the exact current orbit. Ghulheim, cluster stars, flares, vents and small stresses modulate conditions, and no permanent hemisphere shielding is assumed.

The bodies are about seven billion years old, the biosphere about 3.5 billion, and mature enclosed networks roughly hundreds of millions of years old. Those clocks do not certify the same-duration orbital or thermal stability.

## Surface conditions and exploration hypothesis

A probe would encounter a grey-brown, fissured, coral-like mineral–biological cap with localized glassy scars, sulfides, pigments and cryovolcanic residues. Much exposed material is dormant or fossil. No substantial atmosphere is expected, though vents may transiently supply an exosphere. Thermal instruments could locate warm fractures; visible light in accessible cracks would require bioluminescence or locally hot material. Radiation-responsive thermal pulses are not presumed to propagate rapidly through kilometers of insulating roof.

A borehole descending through structurally supported ice/mineral compartments could reach warm basal films and eventually a briny Zone basin. Life-bearing compartments require controlled pressure access. Near-side continuity and far-side collapse are regional geological patterns rather than an assurance that all far-side microbes are absent.

## Open modeling tasks

The written reactions conserve atoms and charge; the substantive open tasks are:

1. **Historical dynamics:** construct the ancestral K-star/dwarf/moon architecture, early satellite capture if retained, detachment encounter, Galactic energy/angular-momentum evolution and present outer-orbit delivery. Specify residence time, orientation, encounters and any eccentricity excitation. The Hill estimates are screening only; no long-term numerical integration was performed.
2. **Dwarf spectrum and environment:** refine the chosen old metal-rich cooling reference with a cloud/disequilibrium spectrum. Model the local star/dust field, particle environment and magnetopause; verify or replace the 10⁵-stars/pc³ and 0.17-W/m² background figures. Compute dose by energy and material column, including secondary radiation. Distinguish historical major outbursts from ordinary current flares.
3. **Satellite evolution:** model synchronization, eccentricity history, tidal dissipation, obliquity, primary tides and orbit decay/migration. The simple low-eccentricity heat estimate is not a long-term circularization result.
4. **Roof and ocean:** choose liquid inventory, inhabited area, shell composition/porosity, depth, temperature, salinity and pressure. Test convection, conduction, advection, support, fracture sealing, tectonic repair and decompression, with a geothermal evolution model that can sustain the required regional flux.
5. **Chemical productivity:** quantify production/transport/regeneration of H₂, CH₄ and organic donors; sulfate/thiosulfate, nitrate/nitrite, Fe(III)/Mn(IV), limited O₂/peroxide and other radiolytic oxidants; inorganic carbon and N/P/S/F/trace nutrients. Account for burial, leakage and mineral passivation. Demonstrate whether net primary production supports the proposed macrofaunal size, density and activity.
6. **PFC and special biochemistry:** define C₈F₁₈/C₁₀F₂₂ phase mixtures, gas solubility/partial pressures, emulsifiers, biosynthesis, fluorine budget and replacement rates. Specify the uranyl photocycle’s donors/acceptors and regeneration, or replace it with a better supported radiation-response mechanism. Define membrane pump stoichiometry and energy coupling. The written reactions conserve atoms/charge but do not supply missing enzymes or yields.
7. **Appearance and assets:** explain localized vitrification through particular thermal events; quantify visible brightness and possible luminescence. Supply or replace the two unavailable named illustrations if desired. The scientific setting does not depend on inventing image contents.

## Physical basis

**Constants.** The calculations use G = 6.67430×10⁻¹¹ SI, M☉ = 1.98847×10³⁰ kg, M♃ = 1.89813×10²⁷ kg, M⊕ = 5.9722×10²⁴ kg, R☉ = 695,700 km, R♃ = 71,492 km, R⊕ = 6,371 km, AU = 149,597,870.7 km, pc ≈ 206,264.806 AU, L☉ = 3.828×10²⁶ W, σ = 5.670374419×10⁻⁸ SI and a 365.25-day year. Rounded adopted references do not imply that their input constants or physical models are exact.

**Orbit and illumination.**

- The two-body period P = 2π√(a³/[G(M+m)]) is about 16.956 days for Ferruva's a = 0.05 AU and Ghulheim's 0.058 M☉, with the small satellite mass included.
- Ghulheim's angular diameter, 2 asin(R/a), is about 0.861°; its direction is nearly fixed for synchronous Ferruva, while its bands rotate.
- The satellite sits at a/R ≈ 133.1 dwarf radii, beyond a hypothetical magnetopause at tens of radii; intermittent tail connectivity is possible, not demonstrated.
- The relative prograde magnetic-pattern period, 1/|1/P_spin − 1/P_orbit|, is about 5.062 hours for a 5-hour spin, assuming aligned prograde motion and nonaxisymmetric structure; the arithmetic does not establish the field geometry or any biological forcing.
- The normal incident flux F = L/(4πa²) is about 3.922 W/m², the spherical mean before reflection F/4 is about 0.980 W/m², and a zero-albedo uniform-emission reference, (F/[4σ])^¼, is about 64.5 K, which is not a local surface or brine temperature.
- At 0.05 pc, isotropic luminosities of 10²⁹ W bolometric and 10²⁶ W X-ray from Sgr A* imply 0.00334 W/m² and 3.34 μW/m². These are different bands and power references: actual high-energy dose needs spectra and transport, and the local stellar and dust field is not included.

**Galactic orbit and survival.** Kepler scaling for a = 0.05 pc around about 4.3×10⁶ M☉ gives a period of 505 years; for the adopted outer eccentricity of 0.05 the pericenter is 0.0475 pc. This neglects extended stellar mass, encounters, relativistic corrections and evolving orbit elements. A Hill screening, r_H ≈ r_peri [M_dwarf/(3 M_BH)]^(1/3), gives about 16.2 AU at pericenter, or 17.0 AU for the circular 0.05-pc reference, so Ferruva's 0.05 AU is about 0.0031 of the pericenter scale. This is a screen, not an integration or a proof of 7-Gyr survival. Eccentric orbits change the flux with distance, and exact directions, occultations and time-dependent spectra are not supplied by these mean calculations. The real black-hole mass and distance and the observing sources are on [[Sagittarius A*]].

**Limits.** These are reproducible analytic references, not an orbital simulation, an atmospheric retrieval or a coupled habitat model.

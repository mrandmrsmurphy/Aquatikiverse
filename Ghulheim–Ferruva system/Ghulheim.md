# Ghulheim

![[images/Ghulheim_seen_from_Ferruva.png]]

*Ghulheim as viewed from Ferruva: false color emphasizes infrared emission; angular scale is illustrative.*

**Ghulheim** is the old, metal-rich brown dwarf primary of [[Ghulheim and Ferruva|GXN-1]], a compact moon system orbiting near [[Sagittarius A*]] in the Milky Way’s Nuclear Star Cluster. Its principal companion, [[Ferruva]], preserves a materially enclosed subsurface biosphere. Ghulheim remains faint in visible light, rapidly rotating and magnetically active, with emission concentrated in the infrared.

The thermal reference uses the **Sonora Bobcat**, [M/H]=+0.5, solar-C/O, 0.058-M☉ evolutionary track. Linear interpolation in age between its 6- and 8-Gyr rows gives 1052.5 K, log(L/L☉)=−5.1425, log g=5.3855, and R=0.0808 R☉. The entries are rounded model quantities, not independent exact constraints or observations. The model is cloudless and isolated: it supports the bulk thermal reference but does not validate the fictional clouds, magnetic field, composition or history.[^model]

A 0.075-M☉ mass would sit at the metallicity-dependent hydrogen-burning boundary and risk being a very-low-mass star, so a lower mass suits a cool, old, metal-rich brown dwarf. Ghulheim need not have “narrowly missed” hydrogen burning. Partial electron degeneracy, cooling and contraction help explain its compact radius; degeneracy does not mean contraction ceases completely.

## Classification

A δ designation would be wrong: the δ gravity suffix belongs to a proposed very-young/very-low-gravity sequence, whereas Ghulheim is an old, high-gravity object.[^gravity] “Peculiar” remains a useful descriptive term for the setting’s enhanced metallicity, clouds and colors, but no specific L/T subtype is claimed without a matching spectrum. Temperature alone does not determine an exact spectral subclass.

## Atmosphere and chemistry

The atmosphere is hydrogen–helium dominated, with water, methane, carbon monoxide and alkali opacity shaping its emergent spectrum. The retained atmospheric inventory includes:

- **H₂O** absorption across the near-infrared.
- **CH₄**, including strong absorption around the near-/mid-infrared methane bands.
- **CO** potentially maintained above equilibrium abundances by vertical mixing.
- **Na and K** contributing pressure-broadened optical/red absorption.
- **FeH and CrH** as possible deeper or hotter-window signatures rather than guaranteed strong features in every band.
- **MgSiO₃, Mg₂SiO₄ and Al₂O₃**, with iron-bearing condensates, in deeper cloud reservoirs; their visible contribution depends on lofting and cloud depth.

The [M/H]≈+0.5 composition is primarily inherited from formation. Swallowed minor bodies may alter trace abundances locally but are not assumed to manufacture the dwarf’s entire bulk enrichment. The detailed equilibrium/disequilibrium spectrum and cloud distribution remain to be modeled.

## Spectral energy distribution

A 1050-K blackbody has a wavelength-density peak near **2.76 μm**. The actual atmospheric spectrum is strongly structured by molecular absorption and emission windows; a maximum in Fν or νFν occurs elsewhere. Near- to mid-infrared radiation dominates the observable thermal output. Atmospheric dynamics may modulate it as the dwarf rotates, while auroral processes may add infrared, radio or occasional optical components.

A spectral energy distribution diagram is not yet available; a future diagram should use this reference and clearly identify any schematic spectrum.

## Internal structure

Ghulheim is modeled with a convective H₂–He envelope over a dense, partially electron-degenerate interior. The atmosphere supplies the radiative boundary; “fully convective” does not imply every atmospheric layer is convective. Heavy elements may be mixed into the deep interior or partially segregated. A fully specified core structure is not inferred from the source’s ingestion history.

The five-hour spin can support a dynamo, but rotation alone does not determine field strength. Early ingestion and encounters may have affected angular momentum; the present spin is an adopted property rather than a reconstructed capture result.

## Magnetosphere and aurorae

The setting retains a kilogauss-scale field and a pressure-variable magnetopause, provisionally at **tens of Ghulheim radii**. No ambient plasma-pressure model has yet established that size. At 0.05 AU, Ferruva lies about **133 dwarf radii** away and is **not assumed to be continuously immersed**. A variable magnetotail or expanded disturbed field may interact with it intermittently; field connectivity and particle access need calculation.

Ghulheim can host aurorae in its own atmosphere. Its field guides and traps charged particles but does not form a permanent photon shield for Ferruva’s near hemisphere. Moon-side luminescence, exospheric emission and particle precipitation are separate from the dwarf’s atmospheric aurorae. Their powers and spectra are unspecified.

For a prograde, aligned reference, the dwarf’s five-hour rotational pattern passes an orbiting Ferruva in roughly **5.06 hours**; nonaxisymmetric structure is required for a modulation signal. The magnetic configuration and biological response need not follow a perfectly repeatable clock.

## Visual appearance

From Ferruva, Ghulheim spans approximately **0.86°**. It remains in a nearly fixed direction because Ferruva is synchronously rotating, while its bands rotate and magnetic activity varies. Copper/umber colors and muted condensate banding are an instrumental or enhanced-color impression. A calibrated model spectrum is needed to quantify naked-eye brightness; false-color artwork should not be treated as that measurement.

## Evolutionary reference

The source’s seven-billion-year body age is distinct from Ferruva’s 3.5-billion-year biological history. For the chosen track, approximate temperature references are:

| Body age | Biological epoch | Effective temperature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.5 Gyr | Abiogenesis reference | 1,291 K |
| 5.0 Gyr | Sundering reference | 1,171 K |
| 6.5 Gyr | Mature sealed habitat | 1,077 K |
| 7.0 Gyr | Present | 1,053 K |

The reference uses selected **+0.5 metallicity, solar-C/O, 0.058-M☉** rows of the Sonora Bobcat tables ([Marley et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07434)), read from the plain-text SEDA mirror with columns M/M☉, age, log L/L☉, Teff, log g and R/R☉. Values at 3.5, 5, 6.5 and 7 Gyr use independent linear-in-age interpolation of the tabulated log luminosity, temperature, log gravity and radius, with no extrapolation, so the interpolated columns need not obey every nonlinear relation exactly. At 7 Gyr this gives a mass of 0.058 M☉ ≈ 60.76 M♃, Teff = 1052.5 K, R = 0.0808 R☉ ≈ 0.7863 R♃, log g = 5.3855, log L/L☉ = −5.1425 and L ≈ 7.203×10⁻⁶ L☉, which the page rounds to about 1050 K, 0.79 R♃ and 7.2×10⁻⁶ L☉. The cloudless, isolated-model assumptions and rounding limit the precision. This is a reference cooling trajectory, not a cloud spectrum, a fit to an observed object or a reconstruction of its encounters and accretion events, and no separate L9 or low-gravity spectral assignment is made.

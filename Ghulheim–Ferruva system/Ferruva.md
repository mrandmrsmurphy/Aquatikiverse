# Ferruva

![[images/Ferruva's_scarred_surface.png]]

*Ferruva’s more intact sub-Ghulheim cap and scarred far hemisphere. The contrast is geological and ecological; it is not a permanent magnetic shadow.*

**Ferruva** is a synchronously rotating, sub-Earth-mass terrestrial companion of [[Ghulheim]], within [[Ghulheim and Ferruva|GXN-1]]. Once illuminated by a K-type star, its surface oceans supported photosynthetic life. Today its airless exterior covers the **Lid**, **Zone** and **Ground**: a mineral–biological roof, regional interconnected briny habitats, and a hydrothermal former seafloor. The system is materially enclosed at the exterior but exchanges heat and chemistry with the interior.

The radius and mass imply the revised density; they do not alone establish an unusually metal-rich core. Ferruva is rocky with an iron-bearing interior and retained water reservoirs. “Earth-mass moon” is descriptive; the adopted mass remains 0.8 M⊕.

Synchronous rotation fixes Ghulheim’s direction in Ferruva’s sky. It does not by itself erase eccentricity, stop tidal dissipation, or provide permanent shielding from Sagittarius A*. Directions to the Galactic Center and passing stars change as Ferruva orbits. The present nearly circular orbit is an adopted low-excitation state; early capture and settling remain under investigation.

## Internal structure

### Lid

The **Lid** is the entire composite roof above the regional Zone, with an outer **tens-to-hundreds-of-meters mineral–biological cap** over a thicker ice/mineral substrate. An illustrative conductive column places the total insulating depth at order **10–15 km**, rather than expecting a 100-m roof to sustain a global warm unsupported cavity. This is a thermal design reference, not a solved global shell.

The cap retains vitrified microbial residues, **PbS, PbCO₃, Al₂O₃, SiO₂**, melanin analogs, **boron carbide**, and layered sulfides. **CdCO₃** occurs in sequestered microdomains where relevant to the Lid biota. Lead, cadmium and actinides are compartmentalized; their mere presence does not establish useful shielding or biological tolerance. Radiation attenuation and secondary dose require composition/column calculations.

Much of the outer surface is fossil or dormant. Living repair communities concentrate in warmer basal pores, brine fractures and vent-connected conduits. Rock supports, mineral pillars and compartment walls limit unsupported spans; this is not one giant empty planetary cavern. Hydrothermal and localized cryovolcanic activity supply repair minerals and some clathrate-bearing residues. Fractures may reseal, freeze or leak; no perfect permanent seal is assumed.

### Zone

The **Zone** is a network of saline, liquid-bearing basins and fractures inherited from the former ocean. The candidate inhabited brines are approximately **275–300 K**, with warmer hydrothermal microenvironments. These temperatures are habitat design targets, not outcomes demonstrated by the shell calculation. Depth-dependent pressure suppresses surface-style boiling; the habitat is not required to sit near its boiling point.

A 10-km column of 920-kg/m³ ice at the adopted gravity gives an overburden pressure of order **80 MPa**; denser minerals, geometry, pore connectivity and fluid pressure alter the local value. Brine composition and phase stability must be checked at the chosen pressure. The ocean volume, connectivity and inhabited area are not yet fixed.

The chemical inventory retains organics, **C₈F₁₈ and C₁₀F₂₂**, dissolved **CO₂/N₂/CH₄**, biofoams, ferrihydrite and clay colloids, amino acids and fatty acids. PFC phases are separate droplets, films or compartments, not uniformly dissolved into water. Slow macrofauna graze particulates, films and symbiotic resources; rich communities cluster around sustained resource fluxes.

### Ground

The **Ground** is the fossil seafloor, fractured by tectonics and hydrothermal circulation. Its inventory includes **pyroxenes, magnetite, Fe₂O₃, uraninite, thorite, graphite and Cu–Fe sulfides**. Chemolithotrophic mats exploit fluid–mineral interfaces. Wet radioactive grains generate radiolytic products locally; organisms must separate useful substrates from damaging oxidants and radionuclides.

### Mantle

A silicate interior contains **peridotite** and appropriate depth-dependent phases such as **ringwoodite, majorite and garnet**. Their exact boundaries require a pressure–temperature model. Convection, mantle melting and crustal recycling sustain hydrothermal systems and mobilize **P, S, N, Fe and Ca** where present in source reservoirs. Much of the long-lived **U/Th/K** heat inventory is assigned to rock/silicate reservoirs rather than an arbitrarily enriched metallic core.

### Core

A metallic **Fe–Ni core** contributes stored heat and possibly dynamo action if its state and convection permit. The present intrinsic field is unspecified. An externally induced response to Ghulheim’s changing field is a separate mechanism; neither is inferred just from a dense core or radiogenic heat. Bulk U/Th/K enrichment of the metal is no longer the default heat source.

## Perfluorocarbon phases and circulation

PFC-bearing hemolymph and brine emulsions are retained as a distinctive biological adaptation. **Gas molecules dissolve physically** in the fluorous phase and exchange according to chemical-potential gradients; a C₈F₁₈–O₂ bonded complex is not assumed. Carriers transport available gas rather than creating oxygen, redox energy or nutrients.

The origin of abundant PFCs remains **under investigation**. Fluoride-rich hydrothermal fluids and Fe–Ni–Cu sulfide interfaces supply a possible ecological context, but they are not a demonstrated route to sequentially fluorinating a carbon chain. An energy-dependent Ferruvan pathway, selective catalysts, fluorine acquisition and containment are speculative requirements. Ongoing chemical loss and replacement costs must be counted.

C₈F₁₈ is a candidate carrier constituent. C₁₀F₂₂ may occur in mixtures, warm microhabitats, or storage/structural phases; it is not assumed liquid everywhere without a phase diagram. PFCs are not general solvents for ions or all small metabolites. High pressure helps retain dissolved gases but does not universally prevent bubbles or degassing during decompression.

## Life on Ferruva

Main article: [[Life on Ferruva]].

The most persistent external expression is the biological–mineral cap, but **hydrothermal chemical disequilibrium and local radiolysis** support most primary production. The three ecological domains exchange material in both directions:

- The **Lid** retains fluids, protects basal communities, and can add episodic radiation-generated chemistry.
- The **Zone** transports metabolites, supports PFC-carrying macrofauna, and hosts redox and fermentation networks supplied with fresh substrates.
- The **Ground** supplies mineral nutrients, reduced fluids and radiolytic products. Heat predominantly escapes outward; it is not continually recycled into new biological work.

The biosphere is approximately **3.5 Gyr** old and includes its earlier photosynthetic era. The sealed configuration is younger. There are no known sapients or civilization. Energy limitation favors slow activity, storage and symbiosis; community stability remains a working ecological premise rather than a productivity calculation.

## Tectonic regime

Ferruva retains a provisional network of roughly **50 small plates**, with characteristic area about **9 million km²** and indicative relative motions around **3 cm/year** in active regions. These are setting assumptions awaiting an interior model, not measurements or values implied by density. Spreading, downwelling and transforms supply fractured pathways; their ability to coexist with a persistent roof requires ongoing repair and compartmentalization. The external relief is muted in some covered regions, while rupture zones and impacts remain prominent.

## Surface and hemispheric contrast

The exposed surface is grey-brown and coral-like, with dark sulfides, irradiated organics, impact glasses and cryovolcanic residues. No precise Bond or geometric albedo is assigned; “low albedo” is a qualitative appearance goal, not a property established for every listed mineral.

The **sub-Ghulheim hemisphere** retains more connected basal habitats and a more intact cap. The **anti-Ghulheim hemisphere** has more ancient impact/thermal scars, collapsed roof sectors and disrupted conduits. This asymmetry is inherited and reinforced by vent placement, circulation and repair; it is not continuous radiation melting of one fixed side. “Vitrified terrain” means localized geological glass and fused residues whose heating events require a history, not a uniformly melted present hemisphere.

Infrared surveys may detect thermal anomalies. Visible light from cracks is limited to bioluminescence or locally hot emission, not ordinary room-temperature IR. Exospheric emission or particle-induced mineral luminescence is a possibility distinct from atmospheric aurorae. The moon is not assumed permanently inside Ghulheim’s tens-of-radii magnetopause.

## Thermal budget

The working heat reference is:

| Contribution | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Ghulheim | About **3.92 W/m²** normal incident flux; at most **0.98 W/m²** global average before reflection |
| Interior | **0.08 W/m²** illustrative surface-equivalent geothermal design input; about **3.7 ×10¹³ W** total |
| Tides within Ferruva | About **4 ×10⁻⁶ W/m²** in a simple synchronous eccentricity-tide example with e=0.001 and k₂/Q=10⁻³; not a full orbital/thermal evolution model |
| Nuclear-cluster radiation | Spectrum and nearby-star field unmodeled; an ambient 0.17-W/m² IR figure is an unverified illustrative background |
| Sagittarius A* | Weak present quiescent input; spectral flares and historical outbursts are episodic rather than an assumed steady warm-ocean power source |
| Charged particles | Access, dose, trapping and deposited power unspecified; not an assumed large continuous heat subsidy |

The adopted geothermal flux is a **requirement to test**, not a consequence of the source’s core composition. A one-dimensional conductive ice approximation with k≈651/T W/(m K), ice-interface temperature 260 K and surface 70 K needs about **10.7 km** at that flux. This only illustrates a viable scale of thermal resistance: convection, mineral layers, brine advection, fractures, local irradiation and long-term heat evolution could change it materially. The 260-K interface is a cold saline/pressure-dependent ice-boundary design reference, distinct from warmer vent-fed liquid habitats below. Its actual melting relation must be checked. The cold surface can radiate energy away while an insulated liquid habitat remains warm.

## Evolutionary outline

Times here are **since abiogenesis**, not since the bodies formed:

- **0–1 Gyr:** K-star-illuminated oceans, photosynthesis and reef-building microbial communities.
- **1.5–1.6 Gyr:** Sundering of the host-star system; the already bound Ghulheim–Ferruva pair loses sustained stellar illumination.
- **From 1.6 Gyr:** Freezing and loss of exposed volatiles accompany retreat into insulated fractures; some of the ocean water remains buried.
- **About 3.0 Gyr:** Mature, connected Zone/Ground habitats and a repairable composite Lid are established regionally.
- **3.0–3.5 Gyr:** Further reorganization and compartmentalization; delivery toward the nuclear region remains an encounter-history problem.
- **Present, 3.5 Gyr after abiogenesis / about 7 Gyr body age:** The pair occupies its adopted nuclear orbit. Its time in this specific orbit is unspecified and need not equal the biosphere’s age.

## Physical basis

**Bulk.** For 0.8 M⊕ and 0.95 R⊕ the density is 5.144 g/cm³, the gravity 8.705 m/s² (0.886 g) and the area 460.3 million km², so fifty equal-area plates would average 9.21 million km² (actual plate sizes and speeds are setting assumptions). The geothermal reference of 0.08 W/m² corresponds to about 3.68×10¹³ W in total; it is a design input that needs an old-planet heat model, not something inferred from density or core enrichment.

**Ice column.** For a one-dimensional, steady, conductive, pure-ice comparison with k(T) = 651/T W/(m K) (an illustrative approximation, from the ice-shell discussion in [Hand 2007](https://cchyba.scholar.princeton.edu/sites/g/files/toruqf3881/files/documents/Kevin%20Hand%20thesis_2007.pdf)), integrating q = k(T) dT/dz gives d = (651/q) ln(T_interface/T_surface). With T_interface = 260 K, T_surface = 70 K and q = 0.08 W/m², d ≈ 10.68 km. This interface temperature is separate from the proposed 275–300 K hydrothermal liquid compartments: pressure and salinity melting behavior must justify the real boundary, since pure ice cannot remain solid against arbitrarily hot liquid. A constant k = 2 W/(m K) gives d ≈ 4.75 km for the same temperatures and flux, which shows the conductivity sensitivity; neither comparison fixes a unique shell thickness, and minerals, porosity, convection, cracks, advection and varying heating can change it. A nominal 10-km, 920-kg/m³ ice column gives ρgh ≈ 80.1 MPa before denser cap minerals, topography and pore-pressure structure, and overburden pressure is not automatically fluid pressure in every compartment. The biology–mineral cap is tens to hundreds of meters thick, but the Lid as a complete roof includes kilometer-scale insulation and supports, and a pressure, heat and support model must also account for the water inventory and persistent circulation. A cold exterior is compatible with a warm insulated interior; there is no global warm exposed sea.

**Tides.** For a nearly synchronous low-eccentricity satellite, P_tidal = (21/2)(k₂/Q) G M_dwarf² R_moon⁵ n e² / a⁶. With e = 0.001 and k₂/Q = 10⁻³ this gives about 4.0×10⁻⁶ W/m² at the present reference, assuming a simplified equilibrium response and omitting obliquity, frequency dependence, primary tides, perturbed motion and thermal feedback; the assigned eccentricity is not evidence of rapid circularization. The small-e damping timescale, (2/21)(Q/k₂)(m/M)(a/R)⁵/n, is of order 84 Gyr, while a constant-response spin-down estimate (moment coefficient 0.33, initial 24-hour spin) is much shorter, of order a million years. Synchronization and circularization cannot be equated, and a satellite formed or captured closer in, or one that retained low excitation, needs a complete evolution model to connect to the present orbit.

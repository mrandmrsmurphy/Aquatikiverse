**Melchior** is a massive gas giant orbiting the star [[Methuselah]] at a distance of 0.25 AU. With an estimated mass of ~340 M⊕ (≈ 1.07 M♃), Melchior plays a central role in the orbital and biospheric stability of its moon system, particularly the habitable super-Earth moon [[Magnum]].

## Physical characteristics

Melchior’s radius is approximately 71,500 km (1.02 R♃), at the upper end of what a cold, ~1 M♃ giant with a ~30 M⊕ core has (about 0.95–1.0 R♃). Its mean density is about 1.33 g/cm³ from its stated mass and radius. Interior models suggest a ~30 M⊕ heavy-element core.

The atmosphere is primarily composed of molecular hydrogen (H₂) and helium (He), with trace constituents such as methane (CH₄), ammonia (NH₃), water vapor (H₂O), and a suite of photochemical products. Deep layers contain cloud decks of various condensates (e.g., water, sulfides, alkali salts), while the upper atmosphere hosts hazes produced by photolysis of methane and other hydrocarbons.

Melchior's formation heat is long gone. A Jupiter-like planet cools roughly as L ∝ t⁻¹·³: its intrinsic temperature falls from ~100 K at 4.6 Gyr to ~17 K at 1 Tyr and ~12 K at 3 Tyr (0.005 and 0.001 W/m²), against 11–19 W/m² absorbed from the star even in the dim RD epoch. Melchior has therefore been heated almost entirely by Methuselah throughout the Red Dwarf (RD), Glow-Up (GU) and Blue Dwarf (BD) epochs; tidal heating by its moons is small. With so little internal heat flux, the planet's interior is largely stagnant and any convective magnetic dynamo is weak (a cold-giant estimate, not modeled).

### Color evolution

Melchior’s visible appearance has changed over trillions of years in response to Methuselah’s increasing luminosity, changing stellar spectrum, and the planet’s evolving atmospheric chemistry:

- **Red Dwarf (RD) phase** (low stellar flux, cool upper atmosphere):  
  Methuselah’s luminosity was only ~0.003–0.005 L☉, and Melchior’s upper atmosphere was cold. Methane photolysis proceeded slowly, but enough UV reached the upper layers to drive the formation of complex hydrocarbons and tholin-like hazes. These hazes, combined with deep, cold cloud decks, produced a dark, muted world dominated by deep rust, brown, and umber tones. The visual impression was closer to a scaled-up, cold Titan than to a bright gas giant.

- **Glow-Up (GU) phase** (rising flux, stronger photochemistry):  
  As Methuselah brightened toward ~0.02 L☉ and its spectrum shifted into the visible, UV and blue photons became more available. Haze production intensified, ammonia clouds began to break up, and new condensate layers (e.g., potassium chloride, sodium sulfide) appeared. The result was a more variegated disc: patchy gray-violet and ochre layers, with magenta-tinged bands and storm systems breaking through the high haze.

- **Blue Dwarf (BD) phase** (current state):  
  In the present BD epoch, Methuselah’s luminosity is ~0.02 L☉ and the stellar spectrum is that of a warm K dwarf. Photolytic hazes in the upper atmosphere are thinner than in the RD epoch, allowing deeper methane- and cloud-rich layers to be visible. Melchior now shows banded structures with bluish-turquoise tones from methane absorption, interleaved with pale gray and reddish-brown belts where sulfide and other condensate clouds dominate. The overall appearance is a hybrid of Jovian-style banding and Neptunian hues, adjusted for a warmer, redder stellar spectrum.

## Orbit and role in the system

Melchior orbits Methuselah at 0.25 AU in a nearly circular orbit (eccentricity ~0.0) with a period of ~118 Earth days and an average orbital speed of ~23 km/s. Its Hill radius at this distance is ~0.033 AU (~4.9 million km), providing ample space for a stable moon system.

Stellar flux at Melchior’s orbit has increased over time as Methuselah brightened:

- **RD phase** (L ≈ 0.003–0.005 L☉):  
  Flux at 0.25 AU ≈ 0.05–0.08 F⊕. The atmosphere was heated by the star and was very cold (equilibrium temperature 119–135 K).

- **GU phase** (L ≈ 0.005–0.02 L☉):  
  Flux at 0.25 AU ≈ 0.08–0.32 F⊕. Rising stellar heating warmed the upper atmosphere; photochemistry intensified and cloud structures became more complex.

- **BD phase** (current, L ≈ 0.02 L☉):  
  Flux at 0.25 AU ≈ 0.32 F⊕. The corresponding full-redistribution equilibrium temperature at this distance is ~184 K for a Bond albedo of 0.4, which sets the baseline for the outer layers of the planet and the energy budget of its moons.

Despite its relatively close orbit, Melchior is not a “hot Jupiter.” Methuselah’s output has remained low by solar standards even in the BD epoch, so Melchior’s atmosphere has never undergone extreme inflation or severe mass loss. The planet is adopted to keep a magnetic field strong enough to shield its moons from much of the stellar wind and charged-particle environment and help preserve their atmospheres, although a cold giant with so little internal heat flux may have only a weak dynamo.

## Moon system

Melchior hosts a compact system of large moons plus at least one subsatellite. Three major moons—Clavelin, Magnum, and Jéréboam—dominate the system’s mass and are locked in a Laplace-like mean-motion resonance. Magnum, in turn, hosts its young, impact-formed subsatellite Piccolo.

### Magnum

Main article: [[Magnum]]

- Mass: ~3 M⊕  
- Radius: ~1.45 R⊕  
- Composition: rocky super-Earth with a global ocean on a rocky seafloor  
- Orbits Melchior at ~12 Melchior radii (~0.86 million km)  
- Orbital period around Melchior: ~5 days  

Magnum is weakly tidally heated (~10¹²–10¹³ W) by its slightly eccentric orbit and resonant interactions with Clavelin and Jéréboam. This tidal heat sustains vents on the seafloor, and with its thick atmosphere and stellar flux from Methuselah it supports a long-lived habitable environment and a multi-trillion-year biosphere. For biological details, see [[Life on Magnum]].

### Clavelin

- Mass: ~0.025 M⊕  
- Approximate radius: ~0.41 R⊕  
- Semimajor axis: ~7.6 Melchior radii (~0.54 million km)  
- Orbital period: ~2.5 days  

Clavelin is a mixed rock–ice body, geologically quiet today. It is the innermost participant in the 1:2:4 resonance chain with Magnum and Jéréboam, helping maintain their orbital eccentricities.

With so little tidal power available to the whole system (≲10¹³ W in total), Clavelin has at most thin brine layers and rare cryovolcanic episodes, not an Io-like surface.

### Jéréboam

- Mass: ~0.04 M⊕  
- Approximate radius: ~0.52 R⊕  
- Semimajor axis: ~19 Melchior radii (~1.36 million km)  
- Orbital period: ~10 days  

Jéréboam is an icy-rock hybrid that forms the outer anchor of the resonance chain. Its orbit, together with those of Clavelin and Magnum, maintains a Laplace-like 1:2:4 period ratio:

> P_Clavelin : P_Magnum : P_Jéréboam ≈ 1 : 2 : 4  

This configuration maintains small non-zero eccentricities in all three moons, generating weak long-term tidal heating. For Jéréboam this heat can at most keep thin subsurface brines plausible, and the surface remains cold and heavily ice-dominated.

### Dynamical role

The three large moons are essential to the stability and long-term energy budget of the system:

- The resonance chain exchanges orbital energy and angular momentum among the moons. Tidal dissipation draws energy from Melchior's spin.
- Tidal dissipation inside Magnum is the only significant heat source, sustaining hydrothermal circulation and a deep chemosphere. The available power is limited: tides on Melchior can supply at most ~8×10¹¹ W (Q = 10⁹) to ~8×10¹² W (Q = 10⁸).
- Clavelin and Jéréboam also experience tidal heating, generally at lower levels than Magnum.

Keeping the moons near their present orbits for ~2.5 Tyr requires a very low tidal dissipation in Melchior (Q ≳ 10⁸–10⁹). For comparison, Jupiter's Q is of order 10⁵–10⁶; at that dissipation the moons would have moved outward by several times their present distances (or inward, if Melchior had locked to its star in ~0.3 Tyr). The high Q is an adopted premise. Melchior's spin energy (4×10³⁴ J) and orbital-angular-momentum budget (4.5×10³⁸ kg m²/s, comparable to Magnum's 1.9×10³⁸) would be consumed by a more dissipative Melchior.

## Evolution across stellar epochs

Melchior’s internal structure has changed little since its formation: it remains a predominantly hydrogen–helium giant with a dense core and a convective metallic-hydrogen mantle. However, its observable atmosphere and energy balance have evolved in step with Methuselah’s luminosity:

- **RD phase**:  
  Heated almost entirely by the dim star. Upper atmospheres were cold, hazes were thick and dark, and visible colors were muted browns and deep reds. Methane absorption bands were present but often masked by tholin-like haze.

- **GU phase**:  
  Rising stellar flux and a shift toward a warmer stellar spectrum increased the importance of external heating in the upper atmosphere. Photochemistry intensified, new haze layers formed, and condensate clouds diversified. Visual contrast between belts and zones increased, and transient storms and vortices became more common at visible depths.

- **BD phase**:  
  At current flux levels (~0.32 F⊕ at 0.25 AU), stellar heating sets the temperature of the observable atmosphere. Haze production remains significant but no longer completely blankets the disc. Banding, storms, and vertical cloud structure are prominent. Color contrasts between methane-rich blue-green regions and sulfide- or dust-laden brownish belts are now observable.

Melchior has not developed a massive ring system, though minor dusty rings or transient arc structures are possible from moonlet collisions and micrometeoroid impacts. Its large apparent size and changing appearance make it a dominant feature in the skies of Magnum and a key reference point for cultures and sciences that arise there.

## Physical basis

**Orbit and irradiation.** At 0.25 AU around a 0.15 M☉ star, Melchior's period is 117.5 d and its orbital speed 23.2 km/s; its Hill radius for 1.07 M♃ is 4.91 million km. It receives 0.32 F⊕ now (0.048–0.08 F⊕ in the Red Dwarf epoch), 8.6 times Jupiter's irradiation. The equilibrium temperature is 191 K for a Bond albedo of 0.30 and 184 K for 0.40, and 119–135 K in the Red Dwarf epoch.

**Bulk.** For 1.07 M♃ and the adopted ~71,500 km radius (a cold giant of this mass is expected at 0.95–1.0 R♃), the surface gravity is 26.5 m/s² and the escape velocity 61.6 km/s.

**Internal heat.** Scaling a Jupiter-like cooling law (L ∝ t⁻¹·³) from Jupiter's ~100 K at 4.6 Gyr gives:

| Age | Internal temperature (K) | Internal flux (W/m²) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Gyr | 164 | 41 |
| 10 Gyr | 78 | 2.1 |
| 100 Gyr | 37 | 0.10 |
| 1 Tyr | 17 | 0.0052 |
| 3 Tyr | 12 | 0.0012 |

Stellar absorption in the Red Dwarf epoch is 11–19 W/m², so after the first few tens of gigayears Melchior's own heat is negligible and the giant is powered by starlight alone.

**Spin and locking.** With a moment of inertia of 2.6×10⁴² kg m², a 10-hour spin carries 4.0×10³⁴ J of rotational energy and 4.5×10³⁸ kg m²/s of angular momentum. Locking to the star takes about 310 Gyr for Q = 10⁵ and far longer for the adopted dissipation, so the 10-hour spin persists. The synchronous radius is 164,500 km for the 10-hour spin, 862,000 km for a 5-day spin and 7.09 million km for the 118-day orbital period, which lies beyond the Hill radius.

**Tidal premise.** The moons stay near their present distances over trillions of years only if Melchior dissipates tides very weakly, Q ≳ 10⁸–10⁹. This is an adopted premise, as is the planetary magnetic field. Epochs are Red Dwarf (RD) 0–1 Tyr, Glow-Up (GU) 1–2.5 Tyr and Blue Dwarf (BD) 2.5 Tyr to the present, as defined on [[Methuselah]].

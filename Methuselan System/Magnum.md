# Magnum

*Ocean-covered super-Earth moon of Melchior*

**Magnum** is a large, ocean-covered super-Earth moon orbiting the gas giant [[Melchior]] in the [[Methuselan System]]. With a mass of approximately 3 M⊕ and a radius of 1.45 R⊕, Magnum possesses a surface gravity of ~1.4 g and supports a dense, biologically active atmosphere. It orbits Melchior at a distance of ~12 Melchior radii (~8.6×10⁵ km), corresponding to a star distance of ~0.25 AU from the K-dwarf star [[Methuselah]].

Magnum is the only known body in the system with an active surface–subsurface hydrosphere and a complex, long-lived biosphere. Life on Magnum has persisted for on the order of 2.5 trillion years, evolving from an ancient chemosynthetic deep biosphere during Methuselah’s Red Dwarf (RD) and early Glow-Up (GU) epochs into a photosynthetically enriched ocean world in the current Blue Dwarf (BD) epoch.

## Orbital configuration

Magnum is the largest of Melchior’s moons and occupies the middle position in a compact resonant system with the inner moon Clavelin and the outer moon Jéréboam. The three moons are locked in a near 1:2:4 mean-motion resonance:

> P_Clavelin : P_Magnum : P_Jéréboam ≈ 1 : 2 : 4

Approximate orbital parameters around Melchior (M ≈ 1.07 M♃):

- Clavelin:
  - Semimajor axis: ~7.6 Melchior radii (~5.4×10⁵ km)
  - Period: ~2.5 days

- **Magnum**:
  - Semimajor axis: ~12.0 Melchior radii (~8.6×10⁵ km)
  - Period: ~5.0 days

- Jéréboam:
  - Semimajor axis: ~19.0 Melchior radii (~1.36×10⁶ km)
  - Period: ~10.0 days

Magnum’s orbit is nearly circular and near Melchior’s equatorial plane, but the resonance maintains a small non-zero eccentricity (about 1–6×10⁻⁴). This eccentricity drives tidal heating in Magnum’s interior at ~10¹²–10¹³ W. Its strength can change as the orbits evolve.

### Subsatellite: Piccolo

Magnum hosts **Piccolo**, a young subsatellite formed by a recent impact on Magnum or on a former moon:

- Mass: ~0.005 M⊕
- Radius: ~0.24 R⊕ (~1,500 km)
- Density: ~2.0 g/cm³
- Composition: rocky–icy, spherical

Piccolo orbits Magnum at a distance of ~45,000 km (about 4.9 Magnum radii), with an orbital period of ~15.2 hours. This orbit lies:

- Within Magnum’s Hill sphere around Melchior (~123,000 km), at about 0.37 of its radius
- Outside Magnum’s fluid Roche limit (~31,500 km for Piccolo’s density)

Magnum rotates once per 5 days, much more slowly than Piccolo orbits, and Magnum's synchronous orbit (178,000 km) lies outside its Hill sphere. Tides raised on Magnum therefore pull Piccolo inward, at about 0.2–1.7 km per year for k₂/Q between 3×10⁻⁴ and 3×10⁻³, which brings it to the Roche limit within roughly 10⁴–10⁵ years. Piccolo is geologically young, perhaps a few thousand years old, and will break up into a ring around Magnum. The tidal heating it adds to Magnum is negligible over its short life. A moon this massive cannot be primordial in a system trillions of years old.

### Rotation and tides

Magnum is tidally locked to Melchior, always showing the same hemisphere toward the giant. Its sidereal rotation period is about five Earth days, equal to its orbital period around Melchior.

Because the system is also in orbit around Methuselah with a period of ~118 Earth days, the apparent position of the star in Magnum’s sky slowly shifts relative to its Melchior-fixed longitude. As a result, the mean solar day is about 5.22 Earth days for the prograde orbit. The interplay between orbital motion around Melchior and around Methuselah produces a pattern of moving “subsolar tracks” across the surface.

Melchior dominates the tides in Magnum’s ocean and lithosphere. Methuselah contributes a much weaker periodic tide. The small eccentricity maintained by the resonance chain drives ongoing flexing and internal friction, a weak but persistent heat source (see Geological activity and heat sources).

## Physical characteristics

Magnum is a rocky super-Earth with a thin global ocean over a rocky seafloor.

Approximate bulk properties:

- Mass: 3 M⊕
- Radius: 1.45 R⊕ (9,240 km)
- Mean density: ~5.4 g/cm³
- Surface gravity: ~1.43 g (14.0 m/s²)
- Escape velocity: ~16.1 km/s

A rocky planet of 3 M⊕ with an Earth-like iron fraction has a radius of 1.40–1.45 R⊕ (densities 6.0–5.4 g/cm³), which is the adopted range. This is the basis of the vent-based biosphere: the ocean is only ~0.1% of Magnum's mass (a 15 km mean depth over 1.07×10⁹ km²), so liquid water touches the rocky seafloor everywhere. A density of 3.7 g/cm³ would need about 30% water and would bury the seafloor under 130–185 km of ocean and high-pressure ice, which Magnum does not have.

The structure is:

- A large iron–nickel core, possibly partly liquid.
- A thick silicate mantle with embedded volatiles.
- A thin global ocean in contact with the rocky seafloor.
- Hydrated crust and sediments, with scattered ice at high latitudes and cold climate excursions.

Magnum’s mantle convection and any core dynamo depend on its thermal history. At ~3 trillion years the original radiogenic heat is gone, so convection and any magnetic field are weak and are sustained mainly by tidal heating and residual primordial heat; this is an adopted picture, not a modeled result.

### Surface and ocean

The surface is dominated by ocean:

- Ocean coverage: ~90% of surface area
- Land coverage: ~10%, mostly as archipelagos, microcontinents, and volcanic plateaus

Estimated ocean depths:

- Typical: ~15 km mean
- Deep basins: a few tens of kilometers
- Shallow shelves: a few kilometers or less

The ocean is compositionally Earth-like (H₂O with dissolved salts), but salinity and chemistry have evolved under the influence of:

- Long-term hydrothermal circulation
- Very slow weathering of limited land area
- Extensive biological processing over trillions of years

Sea ice is present at high latitudes and during colder climatic excursions, but in the present BD epoch Magnum’s surface is largely ice-free in the mid- and low-latitude belts.

## Atmosphere and climate

Magnum’s atmosphere is thick and evolutionarily complex. It has passed through at least three major regimes tied to Methuselah’s luminosity history:

- RD epoch: extreme greenhouse, very high pressure
- GU epoch: transitional, partially melting surface
- BD epoch: temperate, biologically regulated atmosphere

### Red Dwarf epoch (RD)

During Methuselah’s early RD epoch (L ≈ 0.003–0.005 L☉), the stellar flux at Magnum’s orbit was only ~0.05–0.08 F⊕. With a reference Bond albedo of 0.30 and uniform heat redistribution, the stellar equilibrium temperature would have been ~120–135 K.

Instead, Magnum possessed a massive atmosphere with:

- Total surface pressure: ~30 bar
- Dominant components: N₂ (bulk), CO₂ (up to ~15–20%, or about 5–6 bar, the most that can stay as vapor at 200 K), CH₄ and noble gases as minor components

This thick greenhouse layer (a gray optical depth near 6–7 is needed to lift 128 K to 200 K) left the surface near 200 K, with liquid water persisting at depth where the weak tidal and residual heat sustained hydrothermal circulation. The atmosphere was likely CO₂-rich enough for cloud formation and possibly CO₂ ice at cold traps, but global collapse was prevented by internal heat and mixing.

### Glow-Up epoch (GU)

As Methuselah brightened through the late RD and GU epochs (up to L ≈ 0.02 L☉), flux at Magnum’s orbit increased to ~0.32 F⊕. Under a still-thick greenhouse blanket, this produced:

- Surface temperatures in the ~240–260 K range over warm regions and seasons
- Localized brine pockets and thin-ice habitats
- Geothermally warmed openings in the ice

During this time, Magnum’s atmosphere began to lose CO₂ to:

- Silicate weathering and carbonate formation
- Burial in sediments
- Early biological fixation

Methane underwent more rapid photodissociation under stronger UV and visible photons, while nitrogen remained as the dominant background gas. Surface pressures gradually declined from the RD peak but stayed well into the multi-bar regime.

The GU epoch saw the emergence and spread of photosynthesis, with pigments adapted to Methuselah’s shifting M→K spectrum. Phototrophs began to influence atmospheric composition, especially locally over melt zones and shallow seas.

### Blue Dwarf epoch (BD, present)

In the current BD epoch (L ≈ 0.02 L☉), Magnum receives ~0.32 F⊕. Its modern atmosphere is:

- Total surface pressure: ~2.5–4.0 bar (the scale height at 270 K is ~5.5 km)
- Composition (typical, for ~3 bar):
  - N₂: ~55–65%
  - O₂: ~15–20% (about 0.45–0.6 bar)
  - CO₂: ~5–8% (about 0.15–0.25 bar)
  - Ar: ~5%
  - CH₄: trace (rapidly recycled biosignature gas with a short atmospheric lifetime in the presence of O₂)

Magnum has a mean surface temperature of ~270 K and modest latitudinal gradients. Holding 270 K at 0.32 F⊕ needs a greenhouse optical depth of about 4 (Earth's is ~0.8; a bare gray atmosphere of this mass and 600 ppm CO₂ would reach only ~230 K). The adopted answer is a CO₂-rich air (0.15–0.25 bar, which adds roughly +20 K by the logarithmic CO₂ law), a dark red-absorbing ocean (Bond albedo ~0.3) and the collision-induced absorption of 3 bar of N₂ and O₂; the budget is a rough estimate (stated range 255–270 K) rather than a radiative-transfer result. Because only ~10% of the surface is land, silicate weathering draws CO₂ down slowly, so a high CO₂ level can persist. Climate is buffered by:

- The high heat capacity of the deep global ocean
- Strong atmospheric circulation in the thick air
- Long-term biological feedbacks that regulate CO₂ and CH₄ levels

Weather includes large storm systems, extended cloud decks, and frequent fog and mist over cool waters, but the climate is overall stable on million- to billion-year timescales.

## Biosphere

Main article: [[Life on Magnum]]

Magnum hosts an old, structurally complex biosphere with an evolutionary history stretching back roughly 2.5 trillion years.

Broad phases:

**Deep chemoworld (RD and early GU)**

Life originated in hydrothermal vent systems and deep crustal aquifers, fully chemosynthetic and adapted to tidal and geothermal energy sources. Over >1 trillion years (about 0.5–2 Tyr after formation), this deep biosphere produced multicellular colonies, intricate symbiotic webs, and a rich microbial ecology, all in permanent darkness.

**Photosynthetic transition (late GU to early BD)**

As surface conditions warmed and melt zones expanded, phototrophic metabolisms emerged, initially as add-ons to already complex chemosynthetic lineages. Oxygenic photosynthesis appeared and slowly oxygenated surface waters and the atmosphere. Photosynthesis did not start complexity; it was absorbed into pre-existing complex life.

**Present ocean world (BD)**

Oxygenic photosynthesis is now widespread. The ocean supports layered ecosystems from sunlit surface waters down to the deep chemosphere. The dominant macroscopic life forms are not plant–animal analogues in the terrestrial sense but modular “planimals”: mobile or semi-sessile organisms that incorporate photosynthetic modules alongside grazing, filtering, or predation.

A clade of gigantic, radially symmetric macrofauna, known as Radionauts, occupies ecological niches analogous to whales, large filter-feeders, and megaherbivores. These slow-drifting, partially photosynthetic animals can live for millennia, relying on both captured sunlight and grazing.

Terrestrial ecosystems are limited and mostly confined to:

- Coastal and intertidal zones
- Small islands and plateaus
- Microbial mats, lichenoid colonies, and slow-growing sessile planimals in high-light environments

Complex land life never fully rivals the oceanic biosphere due to limited land area, thick air, and the ease of buoyancy in water.

## Geological activity and heat sources

At an age of ~3 trillion years Magnum has no radiogenic heat left: uranium, thorium and potassium-40 are gone after hundreds of half-lives, and any primordial heat is largely lost. Its heat source is tidal dissipation from its slightly eccentric orbit around Melchior, maintained by the 1:2:4 resonance. That heat is limited by the energy the system can supply: tides on Melchior can deliver at most ~8×10¹¹ W at a Melchior tidal quality factor Q = 10⁹ and ~8×10¹² W at Q = 10⁸, and a more dissipative Melchior would have moved its moons by many times their present distances over 2.5 Tyr. Magnum's tidal heat is therefore adopted as ~10¹²–10¹³ W (0.001–0.008 W/m², about 1–10% of Earth's interior heat flux), requiring an eccentricity of about 1–6×10⁻⁴; a resonance eccentricity of 0.01–0.03 would give 3–58 W/m² and cost more energy than Melchior's whole spin (4×10³⁴ J). The low-Q Melchior is an adopted premise, not derived.

Geological expressions of this weak activity:

- Hydrothermal vent systems along seafloor fractures and ridges, which support deep chemosynthetic ecosystems. The total heat (10¹²–10¹³ W) is comparable to Earth's total hydrothermal output, though spread over a smaller area (the Earth comparison is cited from memory).
- Occasional plume-driven volcanism and cryovolcanism on ocean-floor uplifts and rare landmasses.
- Slow, plastic deformation of the lithosphere rather than fast plate tectonics; Magnum likely lacks Earth-style drifting plates but has long-lived upwellings and downwellings.

## Sky and observational appearance

From Magnum’s surface, the sky is shaped by:

- A thick, ~3-bar atmosphere creating strong Rayleigh scattering and deep blue to blue-green/cyan hues in clear air.
- Frequent clouds and hazes, especially over warm currents and upwelling zones.

Prominent objects:

**Melchior**

Dominates the sky on the sub-Melchior hemisphere. With a radius of ~71,500 km and an orbital distance of ~8.6×10⁵ km, it spans about 9.5 degrees of arc—about twenty times the apparent size of Sol from Earth. Bands, storms, and color contrasts on Melchior are easily visible, especially near opposition.

**Methuselah**

Appears as a bright orange star with an angular diameter slightly larger than the Sun’s as seen from Earth (~1.2× solar apparent size). Daylight is slightly dimmer and redder than on Earth but still clearly “day,” not permanent twilight, thanks to the thick atmosphere.

Eclipses of Methuselah by Melchior are common and dramatic: Magnum passes through Melchior's umbra once per 5-day orbit near the eclipse seasons for about 3.2 hours (2.6% of the orbit), producing moving shadows and long penumbral phases over the surface of Magnum.

## Physical basis

**Radius and density.** For a rocky planet of 3 M⊕ the radius is 1.35–1.5 R⊕:

| Radius (R⊕) | Density (g/cm³) | Gravity (g) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.35 | 6.7 | 1.65 |
| 1.40 | 6.0 | 1.53 |
| 1.45 | 5.4 | 1.43 |
| 1.50 | 4.9 | 1.33 |

Magnum adopts R = 1.45 R⊕ (9,238 km): density 5.43 g/cm³, gravity 14.0 m/s², escape velocity 16.1 km/s, surface area 1.07×10⁹ km², ocean-and-rock volume 3.30×10¹² km³, and an atmospheric scale height of 5.5 km at 270 K. The 15 km ocean is 0.09% of the mass. A deeper ocean cannot sit on a rocky seafloor here: a low-density composition of 3.7 g/cm³ (rock 6.0, water and ice 2.0 g/cm³) would need 31% water, and even 1% water is a 129 km ocean, whose base freezes into ice VII at about 2 GPa (185 km depth at 10.8 m/s²) and buries the seafloor.

**Greenhouse.** For a gray atmosphere, T_s = T_eq (1 + 0.75 τ)¼. A surface at 270 K from T_eq = 190.6 K needs τ = 4.0 (4.8 for T_eq = 184 K), against 0.84 for Earth; τ = 1.5 gives 230 K. In the Red Dwarf epoch, 200 K from 128 K needs τ = 6.6, and CO₂ vapor is limited to about 6 bar at 200 K. The CO₂ logarithmic law (5.35 ln(p/p₀) W/m², 0.8 K per W/m²), from a 1.8 mbar baseline (600 ppm in 3 bar), gives only +14, +17, +20 and +22 K for 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 bar of CO₂. The full 270 K therefore also rests on collision-induced absorption by the 3 bar of N₂ and O₂, a dark ocean (albedo ~0.3) and water-vapor feedback, and the estimated range is 255–270 K. The adopted atmosphere has 0.15–0.25 bar CO₂ (5–8% of ~3 bar) and 0.45–0.6 bar O₂.

**Tidal heating.** The synchronous-body formula is 10.5 (k₂/Q) G M² R⁵ n e² / a⁶, with Melchior's mass, a = 8.6×10⁵ km and n = 2π/5 d. For the adopted radius and k₂/Q = 0.003:

| Eccentricity | 10⁻⁴ | 2×10⁻⁴ | 3×10⁻⁴ | 5×10⁻⁴ | 10⁻³ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Surface heat flux (W/m²) | 0.0002 | 0.0008 | 0.0018 | 0.0049 | 0.020 |

The values are five times larger for k₂/Q = 0.015. The adopted eccentricity of 1–6×10⁻⁴ gives 0.001–0.008 W/m², a total of 10¹²–10¹³ W. The energy for this must be supplied by tides on Melchior, and the supply is 8.2×10¹³, 8.2×10¹², 8.2×10¹¹ and 8.2×10¹⁰ W for Melchior Q = 10⁷, 10⁸, 10⁹ and 10¹⁰. Magnum would migrate outward by 0.158 m/yr at Q = 10⁵ (a timescale of 5.5 Gyr), 0.0016 m/yr at 10⁷ (545 Gyr) and 1.6×10⁻⁵ m/yr at 10⁹ (54 Tyr), so a heat supply of 10¹²–10¹³ W requires Q ≳ 10⁸–10⁹.

**Piccolo.** Piccolo is 0.005 M⊕, 1,530 km in radius, of density 2.0 g/cm³, at 45,000 km (4.87 Magnum radii) with a period of 15.2 h. Tides raised on Magnum shrink its orbit at da/dt = −3 (k₂/Q)(m/M)(R/a)⁵ n a:

| k₂/Q (Magnum) | 3×10⁻⁴ | 3×10⁻³ | 10⁻² |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inspiral rate (m/yr) | 170 | 1,700 | 5,660 |
| Time to reach 32,000 km (yr) | 77,000 | 7,700 | 2,300 |

Piccolo is therefore a young impact moon with a lifetime of at most about 10⁵ years.

**Eclipses.** Melchior's umbra is 1.95×10⁷ km long. Magnum lies in shadow for 2.6% of its orbit (3.2 h per 5 d) near the eclipse seasons. Melchior spans 9.5° and Methuselah 0.64° (1.2 Sun-diameters).

**Adopted values.** The 3 M⊕ mass, the rocky seafloor, the 15 km ocean, the 1.45 R⊕ radius, the CO₂-rich greenhouse, the low-dissipation Melchior, the resonance eccentricity and the weak dynamo are adopted or rough values, not derived; the greenhouse is not a radiative-transfer calculation and no tidal evolution of the moons is integrated.

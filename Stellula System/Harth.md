**Harth** is an Earth-sized, Earth-mass, tidally locked terrestrial planet orbiting the ultracool red dwarf [[Stellula]] at a distance of 0.012034 AU. It receives essentially the same stellar flux as Earth (≈1.0013 F🜨) and lies near the center of Stellula's habitable zone. Harth completes one orbit every 1.7048 days and keeps the same hemisphere permanently facing the star.

At the time of first human contact, Harth's native biosphere was in a late pre-Cambrian state, with only simple multicellular life in its oceans and little to no complex life on land. The first and only human expedition, a profit-driven corporate venture, judged the world too biologically primitive to exploit. As it departed for permanent settlement on the anti-stellar side of [[Tito]], the expedition deliberately used Harth as a biological dumping ground, seeding dozens of sites along the terminator with waste, surplus livestock, crops and engineered microorganisms.

Ten thousand years later, Harth hosts a rapidly expanding hybrid biosphere whose primary producers are far-red photosynthesizers: native microbes and the engineered strains the expedition dumped, both using chlorophyll d and f and bacteriochlorophylls that absorb at 700–1,000 nm. Descendants of the dumped Earth animals and plants occupy the shallow seas and the best-lit land, overprinting much of the planet's independent evolutionary history while leaving native lineages in scattered refugia.

## Orbit and rotation

Harth orbits Stellula at 0.012034 AU on a nearly circular, coplanar orbit with a period of 1.7048 days. At this distance the planet travels around the star at about 76.7962 km/s. Strong tidal forces have synchronized Harth's rotation with its orbital period, locking one hemisphere in perpetual daylight and the opposite hemisphere in perpetual night. The equatorial rotation speed is ~272.1 m/s, and the planet's axial tilt is effectively zero with respect to its orbital plane.

From Harth, Stellula has an angular diameter of roughly 4.43°, about eight times larger than the Sun appears from Earth. The water-rich super-Earth [[Tito]] orbits closer to the star and has an adopted near-3:2 mean-motion ratio with Harth: Tito completes three orbits for every two of Harth (period ratio 1.4999). Tito's disc (4.79°) slightly exceeds Stellula's (4.43°) from Harth, which is why the eclipse is total.

The synodic period between Tito and Harth is approximately 3.4102 days. At a central alignment near the substellar point, Tito eclipses Stellula for about 39 minutes from first to last contact, including about 1½ minutes of totality. The depth varies across the dayside. Near the eclipse track the recurring change in light provides a timing cue for some organisms.

## Physical characteristics

Harth is close to a physical twin of Earth, with a mass of 1.0 M🜨 and a mean radius of 6,371 km. Its mean density is 5.51 g/cm³, consistent with a differentiated structure consisting of a metallic core and silicate mantle. Surface gravity is 9.81 m/s², and the escape velocity is 11.19 km/s.

The planet's surface area (5.10×10^8 km²) and volume (1.083×10¹² km³) match Earth's within measurement precision. Slight oblateness (flattening ≈0.0033) arises from rotation, though the tidal lock and short day length mean Harth does not experience seasonal cycles in the way that tilted, rotating planets do.

The stellar flux at Harth's orbit, combined with a roughly Earth-like albedo and greenhouse effect, yields a global-mean surface temperature near 288 K. The thermal contrast between permanent dayside, twilight ring and nightside is moderated by atmospheric and oceanic heat transport, as well as by the planet's inherited Earth-like climate feedbacks. Stellula's red and near-infrared light scatters less (Rayleigh scattering falls steeply with wavelength) and is absorbed more by water vapor, ice and snow than sunlight is, so the true Bond albedo is probably somewhat lower than Earth's 0.30; 0.30 is adopted.

### Tidal heating

Harth is 1.8 million km from a 0.08 M☉ star and in a 3:2 mean-motion relation with the 10.2 M⊕ Tito and the 14 M♃ Gigafusc. N-body integrations give Harth an osculating eccentricity of about 0.01–0.12 near the adopted ratio (about 0.002–0.005 from Gigafusc alone). With ordinary rocky-planet dissipation (k₂/Q ≈ 0.003) the tidal heat flux of a synchronous planet here is 1.4 W/m² at e = 0.001 and 137 W/m² at e = 0.01 (Io's is about 2 W/m², Earth's interior heat 0.09 W/m²), so a conventional Harth would be volcanic to the point of a molten surface. Harth's Earth-like geology is therefore an **adopted speculative premise**: an unusually rigid, low-dissipation interior that holds the tidal heat flux at about 0.1–1 W/m². The required dissipation is orders of magnitude below any known rocky planet; the premise is not derived from the physics.

### Light for photosynthesis

For a 2,000 K blackbody delivering 1 F⊕, the photon flux at Harth is about 56 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ in 400–700 nm (2.4% of the 2,280 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ Earth receives from the Sun above its atmosphere) but about 1,000 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ in 700–1,100 nm (36% of Earth's 2,750). Only 0.8% of Stellula's power falls in the visible band and 9.3% in 700–1,100 nm; the output peaks at about 1.45 µm. Pigments that absorb at 700–1,000 nm therefore matter far more than the green-plant chlorophylls tuned to 400–700 nm.

## Atmosphere and climate

Prior to human contact, Harth's atmosphere was already partially oxygenated by native photosynthetic microbes, with significant N₂ and CO₂ and rising O₂ levels. The deliberate introduction of Earth plants, algae and microbial communities accelerated biological productivity and altered atmospheric composition over the following ten millennia.

At the current epoch, the atmosphere has approximate bulk properties. Gas fractions are expressed on a dry basis, with humidity reported separately:

**Harth Atmospheric Composition** (present epoch; approximate)

| Gas | Volume (%) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **N₂** | ~78.9–79.0% (dry-air balance) | Primary buffer gas; similar to Earth's nitrogen reservoir |
| **O₂** | ~20% | Elevated by Earth-derived photosynthesis; supports complex aerobes |
| **Ar** | ~1% | Inert noble gas, accumulated from crustal outgassing |
| **CO₂** | ~0.03–0.05% | Greenhouse gas; regulated by ocean chemistry and hybrid biosphere |
| **H₂O (vapor)** | variable, reported separately from dry fractions | Controls cloud cover and latent heat transport |
| **Trace gases** | <0.1% | CH₄, N₂O, organosulfur compounds from mixed native/Earth biology |

Total surface pressure is around 1.0 atm, with a scale height of ~8 km. Clouds and weather patterns are strongly organized by the permanent heating pattern:

- A broad, convective plume over the substellar region, with thick clouds and frequent storms.
- High-speed jets in the upper atmosphere flowing from dayside to nightside across the terminator.
- Precipitation belts ringing the dayside and inner twilight zone.

The nightside, lacking direct stellar input, would freeze solid without heat transport. In practice, strong winds and ocean currents carry energy from the illuminated hemisphere, keeping much of the inner nightside above the freezing point of water, especially near the terminator-facing coasts.

Stellula's X-ray and ultraviolet output threatens a 1 atm atmosphere at this distance: the energy-limited loss lifetime at 10% efficiency would be 6 Myr for an X-ray output of 10⁻⁴ of the star's luminosity, 60 Myr at 10⁻⁵ and 600 Myr at 10⁻⁶. The adopted Stellula is quiet (about 10⁻⁶) with a heavy-gas escape efficiency of a few percent, and volcanic and biological outgassing resupply the air; this is an assumption.

The regular Tito eclipses drive short, quasi-periodic dips in dayside temperature and abrupt changes in radiative forcing. Over ten thousand years, some Earth-derived organisms have begun to entrain their physiology to this 3.4102-day cycle, using it as a timing cue for foraging, reproduction or dormancy, especially near the substellar region where the eclipses are deepest.

## Biosphere and evolutionary history

### Native pre-contact life

Before human arrival, Harth's biosphere had undergone a long period of native evolution. Life originated in the oceans and diversified into:

- Extensive microbial mats on coasts and shallow sea floors.
- Planktonic communities of photosynthetic and heterotrophic microbes.
- Early multicellular organisms: soft-bodied fronds, ribbon-like grazers and simple filter-feeders, mostly confined to marine habitats.

Hard skeletons and complex terrestrial ecosystems had not yet evolved. Land surfaces were largely barren, with only thin films of microbial life in moist areas. Oxygen levels were rising but had not yet triggered a native "Cambrian explosion" of complex animal body plans.

### Corporate contamination event

The first human presence on Harth came in the form of a short two-year survey mission mounted by a profit-seeking corporate consortium. Remote sensing had suggested a rich, habitable biosphere; ground truth revealed a world of slime and mud, with no forests, no animals to exploit, and no immediate commercial use.

As the mission wound down and plans shifted to permanent settlement on the anti-stellar side of [[Tito]], the company chose Harth as a convenient biological disposal site. Rather than pay to lift surplus biomass, animals, seeds and waste into orbit, they:

- Established dozens of dump zones along the terminator belt, where temperatures and illumination were most Earthlike.
- Jettisoned surplus livestock, lab animals, pets and pests.
- Dumped crates of crop seeds, weedy plants and soil, and engineered far-red-adapted microbial strains.
- Released stored compost, sewage, medical waste and bioreactor cultures.
- Abandoned malfunctioning equipment, hydroponic systems and contaminated gear.

Each dump zone acted as a compact, self-contained fragment of the late-Earth biosphere: a ready-made food web including producers, consumers, decomposers and parasites, all adapted to oxygen-rich, temperate conditions.

The expedition then departed for Tito with no attempt at remediation, leaving Harth with a ring of highly invasive Earth ecosystems stitched along its most habitable latitudes.

### Ten thousand years later: hybrid biosphere

In the ten millennia since the dumping, Harth has developed a hybrid biosphere. The timescale is short relative to evolutionary radiations, but long enough for Earth species to spread, adapt and collide with native life.

Key features include:

- **Microbial dominance near the terminator.** Earth bacteria and fungi have largely displaced native microbes in soils, shallow sediments and surface waters around the original dump sites and along routes of spread. Native microbes persist in deep ocean, extreme environments and within some native multicellular hosts.

- **Earth-derived land ecosystems.** Land connected to the terminator belt supports plant cover recognizably descended from Earth crops and weeds (grasses, legumes, shrubs), but with only 2.4% of Earth's visible light the plants are low-light scrub: slow-growing, dark-leaved understory forms and mat-forming mosses and grasses, with trees rare and confined to the best-lit sites or to introduced far-red-adapted strains. Earth arthropods, worms and small vertebrates occupy terrestrial niches in these regions, living largely on far-red-fixed biomass from microbial mats and soils.

- **Shallow-sea invasions.** Coastal shelves and inland seas near the terminator harbor mixed communities of Earth algae, invertebrates and fish intermingled with native soft-bodied forms. Competition, predation and novel symbioses are reshaping these ecosystems but have not yet produced radically new morphologies.

- **Native refugia.** Deep oceans, polar nightside basins and chemically unusual environments (hypersaline lakes, hydrothermal systems) remain dominated by native lineages. Here, evolution continues along trajectories only weakly influenced by Earth arrivals, though large-scale biogeochemical cycles have been altered by the changing atmosphere.

- **Limited evolutionary divergence.** Most Earth-derived lineages remain close to their ancestral forms: feral descendants of domestic animals, weeds, and common commensals. Selective pressures from the tidally locked climate and interactions with native biota are beginning to produce geographic variants, but full speciation and large-scale radiations lie mostly in the future.

The net effect is that Harth's eventual path to complex life has been strongly biased toward Earth ancestry on land and in shallow seas, while its deep marine biosphere still preserves much of its independent origin.

## Biogeography

The young hybrid biosphere organizes naturally into a set of broad zones:

- **Terminator belt** – A ring around the planet following the permanent twilight zone, including most of the original dump sites. This region is the primary Earth-dominated biome, with dark scrub, grasslands and sparse woodlands in the best-lit places, resembling a dim echo of Earth's temperate and subtropical belts. Ferals and former domestic species are common here.

- **Dayside interior** – Toward the substellar point, higher temperatures and intense illumination limit terrestrial colonization. Earth plants and animals occupy cooler highlands and river valleys, while native microbial mats still dominate the hottest lowlands and shallow evaporitic basins. Some Earth-derived species have begun to adapt life cycles to the Tito eclipse cycle, using the periodic dimming as an activity window.

- **Inner nightside** – Moving outward from the terminator into the night hemisphere, temperatures drop, but residual heat transport keeps coastal and lowland regions above freezing. Earth plants and cold-tolerant animals have extended into this band, forming boreal-like vegetation near the twilight boundary where usable light reaches the surface. Farther into darkness, ecosystems depend on imported organic matter or local chemical energy.

- **Outer nightside** – Farther into the dark hemisphere, conditions become too cold for most Earth macroscopic life. Native chemoautotrophs, ice-associated microbes and simple multicellular organisms inhabit isolated liquid-water sites, such as geothermal oases and deep subsurface aquifers.

- **Hybrid marginal seas** – Shallow seas and continental shelves adjacent to the terminator host mixed communities where native fronds, worms and filter-feeders coexist with Earth invertebrates and fishes. Food webs are unstable and still responding to the collision of two evolutionary histories.

- **Native deep ocean** – The deep global ocean, especially beneath the nightside and dayside interiors, remains the stronghold of native life. Here, Earth influence is largely confined to drifting microbes and occasional detritus input; the main native lineages continue to diversify in relative isolation.

## Eclipse-driven rhythms

Although ten thousand years is short, some Earth-derived species have begun to exploit the regular Tito eclipse cycle:

- Plankton near the substellar point show vertical migration linked to the 3.4102-day dimming, using the brief decrease in irradiance to surface and feed without overheating.
- Certain feral herbivores in the inner dayside undertake foraging excursions that peak in the cooler hours around an eclipse, retreating to shaded refuges afterward.
- Flowers and spore-producing structures in the hotter dayside fringe initiate opening or release near the onset of eclipses, when pollinators and dispersers are most active.

These early adjustments lay the groundwork for later, deeper integration of the eclipse rhythm into Harth's biosphere as evolution proceeds over longer timescales.

## Human and historical context

At the current epoch, there is no permanent human presence on Harth. The original corporate survey installations have long since decayed or been buried beneath sediments and invading vegetation, leaving only subtle geochemical traces and an unmistakable genetic signal in the planet's living organisms.

From the perspective of later interstellar civilizations or historians based on [[Tito]], Harth is recorded primarily as:

- A scientifically valuable example of an independent origin of life that was partially overwritten by human negligence and cost-cutting.
- A long-term natural experiment in the interaction between two separate biospheres on a tidally locked, near-Earth planet.
- A cautionary tale about uncontrolled biological contamination and the ecological impact of using living worlds as dumping grounds.

Future epochs may see intelligent species arise on Harth from hybrid lineages, inheriting both native and Earth ancestry and eventually turning their attention toward the lights of Tito and the shadowed brown dwarf [[Gigafusc]] that share their sky.

## Physical basis

**Forced eccentricity.** Tito's near-exact 3:2 resonance pumps Harth's osculating eccentricity to 0.01–0.12 (a mean of 0.035–0.049 over 10⁵ years); Gigafusc alone would force only 0.002–0.005. Harth's eccentricity damps in 1.06 Myr. Detuning the period ratio lowers the mean eccentricity only slowly (0.021, 0.012, 0.006 and 0.004 at ratios 1.52, 1.55, 1.60 and 1.70) and the eclipse stops being total above a ratio of about 1.55, so the orbit stays as written and the heating question falls on Harth's interior.

**Tidal heating.** The synchronous-rotator heating is 10.5 (k₂/Q) G M★² R⁵ n e² / a⁶. For k₂/Q = 0.003:

| Eccentricity | 10⁻⁴ | 3×10⁻⁴ | 10⁻³ | 3×10⁻³ | 10⁻² | 3×10⁻² |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Surface heat flux (W/m²) | 0.0137 | 0.124 | 1.37 | 12.4 | 137 | 1,235 |

An Earth-like flux of 0.1 W/m² needs e of about 2.7×10⁻⁴ and 1 W/m² needs about 8.5×10⁻⁴. At the forced eccentricities Harth would be intensely heated unless its tidal dissipation is far lower than a typical rocky planet's, so a quiet interior with a flux of 0.1–1 W/m² is a flagged speculative premise.

**Light and the far-red biosphere.** Photon fluxes for a 2,000 K blackbody at about 1 F⊕:

| Band | Harth (µmol m⁻² s⁻¹) | Earth (µmol m⁻² s⁻¹) | Harth, share of power | Earth, share of power |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 400–700 nm | 55.8 | 2,280 | 0.78% | 36.7% |
| 700–1,100 nm | 1,001 | 2,749 | 9.3% | 27.8% |

Photons are plentiful only beyond 700 nm. Native and engineered strains therefore use chlorophylls d and f and bacteriochlorophylls, and Earth plants survive only as low-light scrub. The Earth values are for a 5,772 K blackbody.

**Adopted values.** The 3:2 mean-motion relation with Tito, the total eclipse and the 0.012034 AU orbit are adopted. Harth's low tidal dissipation, the quiet-star X-ray assumption, the 0.30 albedo and the far-red biosphere are adopted or flagged assumptions, not derived results. The eclipse response is strongest near the substellar track. Later cultures interpreting Tito's lights belong to a subsequent era, with their origins and dates open. The vault title is Harth; the reference epoch is ten millennia after the corporate survey and dumping.

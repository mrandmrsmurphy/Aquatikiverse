**Virans** is a green, methane-rich mini-Neptune orbiting the ultracool red dwarf [[Stellula]] at a distance of 0.0330 AU. With a mass of 13.0 M🜨 and a radius of 3.84 R🜨, it lies just beyond the system's frost line and receives about 0.1331 F🜨 from Stellula. Virans is tidally locked to its star and completes one orbit every 7.7414 days.

The planet is encircled by a compact system of five moons. Three of these – Eirene, Pelagis and Thalure – form a 1:2:4 Laplace resonance chain whose repeating conjunctions provide a natural timekeeping system for observers elsewhere in the [[Stellula System]]. From the surfaces of [[Tito]] and [[Harth]], Virans appears as a pale green disk in the sky, sometimes accompanied by its largest moon Thalure as a faint companion.

Ten thousand years after human colonists contaminated Harth and migrated permanently to Tito's dark side, Virans remains largely untouched, serving primarily as a distant, aesthetically striking object and a precise celestial clock.

## Orbit and rotation

Virans orbits Stellula at 0.0330 AU on a nearly circular path with an orbital period of 7.7414 days. At this distance, the planet travels around the star at about 46.3754 km/s. The orbit is close to coplanar with those of [[Tito]], [[Harth]] and [[Gigafusc]], contributing to the system's frequent alignments and mutual transits.

Tidal forces have synchronized Virans's rotation with its orbital period, so the same hemisphere always faces Stellula. The equatorial rotation velocity at the 1-bar level is ~0.2298 km/s. With effectively zero obliquity and a stationary substellar point, Virans lacks seasonal variations; its climate is driven instead by the permanent dayside–nightside heating contrast, internal heat flux and episodic perturbations from its satellite system.

Virans orbits just beyond the system's frost line at ≈0.0325 AU (where the equilibrium temperature falls to ~170 K and water ice condenses), where volatile ices can condense and be retained. Its Hill radius with respect to Stellula is about 0.0018 AU (~270,000 km), leaving room for a tight but dynamically rich satellite system.

**Dynamics.** Virans lies at 80% of the circumstellar stability limit (0.041 AU for the Stellula–Gigafusc pair), and Gigafusc's period is 4.88 times Virans's, near a 5:1 ratio. Gigafusc raises Virans's osculating eccentricity to about 0.04 (0.07 at most) in N-body integrations; the orbit stays stable in 2,000-year runs and in the longer screens recorded on [[Stellula System#Global dynamical stability]]. Tidal damping inside the planet cannot erase this forced eccentricity.

## Physical characteristics

With 13.0 M🜨 and radius 3.84 R🜨, Virans has a mean density of ~1.26 g/cm³, consistent with a rock–ice core enveloped by a thick hydrogen–helium atmosphere enriched in heavier volatiles. A Neptune-like composition of this mass would be about 3.3 R⊕, so the larger radius needs a hydrogen–helium envelope of roughly 5–10% of the mass (an estimate from published mass–radius relations, not recomputed). The surface gravity at the reference 1-bar level is 8.66 m/s² (~0.88 g), and the escape velocity from that level is about 20.58 km/s.

Virans's equilibrium temperature, given its stellar flux and assumed Bond albedo ~0.45, is ~145 K. Internal heat and atmospheric circulation raise temperatures in the lower atmosphere above this value, particularly on the permanent dayside, while the nightside upper atmosphere cools efficiently to space.

The planet's visible coloration is dominated by methane absorption bands, Rayleigh scattering and a complex hydrocarbon haze, which together give Virans its characteristic soft green hue when viewed in reflected light.

## Atmosphere and appearance

Virans possesses a deep, layered atmosphere with an approximate bulk composition of:

- H₂ and He as dominant constituents
- CH₄ at percent-level mixing ratios
- N₂ and CO at trace levels
- Higher hydrocarbons (C₂H₆, C₂H₂) and photochemical hazes

On the dayside, strong irradiation from Stellula drives convection and high-altitude haze formation. Photolysis of methane and other hydrocarbons produces a thin, diffuse layer that imparts a muted, jade-like tint to the planet. On the nightside, temperatures drop and some hydrocarbon species condense, forming patchy, high-altitude clouds whose distribution is influenced by the motion of the moons.

From Harth at favorable geometry, Virans can reach an angular diameter of nearly 0.9° in the sky, slightly larger than Earth's Moon appears from Earth. Thalure, the largest moon, can sometimes be resolved as a tiny separate point of light trailing the planet, while the remaining satellites are detectable only with telescopes.

## Satellite system

Virans hosts five known moons, arranged within roughly half its Hill radius. The three largest regular satellites – Eirene, Pelagis and Thalure – participate in a 1:2:4 Laplace resonance reminiscent of the Galilean moons of Jupiter, while an inner rocky moon and an outer irregular complete the system.

### Laplace resonance chain

The working model adopts a 1:2:4 resonance chain for Eirene, Pelagis and Thalure.

- Orbital periods:  
  - Eirene: 0.3530 days  
  - Pelagis: 0.7060 days  
  - Thalure: 1.4120 days  

satisfying approximately:

$$
\frac{1}{P_\mathrm{Eirene}} - \frac{3}{P_\mathrm{Pelagis}} + \frac{2}{P_\mathrm{Thalure}} \approx 0.
$$

The idealized periods give the following cycles:

- For every orbit of Thalure, Pelagis completes two orbits and Eirene completes four.
- The relative configuration repeats every 1.4120 days in the exact period model. A near-collinear line-up requires suitable moon phases.
- Eirene and Pelagis come into conjunction every 0.7060 days.
- Eirene and Thalure share a conjunction cadence of about 0.4707 days.

These resonant interactions maintain significant tidal heating, especially within Pelagis, and keep the orbital eccentricities of all three moons from damping away.

## Calendrical cycles and system-wide timekeeping

Because Virans is bright, distinctly green and ringed with a tight, resonant moon system, its satellite dynamics provide a natural set of time markers for inhabitants of other worlds in the Stellula System.

Two families of cycles are particularly important:

- The **Tito–Harth eclipse cycle**, with a period of ≈3.4102 days, during which the super-Earth [[Tito]] regularly occults Stellula as seen from [[Harth]].  
- The **Virans resonance cycles**, especially the nominal 1.4120-day configuration period of Eirene–Pelagis–Thalure and the 0.7060-day Eirene–Pelagis conjunctions.

In a Virans-centered frame, the three resonant moons return to a nearly identical alignment once every 1.4120 days in the idealized period model. From viewpoints on Tito and Harth, suitably projected alignments may appear as subtle, repeating patterns in the brightness and silhouette of Virans and its moon system against the starfield. The Eirene–Pelagis conjunctions at 0.7060-day intervals subdivide this rhythm, providing a finer temporal granularity.

The ratio between the Tito–Harth eclipse period and the Virans triple-conjunction period is:

$$
\frac{P_\mathrm{eclipse}}{P_\mathrm{triple}} \approx \frac{3.4102}{1.4120} \approx 2.4152,
$$

which is not exactly commensurate but nearly satisfies a 29:12 relationship:

- 29 triple conjunctions ≈ 40.95 days  
- 12 Tito eclipses ≈ 40.92 days  

When an eclipse and a favorable moon alignment begin close together, the two clocks return to nearly the same relative phase about 41 days later. The timings differ by about 37 minutes per recurrence, and visibility also depends on the observer’s line of sight. Cultures on Harth and Tito that pay attention to the sky can combine these cycles into intricate calendars and omen systems, treating rare combined events as especially significant.

## Appearance from Tito and Harth

From Tito and Harth, Virans serves primarily as a striking visual object:

- At favorable opposition and minimum distance from Harth (~0.021 AU), Virans reaches an angular diameter of ~0.9°, appearing slightly larger than Earth's Moon does from Earth.
- Thalure can be visible as a tiny light point near Virans's limb, with a maximum angular diameter of a few arcminutes when geometry is ideal.
- Mutual events – Virans passing behind Stellula, its moons casting shadows on the planet, or Thalure being occulted by Virans – produce complex light-curve signatures detectable with moderate telescopes.

For observers on Tito's nightside cities, the green disk of Virans and the flickering patterns of its inner moons offer a slow, celestial counterpart to the rapid, dramatic eclipses that Tito itself inflicts on Harth.

## Human and setting context

In the epoch ten thousand years after the corporate abandonment of Harth, Virans has not been heavily exploited. The main human presence in the system concentrates on Tito's anti-stellar hemisphere with probes and occasional crewed flybys visiting the satellite system of [[Gigafusc]]. Virans has been visited by robotic probes and occasional crewed flybys, which have mapped its atmosphere and moons but left no permanent installations in the harsh conditions near the planet.

Despite its limited direct use, Virans plays an outsized role in Stellula System culture and science:

- Its easily recognizable green disk and regularly dancing moons make it a favorite target of amateur observers on Tito and, in later eras, on Harth.
- The precise, stable resonance-driven cycles of Eirene, Pelagis and Thalure form the basis of alternative calendars, festival schedules and astrological systems.
- Virans's location just beyond the frost line provides clues to the past structure of the Stellula protoplanetary disc and the migration history of the inner planets.

Virans and its moons remain a visually and dynamically rich backdrop rather than a primary stage, a shifting clockwork in the green that marks the passage of days, eclipses and eras in the Stellula System.

## Physical basis

**Bulk.** The 13 M⊕ mass at 3.84 R⊕ gives a density of 1.26 g/cm³, which needs a 5–10% hydrogen–helium envelope (a Neptune-like composition would be about 3.3 R⊕; from memory). The 145 K equilibrium temperature uses a Bond albedo of 0.45 and uniform redistribution.

**Orbit.** Virans lies at 80% of the 0.041 AU stability limit around Stellula, with a Gigafusc-to-Virans period ratio of 4.88; Gigafusc forces its osculating eccentricity to about 0.04 (0.07 at most), and N-body runs are stable at 0.033 AU and 0.030 AU. Its Hill radius is 269,000 km.

**Moons.** The Kepler periods for the listed distances agree with the adopted 1:2:4 chain to within 0.1%. Praxa lies between the rigid-body Roche limit (23,100 km) and the fluid limit (44,800 km), so a small rocky body can survive while debris inside the fluid limit forms a ring. The calendar describes a near recurrence rather than a guaranteed simultaneous visible event: 29 triple conjunctions span about 40.95 d against about 40.92 d for 12 Tito–Harth eclipses.

**Open details.** The density is rounded from the stated mass and volume. The libration of the Laplace angle, absolute phases, tidal-heating eccentricities and the long-term stability of Thalure and Obrum need further orbital and physical parameters.

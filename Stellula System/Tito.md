**Tito** is a tidally locked, water-rich super-Earth orbiting the ultracool red dwarf [[Stellula]] at a distance of 0.009184 AU. With a mass of 10.2 M🜨 and a radius 2.8 times that of Earth, Tito is one of the most massive rocky worlds in the [[Stellula System]]. It completes an orbit every 1.1366 days and always presents the same hemisphere to its star.

At some point in its history, human colonists arrived and established large-scale settlements on the thick ice sheet that covers Tito's permanent nightside, engineering breathable air in settlements within a dense atmosphere and building a planet-spanning network of power lines, transport arcs and illuminated infrastructure along the terminator and dark hemisphere. The glow of these installations is visible from orbit and from the neighboring planet [[Harth]], where developing cultures eventually interpret Tito's night-side lights as evidence of distant sky spirits, giving rise to an "Airplane Cult" focused on the bright, patterned world overhead.

## Orbit and rotation

Tito orbits Stellula at 0.009184 AU with a nearly circular orbit (eccentricity ≈ 0). The orbital period is 1.1366 days, or 27.28 hours. At this distance, Tito travels around the star at an orbital velocity of about 87.906 km/s. The planet has an adopted near-3:2 mean-motion ratio with Harth: Tito completes three orbits for every two orbits of Harth.

Tidal forces from Stellula have synchronized Tito's rotation with its orbital motion, so its sidereal day equals its year. The same hemisphere always faces the star, creating a permanent dayside centered on the substellar point and a permanent nightside centered on the anti-stellar point. The equatorial rotation speed at the surface is ~1.141 km/s, modest compared to the planet's high surface gravity and producing only a small reduction in effective gravity at the equator.

From Tito's surface, Stellula subtends an angular diameter of about 5.80°, more than ten times the angular size of the Sun as seen from Earth. The planet receives a stellar flux of ≈1.719 F🜨, giving an equilibrium temperature of ~286 K for Bond albedo 0.35 and uniform heat redistribution. Greenhouse gases, water vapor and the thick atmosphere raise the global-mean surface temperature to around 320 K, with the substellar ocean reaching ~334 K (~61 °C).

Tito's Hill radius around Stellula is approximately 0.00047 AU (~70,000 km), only about four planetary radii. Natural satellites beyond a few radii would be unstable over long timescales; any surviving moons or large orbital habitats are likely to occupy tight, close-in orbits well inside this limit.

## Physical characteristics

With a mean radius of 17,838.8 km (2.8 R🜨) and a mass of 10.2 M🜨, Tito has a mean density of 2.56 g/cm³, less than half of Earth's 5.51 g/cm³. A rocky planet of 10.2 M⊕ would have a radius of only about 1.9 R⊕ and 3 g of surface gravity, so Tito must be rich in water: an interior of rock and iron under a very thick mantle of water in its liquid, high-pressure ice and supercritical states, which makes up tens of percent of the planet's mass. The exact fraction depends on the interior model and is not computed here; the density is the adopted constraint. Surface gravity is 12.78 m/s² (1.30 g) and the escape velocity is 21.35 km/s.

**Ocean.** Liquid water cannot be deeper than the pressure at which it freezes into dense ice phases (about 1–2 GPa), which at Tito's gravity corresponds to a depth of roughly 100–200 km. Below that lies a mantle of high-pressure ice and then the rock–iron core. The surface of Tito is therefore a global ocean, not continents: land exists only as floating or ice-anchored structures and the thick nightside ice sheet.

**Shape.** Tito's equatorial radius exceeds its polar radius by roughly 0.6%, giving an estimated flattening of ~0.006. The total surface area is about 4.00×10⁹ km² (over seven times Earth's), and the volume ~2.38×10¹³ km³ (nearly 22 times Earth's).

**Heat.** Tidal flexing from its orbit near Stellula and the neighbouring Harth, together with radiogenic heat, powers a hot seafloor and heat flow through the ice mantle. At this distance and mass the tidal heating is far above ordinary rocky-planet values; it is an adopted setting parameter, not computed. The eccentricity damps in about 10⁴ years unless it is forced, so Tito's tides depend on the resonance with Harth.

## Atmosphere

Tito's atmosphere is thick, engineered and chemically active, sitting over a global ocean and a nightside ice sheet. Following human colonization, settlements installed air treatment to support unassisted respiration inside conditioned zones. The ambient atmosphere remains chemically active. The total surface pressure averages 1.7 atm.

The mean scale height is estimated at ~6.5 km, somewhat lower than Earth's, reflecting the higher gravity at similar or slightly higher temperatures. The engineered oxygen fraction (12.3 %) is chosen so that its partial pressure (~0.21 atm) closely matches Earth's sea-level conditions, while the dense atmosphere enhances heat transport. Settlements scrub phosphine and other hazardous gases. At 0.1%, ambient phosphine is 1,000 ppm. [NIOSH phosphine reference](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0505.html)

Phosphine, methane and the other reduced gases are destroyed within days to years in an oxygen-rich atmosphere, so levels of 0.1% PH₃ and 0.2% CH₄ require continuous sources. Ocean-floor outgassing, photochemistry and industrial processes related to the nightside infrastructure supply them; the levels are a stated premise. These gases are important diagnostically for remote sensing, and they contribute to Tito's complex spectral appearance in both reflected and thermal emission.

**Retention.** Stellula's X-ray and ultraviolet output is the main threat to a thin atmosphere over its close-in planets. For Tito, the energy-limited escape rate at 10% efficiency would remove a 1.7 atm atmosphere in about 17 Myr if Stellula's X-ray output were 10⁻⁴ of its luminosity, 170 Myr at 10⁻⁵ and 1.7 Gyr at 10⁻⁶. The adopted Stellula is a quiet ultracool dwarf (about 10⁻⁶), with a heavy-gas escape efficiency of a few percent, so the atmosphere lasts the system's 2 Gyr; volcanic and biological resupply tops it up. This is an assumption, and a more active star would make the air thinner and hazier.

## Climate and hemispheric contrast

Tito's tidally locked state divides its climate into three broad regimes:

- **Dayside hemisphere** – Centered on the substellar point, continuously illuminated by Stellula and receiving the full 1.719 F🜨 flux. A warm ocean (up to ~61 °C at the substellar point) feeds deep convective towers and a thick water-vapor cloud deck. With a global albedo of 0.35 the planet absorbs about 1.1 Earth-equivalents of flux, close to the onset of a moist greenhouse; the dayside cloud deck must reach an albedo of roughly 0.45–0.5 to keep the ocean from running away, which is adopted (the threshold comes from published climate models, cited from memory). Most human activity avoids the central dayside outside of robotic and energy-collection facilities.

- **Terminator band** – The ring of permanent dawn and dusk surrounding the dayside. Strong pressure gradients drive fierce winds from the substellar region into the cooler terminator and on toward the nightside. The terminator hosts dynamic cloud decks, frequent storms and powerful jet streams. Much of the planet's engineered infrastructure is concentrated here to capture sunlight, tap wind power and shuttle heat and materials toward the dark hemisphere.

- **Nightside hemisphere** – Centered on the anti-stellar point, in perpetual darkness with no direct stellar illumination. The thick atmosphere carries heat across the terminator, but the nightside ocean is covered by a thick floating ice sheet, kilometres deep, which is where the settlements stand. Cold traps near the anti-stellar point hold NH₃ and other volatiles.

The engineered climate further moderates extremes on the ice-sheet settlements. Orbital mirrors, microwave beam lines, and geothermal tapping provide controlled heating for major urban zones on the nightside, while active weather modification reduces catastrophic storms along the terminator. The combination of natural circulation and technological intervention keeps much of the anti-stellar hemisphere in a temperate range tolerable for human habitation.

## Colonization and infrastructure

Human colonization of Tito focuses on the nightside, where direct stellar irradiation is absent and temperatures are easier to manage with controlled heating. The first settlements were clustered near the anti-stellar point, gradually expanding outward along the nightside toward the terminator as power generation and transport capacity increased.

Over time, the colonists constructed a planet-spanning network of infrastructure:

- **Terminator arrays** – Continuous rings of solar-collecting towers, orbital mirrors, and rectenna fields along the twilight zone, harvesting Stellula's light and converting it into electrical or thermal energy.

- **Heat and light pipelines** – Superconducting trunk lines and fluid heat exchangers running from the terminator into the deep nightside, supplying power and waste heat to urban regions. These routes trace bright lines across the dark hemisphere when seen from orbit.

- **Nightside cities** – Dense, high-pressure arcologies and surface cities illuminated by industrial-scale lighting, plasma towers and atmospheric glow lamps. Their combined radiance competes with scattered starlight over the hemisphere, outlining urban clusters and transport corridors.

- **Orbital support lattice** – Low-orbit constellations of power satellites, communications hubs and mass-drivers coordinated with Tito’s rotation through scheduled passes. Many of these occupy tight orbits well within the planet's Hill sphere, appearing as moving points of light tracing regular paths over the nightside sky.

From space, the infrastructure forms a web of concentric circles and straight-line connections similar to the patterns seen in artistic renderings: power nodes, industrial hubs and cities linked in geometric circuits that map onto underlying geography, resource deposits and orbital anchor points.

## Appearance from Harth

As viewed from Harth, Tito is a prominent object in Stellula's sky. At closest approach, the super-Earth spans roughly 4.79° in Harth's sky, larger than Stellula's own disc (~4.4°). During precise alignments, Tito completely covers Stellula, producing deep total eclipses on Harth with a synodic period of about 3.4102 days.

Observers on Harth see different fractions of Tito’s illuminated hemisphere over the synodic cycle. Near inferior conjunction, Tito presents its nightside, and during totality its illuminated settlements stand out against the dark disc. Gibbous views favor reflected light and show less of the artificial lighting. In a later era, as Harthian cultures begin to develop optics and astronomy, they detect the structured network of bright spots and lines across Tito's disc, especially during phases where the terminator infrastructure is in view.

To preliterate societies on Harth, the recurring eclipses and the intricate light patterns on Tito suggest deliberate activity by sky beings. Many traditions interpret the bright circuits as paths for chariots, aircraft or spirits, and the periodic darkening of Stellula as intentional signaling or sacrifice. Over time, these interpretations crystallize into an "Airplane Cult": a set of myths, rituals and proto-scientific speculations focused on the belief that the luminous world near the star is inhabited by powerful, technologically advanced entities.

Later, as Harth's own technology advances, Tito becomes the first plausible target for interplanetary exploration and contact, closing the loop between the nightside colonists and the civilizations that spent millennia watching their lights from afar.

## Physical basis

**Bulk composition.** The 10.2 M⊕ mass at 2.8 R⊕ gives a mean density of 2.56 g/cm³, a gravity of 12.78 m/s² (1.3 g) and an escape velocity of 21.35 km/s. This is not a rocky planet: a rocky 10.2 M⊕ planet would have a radius near 1.9 R⊕ and a gravity near 3 g. Tito is therefore a water-rich world with tens of percent water, a global ocean about 100–200 km deep, and a base that freezes into ice VII near 1–2 GPa (at a depth of 156 km for 2 GPa and 12.78 m/s²). A floating ice sheet on the nightside carries the settlements. The 2.8 R⊕ radius also satisfies the condition for the total eclipse of Stellula from Harth (a radius of at least 2.6 R⊕).

**Climate.** Tito receives 1.719 F⊕. A global Bond albedo of 0.35 would absorb 1.12 Earth-equivalents, near the moist-greenhouse onset (cited from memory), so the dayside cloud deck must reflect more: an albedo of at least 0.45–0.5. Mean water vapor is 3–4%, corresponding to a saturation of 0.1–0.2 atm at 320–334 K in the hottest air. Tito's eccentricity damps in only 0.011 Myr, so its small eccentricity is maintained by the 3:2 resonance with Harth.

**Adopted values.** The water fraction, ocean depth, tidal heating, trace-gas levels and the moist-greenhouse albedo are adopted or rough. The 3.4102-day recurrence is a conjunction interval; contact durations and totality are recorded on [[Stellula System#Tito–Harth eclipse cycle]]. The Hill radius is a screening scale; satellite orbits and oblateness are approximate. The Harthian observations describe a later era than the hybrid biosphere's reference epoch.

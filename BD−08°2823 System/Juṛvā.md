**Juṛvā** is the **dark twin** of the [[BD−08°2823 System]]—a cryogenic super-Earth or transition mini-Neptune orbiting at 18 AU. With a mass of ~6 M🜨 and a radius of ~2.2 R🜨, it is believed to be the long-exposed core of a once more volatile-rich world. Its exposed-core interpretation is a hypothesis: an early collision or migration through a more strongly irradiated region could have removed gas, or it may have accreted little gas initially. Its present distant orbit does not establish efficient photostripping. What remains is a chemically oxidized, frozen shell sealed beneath deep cold.

Today, Juṛvā’s observable surface is exceptionally quiet and its chemistry is kinetically inhibited by cold. It preserves, in near-perfect stasis, the oxidant-rich strata produced during the violent youth of the BD−08°2823 system—making it the outermost expression of the system’s radial redox gradient.

## Physical and geological properties

Juṛvā’s interior is a stratified mixture of rock and high-pressure ices, capped by a rigid oxidant crust.

- **Interior** — An ice–rock composite containing water, ammonia-bearing material, and buried reducing minerals beneath frozen surface veneers. High-pressure phases and slow internal circulation remain possible.
- **Crust** — MgO₂ slabs, SO₂ frost, oxidized forsteritic silicates, and iron oxides form a pallid, brittle surface. Chemical interfaces are preserved intact.
- **Tectonics** — No widespread recent surface tectonics is evident. Ancient fractures survive; the history of deeper convection is unresolved.
- **Heat flow** — Low surface flux, with residual radiogenic heat and deep thermal evolution still present. No claim of a wholly frozen, nonconvecting interior is required.
- **Atmosphere** — No substantial atmosphere is adopted. Trace gas can form an exosphere during impacts or local heating, then redeposit, react, or escape according to species and conditions.
- **Magnetism** — No active dynamo. Only weak, localized remanent magnetization remains—a fossil field imprinted during the planet’s volatile-rich era.

The result is a surface archive with very slow turnover. Cold and physical isolation preserve disequilibrium between oxidized veneers and deeper reduced material.

## Visual appearance

From orbit, Juṛvā presents a dark, muted disk with subtle glints along the terminator where peroxide crystals reflect starlight. Its overall brightness is low: dark irradiation residues and micrometeoroid material cover much of the pale substrate, giving the planet a charcoal-gray tone interrupted by exposed bright mineral and frost facets. Dark coloration is attributed to coatings rather than oxidation alone.

Surface texture is dominated by:

- deep, ancient fracture networks  
- oxidant-bright glazes and SO₂ frost plates  
- reddish and ochre streaks marking sulfur-rich exposures  
- faint banded patterns, remnants of flows frozen eons ago  

No clouds form. No aurorae shimmer. No transient plumes disturb the stillness. Juṛvā is visually static, shaped only by the slow accumulation of micrometeoroid dust and the inexorable creep of cryogenic contraction.

At great distance, it appears almost unlit, a quiet obstruction in the outer dark.

## Interpretation

Juṛvā is the **frozen terminus** of the BD−08°2823 oxidation gradient. In contrast to the reactive cryovolcanism of [[Ṭanḍī]] or the chemically active moon system of [[Varuna]], Juṛvā exemplifies kinetic preservation: surface redox reactions are very slow, recent tectonic exposure is limited, and stored chemical energy remains physically isolated. This is not thermodynamic equilibrium.

For planetary scientists, Juṛvā serves as:

- a **chemical archive**, preserving ancient oxidation fronts and mineral strata  
- a **record of atmospheric loss**, testing the possible stripped-core history of a volatile-rich body  
- a **baseline for outer-system stasis**, marking the boundary between dynamic ice giants and quiet oxidant-bearing outer worlds  

Juṛvā is not simply cold—it is chemically preserved, a planetary fossil suspended in the deep outer orbit of BD−08°2823.

## Model status and remaining work

Juṛvā is a 6 M🜨 super-Earth or volatile-rich transition body, not classified as a dwarf planet by its small apparent brightness. The adopted mass and radius imply 1.24 g, 3.11 g/cm³, and 18.5 km/s escape speed. The supplied filename Dark Twin.png resolves to images/Dark_Twin.png; its source description was “Faintly glowing cryogenic planet in darkness with oxidant fissures,” and its false-color caption does not imply visible thermal incandescence.

A cold surface reference of 40–45 K is consistent with low absorbed stellar input; 50–60 K can describe selected warm regions or layers if supported by an energy model. No measured albedo or solved interior model is adopted. Quantify cooling, volatile retention and vapor pressures, coatings, and the competing envelope-history hypotheses. The visually static archive does not require zero heat or complete chemical equilibrium.

**BD−08°2823 b** (also known as **HIP 49067 b**) is an [extrasolar planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/extrasolar_planet) orbiting the [K-type main-sequence star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-type_main-sequence_star) [[BD−08°2823]] in the [[BD−08°2823 System|BD−08°2823 system]], located roughly 135 [light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/light-year) away in the constellation [Sextans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextans). It was discovered in 2009 by the [HARPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Accuracy_Radial_velocity_Planet_Searcher) instrument via the [radial velocity method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/radial_velocity_method).[^hebrard2010]

With a minimum mass of about 14.4 times that of Earth and an orbital period of only 5.6 days, BD−08°2823 b is a close-in, strongly irradiated planet. The setting adopts a compact, strongly eroded Neptune-mass world. Its residual atmosphere and possible mineral exosphere are model-dependent; the radial-velocity discovery does not measure them.

## Classification and formation

By mass, BD−08°2823 b is usually classified as a [hot Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/hot_Neptune). Its very short orbital distance (0.056 AU) makes it a candidate for a highly eroded, chthonian remnant: the exposed core of a former Neptune-like planet that has lost most of its primordial hydrogen–helium envelope.

b is modeled as:

- A radius of ~2.2 R🜨 and bulk density near 7.46 g/cm³ (speculative).
- A differentiated interior with an Fe–Ni–Si metallic core and a mantle of Mg–Si silicates.
- A mantle of magnesium-bearing silicates and oxides. Persistent hot-surface MgO₂ enrichment is not assumed.

Early in the system’s history, BD−08°2823 b likely formed farther from the star and migrated inward via disk-driven migration. Subsequent intense irradiation and stellar wind stripping would have removed a large fraction of its envelope through hydrodynamic escape, leaving a compact, super-Earth-sized residual core.

## Physical and atmospheric properties

At the working stellar luminosity of 0.225 L☉ and 0.056 AU, the zero-albedo, globally redistributed equilibrium temperature is about 810 K. The idealized substellar value is about 1,150 K. These values do not establish an 1,800–2,000 K lava surface. Local hot regions, a deep greenhouse, or tidal heating require separate evidence and energy budgets.

A tenuous mineral exosphere could contain neutral Na, Mg, O, traces of SiO, and corresponding ions. Sputtering, impacts, and localized volcanism are candidate sources. A formal, atom-balanced decomposition bookkeeping equation is:

```text
MgSiO₃ → Mg + SiO + O₂
```

This equation is not an equilibrium vapor model. Species abundances and outflow rates remain under investigation. Any vapor–condensate exchange occurs according to local temperatures and surface supply.

### Exosphere and tail

The setting retains a possible faint ion tail. Stellar wind, radiation pressure, and orbital motion determine its anti-stellar and trailing components. Neutral sodium would supply Na D absorption; Mg and ion-specific lines require their own populations and excitation models. The tail is a narrow-band or ultraviolet target, with no established broadband blue halo.

## Visual appearance

The hot dayside emits chiefly in the infrared and also reflects starlight. Any visible incandescence should be subdued and localized unless an additional heating mechanism is established. The supplied artwork preserves the original molten-world concept as an illustrative hypothesis.

Trace mineral dust or condensates may occur where material cools. A resolved orange-white globe, global molten surface, and a bright metallic halo are not adopted physical measurements.

## Orbit and dynamical environment

BD−08°2823 b orbits BD−08°2823 with:

- Semimajor axis ~0.056 AU
- Orbital period ~5.6 days
- Present working eccentricity ≈0; the discovery fit was 0.15 ±0.15.[^hebrard2010]

Strong tides favor a nearly synchronous spin in the working circular model. No moons are adopted; their absence is a formation and tidal-history choice rather than proof that every conceivable satellite orbit is impossible.

The radial-velocity semi-amplitude of ~6.5 m/s induced on the star is consistent with a moderately massive close-in planet near the transition between hot Neptunes and super-Earths.

## Evolutionary trajectory

In the adopted formation hypothesis, BD−08°2823 b originated beyond the snow line as a Neptune-class planet with a thick volatile envelope. Type I or Type II migration brought it inward into its current short-period orbit while the protoplanetary disk was still present.

Subsequent evolution was dominated by:

- Hydrodynamic escape driven by high-energy stellar radiation.
- Ongoing sputtering and ion pick-up by the stellar wind.
- Progressive loss of light gases and some heavier volatiles.

The setting’s stripped-core interpretation remains a formation hypothesis. The assumed radius and envelope fraction have not been established by the radial-velocity data, and a radius-valley classification is not used as proof of stripping.

Early migration and tides may have removed primordial moons; none are included in the present setting.

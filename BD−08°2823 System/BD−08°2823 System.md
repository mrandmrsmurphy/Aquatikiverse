# BD−08°2823 System

![[images/BD−08°2823_system.svg]]

*Illustrative system diagram; orbital spacing and body sizes are schematic. The table below supplies the reconciled numerical architecture.*

The **BD−08°2823 system** is a chemically diverse planetary system around the real K-type star [[BD−08°2823]], also HIP 49067, approximately 41.38 pc (135 light-years) away in Sextans. Aquatikiverse extends the observed b/c system with five fictional planets, oxidant-bearing debris, an exotic biosphere, and human settlement.

The later detailed articles govern this overview. [[Oxhydra]] supports a roughly three-billion-year-old chemosynthetic biosphere, including the much younger continuant provinces of [[Pumper Beetles]]. [[Prithvi]] was naturally abiotic and cold; its current temperate reference state is an achievement of the Mohan Terraforming Initiative. [[Ṭanḍī]], [[Varuna]], and [[Juṛvā]] preserve distinct cold sulfur–oxidant environments rather than a universally oxidizing bulk composition.

## Stellar environment and observational boundary

*Main article: [[BD−08°2823]]*

The real star is moderately active, with Ca II H and K emission and a rotation period near 26–27 days. Its slightly subsolar iron abundance does not establish magnesium enrichment or explain the fictional planets’ oxidation states. Stellar ultraviolet and particle histories must be modeled separately from bolometric luminosity.

The discovery paper reports b at 5.60 days with minimum mass 14.4 M🜨 and c at 237.6 days with minimum mass approximately 0.33 M♃. The study does not determine their radii, cloud compositions, metallic tails, or the fictional planets. [Hébrard et al., discovery study](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.3202).

By explicit setting choice, c’s working orbit departs modestly from the discovery estimate, accompanied by a small inward movement of Oxhydra. This is a fictional refit, not a reinterpretation established by real observations. A 105 M🜨 true mass is adopted for c in the working dynamical model, close to its reported minimum; a substantially higher true mass would require renewed screening.

## Planets and belts

### BD−08°2823 b

*Main article: [[BD−08°2823 b]]*

The close-in planet retains its observed Neptune-scale minimum mass and short period. The setting adopts an eroded, compact body with a speculative 2.2 R🜨 radius. Its strong irradiation gives a zero-albedo equilibrium temperature near 810 K and an ideal substellar temperature near 1,150 K. A permanent global lava surface and 1,800–2,000 K equilibrium are not assumed.

Possible Na-, Mg-, SiO-, and O-bearing exospheric material and a faint ion tail remain hypotheses. Neutral and ionic lines are distinguished. Early migration and atmospheric loss are candidate histories, not results of the radial-velocity detection. No moons are adopted.

### Oxhydra

*Main articles: [[Oxhydra]], [[Life on Oxhydra]], [[Pumper Beetles]]*

Oxhydra retains 0.86 M🜨, 0.96 R🜨, approximately 9.2 m/s² gravity, a bright oxidant-bearing surface, 27-hour rotation, and a dark captured retrograde moon, Umbra. At its revised 0.56 AU orbit, a high Bond albedo near 0.65 gives stellar equilibrium near 197 K. The approximately 260 K mean surface target requires a substantial H₂–CH₄ greenhouse; several bars are the working regime, with an illustrative 5-bar reference pending a climate and escape model.

The bulk atmosphere is reducing. Irradiated coatings, deposited peroxide, dry mineral stores, and isolated wet habitats retain localized oxidizing chemistry. H₂O₂ is a replenished terminal oxidant; MgO₂ is a storage phase. Hydration controls its release rather than creating a renewable energy source.

Bastions are brine-based carbon–organosilicon organisms with silica-rich structures. Their molecular machinery remains under investigation. The Aqueous Meteor Sequence approximately 40–60 million years ago opened and supplied reservoirs in the Meteoric Hydration Belt. Groundwater, intermittent geothermal recharge, mineral sealing, and siloxyl water retention subsequently sustained selected pockets. Most regions still undergo bloom–dormancy cycles; mobile continuants persist locally.

### BD−08°2823 c

*Main article: [[BD−08°2823 c]]*

The setting adopts approximately 105 M🜨 and a speculative compact radius of 5.7 R🜨, giving density near 3.13 g/cm³. At 0.72 AU, c lies outside Oxhydra and inside Prithvi. Its pale yellow-gray cloud tops have H₂–He bulk gas, methane, ammonia, water, sulfide chemistry, and variable hazes. The former 800–900 K dayside claim is retired; stellar equilibrium is only approximately 226 K before reflectivity and internal heat are included.

A very faint sulfur/oxygen ion nimbus and externally supplied mineral traces remain candidate features needing mass and transport budgets. No regular moons are adopted. The approximately 250 km candidate co-orbital asteroid Phaethon lies near L₄; it is far outside the compact planetary plasma environment and needs its own stability assessment.

### Prithvi

*Main article: [[Prithvi]]*

Prithvi retains 1.1 M🜨, 1.12 R🜨, approximately 0.88 g gravity, 21° obliquity, and the moon Saathi. Current reference coverage is approximately 65% land and 35% standing water, with seasonal variation and substantial polar ice. The Maw of Fire remains a highly active tectonic exclusion zone; managed steppe provinces are early settlement and agricultural centers.

Prithvi is canonically without indigenous life before intervention. Its abiotic oxygen is attributed to earlier water loss followed by survival of a residual inventory after crustal and volcanic consumption. Surveys found no native life but could not observationally exhaust every habitat.

The Mohan Terraforming Initiative introduced engineered microbes, industrial greenhouse forcing, and later managed plants and settlements. The present approximately 295 K reference is an actively maintained terraforming achievement. The natural world was colder, with localized and seasonal liquid water. A proposed fluorocarbon suite and other trace gases needs a spectral climate model and production budget; a lightly modified Earthlike atmosphere alone does not establish the warm state.

The 1.2-bar atmosphere retains an approximately 20.5% dry-air oxygen fraction; nitrogen balances the dry composition after argon, CO₂, and managed trace gases. Variable water vapor is specified separately. Closed bases, partially open trials, carbon management, relay satellites, high-orbit hubs, and a functioning equatorial tether remain canon. The tether engineering awaits a rotation and clearance model. Two further settlement waves depend on ecological robustness and political approval.

### The Peroxide Belt

The belt spans approximately 1.1–1.5 AU. It contains oxidized silicates, desiccated fragments, and patchy peroxide-bearing surface coatings. Candidate origins include collisionally processed planetesimals and fragments affected by early migration. Collisions expose fresh surfaces and supply dust, while irradiation and subsequent reactions alter the coatings.

The belt’s survival, resonant gaps, dust lifetimes, accessible peroxide inventory, and oxidation rates require a minor-body model. It is a chemical transition region, not proof of uniformly peroxide-dominated bulk asteroids.

### Ṭanḍī

*Main article: [[Ṭanḍī]]*

The 2.5 M🜨, 1.2 R🜨 body has approximately 1.74 g gravity, density near 8.0 g/cm³, and escape speed near 16.1 km/s. Its thick cold lid preserves sharp terrain and patchy SO₂ frost. A representative Bond albedo of 0.4 gives approximately 122 K global stellar equilibrium; sunlit and active regions can be warmer.

Rare fractures release SO₂-rich gas and sulfate-bearing dust from localized warmed, pressurized pockets. Background radiogenic heat and stress provide reservoir support; local chemistry may contribute energy. SO₂ frost deposition, sulfate formation, ballistic fallback, and escape are separate processes. The planet has no substantial atmosphere or known biosphere, without requiring zero deep convection.

Sarkon is a sulfur-rich retrograde moon with a working diameter near 1,400 km; Bastil is a bright approximately 800 km-diameter co-formed moon. At least five smaller shepherds inhabit dusty ejecta arcs. Their orbits and tidal contributions remain undetermined.

### Varuna

*Main article: [[Varuna]]*

Varuna retains approximately 17 M🜨 and 3.7 R🜨, implying a mean density near 1.85 g/cm³. A conducting water–ammonia-rich interior is a candidate dynamo source. Its tilted multipolar field interacts with sulfur-rich moon ejecta and stellar wind. The 30–40 Rₚ magnetospheric size is a variable working scale.

Peroxide is concentrated primarily in irradiated ice deposits, trace replenished aerosols, and dust, rather than an assumed abundant deep ammonia–peroxide fluid. Pale yellow, off-white, and bluish-gray cloud patterns, faint ice–silicate/peroxide-bearing dust arcs, and intermittent aurorae remain.

### Juṛvā

*Main article: [[Juṛvā]]*

Juṛvā is a 6 M🜨, 2.2 R🜨 ice–rock super-Earth with approximately 1.24 g gravity. Its dark coatings cover pale oxidant exposures and frost facets, producing a muted charcoal disk with occasional glints. Surface temperatures near 40–45 K preserve ancient interfaces by slow reaction kinetics.

Its interior can retain residual heat or slow circulation while the observable surface remains quiet. Atmospheric absence is a volatile-inventory and phase-retention question, not automatic escape from a massive cold world. A former sub-Neptune envelope is one hypothesis; early impacts, closer-in migration, or limited original gas accretion remain alternative histories. The planet is a chemical archive in disequilibrium, not a state of complete thermodynamic equilibrium. The false-color illustration is embedded on [[Juṛvā]] using images/Dark_Twin.png.

## System chemistry and exact replenishment

The radial theme concerns surface processing and accessible volatile reservoirs. Hydrogen-rich giants and local oxidant deposits coexist through spatial separation, irradiation, chemical kinetics, burial, and ongoing replacement. Slightly subsolar stellar metallicity does not itself produce this pattern.

For Oxhydra, **H₂O₂ must be newly produced**, **MgO₂ stores supplied peroxide**, and **CH₄, H₂, H₂S and reduced sulfides** supply electron donors. **H₂O** must be replaced where lost to escape or inaccessible storage; **MgO/Mg(OH)₂, phosphate, usable nitrogen, Fe, Mn, and dissolved silica** must be delivered or recycled for mineralization, biomass, catalysts, and repair. One mole of CH₄ consumes four moles of H₂O₂, before nonbiological losses. The detailed ledger and candidate production mechanisms are in [[Life on Oxhydra#Chemical replenishment and energy budget]]. Sustainable throughput remains uncalculated.

For Varuna’s moons, irradiated **H₂O ice** supplies material for trace **H₂O₂**, powered by radiation. Sulfur tori require fresh **SO₂, H₂S or sulfur-bearing ejecta** and ionization energy. Ejecta replenish ice–silicate dust; escape, recombination, deposition, chemical destruction, and sputtering remove material. No stored oxidant reservoir is treated as an inexhaustible energy source.

For Prithvi, the Initiative must maintain its selected industrial greenhouse compounds and account for photochemical destruction, deposition, leakage, and deliberate removal. The proposed **CF₄, C₂F₆, SF₆**, monitored **N₂O and C₂H₆**, and CO₂ management are candidates, not a solved concentration recipe. The native abiotic oxygen inventory and current biological/industrial cycles are distinct historical stages.

## Astrobiology and chronology

- **Oxhydra:** native biosphere approximately 3 billion years old; impact-driven regional transition approximately 40–60 million years ago; continuant habitats receive ongoing recharge.
- **Pumper Beetles:** mobile 12–60 mm continuants with chemical/electrochemical senses, structural memory, kin groups, and cooperative engineering. Sentience is an in-setting inference; sapience and symbolic behavior remain unconfirmed.
- **Prithvi:** originally abiotic; now deliberately seeded and inhabited under corporate–state management. Its pre-intervention oxygen remains a false-positive biosignature example.
- **Cold niches:** no life is established on Ṭanḍī, Juṛvā, or Varuna’s moons. Subsurface water and chemical activity alone do not establish biology.

The current colonization epoch has no assigned absolute Earth calendar date. Its relation to other Aquatikiverse systems should be supplied by the universe-wide chronology rather than invented here.

## Orbital screening and limits

To minimize changes, a compact compromise was screened at **Oxhydra 0.56 AU and c 0.72 AU**, with Prithvi remaining at 0.94 AU. The inward shift is 0.06 AU and the outward shift 0.04 AU relative to the supplied positions. A tighter 0.57/0.71 AU trial encountered an Oxhydra–c separation below one mutual Hill radius after approximately 44,046 years. The adopted pair is the smallest successful layout among these tested hundredth-AU compromises, not a mathematically proven global minimum.

The adopted initial Oxhydra–c separation is about 4.78 mutual Hill radii; their initial radial clearance is about 4.18. The c–Prithvi figures are approximately 5.06 and 4.62. These geometric checks are supplemented by direct integration rather than treated as standalone stability guarantees.

A planar Newtonian point-mass screening used a 0.74 M☉ star and the six planets Oxhydra, c, Prithvi, Ṭanḍī, Varuna, and Juṛvā. Masses were [0.86, 105, 1.1, 2.5, 17, 6] M🜨, axes [0.56, 0.72, 0.94, 1.9, 6, 18] AU, and initial eccentricities [0.01, 0.02, 0.005, 0, 0.01, 0.01]. The conversion was 1 M🜨 = 3.0034896×10⁻⁶ M☉, and G = 4π² in AU–year–solar-mass units. All initial periapses were aligned. For zero-based planet index i and phase seed k, the initial mean anomaly was ((i+1)×2.3999632297 + k×0.734×(i+1)) modulo 2π. Kepler’s equation supplied initial positions and velocities, followed by a barycentric transformation.

A direct pairwise velocity-Verlet integrator used a 0.002-year step, sampled orbital elements and separations every 20 steps, and sampled energy error every 2,000 steps. Termination conditions were a separation below one mutual Hill radius, a negative semimajor axis, eccentricity above 0.8, or a semimajor-axis change above 50%.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 1 million years | 3.9542 | 3.34×10⁻⁶ | No termination condition reached |
| 1 | 1 million years | 4.3919 | 6.16×10⁻⁶ | No termination condition reached |

| 0, half timestep | 100,000 years | 3.9547 | 8.35×10⁻⁷ | No termination condition reached |

Across the million-year trials, maximum sampled eccentricities were approximately 0.0696 for Oxhydra, 0.0210 for c, and 0.0924 for Prithvi. Fractional semimajor-axis changes stayed below 0.0062, 0.00017, and 0.0209 respectively. These evolving eccentricities should enter future climate calculations.

This screening omits the close-in b, inclinations, moons, Phaethon, minor bodies, relativity, tides, stellar evolution, and mass loss. It covers a million years rather than the system’s five-billion-year age and does not certify satellite survival or the full architecture. Full three-dimensional integrations with the inner planet, varied apsidal geometry and masses, and longer integrations remain the next dynamical work.

## Model status and remaining work

The system is a reconciled speculative-fiction working model. Remaining research consists of long-term three-dimensional planetary and satellite dynamics; hydrogen retention and atmospheric photochemistry; climate and industrial warming models; peroxide production/loss budgets; exact bastion molecular chemistry; moon heating, irradiation, and plasma supply; and the cold worlds’ thermal and volatile histories. These questions are explicit rather than presented as solved physics.

The later planet, biosphere, and clade articles supersede the earlier general summaries. Original assessment labels, categories, aliases, captions, and useful source metadata remain represented in the vault; obsolete quantitative and causal claims have been superseded. The supplied “seven confirmed planets” category is historical source metadata only. No new real detections are asserted.

**Ṭanḍī** is a frozen, oxidant-rich terrestrial planet orbiting [[BD−08°2823]] at 1.9 AU, lying just outside the system’s Peroxide Belt. With a mass of 2.5 M🜨 and a radius of roughly 1.2 R🜨, Ṭanḍī is a compact, metal-heavy world whose surface is dominated by periclase, oxidized olivines, and magnesium peroxide crusts. The planet lacks a substantial atmosphere and shows no widespread sustained tectonic activity, presenting a bright, desiccated exterior shaped by billions of years of oxidative weathering and micrometeoroid polishing.

Despite its still surface, Ṭanḍī is not entirely inert. Deep radiogenic heating maintains localized reservoirs; possible moon-driven stress may help fracture metastable pockets of sulfur-bearing volatiles in the crust. When these pockets fracture, the planet experiences brief episodes of sulfur-driven cryovolcanism, momentarily interrupting its long silence.

## Cryochemical activity

Ṭanḍī’s most distinctive feature is its rare but striking **cryochemical eruptions**. Background radiogenic heat and local stress maintain sealed volatile reservoirs. Fracture and depressurization trigger eruptions of SO₂-rich gas and entrained sulfate-bearing dust; local chemical reactions can contribute energy. Any transient fluid or slurry is confined to warmed, pressurized subsurface pockets rather than a persistent surface liquid layer.

During an eruption:

- jets of SO₂ and fine sulfate dust escape at low velocities  
- faint orange or yellow hazes drift above the fissure  
- charged aerosols produce thin, UV-scattering halos  
- new frost layers form within hours as the gases re-freeze  

Each eruption is brief. Much slow ejecta returns ballistically, with SO₂ freezing as frost and entrained mineral dust forming pale veneers. Conversion of SO₂ to sulfate requires oxidants and mineral cations and is a separate surface reaction; only a fraction of material escapes the planet. Over geological time these eruptions produce:

- short, fan-shaped albedo features  
- layered crusts of SO₄²⁻ salts  
- yellow-stained fractures across oxidant-white terrain  

Although extremely rare—perhaps centuries between significant events—they expose fresh chemical interfaces alongside the slower effects of impacts and irradiation, making Ṭanḍī a chemically restless but visually static world.

## Interior and geology

Despite a quiet surface, Ṭanḍī’s interior is dense and structured:

- **Crust** — dominated by MgO, MgO₂, forsterite, and high-oxidation silicates; anhydrous and deeply fractured.  
- **Mantle** — iron-rich olivine phases and oxidized silicate frameworks; internal convection may continue beneath a thick, slowly recycled lid.  
- **Core** — Fe–Ni alloy with possible trace segregation of Mn or Mo phases; insufficient convection for a global dynamo.  
- **Magnetism** — only remanent crustal magnetization persists, yielding a weak ~1 μT dipole signature.  

The combination of high surface gravity (~1.74 g) and the absence of erosion produces sharp, stark geomorphology: deep impact basins, uplifted fault blocks, oxidant-bright ridges, and fracture networks spanning hundreds of kilometers. Many basins retain seasonal or permanent SO₂ frost and oxidant-bearing deposits. Stable exposed liquid is absent; pressurized warm pockets can briefly contain fluids.

## Appearance

Ṭanḍī is one of the most reflective bodies in the BD−08°2823 system. From orbit it shows:

- a bright, nearly white crust shaped by peroxide weathering  
- scattered pale yellows from sulfur-stained fissures  
- broad, cold plains broken by stark fault escarpments  
- regions of faintly shimmering oxidant frost near the poles  

During a cryochemical eruption, observers would see thin, ghostly plumes rising against the black sky, casting long shadows but never growing into clouds or spreading into a sustained atmosphere. These ephemeral hazes dissipate quickly, leaving fresh pale crusts that contrast sharply with the older, more micrometeoroid-darkened terrain.

## Moons

Ṭanḍī hosts a compact two-moon system, along with several minor bodies trapped within its weak Hill sphere (~4.3 million km).

### Sarkon

A ~1,400 km-diameter retrograde moon with a sulfur-rich crust. Sarkon’s surface is mottled yellow and gray, with frozen volatile deposits and fractured plains. Its orbital perturbations may modulate fracture timing. Its spin, orbital eccentricity, and distance are not yet fixed; significant tidal heating is not inferred from retrograde motion alone.

### Bastil

An ~800 km-diameter co-formed moon composed primarily of oxidant-white silicates. Bastil has a provisional geometric albedo near 0.7. Its brightness at large phase angles depends on its scattering law. Its ancient surface shows equatorial ridges and unresolved tectonic scars from early orbital evolution.

### Minor shepherds

At least five small bodies under ~100 km orbit within faint dusty arcs of ejecta, likely sourced from past impacts on Sarkon or Bastil. These minor moons are irregular and chemically mixed, carrying both sulfur-rich and oxidant-rich signatures.

## Interpretation

Ṭanḍī is a **frozen oxidant relic**: an airless world defined by long epochs of stillness punctuated by sudden chemical flare-ups. It lacks known surface liquid water, evidence of life, or widespread sustained tectonics, yet remains rich in reactive minerals and metastable volatiles. The planet’s rare cryochemical eruptions are its most conspicuous current surface activity, preserving a record of sulfur–oxidant interactions across deep time. In the context of the BD−08°2823 system, Ṭanḍī represents the transitional zone between the dry oxidant worlds of the inner system and the peroxide-ice giants of the outer reaches.

## Model status and remaining work

The mass, radius, dense oxidized interior, bright crust, rare eruptions, Sarkon, Bastil, and at least five small shepherds are preserved. Albedo 0.4 is provisionally Bond albedo. Global stellar equilibrium is approximately 122 K at 0.225 L☉; the inherited 170–180 K range is restricted to warm local conditions rather than the mean. Near-substellar heating alone can reach roughly 173 K in the ideal zero-redistribution limit.

Sarkon and Bastil sizes are working diameters. Both moons need masses, orbits, spin/tidal models, and stability checks. The weak remanent ~1 μT field is a surface reference estimate, with geometry unknown. Specify reservoir pressure, phase, temperature, source rate, reaction energetics, and plume optics; the surface’s quiet appearance does not require a wholly inactive deep interior.

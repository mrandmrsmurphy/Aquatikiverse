**Varuna** is a cold giant planet in the [[BD−08°2823 System]], orbiting at about 6 AU, well beyond the system’s frost line. With a mass of ~17 M🜨 and a radius of ~3.7 R🜨, it is broadly comparable to Neptune in bulk properties but differs in composition and atmospheric behavior. Its extended envelope is dominated by hydrogen and helium, with methane, ammonia, water, and sulfur-bearing species. Peroxide is concentrated in irradiated deposits and replenished trace particles, not adopted as an abundant constituent of the reducing envelope.

Illuminated weakly by its K-dwarf primary, Varuna’s upper atmosphere is optically complex. Multiple cloud layers and hazes separate into pale yellow, off-white, and bluish-gray bands. Volatile ice clouds and hazes reflect light; trace peroxide-bearing particles may add spectral structure and phase-dependent scattering, giving the planet a variable high-albedo pattern across latitudes.

## Internal structure and magnetism

Below the visible cloud decks, Varuna is modeled as having:

- An outer H₂–He envelope with mixed volatile ices (NH₃, CH₄, H₂O).
- A deep layer of conductive water–ammonia-rich ionic fluids. Abundant long-lived peroxide in this hot reducing mixture is not assumed.
- A compact, rock- and metal-rich core of silicates and iron.

The convecting ionic fluid region is highly conductive and is the likely source of Varuna’s **tilted, multipolar magnetic field**. In canon, this field is strong and irregular, producing a magnetosphere extending 30–40 planetary radii. It captures stellar wind, interacts with moon-driven plasma, and supports a broad sulfur- and oxidant-enriched plasma torus.

## Atmosphere and visual appearance

Varuna’s atmosphere contains:

- **Primary gases** — H₂, He.
- **Secondary volatiles** — CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, H₂S; transient SO₂ and other oxidized sulfur species in replenished upper layers.
- **Aerosols and condensates** — volatile ice particles, trace irradiation-produced peroxide, and externally supplied mineral dust. Mg-bearing, sulfate, or silicate material requires external delivery or an identified transport pathway.

Temperature and composition gradients generate stacked cloud decks:

- Upper ammonia and sulfide clouds that appear pale yellow to off-white.
- Methane-influenced haze layers at levels set by the temperature–pressure profile with bluish-gray tones influenced by methane absorption.
- Darker, sulfur-enriched belts near certain latitudes.

Bright volatile clouds and replenished trace oxidant-bearing aerosols can contribute to:

- Time-variable bright patches and bands.
- Phase-dependent halos and subtle brightening near limb regions.

Auroral activity is present near the magnetic poles. Interactions between the magnetosphere, stellar wind, and moon-sourced plasma produce intermittent ultraviolet and visible aurorae. These events are modest compared to Jovian-scale systems but are persistent on long timescales.

Varuna is encircled by faint, peroxide-rich dust arcs. These rings are composed of micron-scale oxidant grains and ice-silicate fragments shed from small moon impacts and surface activity on outer satellites such as Dren and Charvol. Their visibility depends on particle size and scattering phase function; fine dust may brighten strongly in forward-scattering geometries. Confinement of persistent arcs requires moon interactions or other trapping, still under investigation.

## Moon system

Varuna’s moons form a compact outer-system analog to the Jovian system, but colder and more oxidizing. Tidal heating, subsurface brines, and SO₂ sublimation drive low-level activity that feeds the planetary plasma torus and rings.

Beyond these major moons, Varuna hosts smaller irregular satellites that act as additional dust sources. Collisions and micro-impacts on these bodies help maintain the planet’s thin peroxide dust rings.

## Interpretation

Varuna functions as an **outer-system chemical engine** in the BD−08°2823 system. Its conductive interior, volatile atmosphere, irradiated surface deposits, and active moon system produce:

- Persistent sulfur and oxidant plasma tori.
- Cold but ongoing surface and subsurface activity on selected moons.
- Peroxide-laced ring material and variable albedo features.

Varuna’s environment is defined by slow but ongoing chemical processing. Its activity is assessed through its own heat and mass budgets rather than a blanket claim that Uranus and Neptune are quiescent. It serves as the outer anchor of the system’s oxidation gradient, bridging the dry oxidant worlds of the inner system and the cold, surface-preserved regimes represented by [[Juṛvā]].

## Working satellite architecture and chemical supply

The inherited table is a provisional orbital snapshot, not a demonstrated resonant solution. For a 3.7 R🜨 planet, the five listed axes are approximately 94,300, 141,400, 212,200, 306,400, and 471,500 km, with periods near 0.81, 1.48, 2.72, 4.74, and 9.04 days. No exact resonance is assigned to these rounded values. Persistent strong heating requires a modeled source of eccentricity excitation, and the tabulated eccentricities may need revision.

The moons' irradiated water ice can generate H₂O₂, stored chiefly in cold surface layers. Fresh H₂O ice, energy from particles or ultraviolet radiation, and replacement of sputtered or decomposed H₂O₂ are required. Sulfur plasma needs continuing SO₂/H₂S or sulfur-bearing ejecta, predominantly from Venshal, plus ionization. Torus losses to escape, recombination, and deposition require replacement. Dust rings need continuing ice–silicate ejecta from impacts and vents.

The water–salt ocean of Caldrith and any Oryxis brines require thermal models, salt inventories, and heat budgets. They are not bulk peroxide oceans. Dren’s weak stellar heating does not by itself establish sustained SO₂ sublimation; episodic local heating is the adopted source of significant release.


## Model status and remaining work

The five named major moons and additional unnamed irregulars remain. Their masses, densities, inclinations, tidal parameters, resonances, and long-term stability require a dedicated model. Venshal’s strong heating is a setting hypothesis pending this work. Moon-sourced plasma, aurorae, and a tilted multipolar field are retained; the 30–40 Rₚ magnetospheric extent is a pressure-dependent working scale, not a fixed measured boundary.

At 6 AU the zero-albedo stellar equilibrium is about 78 K. A provisional Bond albedo of 0.4 gives approximately 68 K; a total effective temperature of 95 K would require an intrinsic effective temperature near 88 K, from fourth-power flux addition. These are working thermal parameters for evolutionary modeling. The source image description was “Pale gas giant with faint peroxide rings and moons.”

**BD−08°2823 c** (also known as **HIP 49067 c**) is a temperate Saturn-mass [exoplanet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/exoplanet) orbiting the K-type star [[BD−08°2823]], located approximately 135 [light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/light-year) away in the constellation [Sextans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextans). It was discovered alongside [BD−08°2823 b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BD%E2%88%9208%C2%B02823_b) by the [High Accuracy Radial velocity Planet Searcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Accuracy_Radial_velocity_Planet_Searcher) (HARPS) in 2009, and is part of the [[BD−08°2823 System]].

With a minimum mass of about 0.33 M♃ (~105 M🜨), BD−08°2823 c retains a substantial hydrogen–helium envelope. In-setting models treat it as a compact Saturn-mass gas giant with pale sulfur-influenced clouds and a possible very faint ion nimbus.

## Physical characteristics

The setting adopts 105 M🜨 (approximately 0.33 M♃) and a speculative radius of 5.7 R🜨. These imply a mean density near 3.13 g/cm³, surface gravity near 3.23 g, and escape speed near 48 km/s: a compact, enriched gas giant.

For the fictional architecture, c is moved modestly outward to 0.72 AU and assigned a working eccentricity of 0.02 and period near 259 days. The real discovery fit is retained separately in the reference, with a period of 237.6 days and estimated semimajor axis near 0.68 ±0.02 AU. The setting refit is an authorized departure from those observations, not a claim about a revised real measurement.[^hebrard2010]

The interior model contains a dense Fe–Ni–Si core, rock and compressed water-bearing material, and a substantial hydrogen–helium envelope. MgO, FeO, MnO, salts, and silicates contribute to the heavy-element inventory. An ionic water–ammonia region and an intrinsic dynamo remain possible. Persistent deep MgO₂ or H₂O₂ reservoirs are not assumed in hot, reducing material.

## Atmosphere and photochemistry

H₂ and He dominate the observable atmosphere. Methane, ammonia, water, and hydrogen sulfide supply volatile chemistry and clouds. The visible planet remains pale yellow and gray with subdued banding and bright haze patches.

At 0.72 AU, stellar flux is about 0.434 F🜨 for 0.225 L☉. The zero-albedo equilibrium temperature is about 226 K; reflective clouds lower the stellar contribution. Internal heat and observing depth determine the actual temperature profile. The previous 800–900 K dayside assertion is retired.

Upper-atmospheric photochemistry can produce short-lived sulfur and oxygen species. Schematic elementary channels include:

```text
SO₂ + hν → SO + O
H₂O + hν → H + OH
```

Any SO₂, H₂O₂, sulfate, or atomic oxygen enrichment is local and replenished; it does not make the bulk hydrogen envelope oxidizing. External dust supplies possible Mg-, Fe-, and silicate-bearing material. Cold trapping limits the upward supply of refractory elements. CO and hydrocarbon abundances require a photochemical model rather than categorical absence claims.

## Ion ring and mass loss

![[images/BD−08°2823c.png]]

*Artist’s depiction of the proposed faint ion nimbus.*

The setting retains a tenuous, asymmetric nimbus of sulfur and oxygen ions, with possible trace Na and Mg from externally supplied dust. Its source budget combines upper-atmospheric escape, sputtering, and meteoroid input; prolonged erosion of the bulk envelope is not established.

Candidate species include S⁺, SO⁺, SO₂⁺, O, O⁺, and trace Na⁺ and Mg⁺. A compact magnetospheric torus and material swept into a tail are separate structures. Their density, extent, residence times, field trapping, and ionization rates remain under investigation.

Detection would rely on appropriate neutral and ionic spectral lines, ultraviolet absorption in favorable viewing geometries, or radio diagnostics. The nimbus is nearly invisible in ordinary broadband views.

## Visual appearance

The pale yellow-gray globe shows sulfur-influenced belts, bright volatile clouds, and subtle haze variations. Trace external mineral dust is a spectral possibility rather than an established silicate cloud deck. Any line fluorescence is faint and wavelength-specific; visible metallic flashes are not assumed.

The supplied illustrations retain the original ghostly nimbus concept. Bulk appearance is that of a compact gas giant; its faint surrounding plasma needs sensitive instruments.

## Satellites and co-orbitals

No regular moons are adopted. Early migration offers a possible historical explanation, although the present Hill radius of approximately 5.7 million km leaves geometric room for satellites.

The fictional approximately 250 km asteroid **Phaethon** occupies a candidate tadpole orbit around L₄, about 60° ahead of c. Its surface contains Mg-rich silicates, iron-depleted basalt, and possible irradiation-produced oxidant coatings. Its approximately 0.72 AU distance from the planet at this geometry places it far outside a compact planetary plasma torus. Stellar irradiation and local dust processing dominate its surface environment.

Phaethon’s rotation and irregular shape produce photometric variation. Libration is gravitational. Its long-term co-orbital survival, including perturbations from Oxhydra and Prithvi, remains to be tested.

## Comparative analysis

The volatile envelopes and atmospheric-loss diagnostics of lower-mass worlds such as [GJ 3470 b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GJ_3470_b), [HD 97658 b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD_97658_b), and [TOI-421 b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOI-421_b) provide qualitative comparisons. Their different masses and irradiation prevent direct transfer of mass-loss behavior to c. The adopted body is Saturn-mass, with an unusually compact speculative radius.

## Model status and remaining work

The real discovery is documented in the retained bibliography. c’s in-setting orbit is a deliberate fictional refit. The source Sky coordinates were 10h 00m 47.7s, −09°31′00″, 135 light-years; the primary image description was “Pale clouded Neptune-like planet with a faint asymmetric ring.” The supplied October 19 discovery date is retained as reported. Plasma production, three-dimensional orbital evolution, and Phaethon’s stability remain open modeling tasks.

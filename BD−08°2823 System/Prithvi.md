**Prithvi** is a temperate terrestrial planet orbiting the K-type star [[BD−08°2823]] at a distance of about 0.94 AU. By bulk composition and climate it is the most Earth-like body in the [[BD−08°2823 System]], but it is more land-dominated and has a weaker hydrological cycle than Earth. 

In its natural state Prithvi was entirely abiotic: a nitrogen–oxygen world with seasonal surface water but no indigenous life, and an oxygen inventory generated by photochemistry and hydrogen escape. In Aquatikiverse canon it is now the focus of a staged terraforming and colonization effort led by the Indian-backed corporate consortium known as the **Mohan Terraforming Initiative**. The surface is marginally habitable with technological support, and large-scale attempts to stabilize coupled carbon–oxygen cycles are ongoing.

## Physical and orbital characteristics

Prithvi has a mass of about 1.1 M🜨 and a radius of roughly 1.12 R🜨, giving a surface gravity close to 0.9 g. It orbits BD−08°2823 on a nearly circular orbit with a period of approximately 387 Earth days. At 0.94 AU from a subsolar K-dwarf, Prithvi receives significantly less stellar flux than Earth does from the Sun, but maintains Earthlike mean surface temperatures (~295 K) through an actively maintained engineered greenhouse. This approximately 295 K present reference state is a terraforming achievement; the pre-intervention planet was substantially colder, with seasonal or localized liquid water. It was not a naturally warm Earth analogue.

The planet’s axial tilt is about 21°, slightly less than Earth’s. Seasonal illumination varies with the tilt, while limited ocean buffering permits substantial regional temperature swings. Terraforming management moderates selected climates. A single substantial moon, **Saathi**, orbits at roughly 4.2×10⁵ km and has a mass ~1.2% that of Prithvi. Saathi contributes to axial precession and may reduce obliquity excursions; the long-term spin–orbit behavior remains a dynamical modeling question.

## Surface and geography

Prithvi’s surface is land-heavy: about 65% of the globe is covered by continents and high plateaus, with only ~35% occupied by standing bodies of water. Large, deep oceans are rare. Most surface water is found in:

- Modest polar and subpolar seas.
- Equatorial and mid-latitude basins that fill seasonally.
- Shallow, ephemeral lakes and inland seas that appear and vanish with regional hydrological cycles.

Substantial polar ice caps store a significant fraction of the planet’s water inventory. Seasonal waxing and waning of ice cover drives noticeable albedo shifts and regional climate variability.

Tectonically, Prithvi is active. The most dramatic structure is the **Maw of Fire**, a broad, roughly Pac-Man–shaped complex of subduction trenches, strike-slip fault systems, and overlapping volcanic arcs. This region experiences frequent large earthquakes and near-continuous volcanism. It is unsuitable for permanent settlement and is designated a high-risk exclusion zone by the Mohan Initiative.

Away from the Maw of Fire, the planet hosts:

- A broad equatorial belt of more isolated, predictable volcanoes aligned along major plate boundaries.
- Numerous mountain ranges of moderate height, but no mountain systems rivaling the Himalayas in absolute elevation.
- Extensive highland plateaus, basins, and rift valleys that act as seasonal catchments for water.

One of the notable physiographic features is a wide interior **steppe region**, originally cold and semi-arid, which has become one of the first large-scale terraformed grassland zones.

## Atmosphere and climate

Prithvi’s atmosphere is breathable but originally abiotic in origin. Present-day surface pressure is about 1.2 bar. The bulk composition is:

- Nitrogen (N₂): balance to 100% of dry air after managed trace gases, approximately 78.55%
- Oxygen (O₂): ~20.5% of dry air
- Argon (Ar): ~0.9% of dry air
- Water vapor (H₂O): variable, approximately 2% in the humid reference air; reported separately from dry fractions
- CO₂: ~0.05% of dry air; xenon and other noble gases are traces. Managed greenhouse gases displace an equal fraction of the nitrogen balance.

Prior to terraforming, oxygen accumulated during earlier water loss, photodissociation, and hydrogen escape. Much was consumed by crustal and volcanic sinks; the remaining inventory survived into the pre-landing era. Its original quantity and subsequent sink rates are incompletely constrained. The absence of indigenous biology meant no large-scale buffering of CO₂ or O₂. Methane (CH₄) and clear biogenic disequilibria were effectively absent.

In the current early-terraforming phase, the Initiative maintains an engineered greenhouse using a proposed industrial fluorocarbon suite, provisionally including CF₄, C₂F₆, and SF₆, alongside CO₂, H₂O feedback, and monitored trace N₂O and C₂H₆. The concentrations, overlapping absorption bands, required industrial throughput, atmospheric lifetimes, and acceptable exposure limits are not yet solved. The original trace N₂O and ethane inventory alone is not assumed to provide the entire warming. Their ongoing replenishment and removal are part of climate management.

Climatically, Prithvi is:

- Overall temperate, with a global mean near 22 °C.
- Moderately cloudy, but with weaker, higher cloud decks than on Earth.
- Dominated by arid and semi-arid land climates outside of coastal and steppe regions.

Bare deserts coexist with extensive dry steppe. The relative desert area depends on the managed climate and land classification, and is not assigned a fixed comparison with Earth. Much of the interior landmass falls into dry temperate or steppe regimes, with seasonal lakes and shallow seas forming in basins during local spring and summer, then shrinking or evaporating back during cooler seasons.

Prithvi lacks an ocean-dominated climate engine. Without large, deep oceans acting as heat and carbon reservoirs, its climate responds more quickly to radiative forcing and compositional changes, which is both an asset and a risk for terraforming efforts.

## Terraforming and colonization

Pre-intervention surveys found no indigenous life; Prithvi is canonically abiotic before human intervention. Spectroscopic and in situ non-detections support that classification without constituting exhaustive proof of sterility. The atmosphere’s oxygen content, while high enough for human respiration, lacked detected methane, nitrous oxide, or other convincing biological disequilibria in the surveyed environments.

The **Mohan Terraforming Initiative** is a corporate–state consortium originating from Indian stakeholders, created to develop Prithvi as a long-term settlement world. Terraforming has proceeded in stages:

### Pre-landing microbe seeding

The long preconditioning program began before human arrival; its final microbe-seeding campaign commenced decades before landing. At that stage, automated impactors and atmospheric probes dispersed engineered extremophile microbes across targeted regions. The initial organisms colonized geothermal or otherwise protected niches; industrial warming progressively expanded their range. They were designed to:

- Enhance silicate weathering rates.
- Modulate surface albedo and soil formation.
- Produce or consume specific trace gases (e.g. N₂O, C₂H₆) to tune climate.

This “preconditioning” phase focused on the steppe basins and selected coastal lowlands.

### Initial human landing and closed habitats

Major industrial greenhouse deployment and warming preceded broad open-air agriculture. The first human crews established sealed bases with full internal air recycling. Atmospheric O₂ levels were technically breathable, but not yet biologically regulated. Early colonists relied on:

- Local water extraction from aquifers, ice, and seasonal lakes.
- Greenhouse modules for food production.
- Monitoring networks to track O₂, CO₂, and trace gas evolution and to watch for unstable feedbacks.

### Open-cycle experiments and land management

As engineered biota took hold, the Initiative began limited open-cycle trials:

- Introducing fast-growing, hardy plants in managed steppe zones.
- Deploying artificial CO₂ scrubbers and solid sorbents to offset volcanic and industrial emissions.
- Seeding soil-forming microbes and fungi analogs to deepen regolith and increase water retention.

Some settlements have transitioned to partially open systems, using a controlled mix of local and recycled air. Others remain fully sealed until long-term stability is demonstrated.

The Mohan Initiative maintains centralized control rather than operating as an independent planetary government. Orbital infrastructure includes a satellite relay constellation, high-orbit logistics hubs, and at least one functioning tether or elevator system linking a stable equatorial site to orbit. Two further migration and expansion waves are planned, contingent on ecological robustness and political approval.

## Saathi

Prithvi’s sole natural satellite, **Saathi**, orbits at a mean distance of ~4.23×10⁵ km. With a mass of ~7.9×10²² kg (~1.2% of Prithvi’s mass), Saathi plays a major role in:

- Contributing to axial precession, with possible obliquity stabilization pending modeling.
- Driving moderate ocean and lake tides in the limited standing seas.
- Providing a predictable short-term orbital reference; long-term precession remains to be modeled.

The working model assigns Saathi synchronous rotation with its approximately 30-day orbital period. The earlier nonsynchronous assertion is retired because no maintaining mechanism was supplied. Geologically, it is largely inactive, with an ancient, cratered crust and only minor tectonic or cryovolcanic activity. The moon is used extensively for:

- Orbital logistics and fuel depots.
- Deep-sky and exoplanet observation facilities.
- Seismic and gravitational monitoring relays for early warning of major tectonic events on Prithvi.

## Model status and remaining work

The planet remains naturally abiotic before intervention and biologically seeded at the current epoch. Approximately 295 K is a setting target maintained by substantial engineered forcing, not a demonstrated outcome of the listed atmosphere. At 0.94 AU, the working stellar flux is about 0.255 F🜨. The climate needs a full spectral model, industrial supply/removal rates, and a chronology for the cold natural state, warming, and settlement. No fluorocarbon concentrations are prescribed before that calculation.

Saathi needs a radius, orbital eccentricity and plane, and tidal parameters. Prithvi’s rotation period and tether design are also unspecified; check synchronous altitude, strength, and satellite clearance before specifying the functioning equatorial elevator’s engineering. The 65% land and 35% water values are current reference coverage, with seasonal changes and substantial ice storage.

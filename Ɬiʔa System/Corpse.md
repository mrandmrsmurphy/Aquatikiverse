# The Corpse

![[images/The_Corpse_and_moons.png|300]]

*The cold, airless remnant and its two satellites, g1 and the enlarged atmosphere-bearing g2.*

**The Corpse**, AQ-MV-01 g, is the sixth planetary orbital entry in [[Ɬiʔa System]]. It lost its original hydrogen–helium envelope and now presents a desiccated, mineral-rich, geologically quiet exterior. Deep residual volatiles distinguish a stripped sub-Neptune from a completely dry bare-rock core. Its two contrasting moons remain important laboratories for volatile retention and tidal geology.

## History and quiet interior

Formation approximately 4.5 Gyr ago included volatile-rich material, plausibly beyond the primordial snow line. Early migration into a more irradiated region allowed XUV-driven atmospheric loss, core-powered escape and sputtering to remove the original light-gas envelope. Subsequent gravitational restructuring placed the remnant at its present orbit. The exact migration/scattering trajectory, including the origin of its polar stellar orbit and satellite survival or later acquisition, is unresolved. Current distance alone is not claimed to explain the stripping.

The retained mass and radius favor deep water-rich or other volatile-bearing material beneath a mineral lag, ice-bearing strata and an old rocky exterior. A global pure-rock composition is not adopted. Low present surface activity and a weak intrinsic field do not require complete interior solidification within the first 100–200 million years. Residual thermal structure is uncalculated; some deep phases may remain mobile without visible widespread volcanism.

## Surface, temperature and minerals

The 20-hour spin produces moving day and night. Ancient compositional differences can remain, but no hemisphere is permanently star-facing or permanently frozen. At albedo 0.5 the instantaneous unredistributed substellar reference is approximately 146 K; dark material can be warmer. Rotation, thermal inertia, latitude and seasonal geometry determine actual temperatures. The old universal 80–120-K permanent nightside and 1000-K present lava hemisphere are both retired. There is no need to heat an entire dark hemisphere with implausibly large geothermal flux.

Oxidized basalt, high-silica volcanic glass, rutile, sodium chloride, calcite and gypsum preserve a complex early volcanic, aqueous and evaporitic history. Bright deposits contrast with dark vitrified plains. These are inherited minerals, not calcium salts condensing from a present 150-K vapor atmosphere. Hydrated-mineral persistence depends on local temperature, water activity and kinetics, not a generic triple-point rule. Thermal cracking, impact gardening and charged-particle weathering continue without active plate tectonics.

No substantial atmosphere is retained. Impacts, sputtering and occasional small releases may generate transient exospheric particles; ordinary thermal evaporation of refractory minerals is negligible at the adopted cold temperatures. Absence of an intrinsic magnetosphere does not eliminate induced plasma interactions.

## g1: fractured inner moon

Adopted diameter **1,400 km**, mean density **3.0 g/cm³**, gravity **0.587 m/s² (~0.060 g)** and escape speed **0.91 km/s**. Its reference orbit is moved to **150,000 km** from the primary, with synchronous satellite rotation. It retains chaotic terrain, lenticulae, cryogeysers and a possible subsurface ocean. Slight eccentricity and interactions with g2 may sustain dissipation, but the ocean and heat output remain conditional on a tidal/thermal solution. Bright fissures reflect or scatter light unless an actual emission mechanism is supplied; cold ice is not incandescent.

A tenuous oxygen/water exosphere may result from ice radiolysis and outgassing. It is not a dense breathable atmosphere, nor is its faint corona guaranteed visible to unaided eyes.

## g2: atmosphere-bearing outer moon

Adopted diameter **5,200 km**, mean density **2.1 g/cm³**, mass **0.0259 M⊕**, gravity **1.53 m/s²** and escape speed **2.82 km/s**. This Titan-scale enlargement improves atmospheric retention relative to the original 2,100-km body; an active-star escape calculation is still necessary. Its reference orbit is **238,110 km**, close to an exterior 2:1 period ratio with g1. Resonant libration and maintained eccentricities are targets, not demonstrated facts.

The adopted atmospheric target remains approximately **0.3 bar**, primarily nitrogen and argon with methane and trace carbon monoxide. A thin golden photochemical haze overlies muted blue-green terrain, cold glacial uplands and hydrocarbon basins. Surface conditions around **115–130 K** are a climate target requiring greenhouse and heat-transport modeling, not the result of size alone. Lakes are principally **ethane-rich mixtures**; persistent pure methane lakes require temperatures and methane partial pressures consistent with phase equilibrium. Buried reservoirs and episodic liquid weather remain possible.

Both moons orbit prograde near the primary's equatorial plane in the reference layout. The primary's 20-hour spin places their initial orbits outside its approximately 51,000-km synchronous radius. Their mutual tides, orbital expansion and the enlarged g2's backreaction require evolutionary modeling. A polar stellar orbit does not by itself grant satellite stability; stellar perturbations and the planet's equatorial orientation must be included.

## Discovery and observation

The **First Mirar Survey Probe**, the **Proxima Outbound Mission Archive** and **2197 CE** remain the fictional discovery attribution. The archive name does not place a probe near Mirar's Veil merely because it passed Proxima. Detection is treated as remote survey data attributed to that program; in-situ gravitational mapping at this 50-light-year system is not asserted for the discovery date. Claims of anomalous lensing and precise moon-sensitive transit detection are historical, not established observing geometry.

Mottled salts and glass, rotating light curves and the satellites' hazes/exospheres remain observational signatures. Mutual occultations and stellar transits depend on the viewing direction; the polar orbital inclination is relative to the system plane, not a promise of transits from Earth. The Corpse remains a prototype of envelope loss followed by cold surface quiescence rather than an example of all volatile matter being removed.

## Physical basis

Values use the stellar reference, SI units, spherical bulk geometry, Kepler periods and circular Hill scales; they are not N-body, fluid, climate, atmospheric-escape or evolutionary calculations. Irradiation is the semimajor-axis reference, not an eccentric-orbit average. Gray equilibrium temperature is [1361 S (1 − A) / (4σ)]^¼, and the nonredistributed substellar reference is √2 times that. At 0.400 AU and 206.6166 days the Corpse receives 0.036902 S⊕ and has a gray equilibrium of 102.6 K at a Bond albedo of 0.5; the bulk is 2.6 M⊕ and 1.7 R⊕.

The adopted 20-hour prograde spin puts the synchronous radius at about 51,437 km, and both moon orbits start outside it. g1 sits at 150,000 km and g2 at 238,110 km: the nominal axis ratio is 2^(2/3), and finite moon masses shift the unconstrained Kepler period ratio slightly from exactly two. A complete resonance fit would need chosen orbital elements and a libration test. For an assumed primary k₂/Q = 3×10⁻⁵, an isolated circular constant-Q tide gives initial a/|da/dt| scales of about 21–37 Gyr for the two moons; this is a local migration estimate, not a lifetime, since moon tides, eccentricity, spin evolution, resonance and stellar perturbations are omitted.

g2 is adopted with a radius of 2,600 km and a density of 2,100 kg/m³: 0.0259 M⊕, a surface gravity of 1.526 m/s² and an escape speed of 2.82 km/s. Its 0.3-bar nitrogen/argon atmosphere and 115–130 K climate are setting targets that need retention and thermal calculations, and ethane-rich liquid mixtures, not pure methane, are the expected lake composition. g1's liquid ocean depends on a viable dissipative heat budget and is not guaranteed by its nearness to g2.

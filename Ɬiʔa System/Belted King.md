# Belted King

![[images/Belted_King.png|275]]

*The blue–cyan mini-Neptune, its structured rings and five moons.*

**Belted King**, AQ-MV-01 f, is a ringed mini-Neptune in [[Ɬiʔa System]]. Its translated native name evokes its broad rings and prominence in early astronomy. Its deep volatile envelope, dark vortices and diverse five-moon system remain defining features.

## Atmosphere and interior

Hydrogen and helium dominate the envelope. Methane absorption and molecular scattering produce the blue–cyan appearance. Ammonia, water, phosphine, acetylene and other photochemical products contribute to a vertically structured atmosphere. Water-rich and other volatile layers overlie a rock–metal interior; specific Ice VII/X layers and the envelope mass fraction require an equation-of-state model rather than inference from radius alone.

A Bond-albedo reference of 0.35 gives a gray equilibrium near 166 K. Broad upper-atmospheric values around 150–250 K and deeper levels at 300–500 K are atmospheric design ranges at different pressures, not one measured emitting temperature. Deep heat does not automatically produce a 500-K photosphere. Near-infrared absorption, condensate formation and convection need a consistent pressure–temperature profile.

Zonal jets, alternating belts and long-lived dark vortices survive the faster spin. Illumination moves across the atmosphere; there is no permanently fixed day hemisphere. Upper hazes are photochemical aerosols, with ammonia/water condensates where their phase conditions permit. Methane ice requires an explicitly cold enough layer and is not assigned universally to 150–250-K clouds. A modest intrinsic field remains possible but unconfirmed. Ring-opening variations depend on obliquity and viewing geometry; the old precise century-long precession calendar is retired.

## Rings

Distances are measured from the center, not above the surface.

| Region | Radius in planetary radii | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ring C | 1.50–1.65 | Faint silicate/carbonaceous dust |
| Ring B | 1.65–1.96 | Dominant optically thick icy/mixed band |
| Division | 1.96–2.04 | Low-density gap |
| Ring A | 2.04–2.30 | Bright outer band with sharp boundary |

The full system spans approximately 19,100–29,300 km. Large ice-rich particles, mixed debris and replenished dust can preserve its luminous appearance. Grain temperatures depend on absorption, especially under a red-dwarf spectrum, and optical depth. Fine ice is not assumed to survive indefinitely. Ring mass, age and replenishment remain unassigned. Candidate embedded shepherds may help maintain edges; the Division is not asserted to coincide with a strong resonance of Moon I or II without calculating it. Roche limits depend on particle/body density and cohesion.

## Five moons

These are adopted reference orbits, with the stated identities preserved. They require a coupled integration and tidal evolution calculation before being described as demonstrated long-lived arrangements.

| Moon | Axis from center | Diameter | Orbit and identity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | 60,000 km | 400 km | Low-inclination prograde rocky cratered body |
| II | 110,000 km | 800 km | Prograde mixed rock–ice body; fractured crust and possible buried liquid |
| III | 190,000 km | 1,200 km | Prograde major icy moon; cryovolcanic plains and possible subsurface mobility |
| IV | 250,000 km | Unspecified | Dark-red captured carbonaceous body; e ≈ 0.1, inclination ≈ 20° |
| V | 450,000 km | 350 km | Captured retrograde body; nominal e = 0.05 and inclination = 160° |

Moon V retains its rough, heavily cratered hemisphere and smoother possible impact-resurfaced terrain. Its old 710,000-km orbit is retired; Moon IV is moved inward from 340,000 km. More strongly retrograde geometry reduces one known high-inclination hazard, without proving stability. The 15-hour prograde planetary spin places all five reference moons outside the synchronous radius initially, avoiding the old universally inward tidal torque from retrograde planetary rotation. Outward migration and changing spin still require a finite budget.

Moon III's activity requires eccentricity forcing and dissipation consistent with its heat loss. Nearby moons offer a possible source of perturbations but no particular moon resonance or sustained ocean is claimed as calculated. No large Trojan or horseshoe population is specified; temporary co-orbitals remain possibilities, not discovered objects.

## Physical basis

Values use the stellar reference, SI units, spherical bulk geometry, Kepler periods and circular Hill scales; they are not N-body, fluid, climate, atmospheric-escape or evolutionary calculations. Irradiation is the semimajor-axis reference, not an eccentric-orbit average. Gray equilibrium temperature is [1361 S (1 − A) / (4σ)]^¼, and the nonredistributed substellar reference is √2 times that. At 0.17289 AU and 58.7124 days Belted King receives 0.19753 S⊕, giving 166.6 K at a Bond albedo of 0.35; the bulk is 3.5 M⊕ and 2.0 R⊕.

The adopted 15-hour prograde rotation is tested with a constant-Q stellar torque (k₂ = 0.3, inertia coefficient 0.25, Q = 10⁵–10⁶): the ratio of spin angular momentum to torque is about 59–587 Gyr. Those dissipation parameters are illustrative assumptions, so the spin is plausible to investigate, not a reconstructed 4.5-Gyr spin history. Domingos, Winter & Yokoyama (2006, [MNRAS 373, 1227](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/2006MNRAS.373.1227D)) give restricted-problem satellite-stability boundaries; their coplanar fits are not universal guarantees for inclined multisatellite systems such as the retrograde outer moon. Specific ring resonances, cold-particle lifetime and interior phase assignments remain unmodeled.

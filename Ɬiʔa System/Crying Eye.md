# Crying Eye

![[images/Wet_eye_planet.png]]

*A reasonable approximation of Crying Eye's frozen surface and dark substellar eye; not a calculated ice or emission map.*

**Crying Eye**, designated **Mirar's Veil d / AQ-MV-01 d**, is a water-rich, synchronously rotating planet in the [[Ɬiʔa System]]. A dark substellar opening, the **Furnace**, interrupts a mostly glaciated outer surface. Below the fractured shell, liquid reservoirs, high-pressure ice, mineral-rich fluids and radiogenic exchange provinces support [[Life on Crying Eye|an independently evolved biosphere]] whose principal deep habitats operate at pressures of order **1–3 GPa**.

Its defining biological premise is literal: natural fission provinces are sufficiently widespread in the setting that life evolved to exploit them and eventually incorporated **Corions**, biologically integrated fission systems. This is an explicit major speculative capability. It is not replaced by ordinary radioactive decay, and it is not demonstrated by Earth’s Oklo reactors or by high pressure alone.

## Orbit, climate and the Furnace

Crying Eye occupies the d orbit of the c-centered architecture. At reference albedo 0.3, irradiation can support a comparatively warm substellar patch while leaving a cold global radiative reference. The **frozen-versus-eye** appearance is therefore the design priority, rather than a location outside the habitable zone.

The Furnace combines dark exposed water or brine, thin or broken ice, suspended minerals and upwelling activity. Surface ice, clouds, atmospheric transport, sublimation and oceanic heat determine its eventual size. The 90%-glaciated target is not derived by simply equating local radiative temperature to a melting point. The depth of the opening and access to deeper habitats are also not implied by its dark appearance.

The atmosphere is thin relative to Earth's, but pressure alone does not establish that it is transparent in every UV or infrared band. A photochemical veil and episodic plumes can shield some wavelengths while other energetic radiation penetrates. Outgassing, photochemistry, surface exchange and atmospheric escape require distinct source–sink budgets. A 2-M⊕ water world is not automatically Mars-like in its inability to retain gases.

## Layered interior and pressure geography

### Fractured outer shell

An approximately **20-km** reference ice lid overlies shallower liquid and brine reservoirs. Continent-sized plates, fractures, mineral stains, clathrate/hydrate deposits, cryogenic reef-like structures and crystalline spires/fans remain distinctive surface features. Upwelling vapor and droplets can freeze or degas during eruption. Such structures are chemical/mineral precipitates unless a biological origin is separately established.

This lid does **not** put the immediate ocean roof at several gigapascals. With a reference ice density of 920 kg/m³ and surface gravity, the pressure beneath it is approximately **0.184 GPa**. The main gigapascal habitats lie deeper. The 20-km thickness varies by province, thermal state and time; a permanently uniform conductive lid is not imposed over active regions.

### Liquid, brine and phase-interface habitats

Using a reference brine density of 1100 kg/m³ and constant surface gravity gives illustrative pressures of roughly **1 GPa at 94 km**, **2 GPa at 185 km**, and **3 GPa at 275 km** below the surface. Compression, self-gravity and actual layering alter those depths. These estimates establish pressure scales; they do not establish a continuous all-liquid ocean at every depth.

The oceanic domain includes broad reservoirs, chemically stratified waters, warmer channels, brine-rich interfaces and possible liquid layers separated by ice. Temperature and dissolved species must place each liquid habitat within its actual mixture phase diagram. Upper oxidized regions and deeper reducing provinces are maintained by separate sources and transport rather than a permanently homogeneous mixture of peroxide, ammonia and organics.

Water–ammonia–methanol chemistry remains important, with salts, ions and organic species varying among provinces. Pressure changes phase stability, molecular interactions and reaction chemistry; these organisms are not ordinary terrestrial cells merely placed in deep water. Near-/supercritical fluids occur only in sufficiently hot geological domains or explicitly specified special biological systems, not as the default state of all cold brines. Ice VI/VII are high-pressure ice phases, distinct from clathrates.

### High-pressure ice continents and exchange provinces

Deeper Ice-VI/VII-rich regions form slowly evolving ridges, domes and submerged “continents.” Their interfaces channel fluid flow and provide ecological boundaries. Cryoquakes, freezing/melting, deforming ice and brine pathways redistribute material. Larger-scale exchange may move rock-derived electrolytes and mineral-bearing inclusions through the ice, but its flux must be calculated; the ice is neither automatically an absolute chemical barrier nor an unrestricted nutrient conduit. [High-pressure electrolyte transport research](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-30796-5)

The richest **exchange provinces** are mineral- and fluid-fed corridors that connect reactive rock, water-rich layers and inhabited brines. They are source regions for fission-associated deposits, chemical gradients, trace nutrients and ecological expansion. Vast intervening regions may be sparse or isolated. No requirement is imposed that every high-pressure-ice continent directly exposes rock or that every mineral crosses the mantle unchanged.

### Rock-rich interior and heat

A rock-rich deep interior supplies geochemical and thermal context. Uranium, thorium and potassium occur in appropriate silicate/mineral reservoirs rather than being assigned arbitrary unlimited heat in a metallic core. The deep pressure and temperature ranges are not the normal bodily conditions of organisms in the ocean.

A declared exploratory total internal-heat budget of **10¹⁴ W** corresponds to roughly **0.10 W/m²** averaged over the surface. Within that total, **10¹³ W** is provisionally assigned to all geological and biological fission combined; it is not added a second time as extra heat. These are finite fictional model inputs, not an inferred fuel inventory or validated cooling history. They require an interior/phase/transport calculation, especially because a cold uniform 20-km conductive lid (k ≈ 651/T, with boundaries at 220 and 260 K) would carry only several mW/m². Regional thinning, convection, warmer interfaces and melting must be assessed.

## Natural fission provinces and Corions

Crying Eye's fiction assumes an unusual geochemical and nuclear history that permits **many active natural fission provinces** over the biosphere's history, including the current era. Individual sites activate, fade, become depleted or are replaced by newly exposed resources. Abundant across a planet does not mean spatially uniform, perpetually active or inexhaustible.

Earth's Oklo provides the inspiration that geological fission can occur; it does not supply the planetary distribution, present resource conditions or biological capability adopted here. Stellar [Fe/H], water pressure and ordinary chemical concentration do not alone establish those conditions. The detailed nuclear inventory remains unspecified rather than being replaced by invented stable radioactive elements or an unsupported real-world feasibility claim.

Sessile life first exploited the fluids, radiolytic products and electrochemical gradients around these provinces. Later Corion lineages biologically integrated fission-bearing mineral systems as macroscopic, colonial or host-associated power organs. Literal mobile Corions are retained as a major speculative evolutionary achievement, distinct from ubiquitous micron-scale microbes. Their inheritance, regulation, damage tolerance, waste export and portability carry real ecological costs; they are not universally tiny self-sufficient reactor cells. See [[Life on Crying Eye]].

## Electrical and magnetohydrodynamic phenomena

Conductive brines, changing external fields, fluid motion, electrochemical contrasts and mineralized structures produce electrical activity and magnetic signatures. Biological networks can influence local current paths and redox chemistry. A static field does not itself replenish metabolic energy, and any harvesting draws from finite fluid, stellar, geological or biological work.

Localized changes in hot mineral-rich fluids or melt-bearing regions can disturb electrical conditions. The colloquial **“corium pods”** are not a claim that all deep rock is melted reactor fuel or that radioactive material routinely explodes upward through the entire mantle. Reactor provinces, deep rock melts, ordinary radiogenic heat and biological Corions are different categories.

Deep magnetic anomalies have frequency- and conductivity-dependent coupling through the ocean. Magnetic “lenses” refer to particle/current geometry, not the focusing of neutral neutrons or gamma rays. Oceanic currents can be modified by electromagnetic forces only with corresponding energy and momentum exchange; a transient anomaly is not an unbudgeted global pump.

## False auroras and volatile cycling

The **false auroras** remain luminous events above the Furnace that are not tightly synchronized to stellar flares. Proposed contributors include internally initiated venting and charged aerosols, electrical discharges, and changing conductivity that modulates interaction with stellar plasma. Their visible energy must come from actual electrical, chemical, thermal or particle sources. A deep magnetic pulse alone is not proof of an atmospheric light source.

Geysers and fracture systems also return nitrogen, xenon, water-derived species and organic precursors to the atmosphere. Xenon can have primordial, radiogenic/fission-related and geochemical transport histories; no fixed isotope signal or eruption efficiency is invented. Biological alteration of channels and mineral deposits may influence this cycling, but a literal magnetic field does not simply fling all neutral gas to space.

Surface precipitates include water-rich ice, organics, minerals and regionally permitted volatile phases. A nitrogen-frost blanket is not assumed under a nitrogen atmosphere at this thermal reference. Atmosphere, frost survival and haze production need their own phase and photochemical calculation.

## Biosphere and surface exchange

Main article: [[Life on Crying Eye]]. The native biosphere originated approximately **2.7 Gyr ago**, around **1.8 Gyr after planetary formation**, in pressure-dependent mineral/phase-interface environments. Literal Corions, Magnetrobes, Ammoniaphages, Magnetomycota, Ignitors, Fission Lures and Cryoterrans make up the biosphere. **Carbon Suits are sapient natives**; their detailed body plan and cultural account have not been supplied and are not invented here.

Deep lineages use fundamentally different pressure-adapted organic–mineral chemistry, not an assumed terrestrial protein/lipid/DNA toolkit. Mobile descendants evolved portable boundaries; the surface colonization sequence passes through shallower brine systems, ice veins, vent chambers and protected surface niches. The surface forms do not carry their original 1–3-GPa habitat pressure in an unexplained small shell.

Surface carbon migration is driven by access to useful organic feedstocks, structural carbon and oxidants concentrated near the Furnace and Cryoplate Wastes. It is not a contradiction in which an ocean rich in usable methanol somehow lacks all carbon. Methanol-rich habitats are not necessarily carbon-starved; other basins are limited by the form, availability or transport of carbon. Burial, returning organisms and brine exchange connect surface collection to the deeper food webs.

## Evolution, feedbacks and model status

The Great Waste Crisis, evolution of waste processors, biological incorporation of fission, diversification, conductive reef networks, energetic arms races and surface migration remain the evolutionary sequence. The biosphere article places this sequence on the 2.7-Gyr biological history, while 4.5 Gyr is the planet and star formation age.

Ecological feedbacks can change local mineral distribution, current closure, carbon recycling, habitats and the location of heat release. These are regional, finite, potentially destabilizing processes as well as stabilizing ones. Life does not automatically control all planetary nuclear decay or maintain a planet-wide synchronous living circuit.

The high-pressure nuclear biosphere is explicit speculative fiction supported by physical bookkeeping where available.

## Physical basis

**Pressure with depth.** Below the reference lid, p ≈ ρ_ice g H_ice + ρ_brine g (z − H_ice), with fixed densities (920 and 1,100 kg/m³) and surface gravity rather than a compressible hydrostatic model. It gives only the order of magnitude and depth scale: 0.184 GPa under the 20-km lid, and 1, 2 and 3 GPa at about 94, 185 and 275 km. A real profile needs an equation of state, composition and temperature fields and self-gravity. The 100–300 GPa deep rock is not the inhabited ocean and ice domain.

**Heat and chemical work.** The exploratory budget is 10¹⁴ W of internal heat (about 0.10 W/m²): 10¹³ W of natural and biological fission, counted inside that total, and 9×10¹³ W of other heat. An assumed 0.1% conversion of the fission heat into bioavailable chemical work gives about 10¹⁰ W; this is an assumption, not an observed radiolytic yield or biological efficiency. Other redox and surface-photochemical resources contribute where separately budgeted. Waste heat, chemical storage, biomass, respiration, mineral access and repair are not closed in an energy balance, and no organism density is calculated. Cold ice with k ≈ 651/T between 220 and 260 K carries only several mW/m² across 20 km, so the heat must be carried by convection, variable lid thickness, localized heat flow and melting.

**Limits.** The dimensional orbital and thermal scales, the monotonic pressure–depth estimates, the chronological ordering and the additive energy bookkeeping are checked. Not established: a compressible mixture equation of state or interior solution; climate, ice and escape; the fission resource inventory or the feasibility of a biological reactor; complete ecology and productivity; visible emission; current and magnetohydrodynamic structure; and orbital chain evolution.

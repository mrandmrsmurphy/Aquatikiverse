# Shield Couple

![[images/Shield_Couple.png|275]]

*Sapphire and Rust, sometimes connected in projection by a faint luminous plasma-and-dust band.*

The **Shield Couple**, AQ-MV-01 h, comprises **Sapphire and Rust**, the two outermost planets of [[Ɬiʔa System]]. Their near-equal masses, contrasting atmospheres and nearly perpendicular mutual orbit remain central to their identity. The shared planetary entry contains two physical planets, not a planet and an ordinary small moon.

The retained equal bulk tuples are convenient references, not a required equality of interior composition. Rust can have a larger metal fraction while compensating with a different volatile/envelope structure. Neither density nor a detailed interior fit is treated as an observational constraint that must override the visible identities.

## Compact polar binary

The revised separation is about **10.24 component radii**, rather than the historical million-kilometer separation. Each component circles the common barycenter at about 75,000 km. Mutual synchronous rotation keeps one face toward the companion; changing stellar illumination still produces local days, with geometry affected by the highly tilted mutual plane. Independent arbitrary component spins are not simultaneously asserted.

A wide polar binary can undergo stellar-driven inclination/eccentricity exchange even inside its Hill sphere. Compactness strengthens tidal and rotational apsidal precession, offering a plausible way to suppress that exchange while preserving the polar geometry. A low-eccentricity static-tide estimate with assumed Love numbers k₂ = 0.3 gives precession about twenty times a simple stellar secular-frequency scale at 150,000 km. This is encouraging bookkeeping, not a stability integration: spin-axis states, dissipation, finite eccentricity, stellar perturbations and long-term evolution remain unresolved. No claim of proven gigayear survival is made.

An equal-mass Roche-lobe radius is approximately 57,000 km, well outside each adopted visible radius; cold stratospheres do not fill those lobes. Nonzero tidal deformation is expected. L₁ and L₂ are unstable equilibrium points, not storage reservoirs. Equal-mass classical triangular points do not provide ordinary stable Trojan habitats.

## Sapphire

Sapphire is deep blue beneath high white or pale haze, with a hydrogen–helium envelope containing methane and trace ammonia, argon, krypton and xenon. Condensates occupy layers where their pressure–temperature conditions permit them; methane cirrus and iridescence remain possible in sufficiently cold upper layers. Its ordered, strong intrinsic field produces blue-violet auroral crowns and can reconnect with Rust's weaker field.

A differentiated rock/volatile interior and electrically conducting fluid regions are plausible dynamo settings. A specifically superionic mantle and centuries of unchanging storm geometry are hypotheses, not deductions from bulk density. The polar hexagonal jet remains a characteristic atmospheric pattern, without a required claim that it exceeds Saturn's in strength. Methane-bearing plasma events, layered auroral ovals and eclipse-related cloud changes preserve its visual richness.

## Rust

Rust has a nitrogen–carbon-monoxide-rich observable atmosphere, with methane, argon, krypton, xenon and red-black-violet organic haze. Its weak, multipolar, variable field contrasts with Sapphire's organized magnetism. Local auroral echoes arise through their interaction rather than a second identical dynamo.

Its interior can be more metal-rich, with carbon-bearing material and a different heat-transport history. Deep hot carbonaceous/volatile plumes may carry material into higher atmospheric layers. “Carbon volcanism” describes that deep transport; it does not require molten carbon clouds at a 60–100-K photosphere. Polar cyclones, haze jets, cloud lightning and occasional argon emission remain named phenomena, conditional on appropriate excitation. Neither planet has an accessible terrestrial surface in the adopted envelope interpretation.

Characteristic **75–125-K atmospheric layers** are retained at suitable depths. The outgoing effective temperature is not automatically 125 K: internal luminosity and atmospheric opacity must support any departure from the stellar equilibrium baseline. Different interiors can support contrasting atmospheres without inventing precise present heat outputs.

## Periodic luminous band

The visible thread is a **tenuous, intermittent plasma-and-charged-dust structure**, with a possible escaped neutral component, rather than a dense bridge of ordinary stratospheric gas. Material may be supplied by sputtering, rare high-altitude releases and the shared debris population; the cool bulk atmospheres do not simply stretch to L₁.

Reconnection between the asymmetric magnetospheres accelerates particles and creates localized emission, synchronized auroral brightening and radio/infrared transients. Favorable mutual phase and field geometry periodically organize a band-like feature; enhanced stellar-wind episodes can modulate it. The band may contain moving knots and discontinuities, and projection can connect separated emitting regions visually. It is not a permanent solid cable or a continuously dense material stream.

**An intermittently visible light band is adopted setting canon.** Its particle supply, excitation spectrum, emitted power, optical depth, duty cycle and detectability require a quantitative plasma/emission model. Strong magnetism by itself is not a brightness calculation. The candidate energy sources are finite internal magnetic/convective power and changing magnetic stresses, not indefinite extraction from already synchronous mutual rotation. Sustained power taken from spin or orbit must be accounted for. No fixed luminosity or guaranteed unaided visibility from the inner planets is assigned yet.

## Shared satellites and rings

The four small irregular moons are retained as **shared circumbinary satellites**, replacing two distant moons assigned to each component. Adopted starting semimajor axes are **450,000; 600,000; 800,000; and 1,050,000 km** from the pair's barycenter, with low eccentricities and prograde orbits near the mutual binary plane. Their diameters remain below 100 km; individual masses and names are unspecified. These starting axes are 3–7 binary separations and below one-seventh of the shared Hill radius. The nearly polar geometry relative to the star still requires explicit secular testing; simple Hill containment is not a proof for these moons either. Their old captured origin can survive even though their current reference orbits need not retain extreme inclinations relative to the mutual plane.

Rings remain part of the system's appearance, provisionally as **narrow, replenished circumcomponent dust/debris rings**, rather than an unmodeled broad massive sheet cutting through the binary. Their exact radii and inclinations are deferred until stable zones, particle heating and moon perturbations are fitted. Small-particle rings need replenishment, and neither broad circumbinary icy rings nor four tightly packed component moons are silently assumed.

## Eclipses and appearance

Mutual eclipses and stellar shadows occur in geometry-dependent seasons. A polar mutual plane does not make every conjunction an eclipse from every inner world. At the circular reference closest distance from planet c, each disk is approximately 0.47 arcminutes across and the maximum projected pair separation approximately **2.42 arcminutes**. A close pair may be distinguishable under favorable brightness and seeing; resolving the individual disks requires optics. Its old 16.4-arcminute separation is retired with the wide orbit.

Combined visible magnitude is not fixed at +1 or −5.43 without a bandpass, phase function and stellar spectrum. Eclipse dimming, relative color changes, auroral flickering and rare thread illumination remain observational motifs, with their detectability subject to radiometry. Spectroscopy and inferred interior properties are distinguished: early instruments do not directly assay deep xenon pockets or seismic metal anomalies.

## Formation, history and culture

Both progenitors formed during the system's approximately **4.5-Gyr-old** formation epoch, from different mixtures of rock, carbon-bearing material and volatiles. The separate outer-system encounter **3.8 Gyr ago** established the ancestry of their unusual binary configuration. The encounter's perturber mass and trajectory, capture energy loss and subsequent compactification are not specified as solved. Tides and gravitational exchanges can evolve the pair; primordial gas-disk drag is not invoked hundreds of millions of years after formation. The old 3.6-Gyr stabilization marker can describe a broad relaxation epoch, without declaring the final configuration achieved by that exact date.

Later debris encounters, including the agreed 1.5–2.5-Gyr-after-formation bombardment interval, supplied impacts and temporary captures. Historical claims of dozens of captures or hundreds of deflections are in-universe reconstruction estimates, not measurements. Deuterium/xenon anomalies and siderophile-rich impact material remain possible formation tracers; their exact deep location and origin are hypotheses. Some material can predate the system without proving a particular rogue encounter.

The translated name **Shield Couple**, clay-tablet guardian imagery, paired ideograms and long observational tradition remain. Sapphire and Rust are descriptive translated epithets. Their role as protectors is culturally central: they intercept or redirect some debris, while other encounters can inject debris inward. Universal shielding of the inner worlds is not a gravitational guarantee. The pair remains a powerful symbol of duality and perimeter guardianship, and a research target for binary evolution, contrasting atmospheres and electromagnetic interaction.

## Physical basis

Values use the stellar reference, SI units, spherical bulk geometry, Kepler periods and circular Hill scales; they are not N-body, fluid, climate, atmospheric-escape or evolutionary calculations. Irradiation is the semimajor-axis reference, not an eccentric-orbit average. Gray equilibrium temperature is [1361 S (1 − A) / (4σ)]^¼, and the nonredistributed substellar reference is √2 times that. The barycenter orbits at 1.500 AU with a period of 1500.4175 days and receives 0.0026242 S⊕; the pair has a total mass of 10 M⊕, with each component 5 M⊕ and 2.3 R⊕. Gray equilibrium temperatures are 57.6 K for Sapphire (Bond albedo 0.3) and 55.4 K for Rust (0.4).

The mutual orbit has a semimajor axis of 150,000 km, near-zero reference eccentricity and an inclination of about 90° to the stellar orbit; the total mass gives a period of 2.11608 days. A static two-equal-component tidal apsidal rate, 15 k₂ n (R/a)⁵ with k₂ = 0.3 each, is about twenty times the stellar secular scale n_outer²/n. This proxy omits rotational precession, finite-eccentricity terms, dissipative evolution and spin-axis states, so it offers a mechanism to investigate, not proof that an exactly polar equilibrium survives all perturbations (Verrier & Evans 2009, [MNRAS 394, 1721](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/394/4/1721/1198402), discuss competing secular effects and high-inclination survival). The equal-mass Roche-lobe radius, about 0.379 a = 56,850 km, exceeds the 14,653 km component radius.

Four shared moons orbit at 0.45, 0.60, 0.80 and 1.05 million km on low-eccentricity, binary-coplanar prograde reference orbits; circumbinary and stellar secular forcing must be evaluated jointly, especially at the outer one. Narrow replenished circumcomponent debris rings are provisional.

The periodic luminous band is a tenuous magnetospheric plasma and dust emission with possible escaped neutral material, not cold stratospheric overflow. Field asymmetry and favourable phase organize the transient band, but no luminosity, particle density, duty cycle or observing threshold is established. Synchronous mutual rotation is not a perpetual energy source: internal dynamo power, stellar forcing and any extraction of rotational or orbital work need bookkeeping.

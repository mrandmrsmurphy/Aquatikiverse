# Ɬiʔa System

![[images/Ɬiʔa_system_diagram_with_names.png]]

*Mirar's Veil and its planets. Representative diagram, not to scale; the reference axes below govern.*

> [!important] Model status
> The stellar reference and system-wide history below are adopted, and every world has a fuller article. Reference moon configurations and outer-pair dynamics are adopted with explicit model limits, not claimed as tested long-term solutions.

The **Ɬiʔa System** orbits **[[Mirar's Veil]]**, the fictional active red dwarf **AQ-MV-01**, approximately **15.3 pc (~50 ly)** from Earth toward Ophiuchus. There is **one present star**, formerly part of a binary, and **seven major planetary orbital entries containing eight individual planets** because the outermost entry is the Shield Couple. Asteroids, comets and smaller bodies occupy an inner metal-rich belt and a cold outer debris disk.

The star formed approximately **4.5 Gyr ago**. Its present **0.20 M☉**, **0.25 R☉** and **3200 K** imply **0.005904 L☉**. A recent giant-planet ingestion rejuvenated its rotation and magnetic activity. Baseline luminosity is relatively steady on human timescales while magnetic storms, spots and flares make the environment strongly variable in high-energy radiation and plasma. The first orbital zone is explicitly intended to undergo powerful, recurrent magnetic interaction; particular planetary manifestations are deferred.

Antediluvian humans descended from Enoch, their long lives, divine transplantation, the separate native silicon biosphere and the neighboring ocean biosphere remain setting canon as supplied. Stellar reconciliation neither removes those premises nor settles their detailed biology or conditions. Their fuller articles remain [[Life on Middle Path]] and [[Life on Crying Eye]].

## Orbital scale anchored to c

**The priority is c's received stellar flux.** Preserve **1.0 Earth flux** at the semimajor-axis reference, before eclipses, clouds, atmospheric absorption or surface effects. With the adopted star:

`a_c = sqrt(0.0059043846) AU = 0.07683999 AU`.

This is the circular/semimajor-axis irradiation reference; eccentricity-dependent orbit averaging will be specified when the planetary elements are settled. The inner architecture then uses successive **3:2 target period ratios**, with each entry's ratio stated relative to c. Those ratios set reference axes via `a/a_c = (P/P_c)^(2/3)` in the common central-mass approximation. Finite planet masses and actual resonant phases require a subsequent dynamical fit. No librating resonant angle or long-term survival has been demonstrated.

## Three distinct historical episodes

1. **Early companion removal:** around 100 Myr after formation, a stellar-scale encounter removed the original companion. Survival or reconstruction of compact circumstellar orbits must be accounted for; no arbitrary small rogue is claimed to eject a star.
2. **Outer-system restructuring:** a separate encounter approximately 3.8 Gyr ago established the ancestry of the Shield Couple's unusual orbit. The pair's physical/dynamical reconciliation will determine the actual capture and stabilization history. Later debris scattering includes a broad bombardment interval 1.5–2.5 Gyr after formation; primordial gas-disk drag is not assumed at those late times.
3. **Recent ingestion:** approximately 10 Myr ago a nominal **0.5-Mj** body was disrupted and accreted, helping establish the present **two-day spin**, **4-kG mean unsigned surface field**, **6–8-kG active regions** and **2-kG polar dipole** reference. A finite angular-momentum budget supports the design. Detailed spin evolution and disruption/escape history remain unmodeled.

These are a shared setting history, not the output of an encounter simulation. Present orbital ratios are the post-disturbance configuration; they need not have existed through every earlier episode. Unspecified earlier planet/moon losses may belong to the relaxation history, but no masses or event dates are invented. The contemporary star remains old and fully convective; magnetic rejuvenation is not a reset of its formation age.

## Asteroid and cold debris populations

The system has a metal-rich asteroid population dominated by iron- and nickel-bearing material, shaped by early disruption and later collisional evolution, with a resonant reference near **0.22655 AU**. Its detailed breadth, mass, argon inventory and present collision/dust rate await material and dynamical review. A near-3:2 period pattern by itself does not prove a stable quiet belt or rule out later collisions.

The **outer cold debris disk remains at 3–10 AU**. The simple zero-albedo gray-grain equilibrium reference spans approximately **45–24 K** at the adopted luminosity. Grain size/emissivity, albedo, shadowing and internal activity can change individual temperatures; these are not guaranteed temperatures or volatile-retention results for every body. The ancient near-miss is a dynamical disturbance, not an ongoing heat source. The supplied nominal **0.1-M⊕** disk inventory and H₂O/CO₂/CH₄/Ar composition are inputs for later material review.

## Two-Face

Main article: [[Two-Face (exoplanet)]]. The 2.1-M⊕/1.1-R⊕ super-Mercury follows the adopted **0.05864-AU**, **11.60-day synchronous** reference. Its approximately 75%-mass core is a compositional target requiring an interior fit. A **0.02-atm molecular atmosphere** has a nominal N₂/CO₂/CO backbone. The nightside target is **160–190 K**, with darkness and frozen heavier organic and water-bearing terrain and no exposed nitrogen or light-hydrocarbon ice seas. Volatile-rich buried basins and localized ammonia-bearing reservoirs remain.

Illuminated terrain is generally **400–450 K**, with **700-K peaks confined to exceptional small heated sites**. A temperate strip lies slightly dayward of the geometrical terminator. Tilted intrinsic magnetism and stellar interaction produce an irregular girdle of aurorae, dust/gas emission, local discharges and intermittent plasma tubes; no superionic insulator or planetary capacitor is required. A declared **1%-duty high-connection budget** makes magnetic orbital evolution negligible on the system's age in the illustrative direct-link calculation. The faster-rotating star tends to transfer angular momentum outward through such a link, rather than automatically dragging the planet inward. Climate, dynamo, plasma emission and full chain stability remain model requirements.

## Crying Eye

Main articles: [[Crying Eye]] and [[Life on Crying Eye]]. The 2-M⊕/1.4-R⊕ world uses the stellar reference axis **0.10069 AU**, **26.09-day synchronous** period and **0.582 Earth flux**. The frozen surface with a dark Furnace remains the target; ~90% ice cover is not a calculated climate result. A regional 20-km lid overlies a heterogeneous liquid/brine/high-pressure-ice domain. Principal deep habitats target **1–3 GPa**; reference overburden reaches those pressures at roughly **94–275 km**, rather than putting every habitat immediately beneath the lid at several gigapascals.

**Literal fission Corions** evolve from sessile communities around abundant natural fission provinces into colonial, symbiotic and mobile associations. This is a major speculative biological capability, not a substitution of ordinary decay or Earth-cell biochemistry. The native life uses pressure-dependent organic–mineral/ionic/polymeric structures, with portable boundaries evolving after phase-interface ancestry. Magnetrobes, Ammoniaphages, Magnetomycota, Ignitors, Fission Lures, Cryoterrans and the **sapient Carbon Suits** share the biosphere. Surface migration follows intermediate habitats and seeks useful forms of carbon, oxidants and mineral resources. Regional feedbacks and electrical/radiation defenses remain finite and distinct; a small nuclear organism is not automatically an explosive device. Origin is **2.7 Gyr ago**, within the 4.5-Gyr planetary history.

## Other planets

[[Epilepticus]] uses **0.13194 AU**, **39.14 days** and **0.3392 Earth flux**. Its violence is concentrated in catastrophic shell failures, abrupt regional jerks, explosive cryovolcanism and returning plume curtains. The near-synchronous bulk reference is separate from crustal slip and slower reorientation. There are no rapid whole-planet axis jumps, guaranteed angular-momentum resets or bulk eruptions at tens to hundreds of km/s. Mineral/organic-bearing ice preserves the blue-white, rust and black-green palette.

[[Belted King]] uses **0.17289 AU**, **58.71 days**, **0.19753 Earth flux** and a **15-hour prograde spin**. It has a ring system and five moons. Moon IV orbits at **250,000 km** and Moon V at **450,000 km** with nominal 160° retrograde inclination. The inner three retain 60,000/110,000/190,000-km reference axes. Atmosphere temperatures distinguish emitting layers from deeper warmth; stable icy dust and particular ring resonances require modeling.

[[Corpse (exoplanet)|The Corpse]] orbits at **0.40 AU**, **206.62 days** and **0.03690 Earth flux**, with a **20-hour prograde spin**, an airless mineral exterior and residual deep volatiles. There are no permanent hemispheric thermal contrasts. Its polar stellar orbit is provisional, with secular dynamics unresolved. **g1** is 1,400 km across with ~0.060 g gravity; **g2** is a **5,200-km atmosphere-bearing moon**. Their reference axes are **150,000 and 238,110 km**, near a 2:1 period ratio. Atmosphere retention, lakes, ocean heating and actual resonant behavior remain model requirements.

[[Shield Couple]] orbits at **1.5 AU** with a **~90° mutual plane**, **150,000 km apart** and mutually synchronous every **2.116 days**. Four small moons are shared circumbinary satellites. Compactness offers stronger internal apsidal precession against stellar eccentricity excitation, but no complete stability integration is claimed. The periodic luminous band is adopted as tenuous **plasma/charged dust**, with finite material/power requirements and unsolved radiometry. Sapphire and Rust have contrasting atmospheres and magnetic identities. Formation is **4.5 Gyr ago**, binary ancestry follows the separate **3.8-Gyr-ago encounter**, and later evolution does not use a surviving primordial gas disk.

The calculations behind these values are on each world's page under Physical basis. 

## Middle Path

Main articles: [[Middle Path (exoplanet)]] and [[Life on Middle Path]]. The 1.05-M⊕/1.0247-R⊕ planet, 1.00 g, uses the stellar reference axis **0.07684 AU** (1.0 Earth flux) and a **17.396-day** synchronous period (the **c-day**); a large core gives it 1.00 g. A very light **hemispherical Tethered Moon** of 3,147 km radius, held by active control at the star–planet L1 point above the substellar point and tied to the ground by an active tether, shades a permanent **Stillpoint** about 242 km in radius and keeps 76.7% of the starlight. A dense 1.6-atm, potassium-rich world with a 288-K mean, a hot bright ring, a pinned eyewall, a dry calm eye, a temperate terminator belt and a plateau-ringed nightside with an ice-free antistellar oasis; a **144-μT** dipole along the star direction (declared tidal axis lock and chiral additive), strong star–planet coupling and aurora; a native **silicon biosphere** (about 3 Gyr old, the planet's oxygen source, with the Electric Choirs awakened by the ingestion 10 Myr ago) and a divinely transplanted **carbon biosphere** with Enoch-descended humans, whose technology begins with electricity and magnetism. The Tethered Moon's builders remain unknown. The adopted numbers are scaling arithmetic with declared premises, not a simulation.

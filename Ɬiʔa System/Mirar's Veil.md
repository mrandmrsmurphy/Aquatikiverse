# Mirar's Veil

![[images/Barnardstar2006.jpg]]

*Representative image of a red dwarf: Barnard's Star, not a photograph of the fictional Mirar's Veil.*

**Mirar's Veil** is the fictional, metal-rich, magnetically active red dwarf at the center of the [[Ɬiʔa System]]. Its in-universe survey designation is **AQ-MV-01**; this is an author-created identifier, not an entry in a real astronomical catalogue. It is not GJ/Gliese 1237, Gliese 710, or a component of either real system.

The star is approximately **4.5 billion years old**, with a present mass of **0.20 M☉**, radius **0.25 R☉**, and effective temperature of **3200 K**. These give a bolometric luminosity of **0.005904 L☉**, conventionally rounded to **0.0059–0.006 L☉**. Its approximately **two-day rotation** sustains an unusually strong dynamo after a recent giant-planet ingestion. The star's long-term baseline irradiation is relatively steady on human timescales, while spots, flares and changing magnetic structures make its space weather exceptionally active.

These values are a coherent fictional reference, not precision measurements or a completed magnetic stellar-evolution calculation. The radius is comparatively large for a quiet 0.2-M☉ dwarf; metal enrichment and magnetic inflation are candidate contributions requiring a matched structural model. M5V is retained as an approximate spectral classification rather than a constraint independent of the spectrum, temperature and composition.

## Magnetic activity

Convection and rapid rotation power the dynamo. Ingestion supplies an angular-momentum history; it is not an inexhaustible magnetic battery. A fully convective star can sustain strong organized fields without a solar-style radiative-core interface. The adopted 4-kG unsigned surface average and stronger patches are aggressive but within the broad regime of observed strongly magnetic fully convective dwarfs. A particular field geometry or mean strength is not uniquely predicted by a two-day period. [Observed strong fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.08571)

A dominant, tilted dipole supplies a large-scale organizing field, while spots, multipoles and reconnection create changing active sectors. Magnetic energy is stored and released locally; the star remains a core-hydrogen-burning main-sequence dwarf. The present configuration is active for its age, not chronologically young or near the end of its stellar lifetime.

These are cumulative rates, not separate event populations to add together. The model steepens above 10³⁴ erg and is not extrapolated above 10³⁶ erg; the frequency beyond that modeled range is unspecified. Individual events are irregular and clustered. Many small flares are photometrically modest even though they matter in high-energy bands. The distribution is intentionally much more active than a quiet old red dwarf while retaining a repeatable statistical background. Observed M-dwarf flares support the broad energy regime, not these selected individual rates. [TESS observations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.00443)

Frequent flares do not imply equally frequent escaping mass ejections. Strong closed fields can confine or fragment eruptions, allowing plasma to fall back as coronal rain. Some large events escape and cause severe space weather, but the escaping-CME frequency and kinetic-energy distribution are not fixed by the flare table. The strongest surface field value is not inserted directly into a universal CME suppression threshold. [Magnetic confinement models](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.02828)

## Wind, Alfvén surface and inner-system coupling

The **closed corona**, **Alfvén surface** and **magnetized stellar wind** are different regions. The field does not stop at one spherical “magnetosphere edge.” A planet can exchange Alfvénic disturbances with the star in sub-Alfvénic sectors; connection depends on wind density, velocity, field topology and orbital phase. Closed coronal loops need not reach the orbit for open-field coupling to occur. [M-dwarf wind and interaction models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.16318)

The adopted design explicitly favors a strongly disturbed inner-system environment, especially at b's revised reference orbit, **0.05864 AU**. It does not assert that every planet inside 0.20 AU is permanently connected. A tilted, structured field and changing wind make connection intermittent, reconnection-prone and phase dependent.

A transparent **illustrative radial-wind budget** uses a mass-loss rate of **2×10⁻¹⁴ M☉ yr⁻¹**, speed **400 km s⁻¹**, and an effective open radial field normalized to **50 G** at the photosphere. The open-flux normalization is a declared additional input, not the 2-kG polar dipole or 4-kG unsigned average. A nominal coronal temperature of ~2 MK is a reference input, not the solution of an energy equation.

At b's reference radius this toy budget gives an approximately **2000-nT radial field**, **970-km/s Alfvén speed** and radial-flow Alfvén Mach number about **0.41**. Even a test relative speed including hypothetical rigid wind corotation yields a value about **0.49**. These checks demonstrate that a sub-Alfvénic design is not excluded by simple field/density scaling; they do not solve a rotating stellar wind. The constant-speed radial Alfvén radius would be about **0.142 AU**, not a verified spherical interaction boundary. Adding orbital and azimuthal flow at c and d can change the inferred regime. No exact Alfvén-surface map is adopted.

This supplies a plausible stellar setting for repeated current systems, radio emission, plasma disturbances and strong forcing of the first planet. Its atmospheric effects, available coupled power, surface manifestations and survivability remain for the planet review. A three-dimensional wind/field solution is still required; neither surface magnetic strength nor the toy model validates any particular ground-level spectacle.

## Three episodes of stellar and system history

### Episode 1 — early companion removal

Mirar's Veil formed approximately **4.5 Gyr ago** in a metal-rich molecular cloud, initially with a stellar companion. Around **100 Myr after formation** (approximately 4.4 Gyr ago), a stellar-scale encounter broke up that configuration, leaving Mirar's Veil single. The encounter is retained as a historical hypothesis, with the intruder, departing companion and their energy/angular-momentum exchange requiring an eventual scattering model.

The former companion is provisionally a sufficiently wide companion for compact circumstellar material to have survived before breakup; no numerical binary separation is invented at this stage. The event must be compatible with survival or later reconstruction of the planetary architecture. A low-mass rogue planet is not assigned the job of ejecting a star without an adequate energy budget.

### Episode 2 — outer-system encounter

Approximately **3.8 Gyr ago**, a separate encounter disturbed the outer system and established the ancestry of the Shield Couple's unusual configuration. The event is distinct from companion removal. The perturber's mass and flyby geometry are not fixed; it is a rogue planet of unspecified mass. Capture, dissipation, angular-momentum transfer and long-term survival await the outer-pair review.

Subsequent debris scattering and bombardment, including a broad interval **1.5–2.5 Gyr after formation**, belong to the long relaxation history. Present orbital ratios need not have survived unchanged through every early event. Primordial gas-disk drag is not invoked hundreds of millions of years after formation; any later dissipation must identify its actual material or tidal mechanism. The encounter dynamically excites debris but does not keep it thermally warm for billions of years. It is not yet a demonstrated system-wide impact shield.

### Episode 3 — recent ingestion and magnetic rejuvenation

Approximately **10 Myr ago**, the star consumed a **nominal 0.5-Jupiter-mass giant**. The exact mass and time are reference model choices. This was an actual ingestion in the setting, while its detailed reconstruction remains modeled rather than uniquely observed.

An illustrative prograde precursor orbit has semimajor axis **0.025 AU**, eccentricity **0.88**, pericenter **0.003 AU** and apocenter **0.047 AU**, inside the present b reference orbit. Tidal disruption, debris accretion and angular-momentum transfer brought the material into the star. The orbit is a budget example, not a demonstration of its origin, disruption or safe passage; inner-reservoir destabilization and perturbations of surviving planets require future dynamics. A giant is not swept repeatedly through the entire surviving compact chain by assumption.

For a fully convective moment-of-inertia coefficient **I/(MR²) = 0.205**, spinning from an illustrative prior 50-day period to today's two-day reference requires about **8.61×10⁴⁰ kg m² s⁻¹**. The precursor orbit contains approximately **1.42×10⁴¹ kg m² s⁻¹**: roughly **61% net retention** supplies that difference, leaving room for transfer to expelled material, a disruption disk and magnetic braking. Net retention includes subsequent losses; this is not a calculated accretion efficiency or a recovered spin-evolution curve. The characteristic accretion-energy scale is approximately **1.45×10³⁸ J**, and a thermal-response model must determine its deposition and escape. No persistent luminosity surplus is assigned simply by dividing that energy by elapsed time.

The present 0.20-M☉ mass is **after** ingestion. If all 0.5 Mj were retained, the immediately preceding mass would have been approximately **0.19952 M☉**; actual pre-event mass depends on how much mass escaped. Earlier smaller ingestions and losses of moons remain possible source history without invented masses or dates. The strong-field state is attributed to continuing convection and rapid rotation maintained after spin-up, rather than a hot surface layer of swallowed material.

## Physical basis and limits

The star, its identity, the three-episode history, the statistical activity design and the system reference axes are adopted as a coherent fictional reference. The calculations are arithmetic, an angular-momentum budget and a deliberately simplified wind estimate: the Stefan–Boltzmann luminosity (0.0059043846 L☉), the c axis for unit flux (0.07683999 AU), the ingestion angular-momentum budget (about 61% net retention of the precursor's orbital angular momentum) and the toy wind at b. They are not a stellar-evolution or orbital simulation.

The flare model is the cumulative distribution N(≥E) = (E / 10³² erg)⁻¹ per day over 10³⁰–10³⁴ erg and 0.01 (E / 10³⁴ erg)⁻¹·⁵ per day from 10³⁴ to 10³⁶ erg, with no extrapolation above that. These are statistical rates, not observations, and they do not fix coronal-mass-ejection escape, kinetic energies or particle doses. The magnetic-energy-flux (Poynting) scale of the wind is a dimensional scale, not power intercepted, dissipated or harvested by a planet; obstacle size, conductivity, reconnection geometry and stellar response need planet-level modeling.

The reference does not prove encounter survival, resonance libration, a four-billion-year planetary integration, stellar structure, wind topology, flare statistics or planetary habitability.

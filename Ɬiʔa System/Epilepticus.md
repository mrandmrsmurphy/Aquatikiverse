# Epilepticus

![[images/Blasting_and_shattered.png]]

*Epilepticus during a catastrophic shell-failure and cryovolcanic episode.*

**Epilepticus**, AQ-MV-01 e, is the icy, violently resurfacing fourth planet of [[Ɬiʔa System]]. Its defining spectacles are abrupt crustal jerks, continent-scale fracture systems, explosive venting, enormous returning plume curtains and temporary luminous halos. “Axial recoil” survives as the historical name for these events; it no longer means an abrupt redirection of the whole planet's angular momentum.

## Interior and catastrophic geology

A predominantly rocky differentiated planet underlies an ice-rich outer shell, clathrate-bearing deposits, ammonia–water brine reservoirs and mechanically weak horizons. Metal core and silicate mantle are distinguished. Deep high-pressure water ice can occur if the local pressure, temperature and water inventory permit it; an entire Ice VI/VII mantle is not required by the mass–radius tuple. Diamond-bearing inclusions, if present, originate at appropriate depth and can subsequently be exhumed.

Persistent radiogenic heat and possible tidal dissipation recharge thermal reservoirs. Radioactive decay does not switch on and off. Brine pressure, freezing expansion, differential motion, tidal flexing and regional loading accumulate stress. A rupture can propagate through coupled fault systems, jerk crustal blocks, collapse basins and trigger explosive decompression. Partial decoupling permits shell motion relative to the deeper body. This is not a freely rotating rigid globe sitting above frictionless liquid: mechanical work, coupling and angular momentum remain finite.

Abrupt local and regional motions are the principal violence. Broader shell reorientation and slow true polar wander are possible extensions, rather than fixed tens-of-degrees global jumps every century. Long-term obliquity evolution is distinct from shell slip. The adopted near-synchronous bulk spin is a reference state, not proof of a particular tidal equilibrium.

## Eruptions, fallout and appearance

Most ice grains, brine droplets and fragments are launched below escape speed. An illustrative 0.3–1 km/s vertical launch gives approximately 5–57 km of ballistic rise at constant surface gravity; exceptional faster subescape material can rise farther. These are height scales, not a calculated plume distribution. Vast area, particle column and sunlight scattering make high plumes visible; speed alone does not establish optical brightness. A small accelerated ion component may escape without making escape the purpose of the eruption.

Blue-white water ice contrasts with rusty mineral-bearing frost and black-green organic-rich mixed deposits. Methane and CO₂ are retained in suitable buried reservoirs and clathrates, or appear as transient deposits under appropriate conditions. Pure exposed methane ice is not the universal source of the dark coloration. Fallout reloads basins and faults, sometimes encouraging further failures, but cannot guarantee an endless instability without heat and volatile resupply.

The temporary gas envelope evolves through condensation, photochemistry, sputtering and some escape. Cold heavy gases are not assumed rapidly lost by Jeans escape alone. A globally dense stable atmosphere is not adopted. Active regions can be resurfaced on millennial-to-tens-of-millennia design timescales while inactive terrain preserves older craters; no single global resurfacing clock has been calculated.

## Event cycle and visibility

1. Heat transport, freezing, tidal flexing and loading build stress and pressurize reservoirs.
2. Brittle shell regions store mechanical energy while weak horizons permit differential motion.
3. Failure jerks crustal blocks and propagates fractures across connected provinces.
4. Decompression drives cryovolcanic explosions and tall gas–grain curtains.
5. Most ejecta returns, coating terrain and redistributing loads.
6. Reservoirs recharge and faults evolve over variable intervals, potentially decades to centuries in active provinces.
7. Further failures recur where supply and stress allow; no whole-planet angular-momentum reset is implied.

From the reference orbit of planet c, its disk spans approximately 1.4–5.3 arcminutes in circular coplanar distance estimates. Actual eccentricity and viewing geometry alter these bounds. Limb fans, scattering halos and infrared transients remain observing targets; naked-eye visibility of individual plumes is not established by disk size. Epilepticus remains a case study in destructive coupling between ice, rock, tides and internal heat, with no invented biosphere or engineered driver.

## Physical basis

Values use the stellar reference, SI units, spherical bulk geometry, Kepler periods and circular Hill scales; they are not N-body, fluid, climate, atmospheric-escape or evolutionary calculations. Irradiation is the semimajor-axis reference, not an eccentric-orbit average. Gray equilibrium temperature is [1361 S (1 − A) / (4σ)]^¼, and the nonredistributed substellar reference is √2 times that. At the 0.13193975 AU reference orbit Epilepticus receives 0.33917 S⊕ and has a gray equilibrium of 169–187 K for Bond albedos of 0.6–0.4; this is not a surface or cloud temperature map.

Shell failure with visible returning ejecta is the adopted eruption mechanism; it recurs where supply and stress allow, with finite recharge, and implies no whole-planet angular-momentum reset. Leconte (2018, [arXiv:1809.01150](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01150)) distinguishes bodily reorientation from a change of inertial angular momentum; it does not validate this rapid regional rupture or establish whole-shell jump magnitudes, so the mechanism is a setting premise. Deep phase and diamond occurrences are conditional. Not modeled: compact-chain dynamics, interior phase and equation-of-state structure, climate and escape.

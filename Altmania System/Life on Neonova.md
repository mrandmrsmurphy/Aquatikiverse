**Neonova** hosts a complex, highly alien biosphere suspended within a thick, neon-rich atmosphere. Biological productivity is concentrated in vast aerial ecosystems rather than on the ground. Floating organisms ride relentless global winds, circling the planet roughly every 23–26 Earth days. Ground life is essential to chemical replenishment and recycling, although most standing biological productivity is aerial: many ground organisms are scavengers and chemical recyclers that exist beneath the raining detritus of the sky.

Life on Neonova is built on familiar C–H–N–O chemistry but deliberately avoids free molecular oxygen. Instead, a hydrogen–methane cycle and iron-based respiration drive primary productivity. Volcanism, abundant surface iron, a dense neon–argon atmosphere, and strong thermal contrasts between the blazing dayside and cooler nightside shape all life processes.

## Biochemistry of Neonova
Primary autotrophs, especially Zeppelophytes, harness a simple but powerful redox reaction:

`4H₂ + CO₂ → CH₄ + 2H₂O`

They use atmospheric hydrogen to reduce carbon dioxide, producing methane and water as byproducts. In the high-pressure lower atmosphere (≈17 atm), water remains liquid even at dayside temperatures of 380–400 K, supporting hot lakes and humid lower layers that act as sinks and sources for the biosphere.

Ferrivores, living near iron-rich lakes and basins, exploit a complementary chemical pathway:

`CH₄ + 8Fe³⁺ + 2H₂O → CO₂ + 8Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺`

They oxidize methane while reducing ferric iron (Fe³⁺) to ferrous iron (Fe²⁺), recycling methane carbon without free O₂, while consuming a ferric inventory that must be reoxidized. These reactions couple the atmosphere to the crust and limit methane only if H₂ input, ferric regeneration and transport sustain the required fluxes.

### Solvent and Membranes
Most life uses liquid water with dissolved salts and organics. Hot deep basins and cooler nightside oceans retain the supplied hydrosphere. Aerial habitats require their own pressure/temperature, water activity and vapor-barrier model; surface pressure alone does not establish liquid water everywhere in the sky.

Cell membranes use reinforced lipid bilayers with crosslinked proteins. Lift sacs are **pressure-balanced and H₂-rich**, with redundant compartments and low-mass structural tissues. Neon/argon vacuoles can provide mechanical damping, ballast/control and gas management; argon is not assumed to be lighter than the ambient mixture. Pure neon supplies a modest gas-density contrast, while hydrogen supplies much more. Flexible subambient sacs would collapse without a load-bearing envelope and are not the default mechanism.

Gas permeability, inflation/repair costs, armor mass, thermal expansion, nutrient/water retention and predator punctures constrain size and altitude. The massive Photofloats and Crimson Titans retain broad low-density bodies and explicit lift margins; dense air helps but does not erase their mass budget.

### Energetics and Phototrophy
Although the core metabolism is chemotrophic, Neonova’s proximity to Altmania B (≈0.20 AU; ~5× Earth’s insolation) means that light is abundant, especially in red and near-infrared bands. Zeppelophytes and related clades use pigment systems tuned to 650–900 nm to drive proton gradients and ATP-equivalent synthesis, effectively “boosting” the underlying H₂–CO₂ → CH₄ pathway. This makes them photo-assisted chemotrophs rather than classical oxygenic phototrophs.

Ground and benthic communities rely more heavily on pure chemotrophy, using methane, hydrogen, and redox-active minerals in iron basins and geothermal fields.

## Guilds of Life
### Zeppelophytes
Zeppelophytes are the primary autotrophs of Neonova. Entirely aerial, they drift in the middle atmosphere where winds are strongest and sunlight still penetrates the haze. They:

* Absorb red–NIR light and metabolize atmospheric H₂ and CO₂ to produce CH₄ and H₂O.
* Maintain lift with H₂-rich pressure-balanced sacs; noble-gas compartments serve control/damping or specialized nonlifting functions.
* Shed organic aerosols and detritus that feed a wide range of secondary consumers.

Morphologies range from broad, disc-like light collectors to streamlined gliders that can slightly adjust altitude and latitude to exploit favorable currents.

### Ferrivores
Ferrivores are benthic chemotrophs inhabiting iron-rich lakes and pools, especially near the terminator and nightside basins.

* They oxidize methane using Fe³⁺ as an electron sink, converting it to Fe²⁺ and returning CO₂ to the atmosphere.
* Colonies form mats on basin floors and rock surfaces, often pigmented in deep reds and browns by iron minerals.
* Ferrivores are critical in stabilizing the carbon cycle after early methane buildup, and they continue to recycle organic material that sinks from the aerial realm.

### Methanivores
Methanivores consume methane using oxidants other than Fe³⁺ when local geochemistry allows.

* Potential oxidants include nitrate, sulfate, or metal oxides produced by volcanic processes and lightning-driven atmospheric chemistry.
* They occupy specialized surface and subsurface niches where these oxidants accumulate, such as geothermal margins, mineral-rich seeps, or chemically stratified lakes.
* Methanivores help trim local methane peaks and create chemically diverse microhabitats.

### Aetherblooms
Aetherblooms are secondary consumers of the skies.

* They drift high in the troposphere, absorbing organic aerosols, spores, and decaying biological material.
* Many lineages have evolved sticky or filamentous surfaces that trap passing particles, slowly digesting them in shallow external films.
* Aetherblooms often serve as prey for more aggressive sky predators, forming an intermediate trophic level between primary Zeppelophytes and apex aerial hunters.

### Rustworms
Rustworms are ground-based decomposers and recyclers.

* They break down fallen aerial organisms and reprocess iron-bearing minerals, converting complex organics into simpler compounds that can be reincorporated into chemotrophic cycles.
* Rustworms dominate the benthic zones of lakes and the upper layers of nightside ocean sediments.
* Their activities regulate iron speciation and maintain many of the redox gradients that Ferrivores and Methanivores exploit.

## Evolutionary History
Early life on Neonova likely began with simple floaters performing hydrogen–methane-based metabolism in a denser, hotter atmosphere. Primitive methanogens filled the air with CH₄, thickening the haze and reducing the efficiency of light-driven processes.

A planetary-scale shift occurred when Ferrivores evolved iron-respiring pathways that recycled methane back to CO₂. This “Great Methane Event,” loosely analogous to Earth’s Great Oxygenation Event, lowered atmospheric methane to more stable levels, cleared the haze just enough for deeper light penetration, and strengthened day–night radiative contrasts.

In the post-crisis world, free hydrogen remained plentiful, organic aerosols abundant, and light penetration sufficient for photo-assisted chemotrophy. Sky life diversified rapidly:

* Larger floaters evolved stronger buoyancy systems and broader photosurfaces.
* Predatory and parasitic forms evolved to puncture rival gas sacs and strip tissues.
* Defensive adaptations appeared in response, including thickened skins, toxic exudates, and compartmentalized gas systems to survive partial damage.

This period is often described as Neonova’s “Sky Cambrian Explosion,” an evolutionary arms race driven primarily by aerial competition rather than ground-based predation.

## Clades of the Sky
### Photofloats
Photofloats are the “trees” of the sky: massive, mostly passive organisms that maximize light absorption.

* Broad, layered photosurfaces with deep pigment beds.
* Thick, indigestible outer tissues and toxin-laced coatings to deter predators.
* Slow growth but extreme longevity; entire migration bands may be structured around long-lived Photofloat lineages.

### Cloud Skimmers
Cloud Skimmers are agile, partially motile floaters able to adjust buoyancy or orientation to exploit local air currents.

* Many are primarily phototrophic but supplement energy by capturing aerosols.
* Some can “tack” relative to prevailing winds using asymmetric drag, enabling limited steering within the global flow.
* They are key intermediates between passive Photofloats and fully predatory forms.

### Sky Reapers
Sky Reapers are predatory floaters equipped with tendrils, barbs, and piercing structures.

* Originally evolved to puncture rival gas sacs as a competitive strategy.
* Later developed digestive surfaces and internal cavities to process captured tissues.
* They exert strong selective pressure on Zeppelophytes and Aetherblooms, driving armor, toxins, and compartmentalization.

### Crimson Titans
Crimson Titans are gigantic, heavily armored floaters with redundant gas sacs and multilayered protective tissues.

* Internally compartmentalized so that a breach in one section does not cause catastrophic loss of buoyancy.
* Surfaces often host symbiotic communities of smaller organisms, forming mobile micro-ecologies.
* Their size and inertia make them effectively aerial “islands” drifting along the migration bands.

### Thermovores
Thermovores are apex aerial predators that weaponize internal exothermic chemistry.

* They maintain separate reservoirs of hydrogen and concentrated oxidants (e.g. nitrates, peroxides, or metal oxides) in insulated chambers.
* Rapidly mixing these reservoirs triggers brief, intense heat pulses or jets of hot gas, used to shock, rupture, or deflect attackers and prey.
* To outside observers, these events can resemble muted flashes or thermal flares rather than sustained open flames; carried oxidants can still support energetic oxidation or combustion despite negligible ambient O₂.

Thermovores represent the peak of the aerial arms race, forcing other clades toward either extreme defense or extreme stealth.

## Current State of the Biosphere
Sky life on Neonova is dominated by a quasi-diurnal rhythm set by the planet’s atmospheric circulation rather than rotation into and out of daylight. At Zeppelophyte cruising altitudes, recirculating jets coupled to the day–night overturning circulation carry the reference communities:

* From the intensely irradiated substellar point (SSP),
* Through cooler mid-latitude and terminator regions,
* Into the dark, cold antistellar point (ASP),
* And back again to the SSP,

in roughly 23–26 Earth days.

The substellar region hosts the richest and most dangerous aerial “jungles,” where light is abundant and competition is fierce. Here, most diversification and speciation occur. As organisms drift toward the nightside, metabolic rates slow, reproduction drops, and survival strategies dominate. A smaller set of nocturnal scavengers and chemotrophs exploits the darker half of the cycle, but evolutionary innovation is concentrated on the hot, bright side.

On the surface, change is slower. Ferrivores, Methanivores, and Rustworms recycle fallen material, regulate methane and iron cycles, and maintain the long-term chemical backdrop that supports the atmospheric biosphere. Compared to the ever-moving, conflict-driven aerial realm, ground ecosystems are stable, conservative, and evolutionarily gradual.

## Powered renewal and ecological closure

Adding the two balanced core reactions consumes **4 H₂ + 8 Fe³⁺** and produces **8 Fe²⁺ + 8 H⁺**; the carbon intermediate cancels. A 1.2-Gyr ecosystem therefore requires H₂ resupply and ferric regeneration. The adopted working design adds an illuminated anoxygenic photoferrotrophic regeneration guild at mineral/fluid interfaces, plus hydrothermal/geological H₂ supply and atmospheric/sedimentary material return. Photosynthetic ATP assistance, CO₂ assimilation, Fe(II) oxidation, water/proton handling and hydrogen loss must be quantified separately. No globally free-O₂ pathway is silently imposed.

Great Methane Event stabilization reflects a changed powered source–sink network, not consumption of an infinite initial iron reservoir. Nitrate/sulfate/metal-oxide Methanivore niches and Thermovore oxidant stores have finite generation, recharge and transport costs. Thermovore chambers protect catalysts and tissues against their own brief exothermic discharges. All named guilds, predators, defensive traits, sky islands and historical radiations survive; geographic differences in evolutionary tempo remain setting history rather than a general rule forced by competition.

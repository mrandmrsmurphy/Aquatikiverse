The biosphere of **Thionis** is founded upon a cellular architecture adapted to a sulfur-rich, acidic, low-oxygen environment. Life on Thionis is characterized by thiolipid-based cellular membranes, sulfur-buffered cytoplasmic media, and an entirely non-phosphorus genetic system based on thioester linkages. Organisms operate in a dense, CO₂-dominated atmosphere enriched in SO₂ and H₂S, over cold oceans of liquid SO₂ and locally hydrated SO₂-rich phases.

## Cell Biology
### Membranes
The cellular membranes of Thionisian organisms are composed of thiolipid bilayers, in which long-chain fatty acids are linked via thioester bonds (–CO–S–R) rather than terrestrial ester linkages (–CO–O–R). These thiolipids feature hydrophobic alkane tails and hydrophilic sulfonate or thiol head groups. The membrane design hypothesizes solvent-compatible thioester amphiphiles and protected catalytic interfaces; sulfur substitution alone does not demonstrate acid stability or resistance to SO₂/H₂S. Membranes are further stabilized by embedded sulfurized proteins reinforced with disulfide bonds (S–S linkages), providing mechanical strength in the slushy, sulfurous environment.

### Cytoplasm
The internal environment of Thionisian cells is a viscous gel dominated by SO₂-rich solvated medium with locally hydrated components, polysulfide ions (Sₓ²⁻), trace free H₂S, and minor quantities of water. Aqueous microcompartments have an acidity reference comparable to pH 3–4, while the bulk nonaqueous cytoplasm requires a solvent-specific acidity scale. SO₂/H₂S contributes to redox buffering; defined acid/base conjugate systems regulate proton activity. Viscosity is considerably higher than in terrestrial cytoplasm, slowing diffusion and favoring tightly organized intracellular transport. Specialized antioxidants, including bacillithiol-like derivatives, prevent uncontrolled oxidative damage, balancing the constant redox stress imposed by environmental sulfur species.

### Genetic Material
Thionisian life utilizes a nucleic acid analog known as TNA (Thioester-Linked Nucleic Acid). Unlike terrestrial DNA or RNA, TNA polymers are constructed from:

* **Backbone**: Thioester bonds linking thioglycol-based sugars.
* **Bases**: Modified heterocycles featuring sulfur substitutions for increased acid and UV stability.
* **Secondary structure**: Hydrogen bonds supplemented with disulfide bridges to stabilize base pairing under highly acidic conditions.

Replication/transcription hypothetically couple protected thioester synthesis, strand recognition and controlled redox-mediated crosslinks. Suppression of unwanted hydrolysis, reversible copying and separation of disulfide-stabilized strands remain alien-biochemical assumptions, not demonstrated mechanisms. TNA denotes Thioester-Linked Nucleic Acid, not terrestrial threose nucleic acid.

### Organelles
Thionisian cells are obligatorily compartmentalized:

* **Sulfonucleus** – A bacillithiol-polymer encapsulated core protecting TNA molecules from acid hydrolysis and oxidative attack. It features a double-membrane architecture and tight redox regulation.
* **Carboplasts** – Endosymbiotic organelles specializing in the fixation of CO₂ via sulfur-based anoxygenic phototrophy.
* **Monoplasts** – Endosymbiotic organelles optimized for CO fixation under low light near volcanic and fumarolic environments.
* **Sulfur vacuoles** – Intracellular storage for elemental sulfur (S₈) and polysulfides, serving as metabolic buffers and energy reserves.
* **Alcohosomes** – Vesicular compartments for storage and management of biologically produced thioalcohols, critical for both energy storage and solvent functions.

## Energy Systems
The primary energy pathways of Thionisian cells are sulfur-centered:

* **Primary phototrophy (Carboplasts)**

  `CO₂ + 2H₂S + hν → (CH₂O) + 2S + H₂O`

* **Alternative phototrophy (Monoplasts)**

  `CO + 2H₂S + hν → (CH₂O) + 2S + H₂`

* **Thioalcohol fermentation**

  `Defined carbon/sulfur substrates → specified thioalcohols and balancing coproducts (schematic fermentation; no atom-balanced net pathway asserted)`

* **Methanotrophy**

  `CH₄ + SO₂ → CO₂ + H₂S + H₂`

Energy is stored both in polysulfide-rich sulfur vacuoles and in alcohosome-sequestered thioalcohols, allowing flexible survival strategies under fluctuating light and sulfur availability.

## Trophic Guilds of Thionis
The biosphere of Thionis is stratified into trophic guilds defined by sulfur-driven metabolisms, ecological niche, and structural adaptation.

### CO₂ Phototrophs (Carboplastids)
Primary producers capturing stellar energy and fixing CO₂ via sulfur-based anoxygenic photosynthesis.

* **Reaction**: CO₂ + 2H₂S + hν → (CH₂O) + 2S + H₂O
* **Ecological niche**: Open sulfur plains, shallow acidic pools, upper layers of SO₂ oceans.
* **Adaptations**: Carboplast organelles, sulfur vacuoles, acid-stabilized thiolipid membranes.

### CO Phototrophs (Monoplastids)
Specialized primary producers favoring volcanic and fumarolic environments where CO is enriched.

* **Reaction**: CO + 2H₂S + hν → (CH₂O) + 2S + H₂
* **Ecological niche**: Geothermal fields, volcanic slopes, fumarolic terraces.
* **Adaptations**: Monoplast organelles tuned to lower light intensities and high CO/H₂S flux.

### Thioglycan Grazers (Thiophages)
Primary herbivorous consumers feeding on structural thioglycan polysaccharides of phototrophs.

* **Reaction**: (C₆H₁₀O₅–S)ₙ + n H₂O → n (C₆H₁₂O₆–S) (formal repeat-unit hydrolysis; respiration/energy conservation budget separate)
  (enzymatic cleavage of thioglycosidic bonds)
* **Ecological niche**: Sulfur wetlands, plains, shallow ocean margins.
* **Adaptations**: Acid-tolerant digestive systems, internal greigite skeletal structures.

### Thioalcohol Consumers (Alcophages)
Secondary consumers specializing in thioalcohol-rich tissues or fluids.

* **Representative reaction**: R–CH₂–SH + SO₂ → CO₂ + H₂S + reduced byproducts + energy
* **Ecological niche**: Biofilms, thioalcohol puddles, collapsed phototroph colonies.
* **Adaptations**: Exoskeletal armor, alcohosomes for volatile compound storage.

### Decomposers (Saprothiotes)
Critical recyclers of sulfuric organic matter, closing biochemical cycles.

* **Net reaction**: Dead biomass + S₈ → CO₂ + H₂S + SO₂ (schematic)
* **Ecological niche**: Anoxic pools, sediment layers, deep slush basins.
* **Adaptations**: Robust redox management systems, slow metabolic rates.

### Methanotrophs (Methanophages)
Specialists exploiting methane fluxes near geothermal sources.

* **Reaction**: CH₄ + SO₂ → CO₂ + H₂S + H₂
* **Ecological niche**: Geothermal vents, deep sediment pockets.
* **Adaptations**: Sponge-like bodies maximizing gas absorption, sulfuric respiration cycles.

### Vent Chemolithotrophs (Sulfoventids)
Deep-environment chemotrophs relying on sulfur–iron chemistry where light is absent.

* **Reaction**: 3H₂ + CO₂ + S → (CH₂O) + H₂S + H₂O
* **Ecological niche**: Deep sulfur vents, oceanic fumaroles beneath SO₂ oceans.
* **Adaptations**: Iron–sulfur cluster proteins, thick sulfurized membranes, high-pressure tolerance.

## Major Orders of Thionisian Macrofauna
Following the stabilization of sulfur-rich multicellularity, five dominant Orders of macrofauna emerged, each specialized for Thionis’ atmospheric and chemical environment.

### Pelagotheria (Oceanic Sulfur Beasts)
The Order **Pelagotheria** comprises the earliest large, ocean-going multicellular organisms on Thionis. Broad, flattened bodies supported by greigite-infused internal skeletons cruise the sulfuric seas, harvesting mats of Carboplastid phototrophs and Monoplastid blooms. Locomotion is achieved through undulating rows of six to ten fin-like limbs, distributing weight across sulfur-thickened tissues. Respiration is sulfur-based, with internal vacuolar systems recycling SO₂ and H₂S. Pelagotheres function as the ecological analog of Earth’s early marine megafauna, forming foundational biomass for coastal and open-ocean food webs. Reproductive cycles are tied to sulfur tides and volcanic upwelling events that replenish carbon and sulfur resources.

### Ambutheria (Centaur-like Land Sulfur Beasts)
The Order **Ambutheria** defines the dominant land-based thiophages. These centaur-like forms combine multiple locomotive limbs (six to eight) with specialized manipulative forelimbs. Evolving from smaller amphibious Pelagotherian ancestors, Ambutheres adapted to land by thickening their sulfur-gel muscles and reinforcing greigite endoskeletons to support motion across sulfuric mudflats and plains. Omnivorous diets that include Carboplastids, Alcophages, and scavenged remains demand cognitive flexibility. Mid-sized Ambutherian clades, combining cautious social behavior and generalized feeding strategies, underwent progressive encephalization under predator pressure, resource competition, and environmental variability, eventually producing the first sapient terrestrial species on Thionis.

### Velocicarida (Bug-like Alcophages)
The Order **Velocicarida** consists of swift, small-bodied terrestrial Alcophages adapted for rapid ground movement and opportunistic feeding. Retaining an exoskeletal architecture of thioglycan–sulfur plates, these organisms reduced limb number over time, stabilizing around four-limbed and bipedal forms in several clades. Specialized mouthparts allow precise extraction of thioalcohols and simple sugars from decaying phototrophs and dead thiophages. Despite their small size, Velocicarids are primary scavengers and prey for higher trophic levels, driving sulfur biomatter breakdown. High reproductive rates and metabolic flexibility allow colonization of environments from coastal marshes to highland plateaus.

### Altivolucida (Great Flying Alcophages)
The Order **Altivolucida** represents the largest volant organisms on Thionis, derived from smaller Velocicarid ancestors adapted to aerial exploitation of sulfur cliffs and biofilm forests. Wings composed of thioglycan-reinforced sulfur membranes are stretched between elongated limb bones, enabling soaring on thermals rising from volcanic and sulfurous regions. Efficient thioalcohol oxidation supports bursts of rapid flight interspersed with long resting periods on cliffs or fungal analogs. Altivolucids feed by swooping to extract thioalcohol-rich fluids or soft-bodied prey, then retreating to roosts. Their dominance of aerial niches reshapes sulfuric ecosystems, driving defensive adaptations among ground-dwelling species.

### Planivolucida (Short-Distance Gliding Alcophages)
The Order **Planivolucida** contains medium-sized bipedal gliders retaining reduced but functional gliding membranes along their flanks. Adapted for short-range gliding between sulfuric mesas, cliffs, and dense fungal forests, Planivolucids evolved from opportunistic scavengers into versatile omnivores. Expansion beyond pure thioalcohol harvesting into sugar-rich “plant” analogs and small prey allowed survival during sulfur-bloom collapses. Pressures from unpredictable food availability, predator evasion, and complex three-dimensional movement selected for advanced planning, tool manipulation, and extended social learning. Intelligence in Planivolucids arose as an energy-efficient survival strategy, enabling selective foraging, rudimentary trap-setting, and environmental modification prior to agriculture or metallurgy.

## Sapient Species of Thionis
The sulfur-rich world of Thionis produced two separate sapient lineages, emerging independently under distinct ecological pressures. One lineage arose from slow-moving, multilegged sulfur beasts; the other from agile, gliding sulfur skimmers. Both achieved Paleolithic-level tool use and social complexity through contrasting evolutionary trajectories.

### Ambutherius prudens ("Cautious Walking Beast")
![[images/Ambutherius_prudens.png]]

*Ambutherius prudens.*

*Common name*: Cautious sulfur beast

**Physical morphology:** **Ambutherius prudens** is a mid-sized centauroid organism, 2–2.5 meters in length. Six locomotive legs arranged in a radial hexapedal pattern are reinforced with dense greigite (Fe₃S₄) skeletal structures. The anterior thoracic segment bears two flexible manipulative limbs terminating in three-fingered prehensile digits capable of gripping, digging, and rudimentary tool use. The skin is a tough, acid-resistant thioglycan matrix embedded with sulfurized polysaccharides, giving a leathery brown–yellow appearance. Sensory organs cluster along an elevated cephalic ridge, including compound chemoreceptors, pressure sensors, and primitive magnetoceptive arrays.

**Diet and habitat:** A. prudens is omnivorous, feeding on Carboplastid mats, Monoplastid colonies, Velocicarids, and carrion from larger Pelagotheres stranded in estuarine sulfur flats. It inhabits broad sulfur plains and lowland basins where acidic mist and geothermal upwellings create a persistent haze. Seasonal migrations between sulfur blooms and fungal thickets structure its life cycle.

**Evolutionary pressures leading to sapience:** Intelligence arose from the need to avoid larger predatory Ambutheres and to outcompete rival omnivores for fluctuating resources. Their intermediate body size is too large to hide easily and too small to rely solely on strength, favoring sophisticated behavioral strategies. Selective pressures included tool-assisted scavenging (e.g. using sulfurized sticks to breach phototroph crusts), coordinated group defense, and memorization of shifting resource maps across wide sulfur plains. Early symbolic behaviors include arranging sulfur stones and scoring patterns into biofilm crusts, marking a Paleolithic cultural stage.

### Planivolucis sagax ("Wise Flat-Flyer")
![[images/Planivolucis_sagax.png]]

*Planivolucis sagax.*

*Common name*: Wise sulfur skimmer

**Physical morphology:** **Planivolucis sagax** is a light-framed biped standing 1.2–1.5 meters tall when fully upright. Reduced functional gliding membranes stretch from elongated forearms to the flanks, forming semi-retractable wing folds. Hands possess three flexible fingers with a partially opposable claw, enabling delicate manipulation. The thioglycan exoskeleton is reduced to articulated patches along the back and limbs, balancing protection with flexibility. Intervening skin is an iridescent sulfurous yellow. The cranium is enlarged relative to body size, with pronounced chemoreceptors and photoreceptors tuned to low, haze-filtered light.

**Diet and habitat:** P. sagax is an opportunistic omnivore, subsisting on thioalcohol-rich exudates, young Carboplastid shoots, sulfur-fungal analogs, and small scavenged prey. It nests in cliffside colonies, using its gliding membranes to move between mesas, sulfuric forests, and geothermal ravines. Its ecological range is highly vertical, exploiting niches inaccessible to large sulfur beasts.

**Evolutionary pressures leading to sapience:** Intelligence in P. sagax was driven by severe energy management constraints. Short-burst alcohol metabolism alone could not sustain survival between patchy resources, making endurance feeding, caching, and strategic resource management essential. Individuals capable of planning glides to optimal landing zones, remembering complex terrain, and coordinating small raids on sulfur blooms or fungal clusters gained strong advantages. Manipulative ability evolved under pressure to harvest fragile thioalcohol deposits and craft scraping or digging tools. Early cultural markers include pigment traces on cliff faces and chemical signal markers along migratory paths.

## Energy, material and life-cycle constraints

The atom-balanced CO₂/CO phototrophy, methane/SO₂ and vent H₂/carbon/sulfur expressions are reference balances rather than validated pathways in this solvent. The generic thioalcohol and decomposer schemes require defined substrates/products and environmental activities. Water generated by phototrophy and consumed during digestion is budgeted against the deliberately low-water medium. Stored sulfur requires an appropriate reaction partner and regeneration, not merely a label of energy reserve.

Retained biology includes all seven guilds, five macrofaunal orders, sulfur/greigite skeletons, flight/gliding lineages and both complete sapient profiles. Their rates, material stability, flight power and cold-enzyme throughput remain speculative. Sulfur tides refer to actual oceanic/geophysical rhythms, not an invented satellite. Seasonal bloom/refugium networks support full life cycles; no unsupported agriculture, metallurgy or political relationship is added to the Paleolithic cultures.

**Dunamas** is a temperate terrestrial exoplanet orbiting the K4V star **Altmania B** at a distance of 0.45 astronomical units. Slightly more massive and larger than Earth, Dunamas has 1.05 M🜨 and 1.054 R🜨, yielding a comfortable surface gravity of about 0.95 g🜨. It lies near the modeled inner water-habitable-zone edge of Altmania B and receives nearly the same time-averaged stellar flux as Earth.

The planet is distinguished by an Earth-like nitrogen–oxygen atmosphere, extensive shallow seas, two sizeable moons, and a faint rocky ring system that arcs across the skies at low and mid-latitudes. Trace ethyl formate (C₃H₆O₂) produced by native microbiota lends the air a subtle sweet, fruity scent over large regions of the surface.

## Orbit and Rotation
Dunamas orbits Altmania B with a semi-major axis of 0.45 AU and an eccentricity of 0.06. For a 0.70 M☉ star, Kepler's Third Law gives an orbital period of about 0.36 years, or approximately 132 Earth days, consistent with modeled system dynamics.

The orbital eccentricity produces modest but noticeable variations in stellar flux:

* Periastron: ≈112% of Earth's solar constant
* Apastron: ≈88% of Earth's solar constant

A rotation period of about 25 hours produces familiar day–night cycles, while an axial tilt of 24° generates Earth-like seasons. Seasonal contrasts are slightly enhanced relative to Earth because both axial tilt and orbital eccentricity contribute, with the stronger hemisphere set by their relative orientation.

The orbital inclination of roughly 2° relative to the local system plane is small; stability still depends on the full architecture and orbital phases.

## Physical Characteristics
### Mass, Size, and Gravity
Dunamas has:

* Mass: 1.05 M🜨
* Radius: 1.054 R🜨
* Surface gravity: ≈0.95 g🜨

These values imply a mean density of about 4.9 g/cm³, slightly lower than Earth's but still consistent with a silicate mantle and metallic core. The reduced gravity is generally well tolerated by humans and near-Earth baseline life, making Dunamas a comfortable candidate for colonization.

### Interior and Magnetosphere
Geophysical models suggest a differentiated structure with:

* An iron–nickel core capable of sustaining a planetary dynamo
* A convecting silicate mantle with active plate tectonics
* A comparatively thin crust, especially beneath ocean basins and island arcs

The core dynamo generates a moderate, Earth-like magnetosphere that deflects stellar wind particles from Altmania B and reduces atmospheric escape and surface radiation levels.

## Atmosphere
The surface reference pressure is **0.9 atm**. A normalized illustrative dry atmosphere is **77.96% N₂, 21.00% O₂, 1.00% Ar and 0.04% CO₂**, with trace ethyl formate and other organics represented within rounding. Water vapor varies regionally and renormalizes the local moist mixture. The dry oxygen partial pressure is about **0.189 atm**.

The atmosphere supports the intended human-habitable settlement environment with monitoring of local gases and native biology. Ozone and the atmosphere attenuate ultraviolet/particle exposure differently from the magnetic field; a K4V spectrum does not automatically guarantee UV safety.

Native yeast-, bacterial- and fungal-like organisms produce ethyl formate and related esters in coastal, forested and wetland regions. The faint scent reminiscent of rum and strawberries is retained, especially near deltas and seas. These are functional native analogies rather than a new claim of Earth ancestry, and one ester is not a unique biosignature or a test of safe food compatibility.

## Surface and Hydrosphere
Approximately 76% of Dunamas' surface is covered by water, slightly higher than Earth's ocean fraction. The planet features:

* Numerous small to medium-sized continents and microcontinents
* Highly indented coastlines with extensive bays, fjords, and inland seas
* Broad continental shelves and shallow epicontinental seas
* Long island arcs and archipelagos tracing subduction zones and hotspot chains

The relatively low land fraction and abundant shallow seas promote vigorous coastal and marine ecosystems. Continental interiors tend to be narrower than on Earth, limiting the extent of large arid basins, though interior deserts do form in rain-shadowed regions and during warm phases affected by periapse orientation and seasonal lag.

Hydrological cycles are dynamic, influenced by double-moon tides, ring-shadow cooling patterns, and seasonally shifting storm tracks.

## Rings
Dunamas is encircled by a thin, rocky ring system approximately 14,000–20,000 km center-to-center (2.09–2.98 planetary radii, or about 7285–13,285 km altitude). The rings are composed primarily of dark silicate rubble and ice-poor debris, likely originating from the tidal disruption of a small moon or a captured impactor.

From the surface:

* At low latitudes, the rings are visible as a pale, silvery band, most prominent near dawn and dusk.
* At higher latitudes, they appear as short arcs or vanish below the horizon depending on season and local time.

The rings are geologically young on cosmic timescales and may slowly decay via drag and collisional spreading, potentially seeding additional small moonlets just outside the ring region.

Opposite orbital directions and greater margin against stellar forcing preserve the two moons, intricate tidal beats and visually busy sky. Their radii are explicit working assumptions, not dimensions inferred uniquely from mass. Wider prograde layouts produced escapes or large excursions in the screens. Periods and the short-screen results are under Physical basis below and on [[Altmania System]].

The major moon affects spin-axis precession but is not assumed to guarantee constant obliquity. The combined star/moon torques, dissipation and evolution require a coupled tidal model. An illustrative weak-response history (k₂≈0.3, reference effective Q of order 10,000) preserves a plausible short-day design without treating today's 25 hours as timeless. The retrograde moon tends toward inward tidal evolution; the screen does not prove its 3-Gyr survival.

Tides overlap, strengthen at suitable alignments and interact with shallow-basin resonances, enhancing intertidal diversity and nutrient transport. Eclipse opportunities follow each moon's synodic conjunctions and orbital plane, with local visibility and eclipse seasons; several eclipses per major-moon orbit can involve the faster minor moon, not repeated major-moon solar alignments. Stellar, lunar and ring-shadow events are recorded separately.

## Climate and Seasons
Dunamas has a temperate global climate with a mean surface temperature of about 15 °C (288 K). The average stellar flux at 0.45 AU from a 0.20 L☉ star is close to Earth's, and clouds, gases and ocean transport set greenhouse and albedo balance; pressure alone does not determine the warming.

Key climatic features include:

* **Periastron summers** – Hotter and somewhat drier, especially in continental interiors, with expanded subtropical high-pressure zones and more intense heatwaves.
* **Apastron winters** – Cooler and wetter, with modest polar snowpacks and expanded mid-latitude storm tracks.
* **Strong coastal storms** – Particularly during seasonally changing land/ocean contrasts and the orbital phases of changing forcing, when thermal gradients between oceans and land are enhanced.
* **Monsoon-like regimes** – In some regions, ocean–land temperature contrasts driven by orbital position and axial tilt produce seasonal wind reversals and heavy rainfall.

The twin moons amplify tidal mixing in shallow seas and estuaries, contributing to nutrient cycling and biological productivity. Overall, the climate is robustly habitable but more dynamically variable than Earth's, with a slightly higher prevalence of extreme weather events.

## Potential for Life and Habitability
Dunamas' combination of:

* Near-Earth insolation
* Breathable nitrogen–oxygen atmosphere
* Moderate gravity and pressure
* Abundant shallow oceans and coastlines
* Strong but not overwhelming seasonal and tidal forcing

makes it highly favorable for Earth-like life. Native biospheres are expected to be rich in:

* Coastal and intertidal ecosystems adapted to complex tides
* Shelf-sea communities benefiting from strong mixing and nutrient upwelling
* Island and archipelago biotas with rapid speciation driven by isolation and shifting sea levels

Biogenic ethyl formate is an observed native metabolic product in this setting, although the molecule alone is not a unique remote biosignature, hinting at widespread fermentation-like metabolisms and complex organic chemistry in soils and waters.

Dunamas was one of the favored survey targets and now hosts the human colony referenced in the Dichloran history: habitats require minimal environmental modification, and native ecosystem compatibility requires direct biochemical assessment and controlled separation from Earth-derived organisms.

## Visual Appearance
From orbit, Dunamas remains a blue–white world of shallow turquoise seas, scattered green/tan landmasses, a faint dark rocky ring and two substantial moons. The ring follows a near-equatorial working plane, inclined to the stellar orbit with the planet's 24° obliquity; long-term warp/precession requires a model.

At the adopted lunar-density radii and axes, the major moon is about **0.71°** across and the nearer minor about **1.11°**. The retrograde inner moon moves through the sky in the opposite orbital sense, adding visual interest. Stellar angular size and lunar distance determine whether an eclipse can be total, annular or partial; geometry and observer position remain important.

At low and mid-latitudes the rings appear as pale bands/arcs, with very thin edge-on views near the equator and visibility limits toward high latitudes. Illumination, shadow and brightness vary with season/time; the geometric ring plane does not move merely because the observer's local clock advances. Dawn/dusk contrast, clouds, moons and wave-lit coasts retain the distinctive “familiar but heightened” sky.

## Cultural Perception
The name **Dunamas** derives from the Greek δύναμις (*dýnamis*, meaning “force” or “power”), reflecting the visibly dynamic nature of the planet's tides, skies, and seasons. Early observers and settlers were struck by the constant interplay of moons, rings, clouds, and waves, and the name became associated with resilience and motion.

Among prospective colonists and explorers, Dunamas is often described as "familiar but heightened": recognizably Earth-like, yet enriched by double moons, rings, complex tides, and subtly perfumed air.

## Present settlement and climate scope

Early prospective-colonist accounts precede the adopted settled present. Nasia and Dunamas are the two human colony worlds; no unnamed 0.85-AU planet is inserted. Native ethyl-formate ecology, archipelagos, microcontinents, shelves, shallow seas, fjords, ring shadows, cyclones, monsoons and twin-moon coastal mixing are retained. A 288-K mean is a climate target requiring a spectrum/cloud/ocean model near the inner modeled water-zone edge. Periastron/apastron forcing is global, while tilt-driven summer/winter belongs to opposite hemispheres; distance changes fastest between, not at, the apsides.


## Physical basis

Dunamas's major moon (0.8 lunar masses, 260,000 km, prograde, 14.833 d) and minor moon (0.3 lunar masses, 120,000 km, retrograde at 175°, 4.665 d) have working radii of 1613 and 1163 km at lunar density, subtending about 0.71° and 1.11°. The major moon's eccentricity reaches about 0.120–0.143 in the 3000-year screens while the inner retrograde moon stays at e ≤ 0.013, and these varying eccentricities belong in the eventual tidal and thermal evolution. The 14,000–20,000-km young, ice-poor rocky ring (2.09–2.98 planetary radii) has unsolved optical depth, mass, spreading and moonlet accretion. The present 25-hour spin rests on a weak-dissipation working history (illustrative k₂ ≈ 0.3 and Q of order 10,000).

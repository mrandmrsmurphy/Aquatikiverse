## Altmania System

The **Altmania System** is a wide binary star system located in the fictional constellation of *Velora*, approximately 156.6 light-years (48 parsecs) from Earth. It consists of two K-type main sequence stars, Altmania A (K6V) and Altmania B (K4V), gravitationally bound in a low-eccentricity mutual orbit. With a semi-major axis of about 15 astronomical units (AU) and a total mass of roughly 1.30 M<sub>☉</sub>, their orbital period is approximately 51 Earth years.

Altmania is notable for the stability of its planetary environments and the promising terrestrial planets identified in orbit around its primary star.

## Discovery

The Altmania System was first identified in 2087 during a systematic survey of nearby K-type stars by the Advanced Radial Velocity Array (ARVA) and the Centauri Horizon Telescope (CHT). Variations in radial velocity detected in the spectrum of Altmania A revealed the gravitational presence of a nearby stellar companion. Follow-up observations using long-baseline interferometry resolved Altmania B, confirming the binary nature of the system.

Subsequent spectroscopic and photometric studies uncovered the presence of several planets, including Nasia, a terrestrial world in the habitable zone of Altmania A.

## Stellar Characteristics

### Altmania A

Altmania A is a K6V orange dwarf with a mass of approximately 0.60 M<sub>☉</sub> and a radius of 0.62 R<sub>☉</sub>. Its luminosity is about 0.10 L<sub>☉</sub>, and its effective surface temperature is around 4120 K. It exhibits solar metallicity ([Fe/H] = 0.0) and has a rotational period between 30 and 40 days. Its original 30–40-billion-year main-sequence estimate is a rough stellar-model range; an elementary mass/luminosity scaling does not establish it precisely.

### Altmania B

Altmania B is a slightly larger and more luminous K4V star, with a mass of 0.70 M<sub>☉</sub>, a radius of 0.74 R<sub>☉</sub>, and a luminosity of approximately 0.20 L<sub>☉</sub>. Its surface temperature is near 4490 K. Like its companion, it shows solar metallicity and rotates faster, completing a rotation every 20 to 30 days. Altmania B's original 25–30-billion-year estimate remains a rough model-dependent range.

## Orbital Mechanics
The 15-AU relative stellar orbit has 14.4/15.6-AU apsides, e=0.04 and a 50.95-year period. The component barycentric axes are 8.077 AU for A and 6.923 AU for B. Circumstellar empirical stability comparisons give approximately 3.70/4.11 AU, outside all six known planets. These restricted-particle estimates and the short reference integrations do not prove gigayear multi-planet or lunar survival.

## Potential for Planetary Systems
The actual six-planet inventory is in [[Altmania System]]. A one-Earth-mass, one-dimensional conservative water-habitable-zone comparison gives approximately **0.324–0.610 AU around A** and **0.453–0.839 AU around B**, using the luminosity/radius-consistent temperatures. These are modeled boundaries, not predictions for exotic atmospheres or guarantees of Earthlike climate.

Nasia and Dunamas are close to the inner comparison edges and retain temperate cloud/ocean climates as setting requirements. Thionis and Azotia lie within the broad orbital water-zone comparison but occupy sulfur/ammonia climate states rather than ordinary water worlds. The former simplistic optimistic flux ranges are retired. Stellar tides do not strongly couple the stars at their present separation.

## Physical basis

**Location.** From the supplied J2000 position (RA 02h12m, Dec −11°) and 48-pc distance, an approximate standard heliocentric Galactic transformation gives l ≈ 176.398° and b ≈ −65.124°, hence X ≈ −20.15, Y ≈ 1.27 and Z ≈ −43.55 pc, with +X toward the Galactic center and +Z north. This is a rounded matrix calculation, not an Astropy execution; the frame is defined by [Astropy's Galactic frame](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/astropy/astropy/main/astropy/coordinates/builtin_frames/galactic.py) and [transform implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/astropy/astropy/main/astropy/coordinates/builtin_frames/galactic_transforms.py).

**Stars.** The stellar lifetimes are rough, model-dependent estimates of tens of Gyr. The temperatures follow from the adopted radii and luminosities, and the visual magnitudes would need spectral bolometric corrections; matching a distance modulus is not an absolute-flux validation. Velora is a fictional sky designation, not an IAU constellation.

**Water zone.** A conservative one-Earth-mass water-zone comparison is about 0.324–0.610 AU for A and 0.453–0.839 AU for B ([Kopparapu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1404.5292v1)). A habitable-zone label is not an observed climate; the actual climate states are on the planet pages.

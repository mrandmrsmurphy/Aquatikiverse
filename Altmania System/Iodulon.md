Iodulon is a halogen-dominated terrestrial planet orbiting the orange dwarf Altmania A at a distance of 0.25 astronomical units. Tidally locked and perpetually divided between a searing day side and a frigid night side, it exists at the razor's edge of habitability. Despite surface conditions that would be lethally corrosive to oxygen-based life, Iodulon harbors a thriving biosphere adapted to extreme redox chemistry, halogen volatility, and geochemical disequilibrium. The planetary atmosphere is dense, acidic, and in photochemical disequilibrium, supporting a unique ecology centered on halocarbon metabolism and sulfur dioxide as a dominant intracellular solvent.

## Orbital and Physical Parameters
Iodulon orbits Altmania A, a K6V main-sequence star of 0.10 solar luminosity, completing one orbit every 58.9 Earth days. Tidal forces have synchronized its rotation and revolution, producing a permanent day and night hemisphere. With a radius of 0.95 R⊕ and a mass of 0.856 M⊕, Iodulon maintains a surface gravity of approximately 0.95 g⊕ and a mean density of about 5.5 g/cm³, suggesting a silicate–metal interior beneath its halide-laced crust.

## Stellar Context
Altmania A emits a cooler, redder spectrum than the Sun, with peak energy in the visible-red and near-infrared. At 0.25 AU, Iodulon receives approximately 1.6 times the solar constant of Earth, concentrated on its dayside. Despite the elevated insolation, global habitability is restricted by intense photolysis, strong greenhouse forcing in the substellar region, and sharp day–night thermal gradients.

## Surface and Thermal Conditions
The substellar region remains about 350–370 K. Warmer inner-terminator terrain can reach 270–330 K but is largely unsuitable for exposed SO₂ biology. The richest open communities instead occupy the **nightward terminator around 230–240 K**, where SO₂-rich solutions can persist. Warmer communities occur in pressurized subsurface basins, sealed cultivation chambers and solvent-retaining tissues with explicit vapor barriers.

This cold-side belt retains acidic pools, halide salt flats, oxidized perchlorate deposits, halogen-stained basalt spires, mineral deserts and seep networks. No global plate-tectonic regime is inferred. Radiogenic/secular heat sustains localized vertical transport and resurfacing; the small moon modulates stresses without being assigned an unmodeled dominant planetary heat source. Deeper pressurized habitats connect productive communities and shelter their full reproductive, developmental and dispersal cycles.

This is a design reference rather than a globally uniform retrieval. SO₂ condenses across colder habitats; activities in mixtures, weather and cold traps set its local vapor abundance. The approximately 0.316-bar SO₂ partial pressure matches a pure-fluid comparison near 240 K; a full transport/mixture calculation is still needed. Water is scarce and variable.

**Free F₂ is not a bulk atmospheric component.** Fluoride/HF, fluorinated structural materials and bound fluorine are abundant; transient elemental fluorine is generated in specialized isolated compartments or industrial apparatus and rapidly consumed. It is not treated as interchangeable with fluoride ions. Chlorine/oxyhalogen species carry much of the environmental oxidant cycling.

Dayward convection, photochemistry and halogen weather feed the habitable margin. The terminator has strong condensation/chemical gradients; cold nightside layers are more stratified. HCl/HF frost deposition is confined to locations where the actual species-specific temperature/activity conditions permit it, not inferred from all terrain below 250 K. Photochemical oxidants need a net source against reactions with water, reduced substrates and minerals.

## Internal Structure and Geochemistry
Iodulon possesses a differentiated interior with a modest iron or iron-halide core, sufficient to support residual geothermal activity without necessarily sustaining a long-lived global magnetic dynamo. The mantle is inferred to contain halogen-enriched silicates, and the crust is composed of highly fractured basalt permeated by halide salts, oxidized perchlorates, and sulfates. Geothermal flux drives cryovolcanic venting primarily on the night side and within deeper terminator basins, where volatile-rich fluids ascend through fracture networks.

## Subsurface Ecosystems
Beneath the crust of Iodulon’s night side and deep within its terminator zone lie fissures venting acidic, halide-rich brines warmed by geothermal heat. In these sheltered, lightless environments, chemohalotrophic life persists without photonic input, relying on water–rock interactions and mineral leaching. Cryovolcanic fluids rich in FeCl₃, CuF₂, and perchlorates emerge from fractured substrates, creating redox-active zones where ferric, oxyhalogen and other oxidants interface with simple carbon-bearing molecules such as formate, acetate, or methane.

Representative metabolic pathway:
`HCOO⁻ + 2 Fe³⁺ → CO₂ + H⁺ + 2 Fe²⁺`

These reactions release CO₂ and halide ions, enriching the surrounding medium and sustaining a deep biosphere that functions largely independently of surface illumination. Some lineages methylate metabolic intermediates to generate volatile CH₃Cl and CH₃F, contributing to the atmospheric halocarbon flux. These vent communities constitute the basal trophic guild of Iodulon, supplying carbon, electron donors, and trace metals to surface and near-surface photohalotrophs while buffering ecological function during periods of stellar variation.

## Biosphere Summary
Main article: [[Life on Iodulon]].

Life on Iodulon occupies a narrow circumplanetary belt around the terminator, where redox energy from the illuminated hemisphere and chemical sequestration on the dark hemisphere converge. Dominant strategies include photohalotrophy, halocarbon synthesis, and extreme acidophily. SO₂-rich fluid serves as the principal intracellular solvent, with vapor-retaining structures and protected redox compartments. This is a speculative nonaqueous molecular architecture, not a demonstrated alternative biochemistry. The biosphere is supported by an externally powered redox network that links photochemical producers with subsurface chemosynthetic guilds and recycles halogens between the atmosphere, surface deposits, and crustal reservoirs.

## Small moon and settled context

A small rocky/halide-rich moon has a working mass of 10⁻⁵ M⊕, radius 160 km, prograde axis 80,000 km, e=0.003 and inclination 3° to the planetary orbital plane. It is the Dichloran observatory/foundry destination. Its short point-mass reference screen is documented separately; it is not assigned an uncalculated geothermal power or permanent vacuum-preserved volatile inventory. Lunar ice/mineral stocks require cold storage and replenishment. The present is the era after achieved lunar access and subsequent translated human contact.


## Physical basis

The 1.2-atm reference atmosphere is normalized to 30% HCl, 18% Cl₂, 9% HF, 26% SO₂, 16.48% N₂, 0.5% CO₂, 0.019% halocarbons and other gases and 0.001% H₂O; free F₂ is compartmentalized and transient, not 2% of the bulk air. At 240 K the specified SO₂ partial pressure is about 0.316 bar, close to the pure-fluid saturation comparison ([NIST SO₂](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C7446095&Mask=4)). Cold nightward 230–240 K living belts, pressurized warmer caverns and vapor-retaining cells keep complete coral and hexapod life cycles possible, while warmer 270–330 K open terrain is largely inhospitable. Global cold trapping, chemistry and mixture activities are uncomputed, and fluorine chemistry has its own hazards ([NOAA fluorine chemistry](https://cameochemicals.noaa.gov/report?key=CH764)). The small moon has a working 160-km radius at a density of about 3.48 g/cm³, a newly specified parameter. The orbital screens are on [[Altmania System]] under Physical basis.

**Thionis** is a sulfuric terrestrial exoplanet orbiting the K-type main-sequence star **Altmania A** at a distance of 0.40 astronomical units. It is the outer of the three known planets around Altmania A in the [[Altmania System]]. Thionis receives about 62.5% of Earth's insolation and has a thick, CO₂-dominated atmosphere with high concentrations of sulfur gases. Under pressures of roughly 4 atm, the planet supports extensive surface oceans of liquid sulfur dioxide (SO₂) and SO₂-rich solutions and locally hydrated phases.

Despite low temperatures and an oxygen-free, highly acidic environment, Thionis hosts a complex, fully anaerobic biosphere based on sulfur-centered redox chemistry and non-phosphorus genetics.

Thionis has a mass of about 1.3 times that of Earth and a radius of 1.1 R⊕, giving it a mean density of roughly 5.4 g/cm³. This suggests a silicate mantle and metallic core composition broadly similar to Earth's, with only minor differences in bulk composition. Surface gravity is about 7.4% higher than on Earth, and the increased mass and radius yield an escape velocity of roughly 12.2 km/s, allowing the planet to retain a thick atmosphere over geologic timescales.

Interior models indicate a differentiated structure with an iron-rich core, a silicate mantle, and a crust composed of basaltic material extensively altered by sulfuric chemistry. Radiogenic silicate reservoirs and secular cooling support ongoing geothermal activity. Earlier stresses can affect structure but are not an inexhaustible present heat source; no dominant tidal power is assumed without a coupled response model.

## Atmosphere and Climate
Thionis possesses a massive, highly acidic atmosphere with a surface pressure of about 4 atm. The bulk composition is:

* Carbon dioxide (CO₂) – dominant background gas
* Sulfur dioxide (SO₂) – abundant
* Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) – significant
* Trace hydrogen (H₂) and methane (CH₄)
* Negligible free oxygen (O₂)

This mixture produces a dense, optically thick veil of sulfur clouds and aerosols. CO₂ and SO₂ provide strong greenhouse warming, offset by high planetary albedo from sulfuric clouds and bright SO₂ oceans. The result is a global mean surface temperature near 240 K, colder than Earth but substantially warmer than the no-greenhouse equilibrium.

Climate features include:

* Strong zonal winds driven by rapid rotation
* Intense sulfur storms transporting SO₂ and H₂S between equator and poles
* Acid fogs and haze layers in low-lying basins
* Monsoon-like circulation patterns tied to the eccentric orbit and the distribution of SO₂ oceans

Day–night contrasts are moderated by the thick atmosphere and high heat capacity of the SO₂ oceans, but seasonal contrasts between periastron and apastron remain important drivers of climate variability.

## Surface and Geology
The surface of Thionis is dominated by sulfuric and basaltic landscapes sculpted by low temperatures, heavy atmosphere, and vigorous sulfur cycling:

* **SO₂ oceans and seas** – Extensive liquid sulfur dioxide bodies occupy large basins at low and mid-latitudes, often covered by seasonal sulfurous hazes and slush.
* **Sulfur glaciers** – At higher latitudes and elevations, solid SO₂ and sulfur hydrates flow slowly as glacial masses, carving valley networks in basaltic crust.
* **Basaltic plains** – Dark basaltic provinces, resurfaced by eruptions and lava flows, are heavily altered by acid weathering, forming sulfur-rich regolith.
* **Cryovolcanoes and fumaroles** – Volcanic centers and cryovolcanic vents outgas SO₂, H₂S, CO₂, and H₂, feeding both the atmosphere and the subsurface chemical reservoirs.
* **Sulfuric erosion** – Acid fogs, liquid SO₂ precipitation, and chemical dissolution drive rapid weathering of exposed rock, transporting sulfur and metals into low-lying oceans and basins.

Geologically, Thionis is active. Localized tectonism, sustained heat flow, and volatile-rich magmas maintain long-lived geothermal provinces that play a central role in both sulfur cycling and the deep biosphere.

## Biosphere
Main article: [[Life on Thionis]].

Thionis supports a complex, fully anaerobic biosphere built around sulfur-centered chemistry rather than oxygen–water metabolism. Key features of the Thionisian biosphere include:

* **Solvent system** – Life uses liquid SO₂ and SO₂-rich solvated/gel media with controlled low water activity; locally hydrated phases are identified separately rather than assuming bulk H₂SO₃ hydrate, as the primary environmental and intracellular solvents.
* **Cell architecture** – Cells employ thiolipid bilayers reinforced with disulfide-linked proteins, sulfur-buffered cytoplasm, and TNA (Thioester-Linked Nucleic Acid) genetic material, which is phosphorus-free and adapted to strong acidity.
* **Primary production** – Primary phototrophs (Carboplastids and Monoplastids) perform sulfur-based anoxygenic photosynthesis, using reactions such as:

  - `CO₂ + 2H₂S + hν → (CH₂O) + 2S + H₂O`
  - `CO + 2H₂S + hν → (CH₂O) + 2S + H₂`
* **Chemotrophy** – Vent chemolithotrophs exploit redox gradients involving H₂, CO₂, elemental sulfur, and iron–sulfur minerals in deep geothermal environments.
* **Macrofauna** – Multiple Orders of sulfur macrofauna, including oceanic Pelagotheria and terrestrial Ambutheria, Velocicarida, Altivolucida, and Planivolucida, structure food webs across oceans, plains, and sulfur forests.

Two sapient species, ***Ambutherius prudens*** and ***Planivolucis sagax***, have independently evolved Paleolithic-level cultures using sulfur-hardened tools, chemical signaling, and pigment markings. The biosphere operates entirely without free oxygen; oxygen respiration is replaced by sulfur-centered oxidation/reduction pathways, and atmospheric O₂ remains negligible.

## Relationship to the Altmania System
Within the Altmania A planetary system, Thionis occupies the outermost confirmed orbit, beyond the Earth-analog world [[Nasia]] and the inner halogen world [[Iodulon]]. With a stellar flux of only 62.5% that of Earth and a global mean temperature near 240 K, Thionis lies within the broad modeled orbital water-habitable-zone comparison but occupies a cold sulfur climate rather than an ordinary liquid-water state. However, for sulfur–SO₂–based life, the planet sits within a "sulfuric habitable zone," where surface and subsurface liquids can persist for billions of years under the radiation environment of a stable K-dwarf.

The 15-AU companion allows a compact circumstellar architecture. A short reference screen supports orbital separation for the chosen initial conditions, but secular effects and gigayear tidal evolution remain distinct requirements.

## Atmospheric and biological reference

A normalized dry working atmosphere is 90% CO₂, 8% SO₂, 1.5% H₂S, 0.3% H₂ and 0.2% CH₄ at 4 atm. At 240 K its SO₂ partial pressure is about 0.324 bar, close to the pure-fluid saturation benchmark of 0.318 bar; water and hydrate activities need a mixture model. This reference is not globally uniform across ice caps and vents. The 240-K climate remains a target requiring radiative closure. The 1.2-Gyr biosphere and both Paleolithic sapients are detailed in [[Life on Thionis]]. The 20-hour spin likewise requires an evolutionary dissipation history; active tectonics alone does not fix it.


## Physical basis

The 4-atm dry reference is 90% CO₂, 8% SO₂, 1.5% H₂S, 0.3% H₂ and 0.2% CH₄, and the SO₂ partial pressure of about 0.324 bar is close to the 240-K pure-fluid comparison ([NIST SO₂](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C7446095&Mask=4)). Locally hydrated phases and gel media take the place of a bulk H₂SO₃-hydrate solvent, and acidity is solvent-specific, with aqueous pH comparisons restricted to aqueous compartments. Thiolipid assembly, thioester inheritance and disulfide strand control remain hypothetical. The spin and moon-system limits are on [[Altmania System]] under Physical basis.

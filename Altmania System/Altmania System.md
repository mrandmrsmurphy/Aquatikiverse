# Altmania System

*The Altmania Binary System: Altmania A and B.*

The **Altmania System** is a wide binary star system consisting of two K-type main sequence stars, **Altmania A** (K6V) and **Altmania B** (K4V), gravitationally bound in a low-eccentricity mutual orbit. With a semi-major axis of about 15 AU and an eccentricity of ε ≈ 0.04, the two stars orbit a common barycenter in roughly 51 Earth years. At periastron (closest approach), the separation between the stars is about 14.4 AU, while at apastron (greatest separation) it expands to roughly 15.6 AU.

## Stellar Characteristics
Main article: [[Altmania]].
### Altmania A
Altmania A is a K6V orange dwarf with a mass of approximately 0.60 M☉, a radius of 0.62 R☉, and a luminosity of 0.10 L☉. Its effective surface temperature is about 4120 K. Metallicity is solar ([Fe/H] = 0.0), and the rotational period is estimated between 30 and 40 days. Using standard mass–luminosity–lifetime scalings, its main-sequence lifetime is estimated at several tens of Gyr; the original 30–40-Gyr range remains a rough stellar-model estimate, not a result of an elementary scaling alone.

### Altmania B
Altmania B is a slightly more massive and more luminous K4V star, with 0.70 M☉, 0.74 R☉, and 0.20 L☉. Its surface temperature is approximately 4490 K. Like its companion, it possesses solar metallicity, but has a faster rotation period between 20 and 30 days. Its original 25–30-Gyr lifetime range is retained as a rough model-dependent estimate; mass and luminosity alone do not establish that precise interval.

## Orbital Mechanics
The binary has a relative semimajor axis of 15 AU, eccentricity 0.04, apsides 14.4/15.6 AU and period 50.95 years. Altmania A and B have barycentric axes approximately 8.077 and 6.923 AU. All six planets remain well inside the empirical circumstellar test-particle limits, approximately 3.70 AU around A and 4.11 AU around B.

The adjacent planetary spacings are approximately 16.7/14.1 mutual Hill radii around A and 39.8/28.2 around B. These are encouraging screens rather than guarantees. A's period ratios are about 1.448 and 1.398; B's outer pair is near 2:1 at 2.024. Neither exact resonance locking nor avoidance of low-order commensurabilities is presumed. A short Newtonian reference screen is described under Physical basis; no multi-gigayear integration is claimed.

Direct stellar tides and mass exchange are negligible in the present main-sequence binary. The stars behave approximately as two weakly coupled planetary systems, but secular forcing, spin/tidal evolution, disk truncation and later stellar evolution remain relevant. No guarantee is extended through every future post-main-sequence phase.

## Planetary Systems
### Altmania A Planets
Altmania A hosts three known planets:

- **[[Iodulon]]**, 0.25 AU: a halogen-rich terrestrial world, 0.856 M⊕ / 0.95 R⊕, receiving 1.60 S⊕. Its cold nightward terminator and pressurized habitats support a 1.1-Gyr native biosphere and the jointly intelligent coral–hexapod [[Dichlorans]]. Stable fluorides/fluorinated materials are widespread; free F₂ is localized and contained.
- **[[Nasia]]**, 0.32 AU: 1.04 M⊕ / 1.02 R⊕, about 0.977 S⊕ at the semimajor axis, with temperate oceanic/archipelago geography, native life and a present human colony. Its climate is near the modeled inner water-habitable-zone edge, not demonstrably at the center.
- **[[Thionis]]**, 0.40 AU: 1.3 M⊕ / 1.1 R⊕, 0.625 S⊕, with approximately 240-K SO₂ seas, a CO₂-rich four-atmosphere envelope and a 1.2-Gyr anoxic sulfur biosphere. Both *Ambutherius prudens* and *Planivolucis sagax* maintain independently evolved Paleolithic cultures.

The planets have substantial mutual Hill spacing, but their actual eccentricity and secular/resonance histories are distinct from a simple circular packing argument.

### Altmania B Planets
Altmania B hosts three known planets:

- **[[Neonova]]**, 0.20 AU: **4 M⊕ / 1.5 R⊕**, a rocky super-Earth with a 17-atm neon-rich secondary atmosphere receiving 5 S⊕. Its 1.2-Gyr biosphere is predominantly aerial; H₂-rich lift chambers support the main floaters, with a 23–26-day circulation-driven life cycle. The former 7-M⊕ / 1.7-R⊕ gas-rich summary is superseded.
- **[[Dunamas]]**, 0.45 AU: 1.05 M⊕ / 1.054 R⊕, near-Earth insolation and a temperate 76%-ocean climate, with a young rocky ring, two large moons, native ester-producing ecosystems and a present human colony.
- **[[Azotia]]**, 0.72 AU: 2 M⊕ / 1.30 R⊕, mean stellar flux about 0.388 S⊕. The adopted reference is **260 K**, with ammonia-rich air and ammonia–water seas supporting the 1.5–2.0-Gyr native biosphere. Cooler coasts, uplands and polar slush retain Slushcrawler habitats. The old 205-K/large-vapor mixture is retired.

Both the physical inventory and the names remain six planets total. The two human colony worlds are Nasia and Dunamas.

## Formation and Evolution
The current configuration of the Altmania system suggests an orderly formation history with minimal subsequent dynamical upheaval. The near-circular binary orbit at ~15 AU permitted compact circumstellar disks, whose tidal truncation and lifetimes depend on their detailed dynamics. Planet formation could proceed more or less independently around each component, with stable accretion of material into planetary embryos and limited external perturbation.

Formation scenarios for similar systems permit that additional planets may have formed at larger distances, only to be dynamically ejected during the early chaotic phase of system evolution or gradually destabilized by long-term secular interactions with the binary companion. Some small bodies (asteroids or icy planetesimals) may still exist in distant belts, though no debris disks have yet been confirmed.

Overall, the Altmania system represents a dynamically quiet, long-lived environment capable of sustaining complex planetary systems over timescales that exceed the main sequence lifetimes of most Sun-like stars. With two K-dwarfs and at least two habitable-zone terrestrial worlds plus more exotic SO₂ and NH₃ ocean planets, it supports diverse fictional biospheres and evolutionary trajectories; their complete climate, chemistry and long-term dynamics are not validated by the architectural screens alone.

A both-prograde Dunamian layout and a wider Nasian outer layout produced escapes, close approaches or large excursions in the screens, which is why the tighter, counter-orbiting arrangements are used; they are working references, not stability proofs. Iodulon's small moon has a working 160-km radius (density about 3.48 g/cm³), a newly specified parameter, not a measured object; its observatory and foundry need confined life support, managed volatile stocks and cold storage, and tidal stress modulation is distinct from an uncalculated dominant vent heat source. Dunamas's working moon radii at lunar density are 1613 and 1163 km, subtending about 0.71° (major) and 1.11° (minor); eclipse opportunities follow real conjunctions, planes and local visibility, and all six moon periods are sidereal Kepler comparisons, with stellar forcing producing osculating variations. The 14,000–20,000-km ring (2.09–2.98 planetary radii, young, ice-poor and rocky) keeps an inclined ringed appearance in a near-equatorial reference plane with latitude-dependent surface visibility; optical depth, particle size, mass, strength, spreading and drag, and outer moonlet accretion are unsolved, and no perpetual ring lifetime is assumed. The present 25-hour Nasia and Dunamas spins and the 20-hour Thionis spin rest on weak-dissipation working histories (k₂ ≈ 0.3 and an effective Q of order 10,000 are illustrative, not measured); retrograde tidal decay, stellar spin torques, moon migration and obliquity resonances need a full coupled evolution, and nothing in a short point-mass screen certifies a 3-Gyr moon system.

**Numerical screens.** The screens use Newtonian point masses in AU, year and solar-mass units with a fourth-order Yoshida composition of velocity-Verlet steps. They start from the supplied planetary masses, axes, eccentricities and reference inclinations, with planet and moon phases and nodes varied by integer seed, and conserve momentum through barycentric initialization while monitoring total energy and host-relative osculating elements.

- The eight-body binary-plus-six-planets screen survives 10,000 years in two phase cases (steps of 0.001 and 0.0005 yr); adjacent planets stay radially separated, and osculating eccentricity reaches about 0.095 for Iodulon, 0.069 for Nasia and 0.104 for Dunamas, so the supplied near-circular elements are epoch references, not fixed circular orbits.
- The star–planet–moon screens survive 3000 years in two phase cases for each Nasian and Dunamian layout. Nasia's outer moon Kathy is the most sensitive orbit, with a maximum sampled osculating e of about 0.307 (seed 0) and 0.444 (seed 1) and significant axis excursions; Dunamas's major moon reaches e of about 0.120–0.143 and its inner retrograde moon stays at e ≤ 0.013.
- The Iodulonian small-moon screen covers 300 years in two phase and step cases, and half-step 500-year runs for Nasia and Dunamas and the planetary screen keep the same qualitative survival and comparable orbital envelopes. Total relative-energy errors are about 10⁻¹⁰–10⁻¹², but energy is dominated by stellar binding, so that number does not replace checking the moon elements.

The eccentricities that vary in these runs belong in the eventual tidal and thermal evolution, not the frozen initial e = 0.003. The moon runs omit the binary companion, other planets, oblateness, tides, thermal and rheological feedback, collisions, relativity and external encounters, and the planetary runs omit moons and dissipation, so none of this extrapolates to 3 Gyr. Spin obliquities are not dynamical bodies in the screens. The empirical stability boundaries come from [Holman & Wiegert](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9809315) and the satellite boundaries from [Domingos et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2966.2006.11104.x).

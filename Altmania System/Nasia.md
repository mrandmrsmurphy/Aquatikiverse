**Nasia** is a confirmed terrestrial exoplanet orbiting the K-type main-sequence star **Altmania A**, one component of the wide binary **Altmania System**.
Located approximately 156.6 light-years (48 parsecs) from Earth, Nasia lies close to the modeled inner water-habitable-zone edge, with its temperate cloud/ocean climate retained as a setting requirement.
The planet is classified as an Earth analog due to its similar mass, radius, atmospheric composition, surface gravity, and temperate climate.
Nasia possesses three natural satellites: **Natia**, **Stephanie**, and **Kathy**.

## Discovery and Observation
Nasia was detected in 2094 during a survey of nearby K-dwarfs conducted by the Advanced Radial Velocity Array (ARVA) and the Centauri Horizon Telescope (CHT).
Initial identification relied on radial velocity shifts in Altmania A's spectrum, later confirmed by transit photometry, interferometric imaging, and spectroscopic follow-up.
Nasia rapidly emerged as a promising candidate for human colonization due to its Earth-like conditions.

## Host Star: Altmania A
**Altmania A** is a K6V orange dwarf with a mass of 0.60 M☉, a radius of 0.62 R☉, and a luminosity of 0.10 L☉.
It has an effective surface temperature of approximately 4120 K and solar metallicity ([Fe/H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallicity) = 0.0).
Altmania A orbits the slightly larger Altmania B (K4V) with an average separation of 15 AU and a low eccentricity of ε ≈ 0.04.
The binary separation permits compact circumstellar architectures; actual multi-planet and moon stability needs explicit dynamical assessment.

Nasia's composition is typical of terrestrial planets, with a silicate mantle and metallic core.
Its slightly greater mass and radius relative to Earth produce a surface gravity almost identical to Earth's, supporting comfortable conditions for human physiology.
An active internal dynamo generates a robust magnetosphere and extensive aurorae. The field modifies charged-particle access; atmosphere and ozone provide different shielding, and no complete exclusion of cosmic radiation is asserted.

## Surface and Geography
Nasia's surface is predominantly oceanic, with approximately 77% water coverage.
The land distribution is highly fragmented into a vast network of archipelagos, medium-sized landmasses, and isolated islands.

The planet lacks supercontinents; instead, it features:

* Multiple Australia-sized landmasses
* A large atoll-like structure the size of Madagascar, enclosing a vast shallow lagoon
* Numerous volcanic island chains associated with active subduction zones

Several isolated mountains rise over 6 km directly from the ocean floor, producing dramatic, steep-sided peaks rarely found on Earth.

## Tectonics and Seismicity
Nasia is characterized by hyperactive tectonics involving numerous small plates.
Subduction zones are common, leading to:

* Frequent earthquakes
* High volcanic activity
* Rapid orogeny (mountain building)

This geological activity creates rugged, mineral-rich landscapes and supports vigorous erosion and sediment cycles, enhancing soil fertility across coastal and island regions.

A present 25-hour planetary spin uses a weak-dissipation working history (illustrative k₂≈0.3, effective Q of order 10,000 at the relevant reference forcing); lunar torques and changing response still require a coupled tidal evolution model. Short point-mass survival is not a gigayear tidal validation.

## Climate
Nasia exhibits a generally temperate global climate:

* Tropical zones dominated by humid, ocean-moderated weather
* Mid-latitudes supporting broadleaf forests, temperate grasslands, and storm belts
* Polar regions with small but persistent ice caps

The e=0.021 orbit modulates stellar flux. Which hemisphere experiences the stronger seasonal forcing depends on the spin-axis/periapse orientation; southern-ocean patterns are climate geography, not determined by eccentricity alone.

Large oceanic heat capacity and vigorous atmospheric dynamics promote strong monsoons and powerful cyclonic systems, particularly along archipelago coasts.

## Potential for Life and Colonization
Nasia has native oceanic, island and terrestrial ecosystems, with seasonal oxygen/methane variability among its measured signatures. Early remote surveys identified it as a promising colonization target; the adopted present is later, with a human colony established on Nasia at **0.32 AU**.

Near-Earth gravity, oxygen-bearing air, extensive seas/coasts and fertile volcanic/alluvial soils support settlement. Earth-derived colonists and native biology are distinct lineages unless a separate ancestry is established. Compatibility of food, microbes and pathogens is assessed explicitly rather than inferred from green-looking forests or shared atmospheric gases. Earthlike biome labels describe functional analogies.

Seismic activity, monsoons/cyclones, rugged coastal geography and complex tides remain central settlement constraints. The mystery or origin of humans elsewhere in Aquatikiverse is not imported here: these are the human colonization sites referenced in the Dichloran contact history. The earlier target/prospective wording records the survey era, not the present settlement state.

## Physical basis

Nasia's three moons are Stephanie (2.6×10⁻⁴ M⊕, 50,000 km, 20° prograde, 1.263 d), Natia (0.015 M⊕, 150,000 km, 5° prograde, 6.515 d) and Kathy (1.5×10⁻⁵ M⊕, 330,000 km, 175° retrograde, 21.412 d), each starting at e = 0.003. In the 3000-year star–planet–moon screens Kathy is the most sensitive orbit, reaching a maximum sampled osculating e of about 0.307 or 0.444 with significant axis excursions, so it is not a quiet circular or proven long-lived orbit. The present 25-hour spin rests on a weak-dissipation working history (illustrative k₂ ≈ 0.3 and Q of order 10,000), and Natia influences spin-axis precession without being assumed to fix the obliquity for billions of years. The screens are described on [[Altmania System]] under Physical basis, and a short screen does not certify a 3-Gyr moon system.

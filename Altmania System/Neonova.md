**Neonova** is the innermost known planet orbiting the K4V star **Altmania B** in the [[Altmania System]]. It is a high-density super-Earth with a mass of roughly 4 Earth masses and a radius of about 1.5 Earth radii, enveloped by a very thick, neon-dominated atmosphere. The planet is tidally locked, presenting a permanent dayside and nightside, but its 17-atmosphere envelope and active circulation redistribute heat well enough to maintain stable surface liquid water in the form of hot lakes on the dayside and temperate oceans on the nightside.

Neonova is notable for its unusual atmospheric composition and for hosting a primarily aerial biosphere, with most biological productivity occurring in mid-atmospheric “sky oceans” rather than on the surface.

## Discovery and Observation
Neonova was first detected via radial velocity measurements of Altmania B during a survey of nearby K-type dwarfs undertaken by the Advanced Radial Velocity Array (ARVA) and the Centauri Horizon Telescope (CHT). Subsequent transit detections refined its orbital period and radius. Combined spectral, refractive/pressure and in-situ constraints established the noble-gas-rich atmosphere; methane and carbon dioxide have molecular spectral features, marking Neonova as a key target for comparative exoplanetology.

Ongoing spectroscopic campaigns monitor variations in methane and haze opacity, probing both atmospheric dynamics and atmospheric variability, with biological interpretation requiring chemical background models.

## Host Star: Altmania B
Main article: [[Altmania System]].

**Altmania B** is a K4V orange dwarf with a mass of 0.70 M☉, a radius of 0.74 R☉, and a luminosity of 0.20 L☉. Its effective temperature is about 4490 K, and it shares a wide, low-eccentricity binary orbit with its companion star Altmania A at a mean separation of 15 AU. This wide separation allows each star’s planetary system to evolve largely independently; the short reference integrations preserve orbital separation, while gigayear stability remains a separate requirement.

## Orbital and Rotational Characteristics
Neonova orbits Altmania B at a distance of 0.20 AU with an orbital period of 39.1 Earth days. For a 0.70 M☉ star, Kepler’s Third Law gives:

`P ≈ 0.107 years ≈ 39 days`,

consistent with observations. The orbit is nearly circular (eccentricity close to zero), minimizing seasonal variations due to changing star–planet distance.

Tidal forces have synchronized the planet’s rotation with its orbital motion, resulting in a 1:1 spin–orbit resonance. One hemisphere faces the star permanently (the dayside), while the opposite hemisphere remains in eternal darkness (the nightside). Despite this, the thick atmosphere and strong winds significantly reduce the starkness of the day–night temperature contrast.

The incident stellar flux on Neonova is about five times that received by Earth, but high albedo from haze and clouds, combined with atmospheric dynamics, prevents the dayside from becoming completely uninhabitable.

## Physical Characteristics
With a mass of 4.0 M⊕ and radius of 1.5 R⊕, Neonova has:

* Mean density ≈ 6.5 g/cm³
* Surface gravity ≈ 1.78 g⊕ (≈17.4 m/s²)
* Escape velocity ≈ 18.3 km/s

The high density is compatible with a compressed rock/metal interior; it does not uniquely determine an oversized iron core and silicate mantle, making Neonova a compact, metal-rich super-Earth rather than a gas dwarf. The strong gravity and deep potential well facilitate retention of a massive secondary atmosphere even under intense stellar irradiation.

Interior models suggest a differentiated structure with:

* A differentiated metallic core whose fraction requires an interior model.
* A silicate mantle undergoing active convection.
* A thin but mechanically strong crust overlain by thick, chemically altered regolith in contact with hot lakes and nightside oceans.

Residual heat from formation and ongoing radiogenic heating drive moderate but continuous volcanism.

## Atmosphere
Neonova’s atmosphere is exceptionally thick, with a surface pressure of about 17 atm. Its approximate bulk composition is:

* Neon (Ne) – ~60%
* Argon (Ar) – ~15%
* Nitrogen (N₂) – ~10%
* Carbon dioxide (CO₂) – ~5%
* Methane (CH₄) – ~5%
* Water vapor (H₂O) – ~4%
* Trace hydrogen (H₂) and other gases – ~1%

Neon and argon, both chemically inert, act as dense background gases, while CO₂, CH₄, and H₂O provide a strong greenhouse effect. The upper atmosphere hosts ionized neon and argon layers that produce persistent airglow and occasional bright arcs, especially above the terminator and nightside high latitudes.

Photochemistry driven by intense stellar radiation generates organic hazes from methane, forming a thick aerosol layer that:

* Reduces direct insolation on the dayside.
* Enhances radiative trapping and infrared scattering.
* Softens temperature gradients between hemispheres.

Vertical structure is typically divided into:

* **Upper atmosphere** – Ionized neon, auroral emissions, and tenuous haze.
* **Middle atmosphere** – Organic aerosol/water-cloud layers and the main aerial habitat; methane condensates occur only in sufficiently cold upper regions if the profile permits.
* **Lower atmosphere** – Hot, humid, high-pressure troposphere with active convection and hydrological cycles.

## Surface and Hydrosphere
Despite high insolation, Neonova’s surface can sustain liquid water due to the combination of high pressure and strong atmospheric redistribution.

* **Dayside** – Near the substellar point, the surface hosts hot saline lakes and flooded basins. At ≈17 atm, liquid water can persist at temperatures of 380–400 K (about 107–127 °C), though limited to deep basins and regions shaded by haze. Iron-rich rocks weather chemically, feeding dissolved metals into these lakes.
* **Terminator belt** – Between the blazing dayside and the deep nightside, temperatures and pressures allow more extensive surface liquids, with large inland seas and connected lake chains.
* **Nightside** – Cooler conditions (280–300 K) support broad, temperate oceans and large inland seas under a dark sky illuminated by neon airglow. Ice formation is limited to high-latitude or high-altitude regions where radiative cooling outpaces advective heat transport.

Dominant surface features include:

* Iron-rich basins and weathered plains.
* Cratered highlands with sparse hydrological activity.
* Scattered volcanic fields and geothermal zones, especially near the terminator.
* Shorelines and continental margins carved by waves and chemical erosion in nightside oceans.

Volcanism is moderate but steady, replenishing CO₂, CH₄, and trace gases and sustaining redox gradients for chemotrophic life.

## Climate and Atmospheric Circulation
The adopted climate retains hot 380–400-K dayside basins and temperate 280–300-K surface nightside oceans, under strong reflecting clouds/haze. These are setting targets, not a climate-model result. At 5 S⊕ a gray comparison with effective emission temperature 300 K requires Bond albedo about 0.73; actual cloud/greenhouse spectra must close the outgoing energy budget. High pressure and winds redistribute heat rather than eliminate absorbed energy.

A broad day–night overturning cell operates alongside recirculating jets and vertical motions. The aerial ecological reference follows closed or recurrent trajectories through daylight, terminator and dark-side layers, with altitude/latitude adjustment and habitat retention. A 23–26-day circuit around the 1.5-R⊕ globe corresponds to roughly 27–30 m/s mean progress. A single overturning cell does not guarantee that every organism circles at one altitude or follows the same period.

Sky life alternates active light-harvesting phases, cooler transit, low-energy survival and reactivation. Water retention, nutrient resupply, rainout and reproduction must work throughout those trajectories. A dark/cold cruising layer is distinct from the warmer surface ocean beneath it. The cycle is wind-borne, not rotation of the surface into and out of stellar daylight.

## Biosphere
Main article: [[Life on Neonova]].

Neonova supports a thriving, primarily aerial biosphere embedded within its middle atmosphere. The key features of this biosphere are:

* **Primary metabolism** – Hydrogen–methane chemotrophy and iron respiration:
  * Zeppelophyte autotrophs reduce CO₂ with H₂:

    `4H₂ + CO₂ → CH₄ + 2H₂O`
  * Ferrivores oxidize CH₄ using ferric iron (Fe³⁺), returning CO₂ to the atmosphere and reducing Fe³⁺ to Fe²⁺.
* **Solvent** – Liquid H₂O under high pressure is the main solvent, present in hot dayside lakes, cooler nightside oceans, and humid lower atmospheric layers.
* **Aerial ecosystems** – Gas-buoyant Zeppelophytes dominate primary production in the middle atmosphere, accompanied by secondary consumers such as Aetherblooms and a suite of predatory and scavenging floaters (Cloud Skimmers, Sky Reapers, Crimson Titans, Thermovores).
* **Surface ecosystems** – Ground-based Ferrivores, Methanivores, and Rustworms recycle organic matter in iron-rich lakes, saline basins, and nightside ocean floors, closing methane–carbon–iron cycles and sustaining long-term chemical balance.

Most evolutionary innovation occurs in the aerial zones near the substellar region, where light and free hydrogen are abundant but competition and predation are intense. By contrast, ground ecosystems are chemically vital but evolutionarily conservative.

## Role in the Altmania System
Within the broader [[Altmania System]], Neonova is the innermost of the three known planets orbiting Altmania B, analogous in orbital placement to Mercury but with a far denser atmosphere and active hydrosphere. Its neon-rich skies and aerial biosphere provide a stark contrast to the sulfuric world [[Thionis]] and the more Earthlike [[Nasia]] around Altmania A.

For interstellar observers, Neonova is one of the most conspicuous worlds in the system, combining model-dependent transit features, unusual atmospheric composition, and time-variable methane and haze signatures that hint at active, planet-wide biological cycles.

## Buoyancy and chemical replenishment

The seven listed atmospheric fractions sum to 100%, but their 4% water entry is a **local hot/wet reference**, not a constant abundance over cool oceans or cruising levels. Condensation sets the local water fraction. Noble-gas airglow requires excitation by particles or photons; neon does not glow merely because it is abundant. A 17-atm surface does not establish the pressure at all aerial habitats.

Zeppelophytes and other floaters use **pressure-balanced H₂-rich lift chambers**. At equal pressure/temperature the listed ambient mixture has approximate molar mass 24.64 g/mol: pure H₂ offers large lift, Ne a smaller margin, and Ar is denser than the background. Noble gases serve damping, ballast/control or other nonlifting roles; flexible sacs are not held below ambient pressure without load-bearing structure. Armor and tissue mass, gas leakage, water/nutrient retention and night-phase survival are included in the required lift budget.

The methane–carbon loop consumes H₂ and ferric acceptors. A working ecology includes geological/hydrothermal H₂ sources and an explicitly **stellar-energy-powered Fe(II)-to-Fe(III) regeneration guild** at illuminated mineral interfaces, with water/proton/electron handling and vertical particle/fluid transport. This is a speculative anoxygenic photoferrotrophic design; its actual donor/acceptor yields and nitrate/sulfate side pathways remain unquantified. Rustworms and Ferrivores recycle and transport material but do not recharge the entire cycle without energy. The Great Methane Event and 1.2-Gyr native history remain; there is no Neonovan civilization or human colony assigned here.


## Physical basis

Neonova is 4 M⊕ and 1.5 R⊕ with a 17-atm atmosphere. The seven gas fractions are a local hot and wet reference, not a globally uniform 4% water. H₂-rich pressure-balanced lift sacs provide the main lift; noble gases serve damping and control roles, with Ne giving modest lift at equal temperature and Ar negative lift in the listed mixture. A 23–26-day recirculating jet and vertical trajectory supports full life cycles. Geothermal H₂ and a speculative illuminated anoxygenic Fe(II)-to-Fe(III) regeneration guild power the net redox renewal, and the yields, water, carbon and iron transport and oxygen exclusion are unmodeled. The 5-S⊕ climate requires reflecting clouds and haze and an outgoing-energy model; 17 atm is not itself a cooling mechanism.

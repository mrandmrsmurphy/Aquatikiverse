Main article: [[Laputa]]

**Life on Laputa** consists of a simple but chemically exotic biosphere dominated by microbial autotrophs. The biosphere is in a prolonged oxidation transition in its crustal sinks and regional redox fronts, with atmospheric oxygen already at levels suitable for human respiration. Primary production dominates. Small decomposer communities recycle organic material, but there is no macroscopic grazing or predatory food web.

## Biochemistry

Laputan life is carbon-based and uses water as a solvent, though water availability is severely limited. The chemistry of life is modified by Laputa’s enriched crust and atmosphere:

- **Redox-active rare earths** such as Ce³⁺/Ce⁴⁺ and Nd²⁺/Nd³⁺ form alternative electron transport chains
- **Cerium-bound phospholipids** stabilize cell membranes against oxidative stress and metallic ion flux
- **Selenium metabolism** substitutes for sulfur pathways in anoxic niches, with selenium oxyanions acting as electron acceptors
- Pigmentation is optimized for the spectral output of the K0V star (~550 nm), with light-harvesting molecules absorbing in the 550–750 nm range

Despite the unusual metal chemistry, core biochemistry uses familiar CHNOPS scaffolds with extensive rare-earth coordination, especially in membrane and pigment complexes.

## Primary Clades


### Crystalplasts

- Core Metabolism: `CO₂ + H₂O → CH₂O + O₂`

Photosynthetic microbial mats using CO₂ and H₂O to produce O₂ and organics. These organisms form structured colonies that incorporate crystalline scaffolds, diffraction surfaces, and magneto-responsive pigments. They are responsible for the ongoing oxygenation of the atmosphere. Typical morphologies include aperiodic laminae and fractal fronds. Crystalplasts often appear silvery-green or purple-lavender due to rare-earth pigment scattering.

### Redoxites

- Core Metabolism:
  - `Ce³⁺ → Ce⁴⁺ + e⁻`
  - `Nd²⁺ ↔ Nd³⁺` electron transfers

Non-oxygenic chemotrophs that exploit the Ce and Nd redox couples. These organisms colonize mineral interfaces, brine pools, and crustal fractures. They play a major role in mineral cycling and phosphate precipitation, laying down phosphate-rich crusts of thorium and rare earths. Colonies are deeply colored (blue-violet, ochre) due to pigment–metal complexes and dense metal coordination sites.

### Selenobiontes

- Core Metabolism: `SeO₄²⁻ + organics → Se⁰ + CO₂` (Anaerobic or microaerobic respiration of Se compounds)

Anaerobic chemoheterotrophs that use organic substrates and selenium oxyanions as electron acceptors. Typically found in wet clays, deep salt vaults, or ephemeral brine lenses, they contribute to soil conditioning and the rare organic decomposition processes on Laputa. Their colonies can appear glossy black, red-orange, or metallic gray depending on elemental selenium content and local redox state.

Overall, these clades generate poorly developed soils that are rich in phosphates and lanthanide dust. Ecological structure is minimal: no macroscopic food webs, no predation, and only a limited decay cascade. The land is largely free of macroscopic biological hazards for colonists, but aerosolized metals and dust mean that basic respiratory filtration and surface protection are recommended in many regions.

## Ecosystem Structure

Life is found primarily:

- In microbial mats along brine lake margins
- On salt flat surfaces during seasonal wetting
- In biofilms lining the vertical faces of crystal mountains
- Subsurface at hydrothermal seeps and fumarole edges
- In magnetic null basin fields where aerosols and salts collect under relatively benign field conditions

There is no macroscopic predation or extensive heterotrophic web; Selenobiontes support a limited microbial decay cascade. The ecosystem is stable, slow-evolving, and spatially patchy. Colonists introduced agriculture only in null magnetic zones, and native life is largely noncompetitive with human crops, though biosecurity protocols limit cross-contamination between Earth crops and Laputan microbial communities.

## Atmospheric Effects

The reference gas fractions are 49% Ne, 21% O₂, 24.9% N₂, 5% Ar, 0.04% CO₂, and 0.06% other trace gases, totaling 100%. Water vapor varies locally and displaces a small proportion of this dry reference mixture. At 0.90 atm the oxygen partial pressure is 0.189 atm. Clean low-aerosol air supports unaided breathing for acclimatized healthy colonists; dust events and contaminated basins require particulate filtration. Breathability does not imply that every region is free of inhalation hazards.

Crystalplasts have oxygenated the atmosphere to Earth-like levels and continue to maintain and modulate free O₂. The interaction between phototrophic mats and aerosol-rich winds creates seasonal and regional oxygen variations superimposed on a high baseline. Rare-earth aerosols and ionized metal species interact with this oxygen-rich background to produce complex aurorae, photochemical hazes, and localized oxidative stress zones.

Colonists rely on the native biosphere’s oxidative buffering and trace element cycling for sustainable surface living, but also monitor atmospheric composition for shifts in aerosol load, selenium speciation, and rare-earth particulate density.

## Notes

Laputa represents a rare example of a world caught during a prolonged oxidation transition: orbital and atmospheric conditions are already hospitable to humans, yet the biosphere remains microbially simple. The presence of multiple redox systems, low water availability, and exotic salts makes its biosphere a key subject of study in comparative exobiology and in the co-evolution of atmospheres and low-complexity ecosystems.

## Model status

The selenium endpoint is Se⁰, matching the detailed metabolic description. Selenobiontes are a small chemoheterotrophic decomposer community, compatible with the absence of macroscopic grazing or predation. The atmospheric composition follows [[Laputa]] and totals 100% on a dry reference basis. The <10% liquid surface coverage is distinct from a water inventory ~10% of Earth's, and neither is substituted for the other. The stellar spectral reference (~550 nm) is distinct from the phototroph absorption peak (~610 nm). The ongoing oxidation transition refers to remaining crustal sinks and regional redox fronts despite a high atmospheric oxygen baseline. The biosphere is ~1.2 Gyr old, and human agriculture is a later introduced ecosystem.

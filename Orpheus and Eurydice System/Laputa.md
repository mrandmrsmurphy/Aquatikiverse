**Laputa** is the middle planet of the Orpheus–Eurydice system, located at 0.63 AU from the [[Orpheus and Eurydice|K0V primary]]. The planet is notable for its quasicrystalline crust, exceptionally powerful magnetic field (>1 Tesla at surface), and extensive, structured salt flats surrounding brine pools and rare levitating mountains. It is one of the few worlds where humans can breathe unaided despite its profoundly exotic geochemistry.

## Overview

Laputa formed from a chemically enriched protoplanetary disc around Orpheus. A post-supernova origin for that material is the leading in-universe hypothesis. It inherited a distinctive composition dominated by rare earth elements (particularly Ce, Nd, and Gd), with most actinides retained at depth and localized uranium-bearing minerals exposed at redox fronts; any plutonium is associated with ongoing nuclear production rather than assumed primordial survival. The core features highly conductive, convective iron–neodymium alloys responsible for the persistent and complex magnetic field.

Laputa's surface is defined by low water abundance (~10% of Earth), massive salt flats composed of stable exotic salts, and widespread crystal mountain ranges—some levitating due to magnetic repulsion. Aurorae, brine lakes, and photonic haze from metal aerosols create a luminous, almost hallucinogenic landscape.

## Atmosphere

Laputa's atmosphere is unique among known habitable planets. Instead of nitrogen dominance, it uses neon (49%) as its primary buffer gas, making it breathable but acoustically strange. The atmosphere includes:

- ~21% oxygen (O₂)
- ~24.9% nitrogen (N₂)
- ~5% argon (Ar)
- ~0.04% carbon dioxide; the remaining ~0.06% is other trace gases in the dry reference composition; humidity is variable
- ~0.04% CO₂ (~0.00036 atm at 0.90 atm total pressure)
- trace water vapor and aerosols of Nd₂O₃, Gd₂O₃, LaCl₃, NdF₃
- ppm-to-ppb levels of rare earth aerosols (CeO₂, Nd₂O₃, Se compounds)

The reference gas fractions are 49% Ne, 21% O₂, 24.9% N₂, 5% Ar, 0.04% CO₂, and 0.06% other trace gases, totaling 100%. Water vapor varies locally and displaces a small proportion of this dry reference mixture. At 0.90 atm the oxygen partial pressure is 0.189 atm. Clean low-aerosol air supports unaided breathing for acclimatized healthy colonists; dust events and contaminated basins require particulate filtration. Breathability does not imply that every region is free of inhalation hazards. Neon changes sound propagation; combustion depends on local oxygen availability, pressure, fuel, and heat transport. Trace aerosols contribute to aurorae, optical haze, and crystal growth facilitation, but also UV absorption enhanced by these lanthanide aerosols. Humans must be careful of cold traps, where metal halides and rare oxides collect. Voices carry strangely, and the sky glows with metallic aurorae.

## Geology and Surface Features

Digging is dangerous because of the ubiquitous presence of Uranium. UO₂ is stable under reducing conditions. In an oxidizing mantle or at redox fronts, it can oxidize further to UO₃, (UO₂)²⁺, or other uranyl species, consuming oxidizing equivalents in the process; the detailed products depend on the fluid chemistry. UO₂ may also act as a sink for oxygen in some conditions.

### Salt Flats and Brines

Laputa’s most widespread landforms are its vivid salt flats. These are composed of exotic salts whose exposure hazards vary by compound such as:

- Neodymium fluorophosphate (NdPO₄F)
- Cerium borosilicate
- Thorium orthophosphate (ThPO₄)
- Strontium selenite (SrSeO₃)
- Barium neodymate

These flats surround hyper-saline brine lakes colored by lanthanide oxides and microbial mats. Salt crusts form roughly hexagonal crack networks over a quasicrystalline substrate; the large-scale crack pattern and the substrate’s aperiodic order are distinct structures.

### Crystal Mountains

Laputan mountains fall into two classes:

- **Anchored ranges** — built from ferroquasicrystal skeletons, Ce/Nd feldspathoids, and rigid phosphate ribs; extremely hard, chemically resistant, interfering with phonon and magnon propagation
- **Levitating ranges** — supported by ferroperovskite scaffolds aligned with magnetic field anomalies; porous, partially hollow, magnetically stable. Stabilized by field gradients from buried subsurface closed-field, monopole-like local patterns (not isolated magnetic monopoles) and ancient lattice cores.

The combination of magnetic stress, aperiodic crystalline growth, and tectonic shearing yields vertical landscapes of extraordinary angularity and optical properties.

## Magnetism

Laputa’s global magnetic field exceeds 1 Tesla at the surface and is structured into octant-like magnetic domains. Some domain boundaries contain persistent low-field corridors where appropriately designed electronics can operate with much less magnetic shielding; boundary location and stability are monitored. Colonists cluster along these boundary "dead zones" for infrastructure and data processing, though they do migrate with time.

As an explicit speculative property of the setting, this unusually strong field and the material response supporting floating mountains exceed what is established here from conventional dynamo and magnetic-levitation physics. The setting adopts them without claiming a complete physical derivation.

The field drives aurorae, brine motion, weather dynamics, and mineral segregation. It is generated by a convective neodymium–iron–thorium alloy core, aided by non-Newtonian and metallic hydrogen-like behaviors in exotic compounds, energized by moderate radiogenic heating (~5× Earth). Neodymium, samarium, gadolinium, and other lanthanides exhibit high magnetic susceptibility and magnetocaloric effects.

Both in the ionosphere and at magnetic poles there are high densities of charged aerosols and ionized metals. Intense aurorae are induced by the strong magnetosphere, which are multicolored due to ionized rare earths (e.g., blue/green from cerium, violet from gadolinium).

## The Harmonids

Laputa is orbited by a rare binary moon system known as **The Harmonids**. This pair of natural satellites—**Ferroa** and **Halitrax**—orbit a common barycenter, which itself orbits Laputa at a nearly circular orbital semi-major axis of approximately 110,000 km from Laputa’s center. The adopted configuration is nearly circular, prograde, and close to coplanar, with the moons synchronously rotating relative to their mutual orbit. It is dynamically plausible under the first-order checks recorded below and visually distinctive, forming a paired celestial dance in the Laputan sky.

**The Harmonids: Binary Moon Parameters**

| Name | Radius | Mass (Earth = 1) | Composition | Orbital Features |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ferroa | ~2,500 km | 0.020 | Low-density porous/volatile-rich bulk with a small ferrocrystalline Nd–Fe–Gd core | Induced magnetic field; metallic crust |
| Halitrax | ~2,200 km | 0.015 | Low-density porous/volatile-rich bulk with rare-earth silicate and salt crust | Electrostatic activity; selenite and borate crusts |

The two moons orbit each other at a center-to-center semi-major axis of ~7,000 km with a mutual period of ~8.65 hours. Their shared barycenter completes one orbit of Laputa every ~3.10 Earth days. The constant gravitational and electromagnetic interaction between the moons results in measurable effects on Laputa’s ionosphere, brine lake tides, and auroral patterns.

Ferroa exhibits weak magnetic field induction from Laputa’s global field and contributes to magnetic interference patterns in the upper atmosphere. Halitrax, by contrast, is electrically inert but rich in electroactive salts, and may discharge static potentials during magnetospheric storms.

To the unaided eye, the Harmonids appear as two large, resolved moon discs, approximately 2.6° and 2.3° across at their nominal distance, often passing before or behind each other. Mutual eclipses, rare syzygies, and color-shifting diffraction from their surfaces are among the most celebrated astronomical phenomena observed from Laputa’s surface.

The Harmonids play a central role in Laputan colonial navigation, calendar systems, and symbolic astronomy. Their orbital dynamics and field modulation patterns are actively studied for long-range weather and magnetosphere modeling.

## Biosphere

Main article: [[Life on Laputa]]

Laputa hosts an oxygenic but otherwise simple biosphere dominated by:

- **Crystalplasts** — photosynthetic microbial mats using chlorophyll analogues and crystalline light-harvesting structures
- **Redoxites** — Ce/Nd redox chemotrophs
- **Selenobiontes** — anaerobic selenium metabolizers

The native biosphere has no macroscopic heterotrophs or predators; the introduced human agricultural ecosystem is separate. The ecosystem is structurally microbial, with widespread colonization of brine margins, salt crusts, and hydrothermal seams.

## Human Colonization

Human colonists arrived in the 24th century using continuous-thrust propulsion. Laputa required minimal terraforming: its oxygen levels were already rising due to natural biogenic activity (akin to Earth's Great Oxidation Event). Colonists inhabit magnetic null zones and adapt to unusual acoustics, aerosol filtration, and architectural challenges posed by the magnetic environment.

Architecture incorporates ferrocrystalline materials and aperiodic geometry to resist resonance and optimize space within magnetically complex terrain. Laputan architecture encodes quasiperiodic symbology—e.g., 5-fold motifs representing the five cardinal field directions (N, S, Radial+, Radial−, Null). Habitation zones resemble aperiodic mazes, which is both protective and ceremonial. Technology relies heavily on photonics, spintronics, and field-aligned computing. In highly magnetic areas, computing uses field-tolerant or shielded systems, including suitable magnonic processors, mechanical mechanisms, and optical crystals. Magnonics and spintronics are magnetic technologies, not automatically immune alternatives to electronics.

## Physical basis

**Orbit and atmosphere.** An orbit of 0.63 AU, a period of ~205 days and ~1.01 Earth flux are mutually consistent with Orpheus's 0.79 M☉ and 0.40 L☉. The dry atmosphere sums to 100%: 49% Ne, 21% O₂, 24.9% N₂, 5% Ar, 0.04% CO₂ and 0.06% other trace gases, which gives an oxygen partial pressure of 0.189 atm at 0.90 atm total pressure. Clean-air breathability is distinct from particulate exposure; [NASA's atmospheric environment guidance](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/6-0-natural-and-induced-environments-vol-2/) separates oxygen supply from contaminant control.

**The Harmonids.** The moons have masses of 0.020 and 0.015 Earth masses, mean radii of 2,500 and 2,200 km and a 110,000-km barycentric orbit about Laputa. Their bulk densities are ~1.83 and ~2.01 g/cm³: enriched crusts and small metallic cores sit within lower-density, volatile-bearing interiors, so the moons are not entirely metallic. Their orbits are nearly circular, coplanar and prograde with mutually synchronous spins. [Eggleton's volume-equivalent Roche-lobe approximation](https://articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1983ApJ...268..368E) gives a limiting center separation of ~6,210 km for the stated volume radii, so the adopted **7,000 km** is a modest margin above that first-order limit (a 5,000-km separation would lie inside it). At 7,000 km the volume-equivalent Roche lobes are ~2,828 and ~2,480 km, exceeding the mean radii; tidal shapes are expected, and perfectly spherical surfaces are not assumed. The mutual period is **8.65 hours** and the pair's orbital period around Laputa is **3.10 Earth days**. The mutual Hill scale at 110,000 km is ~20,510 km and the adopted separation is ~0.34 of that, within the ~0.5-Hill scale often used for prograde binary survival ([three-body Hill-stability research](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/466/1/276/2617724)). These are consistency checks for a plausible working configuration, not a full tidal or N-body proof of gigayear survival or an exact mathematical minimum; the setting adopts the configuration as sufficiently stable for its present narrative.

**Geochemistry and climate.** Uranium oxidation consumes oxygen; it does not release it. Salt exposure hazards are compound-dependent. The surface temperature is a climate-model assumption, not an airless equilibrium temperature, and the ~9-km atmospheric scale height is representative and varies with composition and temperature.

**Speculative physics.** The >1-tesla field, quasicrystalline magnetic materials and levitating mountains are speculative premises, not claimed to follow quantitatively from ordinary neodymium magnetism alone. Low-field habitation corridors reduce engineering constraints but do not guarantee universal equipment compatibility or complete radiation protection. "Null" is one of five cultural navigation categories, not a fifth spatial axis. The rounded planetary figure is an adopted structural model, not derived solely from rotation.

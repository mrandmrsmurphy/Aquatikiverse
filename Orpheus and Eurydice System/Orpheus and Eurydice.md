**Orpheus and Eurydice** is a hierarchical stellar remnant system consisting of a K0V main-sequence star proposed to be second-generation (**Orpheus**) and a wide-orbit neutron star (**Eurydice**) associated with a proposed atypical post-supernova evolutionary pathway. The system is notable for the hypothesis that Orpheus formed from fallback and companion-disruption debris following a core-collapse event, with a massive, inert neutron star retained as a distant companion. Three planets—[[Alchemis]], [[Laputa]], and [[Dysmorph]]—orbit Orpheus and exhibit extreme chemical enrichment from the system’s violent origins.

## Overview

The present system is a binary: Orpheus and Eurydice. Its heavy-element abundances and neutron-star companion motivate a proposed history involving supernova ejecta, stellar interaction, and an enriched planet-forming disc. Orpheus’s formation from fallback and disrupted-companion material is an uncertain reconstruction, not a directly observed sequence. The original number and fates of progenitors, mass-transfer episodes, and neutron-star kick are not established.

Heavy-element enrichment describes relative metal abundances, not a bulk composition dominated by metals. Orpheus remains a hydrogen-burning star.

## Orpheus (K0V Primary)

Orpheus is a stable, metal-rich K-dwarf with a luminosity of ~0.40 L☉ and an effective temperature near 5250 K. Its spectrum shows extreme heavy-element enrichment consistent with fallback accretion. Despite its unusual formation pathway, Orpheus is modeled as a hydrogen-burning K0V main-sequence star with an estimated lifespan of ~25 Gyr.

Its habitable zone lies between roughly 0.62 and 1.07 AU, encompassing the planet [[Laputa]].

## Eurydice (Neutron Star Companion)

Eurydice is a neutron star without detected coherent pulsations, hypothesized to have undergone recycling located ~20–40 AU from Orpheus. Its spin period is estimated to exceed 20 ms; the recycling and spin-down history remain uncertain. A lack of detected pulses does not establish the absence of all radiation. Its residual magnetic field (~10⁸ G) and diminishing high-energy output are detectable only through sensitive instrumentation.

Although gravitationally quiet at planetary distances, Eurydice contributes subtly to long-period perturbations in the outer system and may produce rare microlensing events.

The three planets subsequently developed distinct environments: photonic-redox life on Alchemis, a microbial water-based biosphere and later human colony on Laputa, and an inorganic halide biosphere on Dysmorph. Generation labels are avoided because the preceding stellar and planetary generations have not been established.

## Planetary System
Main article: [[Orpheus and Eurydice System]]

Three planets orbit Orpheus:

- **[[Alchemis]]** — an inner, high-gravity catalytic world dominated by platinum-group chemistry and photonic automata.
- **[[Laputa]]** — a habitable-zone, highly magnetic, quasicrystalline world colonized by humans.
- **[[Dysmorph]]** — a dense, gravitationally irregular outer planet heated by actinide decay.

All three worlds are enriched with heavy elements uncommon in typical proto-planetary disks, consistent with their supernova-derived origin.

## Scientific Significance

The system motivates study of chemically enriched planetary environments, possible post-supernova fallback formation, and a wide neutron-star companion. These are model-dependent interpretations, not proof of a unique formation sequence. Laputa’s colonized water-based biosphere and the inorganic biospheres on Alchemis and Dysmorph provide distinct settings for comparative planetology.

## Physical basis

The adopted 0.79 M☉, 5250 K and 0.40 L☉ agree with a radius of about 0.765 R☉ (L = R²(T/5772 K)⁴). Formation and recycling histories are explicitly uncertain. The system has two current stellar objects and three planets, and the 20–40 AU companion distance is an observational estimate, not a specified periastron or apastron range.

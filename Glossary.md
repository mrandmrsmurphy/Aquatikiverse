# Glossary

Terms, symbols and conventions used across the pages. For fuller treatments see the [[Encyclopedia]]. Return to [[Home]].

## Units and symbols

- **M⊕, R⊕**: Earth mass and Earth mean radius. Planets, moons and dwarf planets are given in these.
- **M☉, R☉, L☉**: solar mass, radius and luminosity. Stars and brown dwarfs are given in these.
- **AU**: astronomical unit, the mean Earth–Sun distance.
- **ly, pc**: light-year and parsec; 1 pc is about 3.26 ly.
- **F⊕ (or S⊕)**: stellar flux received by Earth; a world at 1 F⊕ receives as much light as Earth.
- **g/cm³**: density; Earth's mean density is about 5.51 g/cm³.
- **K**: kelvin, the temperature unit used on every page.
- **bar**: pressure unit; 1 atm is about 1.013 bar.
- **Myr, Gyr**: million and billion years.

## Orbits and planets

- **Semimajor axis**: half the long axis of an orbit; the orbit's size.
- **Eccentricity**: how stretched an orbit is, from 0 (circle) toward 1.
- **Obliquity**: the tilt of a world's spin axis relative to its orbit; it drives seasons.
- **Tidally locked**: rotating once per orbit, so one face always points at the host.
- **Hill radius**: the distance within which a body's own gravity dominates over its host's; the outer limit for stable moons.
- **Roche limit**: the distance inside which tides tear apart a body held together only by its own gravity.
- **Escape speed**: the speed needed to leave a world's gravity.
- **Circumbinary**: orbiting two stars together rather than one.
- **Co-orbital (Trojan)**: sharing one orbit with another body, near a stable point 60° ahead or behind.
- **Tidal heating**: internal heat made by tidal flexing of a world on an eccentric or driven orbit.

## Climate

- **Bond albedo**: the fraction of all incoming starlight a world reflects.
- **Equilibrium temperature**: the temperature of a gray body in balance with incoming light, with no greenhouse effect.
- **Mean surface temperature**: the adopted average temperature at the surface, including greenhouse warming. Every `temperature_mean_k` property means this.
- **Habitable zone**: the range of orbital distances where a world could hold liquid water at its surface, given a suitable atmosphere.

## Stars

- **Spectral type**: the letter-and-number class of a star (O, B, A, F, G, K, M, hottest to coolest), with a luminosity class such as V for main sequence.
- **Metallicity ([Fe/H])**: a star's iron abundance relative to the Sun, in dex.
- **Brown dwarf**: an object between planet and star in mass, too light to sustain hydrogen fusion.

## Chemistry and biology

- **Solvent**: the liquid in which a cell's chemistry runs; Earth's is water, others here include ammonia–water, liquid sulfur dioxide and liquid halides.
- **Redox**: chemistry in which electrons move from a donor to an acceptor; the energy released is what life harvests.
- **Redox couple**: a donor and acceptor pair, such as hydrogen and oxygen.
- **Primary metabolism**: the main way a biosphere gets energy.
- **Phototroph, chemotroph**: an organism that gets energy from light, or from chemical reactions.
- **Backbone**: the element whose chains build the large molecules of an organism (carbon on Earth).
- **Biosphere**: all the life of a world and the environments it occupies.

## Conventions on world pages

- **Adopted value**: the number the page uses, chosen from the calculation or from a deliberate speculative choice.
- **Physical basis**: the section giving the calculation behind a page's numbers.
- **Model status**: the section stating which parts of a page are calculated and which are speculation.
- **Properties**: the frontmatter at the top of each page; the same quantities in common units so worlds can be compared.

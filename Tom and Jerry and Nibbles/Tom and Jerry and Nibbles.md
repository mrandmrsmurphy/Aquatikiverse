# Tom and Jerry and Nibbles

![[images/Black_hole's_accretion_disk.jpg]]

*Accretion structure schematic; body sizes, separations, spectrum, and apparent brightness are illustrative.*

**Tom and Jerry and Nibbles** is a fictional black-hole planetary system in **DGS-17**, the remnant nucleus of a disrupted dwarf galaxy. **[[Tom (black hole)|Tom]]**, a roughly five-million-solar-mass Kerr black hole, hosts **[[Jerry (gas giant)|Jerry]]**, a 12-Jupiter-mass planet with rings and surviving moons. **[[Nibbles (habitable moon)|Nibbles]]** is a primordial rocky companion with an ancient biosphere and the sapience-emergent **[[Pedonpods]]**.

Nibbles’ habitability governs the revised architecture. The original eight-hour orbit, 1.65-hour moon orbit, and extreme proximity were superseded because the rocky moon did not fit between disruption and stable satellite boundaries. The current wider layout supplies geometric margin and a bolometric heat budget, while retaining a low-visible-light, aurora-influenced, nitrogen-redox ecology. A physical accretion spectrum and quantitative radiation, climate, and tidal models remain necessary.

## DGS-17 and history

DGS-17, “Dynamical Ghost Satellite 17,” is a compact spheroidal remnant only tens of parsecs across. Its ancient dense nucleus and enriched stellar populations preserve the history of a dwarf galaxy stripped by a larger host. Little current star formation or gas remains. The present stellar density profile and any residual dark-matter envelope are unquantified. The remnant follows a working eccentric, retrograde galactic orbit; the parent galaxy and full space motion are not assigned.

Tom’s proposed history starts with a massive stellar seed in a dense protocluster, followed by gas accretion, stellar feeding, and mergers. These processes can grow a black hole; the precise route to its current mass and assumed high spin is not uniquely established. Ancient activity episodes differ from the present low-Eddington state.

Jerry formed in a K-dwarf binary environment, originally near a roughly 3 AU planetary orbit. **Nibbles was already its moon**, and life originated beneath that moon’s ice. The binary geometry and original planetary stability need a formation model.

Later multibody encounters and orbital energy loss transferred Jerry’s subsystem to Tom. A passing molecular cloud can be a precursor perturbation, but is not the capture mechanism by itself. The parent K-dwarf was subsequently disrupted after Jerry and its surviving moons detached; they are not assumed to have passed together through a moon-stripping stellar-disruption pericenter. Capture, settling, retained moon orbits, and thermal consequences remain research questions.

The biosphere is **older than two billion years** in total. Time spent in the present black-hole environment is not specified. Later warming and increased external energy exposed surface habitats and promoted new ecological radiations, without requiring all ancient life or Pedonpod evolution to have occurred in the present accretion state.

## Tom

*Main article: [[Tom (black hole)]]*

Tom’s mass is approximately 5×10⁶ M☉, with provisional spin a* ≈0.98–0.998. Its nonrotating Schwarzschild radius is approximately 0.0987 AU; the assumed Kerr coordinate horizon is approximately 0.0525–0.0592 AU. At the revised planetary distance, the nonrotating lensing-shadow diameter is about 0.59°, distinct from the horizon and from the visible accretion structure.

The adopted bolometric luminosity is 2,000 L☉. This is very faint compared with Tom’s Eddington luminosity but supersedes the former subsolar power budget. It supplies approximately 0.80 Earth flux at 50 AU in an isotropic reference. A hot radiatively inefficient flow is a candidate accretion regime; any cooler component, optical/infrared emission, corona, and reprocessing need a consistent spectral model. [Accretion-flow framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0586).

Low-power polar jets, weak radio/IR continuum, and episodic flares remain. Their orientation and energy spectrum must avoid destructive exposure of inhabited regions. Tom does not possess a material magnetic surface: external fields arise from accretion plasma and associated currents. Planetary orbits lie outside dense material, with any diffuse plasma interactions modeled separately.

Nibbles’ habitability requires useful absorbed heating and tolerable surface particle/ionizing dose simultaneously. The total luminosity alone establishes neither the spectral split nor radiation safety. Dim visible illumination is a spectral design target, not a consequence of low Eddington ratio by itself.

## Jerry

*Main article: [[Jerry (gas giant)]]*

Jerry retains its pale, high-albedo “wam Jupiter” character, H/He envelope with nitrogen, ammonia, methane, sulfur chemistry and hazes, a proposed approximately 27 M⊕ heavy-element core, and a deep metallic-hydrogen region. The adopted mass and radius imply density near 11.2 g/cm³, gravity near 246 m/s², and escape speed near 197 km/s.

The planet’s eight-hour spin is no longer locked to Tom. Its 50 AU orbital distance leaves a Hill radius of approximately **6.84 million km**. The conventional circular coplanar prograde satellite boundary is roughly **3.35 million km**, before additional perturbations and relativistic corrections. These are screening estimates, not survival guarantees. [Satellite stability study](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2966.2006.11104.x).

The approximately 3 mT equatorial cloud-top field supports a candidate auroral/magnetospheric environment. An ideal dipole would give about 1.5 μT at Nibbles’ axis, but pressure balance and plasma currents determine actual coverage and precipitation. Strong fields can divert particles and trap them; protective and hazardous effects must be calculated separately.

Nibbles is the only substantial moon with a specified orbit. Other primordial survivors, captured irregulars, and a retrograde fragment remain; the source’s ≥10-major-moon census is provisional rather than silently replaced with a smaller count. Their sizes, masses, orbits, and mutual stability are still unknown.

The three rings retain their origins and identities: a dense equatorial A belt, charged gossamer B dust, and inclined C debris from **Thyre**. Warm exposed ice cannot be assumed primordial and permanent, so the working rings are rock/dust dominated with transient or shielded ice. Inclined debris precesses, warps, and requires a finite age or continued input. The detailed page supplies provisional radial zones inside Nibbles’ orbit.

## Nibbles

*Main article: [[Nibbles (habitable moon)]]*

The 0.4 M⊕, 0.7 R⊕ moon has density about 6.43 g/cm³, gravity about 8.0 m/s², and escape speed about 8.46 km/s. At a one-million-km orbit, it is beyond the approximately **231,000 km fluid Roche estimate** and at approximately **0.146 of Jerry’s Hill radius**. Its synchronous period is near 44.76 hours. Jerry’s own faster spin favors outward planetary tidal torque in the present prograde configuration; dissipation rates and long-term evolution remain unmodeled.

**Jerry is fixed in Nibbles’ sky. Tom moves.** The direct illumination cycle is about 46.26 hours, with eclipse geometry, source variability, and orbital perturbations superimposed. Jerry spans approximately **9°** from the moon’s center. It remains visually enormous, with changing phase, rotating bands, ring aspect, and auroral structure; it is not a permanent full, reflected lantern.

For the circular coplanar point-source reference, eclipses remove approximately 2.5% of direct irradiation and can last roughly 1.2 hours centrally. An extended emitting disk, inclination, and atmospheric geometry alter those figures.

### Thermal and radiation budget

An isotropic 2,000 L☉ source at 50 AU supplies approximately **1,089 W/m²** incident bolometric flux. Taking Bond albedo **0.30** and the approximate eclipse duty gives about **186 W/m² globally averaged absorbed input**, or an effective radiative temperature near **239 K**. A roughly **35–45 K greenhouse** supports a target of **275–285 K** in inhabited regions. This supplies an energy-consistent target; spectrum-dependent climate simulation is still required.

Jerry’s intrinsic emission contributes only about **0.175 W/m² normal incident flux** at the moon axis, before local absorption and geometry. It cannot explain a strong whole-hemisphere temperature contrast. The retained near-side temperate plains and seas and far-side cold provinces instead depend on topography, ocean/atmosphere circulation, albedo, geothermal history, and ice feedbacks. Both sides have direct Tom day/night cycles; no permanently dark far hemisphere is assumed.

Radiogenic/geothermal heat, residual tides, and atmospheric greenhouse feedbacks affect climate. Tides require a rheological model rather than a declaration of negligible heating. A simple diagnostic at k₂/Q =0.003 gives approximately **0.30 W/m² at e=0.001** and **7.5 W/m² at e=0.005**; changing rheology or interpreting short-period forced elements changes that estimate. A thermal/tidal calculation must establish whether the intended seas and aquifers survive without runaway heating or atmospheric loss.

The host’s high-energy spectrum, atmosphere, particle belts, current paths, surface dose, and escape rate are unresolved acceptance criteria. Surface radiation resistance cannot replace shielding. Native habitability is canon; human breathability is not.

### Atmosphere

A normalized **1.3-atm wet-air reference** is N₂ 94.583%, CO₂ 2%, H₂O 2% with local variability, O₂ 0.5%, Ar 0.9%, combined NO/NO₂ 100 ppm, CH₄ 50 ppm, and NH₃ 20 ppm. The moon page gives corrected partial pressures. Nitrogen is the bulk gas; reactive nitrogen dominates particular biological electron-acceptor pathways, not total atmospheric mass.

HNO₃ aerosols are specified through future particle/loading models rather than a fictitious gas partial pressure. The old **10–30 DU ozone column** remains a test target, not an established UV shield: NOₓ-driven ozone destruction and acid chemistry must be included. Violet and green phenomena are principally candidate auroral/airglow emissions, not automatically colors produced by acid or ozone clouds.

## Biosphere and Pedonpods

*Main articles: [[Life on Nibbles]], [[Pedonpods]]*

Nibbles’ water-solvent carbon biosphere originated in primordial sub-ice habitats. Deep aquifers and geothermal communities preserve that legacy. Later surface habitats support nitrate/nitrite respiration, methane-oxidizing communities, mineral redox guilds, atmospheric oxidant processors, and complex symbioses.

The main external biospheric contribution is **radiation-driven oxidant formation**, followed by delivery to food webs. **Aurorotrophy/voltfarming** is a supplementary, speculative mechanism needing accessible potentials, usable current or photons, conversion efficiency, and repair costs. Magnetic orientation, stored charge, and oxidant recycling do not create indefinite power.

| Functional guild | Retained ecological role |
| --- | --- |
| Nitrovores | Nitrate/nitrite and related acceptor respiration using reduced donors |
| Methanoclasts | Methane oxidation in seep and mat habitats |
| Sulfopteroids | Specified sulfur transformations in vent/mineral settings |
| Aeronoids / ozoneclimbers | Upper-atmospheric oxidant capture, transformation, and downward redistribution |
| Aurorotrophs / voltfarmers | Supplementary current or photon harvesting; mechanism unverified |
| Symbiomorphs | Multicellular partnerships distributing redox functions |

Oxidants **NO₃⁻, NO₂⁻, NO/NO₂, HNO₃, H₂O₂**, and short-lived **ONOO⁻** must be produced or replaced. Reduced donors **NH₃/NH₄⁺, CH₄, H₂, H₂S, organics, Fe²⁺ and reduced minerals** need geological supply or energetically supported regeneration. Water, phosphate, and trace nutrients require circulation and replacement of losses. N₂, CO₂, H₂O, sulfate, and oxidized minerals are exchanged products/reservoirs; closing a material cycle does not eliminate its energy requirement. Balanced examples and the resource ledger are in [[Life on Nibbles]].

**Pedonpods**, *Pedonpoda volticognita*, are a species of 1.5–2.2 m, 60–120 kg hexapodal centauroids. Their rear limbs store energy for leaps, middle legs stabilize them, and forelimbs manipulate objects. Cranial mineral-bearing tendrils provide field cues; an electrochemical nervous system processes those cues rather than external fields writing memories directly.

Their nitrate stores, ferric fallback chamber, conductive/insulating composite armor, and symbiotic gardens support pulse-active behavior. Ferric respiration still needs donors and acceptors. Armor reclamation recovers iron nutrients, not missing oxidants. Nest construction modifies local electrical and chemical conditions, not a whole planetary magnetic field.

The later species page governs their current stage: emerging sapience, early toolmaking, patterned communication, cultivated symbionts, ritual shedding, and nascent technoculture. An established industrial civilization is not assigned. They follow terrain and resource fronts while timing activity to illumination, eclipses, and magnetic conditions; routine global pursuit of the rapidly moving terminator is not required.

## Magnetic variability and observation

Magnetic “seasons” is an ecological name for changes in particle access, plasma supply, field tilt and rotation, reconnection, orbital geometry, and accretion variability. Periods, precession rates, amplitudes, and phase relations remain unspecified. Tom’s exterior field belongs to its plasma environment; Jerry’s internal dynamo and the external flow must be modeled separately.

The near-side sky contains stationary Jerry, moving Tom, varying planetlight, and intermittent aurorae. The far side lacks a direct view of Jerry but still experiences direct disk illumination. Lensing can distort background sources; unaided bright arcs, “Einstein ripples,” or a particular visible disk color require source contrast and a radiative-transfer model. Conceptual illustrations preserve the intended scene without supplying physical scale or a validated spectrum.

## Preliminary orbital screening

A direct planar Newtonian three-body screen used a 5×10⁶ M☉ Tom, a 12 M♃ Jerry, and a 0.4 M⊕ Nibbles, with nominally circular initial orbits at 50 AU and 1,000,000 km. This checks immediate orbital geometry and repeated external perturbations; it is not a full relativistic, dissipative, multi-moon evolution model.

| Trial | Duration | Moon–Jerry sampled distance range | Maximum osculating moon eccentricity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Initial moon phase 0 | 10,000 years | 995,550–1,002,031 km | 0.005275 |
| Initial moon phase 2.399963 radians | 1,000 years | 996,762–1,003,739 km | 0.005513 |
| Initial moon phase 4.799926 radians | 1,000 years | 997,170–1,006,644 km | 0.006685 |
| Phase 0, half timestep | 1,000 years | 995,494–1,002,027 km | 0.005271 |

No trial reached the screening termination boundaries of 0.0016 AU (about 239,357 km) or 0.02 AU (about 2.99 million km) moon–planet separation. These are screening thresholds, not a fitted stability edge. All sampled separations remained comfortably outside the nominal Roche estimate and inside the conventional prograde region.

The integrator used direct pairwise Newtonian accelerations and velocity-Verlet steps of 10⁻⁵ year, halved for the convergence comparison, with diagnostics every ten steps. Units were AU, Julian year, and M☉, with G=4π²; 1 M♃=0.000954588 M☉, 1 M⊕=3.0034896×10⁻⁶ M☉, and 1 AU=149,597,870.7 km. Initially Tom was at rest at the origin, Jerry at (50,0) AU with circular relative speed, and Nibbles at a radius of one million km around Jerry with a circular prograde relative speed. Moon phase was k×2.3999632297 radians for k=0,1,2. The full three bodies were subsequently allowed to move.

Osculating eccentricity contains short-period external forcing and is not automatically the free eccentricity appropriate for a two-body constant-Q heating formula. The sampled values therefore flag a tidal-model requirement rather than supply a validated heating rate. The screen omits inclinations, Kerr/relativistic terms, tides, spin/oblateness, other moons, rings, encounters, accretion evolution, and mass loss. It does not establish survival over the system age or through capture. At 50 AU, GM/(rc²) is approximately 0.001: relativity is smaller than in the old layout but remains a precision and secular-evolution task.

## Model status and remaining work

The current architecture removes the old Roche/Hill contradiction and supplies a candidate thermal budget. It does **not** establish a fully solved habitable black-hole system. Required work includes:

- A physically realizable spectrum and angular emission at the adopted luminosity, with surface dose and atmospheric retention compatible with life.
- A radiative–convective and circulation model reaching the retained regional seas and cold provinces; photochemical ozone, NOₓ, acid, and aerosol calculations.
- Long-term strong-field three-dimensional dynamics, tides, spin evolution, and a full moon/ring inventory; a capture trajectory preserving primordial Nibbles.
- Oxidant production and destruction yields, geological donor supply, nutrient transport, repair costs, and energy sufficient for the retained food webs and large Pedonpods.
- Exact capture epoch, parent galaxy and Earth distance, DGS-17 density and mass profile, jet/flare history, and evolutionary chronology.

These uncertainties are explicit research tasks rather than claims that all mechanisms have been demonstrated. The old short periods, subsolar luminosity, 80° Jerry, permanent reflected twilight, and asserted gigayear stability are intentionally superseded.

## Scientific significance and comparisons

The setting explores habitable moons around nonstellar primaries, nitrogen-redox ecology, radiation adaptation, and the separate roles of field sensing, current harvesting, and atmospheric oxidant production. It is a speculative case study rather than an observed “confirmed planet” system. That inherited category is source metadata only.

Real comparison topics include [HLX-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HLX-1), [Andromeda’s nucleus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andromeda_Galaxy#Nucleus), [NGC 4395](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NGC_4395), and [NGC 404](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NGC_404). Their existence is not evidence of Tom or its biosphere.

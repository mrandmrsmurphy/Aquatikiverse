**Jerry** is a warm super-Jovian gas giant orbiting the low-mass supermassive black hole [[Tom (black hole)|Tom]]. Once a stable planet of a K-type star, Jerry was flung into a radically new context following the tidal disruption of its native system. It now serves as the primary planetary body within the [[Tom and Jerry and Nibbles]] system, with a substantial ring complex, an intense magnetosphere, and a habitable moon: [[Nibbles (habitable moon)|Nibbles]].

## History

Jerry began its existence orbiting a K-type dwarf star at roughly 3 AU, as part of a binary star system. With a mass ~12 times that of Jupiter and a radius of ~1.1 R♃, Jerry was slightly inflated by internal heating and retained a complex satellite system. Its atmosphere was enriched in ammonia, methane, nitrogen, and photochemically active hydrocarbons, giving rise to a high Bond albedo (~0.6) and a pale, pearl-like appearance.

After billions of years in its original environment, external encounters altered the stellar system’s trajectory. Jerry and its primordial moons, including Nibbles, became bound to Tom through an unresolved sequence of multibody exchange and orbital energy loss. A molecular-cloud encounter is a possible precursor perturbation, not a sufficient standalone capture mechanism. The K-dwarf’s later tidal destruction occurred after the planetary subsystem had detached; the moon system is not assumed to have survived a star-disrupting close passage.

Today, Jerry’s reference orbit is 50 AU, with a period near 57.75 days and small reference eccentricity. Its retained approximately 8-hour spin is independent of Tom’s orbital period. The capture and settling episode disrupted the precursor system, but the setting preserves Nibbles as a surviving primordial moon. Details of retention and settling remain under investigation.

## Physical Characteristics

Jerry’s outer envelope is composed primarily of hydrogen and helium, with N₂, ammonia, methane, and sulfur-bearing compounds contributing to cloud opacity and coloration. The planet supports high-altitude ammonia ice clouds, photochemical hazes, and sulfur radicals that interact with its intense auroral and magnetospheric activity.

Beneath its cloud tops lies a deep and convective metallic hydrogen shell, surrounding a dense rock–ice core of approximately 27 M⊕. The adopted internal luminosity is 2.2×10¹⁸ W, corresponding to about 28 W/m² at its 1.1 R♃ surface and an intrinsic effective temperature near 149 K. Contraction, capture-era heating, and any present dissipation need an age-dependent cooling model; a persistent ohmic or tidal contribution is not inferred without an energy source.

At 50 AU, free-falling spacecraft experience small human-scale differential accelerations, while tides still constrain moon orbits. The planet’s deep H/He envelope, compressed heavy-element core, and metallic-hydrogen region remain the working structure. The adopted mass and radius give approximately 11.2 g/cm³ density, 246 m/s² gravity, and 197 km/s escape speed.

## Magnetosphere

Jerry’s working cloud-top equatorial field is approximately 3,000 μT (3 mT). Its multipolar contributions, tilt, and plasma loading are not yet fixed. An ideal dipole extrapolation to Nibbles’ one-million-kilometre orbit gives roughly 1.5 μT before tilt, currents, and compression; this is a field-scale estimate rather than a complete magnetosphere.

Jerry interacts with a dilute external plasma, moon ejecta, and material from the wider accretion environment. The boundary radius must be calculated from magnetic and external pressures before continuous coverage of Nibbles is assumed. Particle trapping, precipitation, atmospheric shielding, and radiation injury are distinct outcomes.

Auroral emission from H₂, H₃⁺, nitrogen-bearing species, and sulfur-bearing plasma remains part of the visual setting. Individual bands and colors require excitation and atmospheric models. Electrical power comes from plasma motion, rotation, or external radiation/particles; a static magnetic field is not an energy reservoir that powers life indefinitely.

## Moons

Nibbles is the principal habitable moon and a retained primordial companion. The older local census of **at least ten major moons** remains a provisional count of substantial survivors, not a fully specified set of Galilean-scale bodies. No count is silently reduced here: the other moons need names or identifiers, masses, and orbits before the census can be treated as a validated architecture.

Other survivors include primordial moons, captured irregulars, and at least one retrograde fragment. At 50 AU, Jerry’s elementary Hill radius is about 6.84 million km. Conventional circular, coplanar prograde stability estimates extend to roughly 3.35 million km, before mutual perturbations, eccentricities, and relativity are included. This leaves much more room than the old compact layout, but an orbital inventory and tidal history remain necessary.

### Rings

The three-belt concept and the destroyed moon **Thyre** are preserved. These are provisional radial zones chosen inside Nibbles’ orbit; ring confinement and precession have not been simulated.

| **B — Gossamer** | ~1° | ~120,000–450,000 km | Micro-moon impacts and dust ejection | Fine charged grains, optically faint and variable; radial overlap with A is permitted |

Shepherds and resonances are candidate confinement mechanisms. Optical depths, particle sizes, masses, source rates, collision times, thermal survival, and damping are unspecified. Warm exposed ice loses material; scattering and thermal emission are described separately.

## Current Environment

Jerry orbits at a 50 AU reference distance around 5×10⁶ M☉ Tom, whose adopted bolometric luminosity is 2,000 L☉. The isotropic reference irradiance is approximately 1,089 W/m², or 0.80 Earth flux. Actual irradiation depends on source spectrum, angular emission, and orbital alignment.

With Bond albedo 0.6, Jerry’s stellar-equivalent equilibrium contribution is about 209 K. Combining absorbed external radiation with the working internal luminosity gives a total effective reference near 221 K. These temperatures do not prescribe cloud-deck depths or prove ammonia-cloud chemistry.

The circular speed is approximately 0.0314c. A Schwarzschild circular-orbit clock reference is about 0.15% slower than a distant stationary clock; Kerr spin, geometry, and observer conventions require more precise treatment. The former 0.2c speed and few-percent clock effect are superseded.

Nibbles’ orbit at one million km is approximately 0.146 of Jerry’s Hill radius and beyond its approximately 231,000 km fluid Roche estimate. Jerry’s 8-hour spin is faster than the moon’s approximately 44.76-hour orbit, so the working prograde satellite lies outside the synchronous radius and tends to migrate outward under ordinary tidal torque. Rates require dissipation and spin evolution; circularity alone does not freeze the orbit.
